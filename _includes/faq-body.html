Should I choose an automatic or semi-automatic bike in Hanoi?

Automatic scooters (like Honda Vision or Air Blade) are the easiest for stop-start city traffic because there is no gear shifting. Semi-automatic bikes (like Honda Wave) still have gears but no hand clutch, and many riders find them lighter and simpler to park. Choose based on your riding experience.

Licences and legal rules

Do I need a licence to ride a motorbike in Vietnam?

For bikes above the moped class, yes — you need a licence valid for that class of motorcycle. Mopeds — two- or three-wheel vehicles with engines of 50cc or less (or electric motors up to 4 kW) designed for no more than 50 km/h — require no driving licence, though riders must be at least 16. Normal road speed limits still apply to every bike. See Vietnam motorbike licence classes explained.

What licence do tourists need to ride a motorbike in Vietnam?

Tourists need either a Vietnamese motorcycle licence, or a licence from their home country plus an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, carried together, with a matching motorcycle category. Read the tourist riding guide for the details.

What is an A1 licence in Vietnam?

A1 is the standard Vietnamese motorcycle licence class. Under the current law (effective 1 January 2025), A1 covers two-wheel motorbikes up to and including 125cc, or electric two-wheelers up to and including 11 kW. See what an A1 licence lets you ride.

What can I ride with an A1 licence?

An A1 licence covers two-wheelers up to and including 125cc or electric two-wheelers up to and including 11 kW, from age 18. Bigger bikes need the A class. See A1 licence rules.

Is an International Driving Permit (IDP) valid in Vietnam?

Yes, but only if it was issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, is carried together with the underlying national licence, and covers the motorcycle category you are riding. See IDP rules for motorbikes in Vietnam.

Does Vietnam accept the 1949 Geneva Convention IDP?

No. Vietnam recognises 1968 Vienna Convention permits, not 1949 Geneva Convention permits, for driving on an IDP. Check which convention your country issues before relying on an IDP — see the Vienna Convention guide.

What is the 1968 Vienna Convention and why does it matter for riding in Vietnam?

It is the international treaty on road traffic that Vietnam acceded to in 2014. Vietnamese law gives domestic effect to it by recognising IDPs from member states when carried with the national licence. See the Vienna Convention and IDPs in Vietnam.

Do I need a licence for a 50cc bike in Vietnam?

No driving licence is required for mopeds (xe gắn máy): two- or three-wheel vehicles with engines of 50cc or less (or electric motors up to 4 kW) designed for no more than 50 km/h. Riders must be at least 16, and ordinary road speed limits still apply — the 50 km/h figure is a design classification, not a licence to ride at 50 everywhere. See 50cc licence rules.

Do I need a licence for an electric motorbike in Vietnam?

It depends on the motor's power. Electric two-wheelers up to 4 kW designed for no more than 50 km/h are treated as mopeds with no licence required; above that they fall under the A1 (up to 11 kW) or A licence classes. Pedal-assist electric bicycles are a separate non-motorised category. See electric bike licence rules.

What happens if I ride without the correct licence in Vietnam?

Riding without a licence appropriate for the bike is fined under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP: 2–4 million VND for bikes of 125cc or under, and 6–8 million VND for bigger bikes. See motorbike fines in Vietnam.

Safety and traffic rules

Is a helmet required by law in Vietnam?

Yes. Riders and passengers of motorbikes, mopeds and pedal e-bikes must wear standard-compliant helmets, properly fastened. The fine for a helmet offence is 400,000–600,000 VND. See the Vietnam helmet law explained.

Can I drink alcohol and ride a motorbike in Vietnam?

No. Vietnam applies zero tolerance to alcohol for all riders — any detectable alcohol is an offence. Motorbike fines start at 2–3 million VND in the lowest band and rise with alcohol level, with licence suspension possible. See drink-driving limits for motorbikes.

What are the most common motorbike fines in Vietnam?

Common fines under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP include helmet offences (400,000–600,000 VND), phone use while riding (800,000–1,000,000 VND), and red-light, wrong-way or sidewalk riding (4–6 million VND). See the fines overview.

Can I use my phone while riding?

No. Using a phone while riding a motorbike is fined 800,000–1,000,000 VND under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. If you need navigation, mount the phone and set the route before you move.

What happens if I run a red light or ride the wrong way?

Red-light running, riding against traffic and riding on the sidewalk are each fined 4–6 million VND for motorbikes under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. See the fines overview.

Do passengers also need helmets?

Yes. The helmet law covers both riders and passengers, including on pedal e-bikes. Children under 6 are exempt from the passenger helmet fine, but carrying them requires appropriate safety measures.

What should I check before riding a rented motorbike in Hanoi?

Check brakes, lights, horn, tyre condition and fuel level before leaving, and photograph any existing scratches. Practise the clutch or throttle in a quiet spot first, and confirm your licence situation is valid for the bike you rented.
