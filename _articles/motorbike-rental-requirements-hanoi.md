[Shops in Hanoi]({{ '/topics/rental/' | relative_url }}) ask for very little before handing over a bike. The law asks for more. Here is the honest split between the two.

## The short answer

Hanoi motorbike rental requirements come as two different lists, and only one of them is law.

- **The law asks the rider for** a licence valid in Vietnam that fits the bike (none for the moped class), a minimum age for the bike's class, a helmet, and the vehicle's papers on the road.
- **A shop asks the renter for** whatever its own contract needs: an identity document for the agreement, a deposit and the rental price. Those are the shop's terms, so confirm them with the shop you choose.
- **A rental agreement is not a licence.** Renting and riding legally are separate questions, and only the second one is answered by the law.

## Requirements at a glance

The table lists what the law sets for the rider (Law 36/2024/QH15, as consolidated in 55/VBHN-VPQH).

| Requirement | Applies to | Article | Read more |
|---|---|---|---|
| A licence valid in Vietnam that fits the bike | Riders above the moped class | 56 and 57 | Licence by bike, below |
| Minimum age | Set by the class of bike | 59(1) | Age, below |
| No licence for a moped | Design speed up to 50 km/h, with an engine up to 50 cc or an electric motor up to 4 kW | 34(1)(g) and 56(4) | Licence by bike, below |
| An IDP with the national licence | Foreign riders on a 1968 Vienna Convention licence | 57(6)(b) and (8) | A licence from abroad, below |
| A helmet | Rider and passenger | 33(2) | Helmets, below |
| Licence and vehicle papers on the road | The rider | 56(1) | Papers to carry, below |

The shop's own terms follow in the next section.

## What the rental shop requires

In practice: an [identity document for the rental agreement]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), a deposit - typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions - and the agreed rental price up front. Payments run by cash, bank transfer or QR code. A phone number lets the shop reach you about the bike. Nothing else is normally needed to sign the contract.

None of this is a rule of Vietnamese law, and each shop writes its own terms. Which end of the deposit range applies turns on the bike and the terms of the rental, so get the figure for your bike before you pay. The checklist near the end of this page turns the open points into questions you can put to a shop.

## What Vietnamese law requires to ride

The shop's requirements and the law's requirements are different lists. To ride legally you need:

- A [licence valid in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}). For most visitors this means a home-country motorcycle licence plus a [1968-convention IDP]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}), or a Vietnamese licence. At 50 cc or less, no driving licence is needed, provided the design speed is also 50 km/h or below.
- Minimum age: 16 for a moped (50 cc or less), 18 for an A1-class motorcycle.
- Compulsory third-party insurance on the vehicle - ask to see the certificate.
- A helmet for rider and passenger.

### Licence class by type of bike

The class depends on the machine, not on its model name. Articles 34(1)(g), 57(1) and 59(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 give the groups a renter meets:

| Bike | Licence | Minimum age |
|---|---|---|
| Moped: design speed up to 50 km/h, with an engine up to 50 cc or an electric motor up to 4 kW | None | 16 |
| Two-wheel motorbike up to 125 cc, or electric up to 11 kW | Class A1 | 18 |
| Two-wheel motorbike above 125 cc, or electric above 11 kW | Class A | 18 |
| Three-wheel motorbike | Class B1 | 18 |

Ask what engine size the particular bike has and compare it with your licence class, because a model name alone does not settle the question. A Vietnamese licence issued before 2025 keeps the scope it had then, as the licence-classes guide linked in the first point above sets out.

### Age

Article 59(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the ages in the class table above, and Article 56(4) requires a moped rider to know the traffic rules and have the skill to control the vehicle.

A rider of 16 or 17 is therefore limited to the moped class. Ask whether the shop applies a minimum age of its own above the legal one before you travel. The [minimum-age guide]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the penalty for riding below the legal age.

### A licence from abroad

Article 57(6) of the same law names the licences that count on Vietnamese territory. For a foreign renter the routes are:

- **A Vietnamese licence**, obtained by passing the test or by exchange, as the next subsection sets out.
- **A national licence plus an IDP**, both fitting the bike, issued by a state party to the 1968 Vienna Convention (Article 57(6)(b)). Carry both: Article 57(8)(b) requires it, and under Article 57(8)(a) the IDP can run for three years at most from its issue date and has to stay within the validity of the national licence.
- **A licence from a country with a mutual-recognition treaty or agreement** with Vietnam, or one recognised under a treaty Vietnam has joined (Article 57(6)(c) and (d)). Check any such route against an official source first.

An IDP alone does not make you legal, and a 1949-convention permit is not the 1968 kind that Article 57 names. [Which countries' IDPs are valid in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the member states. An IDP issued by Vietnam itself under the 1968 Convention is not valid inside Vietnam (Article 30(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA).

### Exchanging a foreign licence

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, in force from 1 July 2026, governs exchange. Under Article 24(1)(a), a foreigner residing, working or studying in Vietnam with a national licence still within its term can be considered for exchange into the matching Vietnamese class. The foreigner must also hold a diplomatic or official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more remaining, or a valid permanent residence card.

The exchange route excludes IDPs and any foreign licence past its term (Article 24(1)(c)). The Vietnamese licence issued runs only for the term shown on the qualifying document and on the foreign licence (Article 24(5)(a)), so a rental that outlasts your visa is worth planning around.

### Papers to carry while riding

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists what a driver carries on the road: the vehicle registration certificate, the driving licence that fits the vehicle, the technical inspection certificate where the law requires one for that vehicle, and the compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate of the vehicle owner. Where papers are already linked to an electronic identification account, that account can be used to show them instead (Article 56(3)). The [documents-to-carry guide]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what follows when one is missing.

The licence is yours to bring. The vehicle papers belong to the bike, so at handover ask how the registration and compulsory insurance certificates will be shown if you are stopped. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance and customers arrange their own, so read your travel or medical policy for motorcycle exclusions before you ride.

### Helmets

Article 33(2) makes a helmet compulsory for the rider and any passenger on a motorbike or moped: it must meet the national technical regulation, and the strap must be fastened properly. Whether the rental comes with helmets is for the shop to say.

## The gap, and what it costs

A rental agreement does not check or replace your licence: the rule applies to the rider on the road, and the traffic police enforce it. Riding with no valid licence is an offence under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and the bike can be held until the situation is resolved. The fine amounts sit in that decree, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended from 15 August 2026, so confirm any figure against the current decree text. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains what counts as riding without a licence.

An unlicensed rider who also causes an accident faces much worse, including potential criminal liability. Renting is easy; riding legally is the part only you control. The related guide on renting without a licence gives the longer account of the risks. The licence-free moped class in the table above is the lawful alternative if your paperwork does not reach a motorcycle.

## Requirements that are discretion, not law

Beyond the law, a shop's own conditions are matters to ask about, not rules to assume: whether it has a minimum age above the legal one, what it expects of a rider new to scooters, which identity document it accepts and how it takes the deposit. Some ask to hold your passport against the deposit; [do Hanoi rentals keep your passport?]({{ '/articles/do-rentals-keep-your-passport-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains why you should insist on a cash deposit instead.

## A checklist before you book

### Licence and age

- Does my licence cover the engine size of the exact bike, and is it still valid on the day I plan to return it?
- If I use an IDP, is it the 1968 kind, and do I carry the national licence it goes with?
- Is every rider at least the legal age for the bike, and does the shop set a higher minimum?

### Documents and money

- Which identity document does the shop accept for the agreement?
- What deposit applies to this bike, and what does the contract say about when and how it comes back?
- How will I pay the rental price and the deposit?
- What does the contract say about a late return, damage, a lost key and consumables such as tubes and tyres?

### The bike and the papers

- Can I see the registration and compulsory insurance certificates for this bike?
- Does the rental include a helmet for every rider?
- What does my own travel or medical insurance say about riding a motorbike?

Bring the passport, bring a valid licence combination, budget the deposit, and the requirements are met. Confirm the shop's own terms first. [Confirm current bike availability]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) with the shop before you travel, because availability of any model can change.

## Quick answers

### Can I rent with only a passport?

A passport is an identity document, not a licence. Which papers a shop wants to see is for the shop to say, but the law is the same whatever a shop accepts: above the moped class you need a licence valid in Vietnam that fits the bike, or you are riding unlicensed.

### Is the deposit the same for every bike?

No single figure applies. The deposit depends on the vehicle and the conditions of the rental and typically sits between 2,000,000 and 5,000,000 VND, so ask for the amount for your bike before you pay. [The deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains what the money is for.

### Does an Indian or other foreign licence work on a Hanoi scooter?

It depends on the country that issued the licence and on its class, not on the nationality of the rider. Check whether the country is a party to the 1968 Vienna Convention or has a recognition treaty with Vietnam, and that the licence covers the size of the bike. If it does not count, the options include exchanging it once you qualify as a resident or renting in the licence-free moped class.

### If two of us will ride, who needs papers?

Every person who rides above the moped class needs a licence that fits the bike, carried on the road; a foreign national licence goes with its 1968 IDP unless a recognition treaty applies. Every rider must also meet the age rule. A passenger who is not riding needs no licence but must wear a helmet. [The guide for foreign renters]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the foreign-licence cases in more detail.
