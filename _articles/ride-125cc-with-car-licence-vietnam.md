No. A car licence — Vietnamese or foreign — does not cover a 125 cc motorbike in Vietnam. The [licence classes]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) are separate by design: Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines car classes (B, C, D and the trailer classes) and motorbike classes (A1, A, B1) as distinct categories covering distinct vehicle types. Nothing in the car classes reaches two-wheeled machines.

## What Article 57 gives class B, and what it gives A1

The legal answer sits in two sentences of the Law. This page reads Law 36/2024/QH15 in its consolidated text, 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes the amendments of Law 118/2025/QH15; the English renderings are our translations of the Vietnamese. Article 57(1)(a) defines the class that holds a 125 cc machine:

> Hạng A1 cấp cho người lái xe mô tô hai bánh có dung tích xi-lanh đến 125 cm3 hoặc có công suất động cơ điện đến 11 kW;

In English: class A1 is issued to a driver of a two-wheeled motorbike with a cylinder capacity of up to 125 cm³, or an electric motor of up to 11 kW. Article 57(1)(d) defines class B, the ordinary car class:

> Hạng B cấp cho người lái xe ô tô chở người đến 08 chỗ (không kể chỗ của người lái xe); xe ô tô tải và ô tô chuyên dùng có khối lượng toàn bộ theo thiết kế đến 3.500 kg; các loại xe ô tô quy định cho giấy phép lái xe hạng B kéo rơ moóc có khối lượng toàn bộ theo thiết kế đến 750 kg;

In English: class B is issued to a driver of a passenger car of up to 8 seats (not counting the driver's), of a goods car or special-purpose car with a design total mass of up to 3,500 kg, and of the cars of class B when they tow a trailer with a design total mass of up to 750 kg. Class B names cars and nothing else: no motorbike, no two-wheeler and no mention of class A1.

### How the Law says one class includes another

The Law is explicit when a class takes in another. Class A includes "the kinds of vehicle prescribed for class A1" (Article 57(1)(b)), and so does class B1 (57(1)(c)). Class C1 includes the vehicles of class B (57(1)(đ)), class C those of B and C1 (57(1)(e)), and the D classes continue the chain. No car-class point, (d) to (p), names A1, A or B1, so the car classes are a separate branch of the list, not a higher rung of the same ladder.

Article 56(1) then asks every driver for a licence with points left, in force and suited to the type of vehicle being driven, with one exception: the moped rider of clause 4. A holder may drive the classes written on the licence (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(4)), so the question at a stop is whether those classes suit the machine.

## What happens if you try

Under the fine schedule of the 2024 decree text (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP), riding a 125 cc scooter with only a car licence is treated as not meeting licence conditions for that machine — the same band as [having no licence at all]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), whose figures follow that 2024 text, not the amendment made by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, in force from 15 August 2026. This page quotes no amount, so check any figure against the current decree. The checkpoint does not average the two categories; it checks the licence against the machine in front of it.

The duty falls on the rider: Article 56(1) is addressed to the driver, and Article 9(7) lists among the prohibited acts handing a motor vehicle to a person who does not meet the legal conditions to ride it. Insurance is a separate matter. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance; read your own policy for any licence conditions before you ride.

## Where the confusion comes from

Some countries fold light motorbikes into car licences, and visitors assume the same carry-over. Vietnam does not. The other common confusion is the exchange: a foreigner [exchanging a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) receives Vietnamese classes matching the original — a car-only licence exchanges into car classes only, with no motorbike class appearing by accident.

## A foreign car licence and an IDP

The foreign version of the question gets the same answer for a car-only card. Each foreign route in Article 57(6)(b) to (d), and the duty to carry both documents in Article 57(8)(b), ask that the licence suit the type or class of vehicle; [the guide to IDP rules for motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) sets out both provisions.

A car-only category is a licence for cars. Each document has to suit the vehicle, so on our reading a car-only national licence, with or without an international driving permit, does not suit a motorbike. No text read for this page maps a foreign category letter to A1, so compare the category lists yourself and ask the issuing authority.

A home card that also carries a motorcycle category is a different case. The motorcycle entry, not the name of the card, has to suit a 125 cc machine, and no text read for this page says how an official reads a foreign motorcycle category against A1. Ask the traffic police for a written answer before you build a trip on it.

## The one thing a car licence does help with

Article 4(1)(c) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA waives part of the test for a car-licence holder:

> Thí sinh dự sát hạch lái xe hạng A1, A có giấy phép lái xe ô tô còn hiệu lực được miễn sát hạch lý thuyết.

In English: a candidate sitting for a class A1 or A driving test who holds a car driving licence in force is exempt from the theory test. The Circular is in force from 1 July 2026 (Article 41(1)). Under Article 41(6), Article 4(1) of the earlier Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, which carries the same exemption, remains in force until 28 February 2027 inclusive. Three limits apply.

- **Only the theory paper goes.** A motorbike candidate still rides the practical tests (Article 4(2)(a)), and the dossier still carries a training certificate or confirmation of a completed course (Article 16(2)(a)) and a valid health certificate (Article 16(2)(b)); [the A1 test guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) gives the dates on which each test part applies.
- **The clause says "a car driving licence in force".** It does not say whether a licence issued abroad counts, or one issued by exchange. Ask the traffic police department in writing before relying on the waiver.
- **It lifts a test requirement, not the licence rule.** Until a motorbike class (A1, A or B1) is on your card, Article 56(1) leaves a 125 cc outside what a car licence allows.

## Letters that mislead: B1 and the dates on a card

The letter B1 needs care. Article 57(1)(c) gives class B1 to drivers of three-wheeled motorbikes together with the kinds of vehicle prescribed for class A1, so a B1 under the current Law does cover a 125 cc two-wheeler.

A card issued before the Law took effect on 1 January 2025 (Article 88(1)) is different. While it has not been exchanged, Article 89(2)(đ), (e) and (g) lets an old B1 or B2 go on driving passenger cars of up to 8 seats and goods vehicles under 3,500 kg (tractors too under (e) and (g); (đ) is the automatic-only B1), and none of the points of Article 89(2) that deal with a car class names a two-wheeler. The old three-wheel class was A3 (Article 89(2)(c)), which Article 89(3)(c) turns into the new B1 on exchange. Read the issue date on the card as well as the letter.

## What to do instead

Three routes exist. If you hold a foreign motorbike licence with a visa or temporary residence card that still has 90 days or more left (or a permanent residence card), exchange it. If you hold only a car licence, you sit the A1 test as anyone else does, with one difference: a car licence in force waives the theory paper (whether a foreign one counts is not stated), though the practical tests are still sat. If you are a tourist with no motorbike entitlement at all, you cannot legally ride a 125 cc rental in Vietnam, and no rental paperwork changes that.

The 125 cc question has a short answer wherever it is asked, and the answer is always the class on the licence, not the engine size of the habit.

### The A1 route in outline

[The step-by-step A1 guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers age 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), the training course, the dossier and the test parts in order. Article 4(1)(c) drops the theory paper for a car-licence holder, and the sources read for this page give that holder no shorter course or different dossier.

Until a motorbike class (A1, A or B1) is on your card, the legal choices are a bike inside every limit of the moped definition in Article 34(1)(g) (an engine of 50 cm³ or less, or an electric motor of 4 kW or less, and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h), which needs no licence from age 16 (Articles 56(1) and 59(1)(a)), or not riding.

## Questions people ask

### Does an automatic scooter need a different licence from a manual motorbike?

Not under the Law. Classes A1 and A are set by engine capacity or motor power, with no mention of the gearbox (Article 57(1)(a) and (b)), and a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle that is not a moped is a motorcycle (Article 34(1)(e)). An automatic scooter with a 110 cm³ engine therefore needs a motorbike class.

### Does a car licence let me ride a 50cc bike?

The car licence is not what makes it lawful. A moped needs no licence of any class (Article 56(1)), only the knowledge of the traffic rules, the skill and the age and health that Article 56(4) asks for (age 16, Article 59(1)(a)), but the bike has to meet every limit, including the 50 km/h design speed, so a "50cc" badge is not enough. [What counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) gives the definition and four checks.

### Can I ride an electric scooter on a car licence?

A moped-class electric scooter, at 4 kW or less and a design speed of up to 50 km/h, needs no licence. A stronger or faster one is a motorbike, in class A1 up to 11 kW, and a car licence does not supply that class. [The electric motorbike and car licence guide]({{ '/articles/can-you-ride-electric-motorbike-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) puts it in electric terms.

### Is there a learner permit I can ride on while I study for the A1?

No motorbike learner permit exists in the texts read: [the learner permit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) records that neither the Law nor Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA names one. Above the moped class, the licence is needed whenever the bike is on a public road, so a car licence does not open a supervised stage for a 125 cc.
