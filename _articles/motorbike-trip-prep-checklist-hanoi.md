A good motorbike trip out of Hanoi is mostly decided before you roll out of the driveway. Whether you are riding to Tam Dao for the day or planning a week in the mountains, the same preparation sequence applies — bike, papers, route, weather, gear, and the rental conversation.

## The bike

Two days before, do the mechanical basics: tyres (pressures and tread), chain (lube and slack), brakes (pads and lever feel), oil level, lights and horn. For anything beyond a day trip, read the servicing-before-long-trip guide and have a shop look over the bike — a loose chain in the mountains is a trip-ending problem. Carry the small roadside kit: pump or puncture kit, basic tools, spare fuses and a tow strap.

If you are renting, inspect the bike at pickup as if you were buying it — photograph existing damage, test both brakes, and ask when the bike was last serviced. Tell the shop where you are going: distance limits on day rentals are a real thing, and a bike that is fine in the city may not be the right bike for the hills.

## Documents

Carry your licence (and IDP where required), the bike’s registration papers, your rental agreement, and insurance details if the bike has them — see the documents-to-carry guide for the full list. Rural police checks are more likely on festival days and main corridors.

## Route and timing

Plan the route the night before — not which turns, but the shape of the day: distance, the halfway point, the fuel plan, and the time you must turn around to be home before dark. Rural roads after dark are a different, worse experience (see the unlit-rural-roads guide). Build in a margin: at an average of around 30 km/h including stops, 100 km each way is a long day.

## Weather and season

Check the forecast for the destination, not Hanoi — mountains make their own weather (the riding-seasons guide covers the calendar). If storms are forecast for the afternoon, the trip happens in the morning.

## Personal kit

- Water and food for longer gaps between towns
- Rain layers and a dry change
- First-aid basics (the riders’ first-aid guide lists them)
- Cash — small notes for parking, ferries and rural shops
- Phone charged, offline maps downloaded, phone mount fitted
