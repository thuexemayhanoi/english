Twelve points to zero, and the licence switches off. Under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a licence that has [lost all 12 points]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-points-system-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cannot be used for driving — not for a scooter to the shop, not "just locally". Riding on it is treated as riding without a valid licence, with the corresponding fines.

The Law's own wording is narrower and more exact than that headline. Article 58(3) bars the holder from driving a road vehicle on that licence, and Article 56(1) requires the driver, moped riders aside, to hold a licence that has points left, is in effect and matches the vehicle. The penalty belongs to Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which this page does not quote. A later decree, 238/2026/NĐ-CP, amended it and applies from 15 August 2026, so read the current text for the fine and for what it says about a licence with no points left. The first paragraph's summary that this counts as riding without a valid licence is not a quotation: the Law itself says only what Articles 56(1) and 58(3) say, and the decree text was not read for this page. Its "scooter" also means one that needs a licence; whether Article 58(3) reaches a moped, which needs none, is a question the texts leave open.

This page follows a licence from the day its balance reaches zero: the bar, the wait, the knowledge test, what a new card does and the questions the text leaves open. How points are deducted, and how they return without a test after twelve clean months, are explained in [the guide to the points system]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-points-system-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Article 58 is word for word the same in the Law as first issued and in the 55/VBHN-VPQH consolidation. Article 88 starts the Law on 1 January 2025, and the exceptions it lists do not touch Article 58.

## The mandatory wait

Restoration is not automatic and it is not instant. The holder must wait at least six months from the date the points reached zero. Article 58(3) sets a minimum, not a timetable: the test opens only once that period has run, and the clause names no latest date and no schedule. Nothing in it shortens the wait.

The twelve-month clock in clause 2 does not help here. That clause concerns a licence that has not been fully deducted, so it does not reach a balance of zero, and the way back that Article 58 gives a zero balance is the test in clause 3. Nothing in the article returns a zero-balance licence to 12 points through the passage of time alone.

### Which day starts the six months

Clause 3 counts from the day all the points were deducted. Clause 1 adds that a deduction is entered in the road traffic order and safety database as soon as the penalty decision takes legal effect, and that the person concerned is notified. The Law does not say whether the clock starts on the day the decision took effect, the day the entry was made or the day the notice arrived. Ask the traffic police which date stands on the record for your licence, and keep the answer in writing.

As arithmetic only, if the record showed the last points deducted on 12 March, six months later falls on 12 September. The text says "at least" and does not say how months are counted or whether the test can be taken on that very day, so treat the date you work out as the earliest possible and ask for the actual one.

## What a zero balance bars

### Driving on that licence

Article 58(3) is short. Where a licence has had all its points deducted, the person holding it may not drive a vehicle that takes part in road traffic on that licence. Three things follow from the wording itself.

- The clause mentions neither a class nor the vehicle involved in the violation that emptied the balance: it speaks of the licence.
- It is not limited to motorbikes, because it speaks of road vehicles in general.
- It is a bar on driving, not a cancellation. Article 57(7) gives only two grounds on which a licence has no effect, a term that has run out and a revocation under Article 62(5), and a zero balance is neither. [The licence validity and renewal guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) deals with terms and revocation.

### Mopeds

Article 56(1) makes one exception to the licence requirement: the moped rider. In place of a licence, Article 56(4) asks that rider for knowledge of the traffic rules and skill at controlling the vehicle, and it takes the age and health conditions from Article 59; the age is 16 (Article 59(1)(a)). The definition in Article 34(1)(g) turns on the number of wheels, design speed and engine size, not on body style, so a scooter is a moped only if it fits that definition; [what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) works through it.

Clause 3 speaks of driving on that licence, and none of the texts read for this page says how that fits a rider who needs no licence for the vehicle. Do not assume the clause lets a zero-balance holder ride a moped, and do not assume it bars one. Put the question to the traffic police in writing.

### Riding on it anyway

The Law states the bar; the penalty for ignoring it is set in the decree. [The fine guide for riding without a licence]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) carries bands from the 2024 text of that decree. Because that decree was amended with effect from 15 August 2026, treat any figure there as a 2024 figure until it has been checked against the current text.

## The knowledge test

Article 58(3) is specific about three things. The traffic police force organises the test. The test examines knowledge of the legal rules on road traffic order and safety, as provided in clause 7 of Article 61. And the clause states a single result. Pass, and the licence is restored to the full 12 points.

The clause does not tie the number of points to a mark: the restoration is to the full 12, in one step. It also names no training course and no practical test.

Compare the theory test for a new licence. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA lists its question topics in Article 4(1)(a): the legal rules on road traffic order and safety, driving technique, driver conduct and traffic culture, and the prevention of harm from alcohol and beer when taking part in traffic, with further topics for class B1 and above. Clause 3 names only the first of those as the content of the points check, and nothing in the texts read for this page says that the new-licence paper is the one used. [How a first A1 licence is obtained]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows the new-applicant route.

### Who sets the content

Clause 7 of Article 61 is the power to prescribe the form, content and procedure of tests for the issue of driving licences. In the Law as first issued that power belonged to the Minister of Transport. Law 118/2025/QH15 moved it to the Minister of Public Security with effect from 1 July 2026, and the 55/VBHN-VPQH consolidation carries the new wording. A guide written before that date may therefore name a different ministry.

Article 58(6) divides the rest between two rule-makers. The Government is to work out clause 1 as well as the procedure and authority for taking points off and putting them back, and the roadmap for applying the article; the Minister of Public Security is to work out clause 3.

Neither Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, the current circular on driver tests and licences, nor Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, which it replaced from 1 July 2026 apart from the parts it keeps in force, has an article on licence points or on this test. This page therefore describes no syllabus, question count, pass mark or fee: no text read for it sets one.

### What the text leaves open

Clause 3 is silent on the practical side, and so are the Law's other articles and the two circulars read for this page. These are the questions to put to the traffic police, in writing where you can:

- How and where do I apply for the test, and from what date?
- What does it contain, how long does it last and what is the pass mark?
- Is there a fee, and which documents are asked for?
- In which languages is it offered?
- What follows a result that is not satisfactory, and how soon can I try again?
- How will the restored balance be recorded, and how can I confirm it?

Keep each answer with its date and the name of the office that gave it.

## Points versus suspension

Zero points is its own condition, separate from a suspension order. A [drink-driving suspension]({{ '/articles/drink-driving-limits-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) suspends the right to use the licence for its stated months; a zero-point licence is unusable until restoration completes. The two can chase each other — a suspension that ends does not restore points, and a points balance does not lift a suspension.

Article 58 never mentions a suspension and sets no length for one, so a suspension's dates have to be taken from the decision that imposed it. The six months run from the day the balance reached zero, and the record of the deduction that took it there is the paper for that. Keep the two apart, because they answer different questions: when the test opens, and when the suspension ends.

## A new card does not clear a zero balance

Article 58(4) carries the balance across: the licence that comes out of an exchange, a reissue or an upgrade has the same number of points as the one that went in. For a licence at zero that means a new card also at zero, so replacing a card or moving up a class is no way round the six months and the test.

The Law does not say whether a holder at zero can obtain an exchange, a reissue or an upgrade in the first place. Article 62(4) holds back the issue, exchange or reissue of a licence while the person has not finished what the competent authority requires to settle a violation case, and it says nothing about points. [The guide to replacing a lost licence]({{ '/articles/lost-motorbike-licence-replacement-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows the reissue route in full.

## Where you stand

| Your situation | What the text gives | What to keep or ask |
|---|---|---|
| Points left, no deduction for 12 months | Clause 2: back to 12, no test | The date of the latest deduction |
| Balance at zero, under six months | Clause 3: no driving on that licence; the test is not yet open | The date the balance reached zero |
| Balance at zero, six months or more | Clause 3: the test is open to you; the bar lasts while the balance is zero | How and where to apply |
| New card or new class, balance at zero | Clause 4: the new licence carries zero | Which balance the new licence shows |
| A suspension running as well as a zero balance | Two separate clocks | The penalty decision and the record of the last deduction |

## Foreign licences, IDPs and rentals

Article 58 speaks of the points of a licence and draws no line between holders, so a Vietnamese licence that a foreign resident obtained by exchange is within clause 3 like any other. [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out that route. The article does not mention an IDP at all. [The section on IDP holders in the points-system guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-points-system-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what the Law does and does not say, and [the IDP rules for motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) explain how the permit and the national licence work together.

If you rent a bike in Hanoi, the licence duty falls on the person driving (Article 56(1)). A holder whose balance is at zero should not plan a rental that depends on that licence until the balance is restored. ## Keeping track of the balance

Article 58(1) makes a running total possible in two ways: a deduction is entered in the database as soon as the penalty decision takes legal effect, and the person concerned is to be notified. Clause 1 also leaves the number of points taken for each violation to its nature and degree, so Article 58 itself does not fix how many deductions empty a balance. Neither the Law nor the circulars read for this page describe how the notice reaches the person, or whether the public can consult the database; clause 6 passes the procedure to the Government. The working record is therefore the penalty decision you were given and anything sent to you afterwards. Whether a decision states the points taken is for the decree to say, so check each one and keep a running total of your own.

Checking it after any fine is the cheap way to avoid the six-month surprise. Because the text does not say how a balance is looked up, the first question for the traffic police is how to ask for yours.
