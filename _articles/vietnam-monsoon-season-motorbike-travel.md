Vietnam is 15 degrees of latitude tall, and it does not have a weather — it has three of them, stacked. Weather guides are unanimous: the country's length and topography mean the north, the centre and the south run on different monsoon clocks, and the question "when should I visit Vietnam on a motorbike?" is really three questions. Here is the rider's version.

## The three zones

**The North (Hanoi, Ha Giang, the northwest).** Four real seasons. Summer (May-August) is hot and wet with heavy summer downpours; winter (December-February) is cool, grey and damp — the mountains can drop near freezing on the high passes, and Ha Giang in January is a cold, foggy proposition. The dry, stable windows that touring operators recommend most for the north are March-April and October-November. Detail for Hanoi specifically is in our Hanoi seasons guide.

**The Centre (Hue, Da Nang, Hoi An, Nha Trang).** The flip of everyone's intuition: the centre's wettest, most dangerous months are the autumn — roughly September-November — when the coast takes typhoons and monsoon rain in succession, and Hue earns its reputation as the wettest big city in Vietnam. Riding guides put the central coastal route's best window at March-August.

**The South (Saigon, the Mekong Delta, Phu Quoc).** The simplest clock: dry from roughly December-April, wet from May-November — but "wet" means intense predictable afternoon downpours, not all-day rain. The delta rides well in the dry half-year, best December-March.

## Planning rules for riders

1. **Never plan one continuous ride to dodge all three wet seasons** — the windows do not align. Instead, pick your season and your region together: winter riding belongs to the south; the northern mountains belong to spring and autumn; the coast belongs to the summer half-year.
2. **For a full Hanoi-to-Saigon ride, the least-bad months are the late-spring pair** — the north is drying, the centre is starting its good window, the south is still dry enough.
3. **Rain strategy is a riding skill, not a calendar.** In every zone, rain arrives in the afternoon in the wet months: ride at dawn, be somewhere with a coffee by 2pm. Our after-rain surfaces guide covers the first-half-hour-after-rain problem, which is when most wet-weather crashes happen.
4. **Fog is the north's special tax:** mountain mornings in the humid months can erase visibility, and our low-visibility guide is required reading for anyone riding passes.
5. **Typhoons are forecastable; respect them.** Central-coast storms are tracked days ahead — never ride into a typhoon warning along the QL1A; wait it out inland.

## What the seasons mean for the bike and gear

Wet-season riding anywhere in Vietnam: full wet gear or accept being soaked twice daily, dry bags for electronics, and a helmet visor that handles rain (our helmet gear guides cover visors and ventilation for the climate). Cold-season northern mountains: real layers — the passes at altitude are a different climate from the Hanoi valley below.

The one-sentence version: match the month to the region, start early, and read the two surface guides above before your first wet corner. The reward for planning around the monsoon is a Vietnam that is always, somewhere, in its best light.
