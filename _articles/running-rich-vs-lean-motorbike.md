An engine's air-fuel mixture is a ratio, and everything the engine does — fuel economy, heat, spark plug colour, exhaust smell — reports on it. Too much fuel is rich; too little is lean. Vietnamese riders meet both: rich from short-trip city riding and clogged air filters; lean from intake leaks and gummed carburettors.

## The signs of rich

- Black, sooty spark plugs — the plug is the honest witness; read per [Reading Your Spark Plug: Colour Tells a Story]({{ '/articles/reading-spark-plug-condition-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).
- Black smoke or a wet, petrol-scented exhaust tip; smoke colours decoded in [What Exhaust Smoke Is Telling You]({{ '/articles/exhaust-smoke-motorbike-meaning/' | relative_url }}).
- Fuel disappearing faster than it used to — rich running is visible in the wallet, see [Improving Fuel Economy on a Motorbike]({{ '/articles/improving-fuel-economy-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).
- A fouled plug that hesitates or misfires at low revs; rough-idle diagnostics are in [Rough Idle: Troubleshooting a Motorbike That Won't Idle Smoothly]({{ '/articles/rough-idle-motorbike-troubleshooting/' | relative_url }}).
- Oily-black deposits around the exhaust outlet without any oil leak anywhere.

Rich is the safer failure direction — engines run cool and lazy — but it fouls plugs, washes cylinder walls of oil, and wastes fuel. Common causes: a clogged air filter (the single most common cause after neglect), a stuck or overused choke, a worn carburettor with a flooding float, or a gummed injector dripping instead of spraying — the injector-side story is in [Fuel Injector Cleaning: When It Helps and When It Does Not]({{ '/articles/fuel-injector-cleaning-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

## The signs of lean

- Whitish or blistered spark plugs.
- Surging, hesitant response off idle; a "hunting" idle; the engine running hot and losing power as heat builds — the hot-stall failure mode is in [Stalling When Hot: When a Warm Engine Gives Up]({{ '/articles/stalling-when-hot-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).
- Popping on deceleration that was not there before, decoded in [Exhaust Popping and Backfiring]({{ '/articles/exhaust-popping-backfiring-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).
- Hard starting when hot, better when cold.
- Hesitation that improves slightly when the choke is partly applied — the single most convincing lean clue on a carburetted bike.

Lean is the dangerous direction. Lean mixtures burn hot: they overheat engines, hammer pistons, and accelerate wear dramatically. A bike that runs lean under load should be ridden gently until fixed, not ridden to see if it improves.

## Finding the fault

Both conditions share a diagnostic ladder, cheapest first: check the air filter (clogged = rich), check for intake leaks (lean), verify the choke returns fully, check the plug colour trend, then hand the carburettor or injection system to a proper cleaning and setup. Carburettors drift with wear; fuel injection drifts less but clogs — see [Carburettor vs Fuel Injection on Vietnamese Motorbikes]({{ '/articles/carburettor-vs-fuel-injection-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for the system differences.

## What not to do

Do not chase mixture by ear alone, and do not copy another bike's jetting or settings: altitude, exhaust and engine condition make each bike's correct setup its own. Mixture diagnosis ends with either a clean bill of health or a mechanic with the manual — not with an online recommendation and a screwdriver.
