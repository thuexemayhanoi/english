Vietnam's 50cc market is small and informal — which makes resale a different game from the mainstream scooter trade. Value is decided by a handful of factors, none of which is the age on the registration.

## Condition beats age

In a market with thin official supply, buyers judge the machine in front of them. A fifteen-year-old 50cc that starts cold, holds idle, brakes straight and wears decent tyres sells quickly. A five-year-old one that smokes on start-up does not. The inspection checklist our buyers use is the same list you should be able to pass as a seller — that symmetry is the whole market.

## Paperwork is half the price

Registration that matches the frame, a clean ownership story, a current compulsory-insurance certificate: papers are what turn "interesting bike" into "buyable bike". Buyers discount heavily for paperwork gaps and walk away from "papers coming later" — as our buying guide warns from the other side of the deal.

## Original parts hold value; bad modifications destroy it

The patterns that protect value:

- original bodywork and bars, or quality replacements (our genuine-versus-fake parts article explains how to tell)
- a service history — even informal, hand-written notes in a notebook beat no story
- popular consumable sizes: mainstream tyres and tubes, adaptable cables, standard batteries

The patterns that destroy it: cut wiring, shade-tree electrical "fixes", mismatched wheels, and crash repairs that were painted rather than straightened.

## The grey-import premium

Japanese-market 50s trade in a small enthusiast market with its own rules: provenance and condition command a premium, and patient sellers hold price while hurried sellers crater it. The support-network trade-offs are covered in our grey-import guide — anyone buying yours will have read the same article, so price accordingly.

## The habits that protect value while you ride

- maintenance kept genuinely current: oil, chain, brakes, tyres
- parking under cover; sun and rain age plastics and seats faster than kilometres do
- minor damage fixed early — a cracked fairing left unrepaired becomes the buyer's first negotiating weapon

## When selling

Sell into the market's season (school-year start, when teenagers and parents are looking), present the bike cleaned with papers laid out, and let the buyer run the cold start. Resale value is not an event — it is the running-cost article practised every month you own the bike.
