Short answer: be 18 or older, enrol at a licensed training centre, complete the course, pass a [computer theory test and a practical test]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) in the figure, and pay the state fees. The A1 licence covers motorbikes up to and including 125 cc or 11 kW — the category that includes 110 cc scooters and 125 cc models such as the Honda Air Blade 125.

## The route in one table

Five steps lead from no licence to an A1 card. The table gives what the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15, consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH) or Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security says about each, and the sections below follow the same order.

| Step | What the text says | More |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Age and health | 18 or over on the day of the sitting; health suited to the vehicle, shown by a health certificate (Law, Article 59; Circular, Articles 15 and 16) | [The minimum age guide]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| 2. Training | Finish the programme for the class, at a training facility or in another form the Government regulates (Law, Article 60) | [The learner-permit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| 3. Dossier | The training centre files the request and candidate list; each candidate adds a completion paper, a health certificate and, for a foreigner, a residence paper (Circular, Article 16) | The dossier section below |
| 4. Tests | A computer theory paper, then two yard tests, in that order (Circular, Article 4) | The test section below |
| 5. Issue | An electronic licence for those who pass, or a printed card on request (Circular, Articles 18 and 19) | The issue section below |

## Who qualifies

Article 59 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) sets the age for an A1 licence at 18. Health requirements apply: health conditions are checked before the test, and the health certificate is part of the dossier a training centre assembles for you.

### Age

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA counts age up to the date of the sitting (Article 15(1)), and 18 is the age for classes A1, A, B1, B and C1 alike (Law, Article 59(1)(b)). Below it, Article 59(1)(a) lets a person of 16 or over ride a moped without a licence class; [what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) shows how narrow that definition is.

### Health

Article 59(2) of the Law asks for health suited to the vehicle and leaves the standards and the medical examination to the Minister of Health, so this page lists no medical criteria. The dossier needs a driver's health certificate still in force: electronic, or a paper one from a qualified medical facility if the system fails (Circular, Article 16(2)(b)). Health matters after the test too, because Article 62(5)(a) of the Law lets a licence be revoked when a medical facility concludes that the holder does not meet the health conditions for the class.

### Residence papers for a foreign candidate

A foreign candidate adds a copy of one residence document: a diplomatic identity card, an official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left, or a permanent residence card that is still valid (Article 16(2)(c)). The text does not say on which day the 90 days are counted, so ask the Traffic Police Department (Phòng Cảnh sát giao thông, the office that approves dossiers and sets up the sitting under Article 9) which date it uses, and file with a margin.

## The steps in order

1. Enrol at a training centre that meets the standards for motorbike training facilities. Foreigners can enrol too, but the exam is conducted in Vietnamese — [there is no English-language test]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}). The circular states no language for the paper.
2. Complete the training course. Since 1 September 2025, Circular 14/2025/TT-BXD fixes minimum training hours before a candidate can be registered for the test (class A: 32 hours, including 20 theory and 12 practical; A1 runs a shorter programme, 12 hours with 9 theory and 3 practical, in the table that Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD set from 1 July 2026). Attendance at the full course is a precondition for sitting the exam.
3. Sit the theory test: 25 questions on a computer, pass mark 21/25 for A1, as the Government portal's account of the procedure from 1 January 2025 gives it (the Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA appendix was not read for this page, so confirm it with the centre), for a sitting before 1 March 2027; from 1 March 2027 the paper is the 40-question, 27-minute paper of Appendix I of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, with 36 correct answers needed to pass.
4. Sit the practical test in the figure: the circuit of stops, starts and manoeuvres described in Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA (its Article 4(2)(a) names four exercises: a figure of eight, a straight line, a barrier line and a bumpy road) for sittings up to the end of 28 February 2027, and in Appendix I of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA from 1 March 2027, with the second yard test following from 1 January 2028.
5. Pay the state fees and receive the licence.

The 25-question figure comes from the Government portal's account of the A1 and A test procedure from 1 January 2025, published on 31 December 2024. The text of Appendix II of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA was not read for this page, so ask the centre to confirm the question count and pass mark for your sitting date.

### Choosing the course

Article 60(1) of the Law requires training in theory and practice under the programme set for the class, and Article 60(8) gives the Minister of Construction the programme and the standards for motorbike training facilities. The hours in step 2 are minimums; [the training-hours guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the full table and the reason no motorbike learner permit exists.

Choose the class by the bike you mean to ride. A1 stops at 125 cm³ of cylinder capacity or 11 kW of electric motor power, and a two-wheeler above either line needs class A (Law, Article 57(1)(a) and (b)), with the longer course in step 2. [The classes explained guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) sets A1, A and B1 side by side, and the engine figure on the bike's own papers decides, so ask for it before you enrol.

## What goes into the dossier

Under Article 16(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA the training centre files the test request: a report asking for a sitting, the list of candidates and each candidate's own papers. Article 16(2) lists four items for a candidate:

1. A primary certificate, a training certificate or a confirmation that the course is complete (item a).
2. A driver's health certificate still in force (item b).
3. For a foreigner, a copy of one residence paper from the list above (item c).
4. For a candidate who did not pass an earlier sitting and registers again, the summary record of the earlier results (item d).

Article 9(1) allows two ways to file: online through the National Public Service Portal, the national identification application or the Police applications, or straight to the Traffic Police Department when the system fails or the infrastructure is not ready. A dossier received directly is checked against Articles 15 and 16, and for an invalid one the filer is told and guided to complete it (Article 9(2)). [The A1 licence cost guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-cost-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what candidates pay; this page quotes no fee, because the articles of Circular 108 on the A1 test and issue name none.

## The test in outline

A motorbike candidate sits three parts in a fixed order: a theory paper on a computer, then two riding tests in the yard (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 4(1)(b), 4(2)(a) and 4(5)(a)). Classes A1 and A have no road test, because Article 4(3) places that test on the car, truck and bus classes. [The A1 test guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) goes through what each part asks; this page keeps to the order and the rules around it. That guide also sets out the pass mark for each part.

### Which rules apply on your sitting date

Article 41(6) keeps Article 4(1) to (3), Appendix II (the A1 and A procedure) and other listed appendices of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA in force until the end of 28 February 2027. Article 41(2) applies the new theory and yard content of Article 4(4) from 1 March 2027, except the content of the second yard test, which follows from 1 January 2028. For A1 and A that content is Appendix I: the theory paper, a first yard test, and a second yard test of 11 situations over two laps.

Article 42(2) adds a rule for repeat candidates: a part missed or not passed under Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA is sat again under that circular, and from 1 March 2027 under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA. Because the dates differ by part, ask the centre which version your sitting date falls under.

### A car licence and the theory paper

A candidate for A1 or A who holds a car licence in force is exempt from the theory paper (Article 4(1)(c)). The article does not say whether a car licence issued abroad qualifies, so put that question to the Department before relying on it.

## After you pass: issue of the licence

Article 62(1) of the Law issues the licence to a candidate who passes. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(2), says it is issued in electronic form or on PET material, and Article 19(1) has the Traffic Police Bureau (Cục Cảnh sát giao thông) issue the electronic licence. A printed PET card is an option on request: it is returned at the Department, through the training centre or test centre where there is one, or by post at the candidate's request.

Check one block before issue. Article 19(3) puts off the test for a person who has not finished what the authorities require to settle a road-traffic violation, and Article 62(4) of the Law does the same for issue. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how riding without a licence is framed; its figures follow the 2024 decree text, so check any amount against the amended decree.

What the card then lets you ride is covered in [what an A1 licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}): a holder may ride the classes written on the licence (Article 18(4)).

## Where foreigners fit in

The procedure above is for people who do not hold any motorbike licence. A foreigner with a valid national motorbike licence and residence papers valid for 90 days or more normally exchanges it instead — a lighter procedure that asks for a dossier and a fee obligation rather than a course and a test. Check your own situation before paying for a course you may not need.

### Exchange, test or neither

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA words the exchange as a duty. A foreigner or a Vietnamese resident abroad who needs to drive in Vietnam and holds a national licence goes through the exchange for the matching Vietnamese licence, unless a driving-licence treaty that Vietnam has joined provides otherwise (Article 18(7)). The routes sort by situation:

- A national motorbike licence in term and a qualifying residence paper: the exchange, set out in [how foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).
- No motorbike licence, or a licence that cannot be exchanged: the route on this page, with [the expat route guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) comparing it with the others for a long stay.

## How long it takes

From enrolment to licence, the total depends on the length of the course and the test schedule at the centre, and the texts fix only a few points along the way:

- The electronic dossier goes in at least 7 working days before the sitting (Circular, Article 9(1)).
- The Department notifies the test centre and the candidates of the time and place at least 2 working days before the sitting (Article 9(5)).
- The Bureau issues the electronic licence within 2 hours of the end of the sitting, and a printed card on request is returned within 3.5 working days of it (Article 19(1)).

The hours in step 2 count instruction time, not calendar days, so ask the centre for its course schedule and the date of the next sitting before you pay. The licence itself is issued with no expiry date for motorbike classes. Article 57(5)(a) of the Law gives classes A1, A and B1 no time limit; a licence obtained by exchange differs, because its term follows the papers behind it.

## Questions people ask

### What happens if I do not pass a part?

Article 12(1)(d) lets a candidate who did not pass register for another sitting, and the clause names no cap on attempts. The dossier then carries the summary record of the earlier results (Article 16(2)(d)).

### Is a licensed course the only way to prepare?

Article 60(2) of the Law mentions "other forms of driver training" next to training facilities, and Article 60(7) leaves the Government to define them. The texts cited here do not describe those forms, and Article 61(1) lets only a person who has completed the programme register for the test, so ask a centre or the Department before relying on any shortcut.

### Is there an English-language sitting?

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA names none. Its one language provision for the test is Article 14: a paper theory test by question and answer for A1 candidates from ethnic minorities who cannot read or write Vietnamese, which is written for those candidates and not for foreign ones.
