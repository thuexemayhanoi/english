The Imperial Citadel of Thang Long is Hanoi's deepest history made visible: a thousand-year centre of regional power — built on the remains of a seventh-century fortress, layered through the Ly, Tran, Le and Nguyen dynasties, and used as a command bunker again in the twentieth century — inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage site in 2010. For a rider, it is the easiest major landmark in the city: central, walled, and built around the kind of formal parking the Old Quarter can only dream of.

## What you are visiting

The citadel's central sector is an archaeology-first site: glass-covered excavation trenches showing foundations and roads of successive dynasties, the Flag Tower standing at its corner, palace gates rebuilt and restored, and below the grass, the bunker complex from the Vietnam War era. Two hours is a comfortable first visit; the site rewards reading the display panels rather than racing between photo spots.

## The route

The citadel sits on Hoang Dieu street, just west of Ba Dinh — between the President Ho Chi Minh mausoleum complex and the old Ministry of Defence lands. From Hoan Kiem, the ride is ten minutes: out along the central spine streets toward Ba Dinh, then north along Hoang Dieu. The approach roads are wide and quiet compared with the Old Quarter; the only complication is the security seriousness of the government quarter — follow the posted vehicle flow and do not improvise parking near official buildings.

## Parking and access

There is attended parking at the visitor entrance. Bring ID — sites in this quarter may check it — and buy tickets at the gate. As with all heritage sites in Hanoi, check current opening days before riding out: the citadel has periodic closures for state events, and the mausoleum complex beside it keeps its own separate schedule with its own queues.

## Making the visit work

Pair the citadel with its neighbourhood: the Ba Dinh guide covers the government quarter, and the museums and pagodas rides start from the same corner of the city. A morning citadel visit, lunch toward the Old Quarter, and an afternoon at the Temple of Literature is Hanoi's most natural heritage day on a bike.
