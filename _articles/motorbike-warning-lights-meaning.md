Modern scooters sold in Vietnam carry real dashboards, and the warning lights on them are the cheapest diagnostics you will ever own. The catch is that many riders ignore them, and a few of them genuinely mean "stop now."

## The ignition ritual

When you switch on, every light on the dash should come on briefly and then go out as the system checks itself — the process mirrors what lights-and-electrical-checks-motorbike describes. A light that stays on is a message, not a design feature.

## Red means stop

Oil pressure (usually an oil-can icon, red or combined with the engine light): stop the engine as soon as it is safe. An engine running without oil pressure destroys itself in seconds — checking-engine-oil-motorbike explains level checks, but once the light is on, level checks come after stopping. Engine management light, steady: get it looked at soon, riding gently. Flashing: stop and call a shop — a flashing engine light usually means active misfire. Temperature light on liquid-cooled bikes: stop, engine off, let it cool — the full picture of overheating is in overheating-motorbike-vietnam.

## Amber means soon, not now

Fuel light: find a station — Vietnamese stations are dense in cities but sparse on rural stretches. Battery or charging light: the bike is running on battery alone; you may have kilometres, not hours, before the engine dies — dead-battery-motorbike-vietnam covers the recovery sequence. ABS light (where fitted): if it stays on after the bike has rolled a few metres, the ABS has a fault; the base brakes still work but treat stopping distances as longer.

## Older bikes, honest truth

Much of Vietnam's fleet has no warning lights at all — a neutral light and a fuel gauge is the whole dashboard. On those bikes, the weekly checks ARE the warning system: oil level, brake feel, tyre pressure, chain condition. Treat dashboards as an upgrade over checks, never a replacement for them.
