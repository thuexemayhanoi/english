The xe đạp máy — a pedal bicycle with an electric assist motor — is Vietnam's lightest powered two-wheeler category, and legally it is treated closer to a bicycle than to a motorbike. Under the definitions of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), a pedal bike with an auxiliary electric motor that cuts out at 25 km/h and stops helping when the rider stops pedalling is classified outside the motorised categories.

## What that classification means

No licence is required — there is no licence class for the category. No registration as a motor vehicle applies, though e-bike registration stickers apply in practice in some cities. The minimum age for riding one in traffic is 16, per the same age provision that governs mopeds.

## What still applies

The helmet rule reaches further than the licensing rule. Article 31(3) of the law requires helmets for riders and passengers of motorised pedal bicycles — pedal e-bikes included — and the 400,000–600,000 VND fine band applies. The traffic rules apply in full: signals, direction of travel, sidewalk prohibition, pedestrian priority. A pedal-bike rider can be fined for counterflow riding exactly as a scooter rider can, in the band set for the category.

## The 25 km/h line matters

The classification assumes the motor behaves as specified: assistance ending at 25 km/h, no throttle-only operation into motorbike speeds. A modified e-bike ridden at motorbike speed is a classification violation — the machine no longer matches its category, and enforcement treats the mismatch as riding the wrong class of vehicle.

## For Hanoi riders

Pedal e-bikes are the popular choice for short student commutes and slow Old Quarter trips. They are also the category foreign visitors most often assume needs nothing at all — no helmet, no age check, no rules. The first assumption is the expensive one: helmets are compulsory, and checkpoints near tourist streets do check.
