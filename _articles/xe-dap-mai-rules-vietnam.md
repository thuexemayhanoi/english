The xe đạp máy — a pedal bicycle with an electric assist motor — is Vietnam's lightest powered two-wheeler category, and legally it is treated closer to a bicycle than to a motorbike. Under the definitions of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), a pedal bike with an auxiliary electric motor that cuts out at 25 km/h and stops helping when the rider stops pedalling is classified outside the motorised categories.

The practical result has three parts. The Law asks no licence of the person riding one, it names no minimum age for the category, and it still requires a helmet. Article numbers on this page follow the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes Law 118/2025/QH15; none of the clauses relied on here is marked as amended in it.

## What that classification means

No licence is required — there is no licence class for the category. Three clauses carry that reading. Article 57(1) lists the classes, from A1 up to the heavy-vehicle and trailer classes, and none is for a motorised bicycle. Under Article 56(1) the licence duty falls on the driver (người lái xe), a word that Article 2(9) defines as the person who controls a motor vehicle; the person who controls a non-motorised vehicle is a separate entry in the same definition. Article 9(1) bars controlling a motor vehicle without the licence the law requires, and a motorised bicycle is not one. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, which governs driver tests and licences, does not mention the category at all.

The line is drawn in Article 34. Clause 1 lists the motor vehicles (xe cơ giới) and clause 2 the non-motorised vehicles (xe thô sơ): the plain bicycle at point (a) and the motorised bicycle at point (b). Point (b) describes a bicycle with motor assistance, the electric bicycle (xe đạp điện) included, whose motor drive ends in two cases: the rider stops pedalling, or the speed reaches 25 km/h. It does not call the motor electric; it names the electric bicycle as one kind the term includes. Article 34(1)(g), the moped definition, closes by excluding the motorised pedal bicycle, so the two are kept apart in the text; [what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) takes that definition limit by limit.

No registration as a motor vehicle applies, though e-bike registration stickers apply in practice in some cities. The first half follows from Article 35(1)(a), which makes registration and a number plate a condition for motor vehicles and special-use machinery; the motorised bicycle is neither. The second half is the page's earlier remark, and no text read for this page mentions a sticker. Treat that half as unverified.

Article 35(5) leaves the operating conditions of non-motorised vehicles to the Government, and Article 35(6) leaves their area of operation to the provincial People's Committee, so a local rule is possible. No Hanoi decision under those clauses has been read for this page: confirm any sticker or area rule with the local authority.

### Age: not the moped's 16

The minimum age for the category is not 16 under the moped provision. Article 59(1)(a) lets anyone of 16 or older drive a moped (xe gắn máy), Article 34(1)(g) leaves the motorised pedal bicycle out of the moped definition, and Article 56(4), which sets the rider conditions for moped riders, is written for the same vehicle. An earlier version of this page gave 16 as the age for this category; the text does not support that.

What the Law says instead is narrow. Article 59(1) sets ages for drivers (người lái xe) and operators of special-use machinery, and its first five points tie minimum ages to the moped, to licence classes and to special-use machinery. None names the motorised pedal bicycle, so the text gives no age for the category. It does not say that any age may ride one either, and no text read for this page settles whether another rule sets an age, so a parent or a renter should not assume an answer in either direction. [The minimum-age guide]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the full ladder for mopeds and licence classes.

## What still applies

The helmet rule reaches further than the licensing rule. Article 31(3) puts a helmet on the rider and on any passenger of a motorised pedal bicycle; the helmet must be built to the national technical regulation and worn with the strap fastened in the proper way. The Law leaves the fine to a decree and no amount is quoted here: the amendments that Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP made to Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026 mean the band in [the helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the 2024 figure and needs checking against the current text. [Fitting a helmet properly]({{ '/articles/helmet-fit-guide-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the helmet itself.

### Passengers, conduct and lights

Article 31(1) lets the rider carry one person, and a second only when that second person is a child under 7; two carried people is the ceiling. The motorbike clause allows extra riders in four cases: a child under 12, an emergency patient, a person under escort and a frail or disabled person (Article 33(1)); Article 31(1) does not borrow them.

Article 31(2) applies two lists from the motorbike article to this category. The rider must avoid the acts in Article 33(3), among them riding abreast, riding into the part of the road set aside for pedestrians and other vehicles, using an umbrella or an audio device (hearing aids excepted), and letting go of both hands. A passenger must avoid the acts in Article 33(4), among them carrying bulky loads, using an umbrella, and standing on the seat or sitting on the handlebars.

Article 31(4) adds three rules for any non-motorised vehicle: single file, the part of the road set aside for such vehicles where there is one, and, from 18:00 to 06:00, lights or a signal at the front and rear. That is not the headlight clause of Article 20(1), which is written for drivers of motor vehicles and special-use machinery.

### Rules written for every road user

The traffic rules apply in full: signals, direction of travel, sidewalk prohibition, pedestrian priority. Read "in full" as shorthand, because some rules are written for motor-vehicle drivers only: the licence, the registration plate, and the speed and safe-distance rules of Article 12(1) among them. The ones below are addressed to anyone who controls a road vehicle, so they reach the rider:

- Article 10(1): keep to the right in your direction of travel, use the lane or part of the road meant for you, and obey road signs and signals.
- Article 12(2) and (3): keep a speed suited to the road, the traffic, the weather and the terrain, and observe, slow down or stop where pedestrians are crossing or where markings are provided for them, and near schools, hospitals and markets.
- Article 9(2) and (3): no alcohol in the blood or breath and no drugs; the alcohol clause states no threshold.
- Article 9(5) and (6): no racing or weaving, and no holding and using a phone or other electronic device while the vehicle is moving.

Article 33(3)(b), applied through Article 31(2), is the clause behind the sidewalk shorthand; it speaks of the part of the road set aside for pedestrians, not of a sidewalk by name. [The sidewalk riding guide]({{ '/articles/riding-on-sidewalk-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives its fine as the 2024 decree set it, a figure to check against the current text.

A pedal-bike rider can be fined for counterflow riding exactly as a scooter rider can, in the band set for the category. That sentence carries over from an earlier version of this page and was not checked against the current decree. Fines come from Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and the changes Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP made to it took effect on 15 August 2026; the Law itself says only that road users keep to the right in their direction of travel (Article 10(1)). Check the current decree for the band that applies to a motorised pedal bicycle. [The wrong-way riding guide]({{ '/articles/wrong-way-riding-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the 2024 band for motorbikes, which may not be the band for this category.

## The 25 km/h line matters

The classification assumes the motor behaves as specified: assistance ending at 25 km/h, no throttle-only operation into motorbike speeds. Article 34(2)(b) states the test in the machine's own behaviour, and both limbs must hold. The definition limits the motor, not the rider; it speaks only of when the drive is cut. A motor that keeps driving past 25 km/h, or drives with no pedalling at all, does not match the wording.

A machine that fails either limb is not described by point (b), so it is tested against the other definitions in Article 34(1): the moped limits of point (g), a design speed up to 50 km/h and, for an electric motor, power up to 4 kW, or the motorbike definition in point (e). [The licence guide for electric two-wheelers]({{ '/articles/electric-bike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works through the groups, throttle-only machines included, and [pedal e-bikes and scooter-style electrics compared]({{ '/articles/pedal-e-bike-vs-scooter-electric-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets the two formats side by side. [Riding an electric moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-electric-moped-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the guide for a younger rider on the moped side of the line.

The Law does not spell out what happens to a bicycle whose motor has been changed to drive past 25 km/h. Article 2(7) defines modification (cải tạo) as a change to the characteristics of a vehicle that already has a registration and plate, or of a used imported vehicle, that changes its vehicle type, and Article 9(11) prohibits unauthorised modification; Article 35(1) asks no certificate or plate of this category, so those words do not settle an altered pedal bike. Get the traffic police's reply in writing before riding one.

## Lanes, expressways and local limits

Where lane markings divide one direction of a carriageway, Article 13(3) sends non-motorised vehicles to the right-most lane and motor vehicles to the lanes on their left, and Article 13(1) asks slower traffic to keep right. Article 25(3) keeps non-motorised vehicles, mopeds, motorbikes and pedestrians off expressways, apart from people, vehicles and equipment serving the management and maintenance of the expressway; [the expressway guide]({{ '/articles/motorbikes-on-expressway-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the motorbike side, with a fine figure that follows the 2024 decree. The local layer of Article 35(6) matters for a longer route: ask what area rule applies before assuming none does.

## Questions riders ask

### Does a tourist need an international driving permit for a pedal e-bike?

For a machine that truly fits Article 34(2)(b), no clause read for this page asks the rider for any licence, Vietnamese or foreign, so the permit question does not arise. It arises only if the machine turns out to be a motorbike under Article 34(1)(e); a moped needs no licence either (Article 56(1) and (4)). [The motorbike IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) cover the permit for those machines.

### Is there a speed limit for a motorised pedal bicycle?

Article 12(1) sets speed and safe-distance rules for drivers of motor vehicles and special-use machinery, not for this category. Article 12(2) still asks every rider for a speed that suits the road, the traffic and the weather. The 25 km/h in Article 34(2)(b) marks where the motor's drive ends; it is not a limit written for the rider. No decree or local speed rule for this category was read for this page.

### Does a motorised pedal bicycle need insurance?

Article 56(1)(d) lists the compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate of the owner of a motor vehicle among the papers a driver carries, and the Law text read for this page names no such certificate for riders of non-motorised vehicles. A separate decree sets out compulsory insurance; this page has not read it and makes no claim about whether it reaches the category.

### What must I do after a collision?

Article 80(1) applies to anyone who controls a road vehicle that causes, or is involved in, a road accident that harms life, health or property. The duties are to stop, warn others of the hazard, leave the scene untouched, help the injured and inform the police, the nearest medical facility or the People's Committee. Point (b) also asks the person to stay at the scene until the police arrive, with exceptions for emergency care, and point (c) asks for identifying information to be given to the competent authority. [The accident duties guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the sequence.
