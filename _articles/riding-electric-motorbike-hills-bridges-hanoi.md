Hanoi is flat in the way that contains a surprise every few kilometres: the Nhat Tan and Long Bien bridge approaches, the ramps into parking garages, the road up to the dyke. On a petrol scooter you barely notice them. On an electric, every climb is paid for in range — and a few are worth thinking about before you ride them.

## What a climb costs

An electric motor at low speed is at its strongest — full torque from zero — so pulling away uphill is easier than on a petrol scooter. What suffers is the battery: sustained climbing draws maximum power continuously, and the range display drops faster than the road rises. Official range claims (the Evo's up-to-165 km, the Weaver 200's 200 km at 35 km/h) are flat-road standard-condition figures; a day of dyke ramps and bridge approaches will not match them. Budget conservatively on hilly routes — our real-world range guide explains the shrinkage factors.

## Which machines handle it best

| Machine (verified figures) | Hill character |
|---|---|
| Dat Bike Weaver 200 (6,000 W, 165 kg max load) | Climbs with authority; A1 licence required |
| VinFast Feliz II / Viper (3,000 W) | Confident city climbs; licence-free class |
| VinFast Evo (2,450 W) | Adequate with a passenger on longer ramps |
| VinFast Amio (800 W, 30 km/h) | Bicycle-class machine — bridges yes, long steep climbs slowly |

Riding two-up up a bridge approach is the honest test of the licence-free class: legal, possible, and noticeably slower than solo.

## Hill starts, electric edition

The twist-and-go transmission makes hill starts nearly foolproof: hold the rear brake, feed the throttle, release — no clutch to balance, no roll-back stall. The technique that stays worth practising is the pause on the ramp in bridge traffic: keep the brake covered rather than holding pure throttle, because the silent motor gives following drivers no audible cue that you are about to move.

## Riding down the other side

The descent is where the electric pays some range back — regenerative braking on machines so equipped tops the pack up slightly while controlling speed, and our regen guide explains how to use it smoothly. Combine it with the bridge-riding rules in the crossing guide — lane position and predictability matter more than drive type — and the hills of Hanoi stop being a range question at all.
