The Hung Kings Temple complex on Nghia Linh mountain in Phu Tho province is where Vietnam honours its legendary founders — the destination of the country’s biggest ancestral pilgrimage on the tenth day of the third lunar month. It is about 90 kilometres northwest of Hanoi: a solid half-day-plus ride, and one of the most meaningful long day trips from the capital.

## The route

The classic line runs northwest past Noi Bai and along the old National Highway 2 corridor toward Viet Tri city, then a short hop to the temple complex at the foot of Nghia Linh mountain. Note that motorbikes cannot use the Hanoi–Lao Cai expressway — the old highway is the legal two-wheel route, and it is a good ride: delta towns, rivers and, as you approach Phu Tho, the first real hills. Count roughly two to two and a half hours each way with stops.

Fuel and food are easy along the corridor. Tank up in Viet Tri before the final stretch.

## The complex

From the gate, a stone stairway climbs through old forest to a chain of temples: Ha temple, Trung temple and the upper temple on the summit, plus the Hung Kings’ tomb and a museum. The full climb is several hundred steps — steady work in the heat, so carry water. The summit temple, where the nation’s founders are said to have performed rituals, looks across rolling hills and tea country.

The Gieng temple courtyard holds the famous 700-year-old well, and the museum displays bronze drums of the Phung Nguyen culture. Modest dress applies throughout.

## Timing

- Outside the festival, weekday visits are calm and half a day on the ground is ample.
- On the Ancestors’ Festival (tenth day of the third lunar month) the site is a sea of pilgrims — extraordinary, but not a quiet ride day.
- Start early; the stair climb is best before noon.
