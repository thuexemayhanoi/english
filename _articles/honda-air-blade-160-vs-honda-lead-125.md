Both Hondas share engines from the same family, liquid cooling and automatic practicality - but they answer different questions. The Air Blade 160 is built around pace; the Lead 125 around cargo. Figures below are Honda Vietnam's own (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## The numbers side by side

| | Air Blade 160 | Lead 125 |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 156.9 cc, liquid-cooled | 124.8 cc, liquid-cooled |
| Max power | 11.2 kW @ 8,000 rpm | 8.22 kW @ 8,500 rpm |
| Max torque | 14.8 Nm @ 6,000 rpm | 11.7 Nm @ 5,250 rpm |
| Kerb weight | 113 kg | 114 kg |
| Seat height | 775 mm | 760 mm |
| Claimed economy | 2.23 L/100 km | 2.1 L/100 km |
| Fuel tank | 4.4 L | 6.0 L |
| Underseat storage | (not published as a headline figure) | 37 L - two full helmets |
| ABS | (not listed as standard) | ABS version available |

## What the numbers mean

- **Performance:** the 160 has a clear advantage in both power and torque - the difference you feel carrying a passenger or overtaking outside the city.
- **Range:** the Lead's 6.0-litre tank is the biggest in this group; despite similar consumption figures it goes noticeably further between fills.
- **Storage:** no contest - the Lead's 37-litre bay holds two full-face helmets. If you carry things daily, the Lead wins on usability alone (see the storage comparison).
- **Safety:** the Lead is offered with ABS - see which scooters have ABS in Vietnam.

## Which should you choose?

Air Blade 160 for pace, motorway confidence and looks. Lead 125 for storage, range and the lower 760 mm seat. Renting? The Air Blade category has a published rate at Nguyen Tu (200,000 VND/day, 800,000 VND/week, 1,400,000 VND/month); for the Lead, contact us to confirm current availability.
