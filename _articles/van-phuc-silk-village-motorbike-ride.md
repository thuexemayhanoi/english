Van Phuc, the silk-weaving village on the Nhue river just off Nguyen Trai road in Ha Dong, is the easiest craft-village ride from central Hanoi: about 8 km southwest of the centre, roughly half an hour by motorbike on a straight corridor. It is the natural first stop on any southwest riding day.

## The route

The published routing is simple: pick up the Nguyen Trai – Le Van Luong – To Huu corridor heading southwest and follow the signs to Ha Dong. The whole ride is urban-arterial riding — divided road, buses, steady scooter flow — until the last stretch, where you turn off Nguyen Trai into the village lanes by the river. Leave the main road early enough to avoid missing the turn: the village sits behind the corridor, not on it.

Timing matters more than the route. Outbound against the morning commuter wave is pleasant before 08:00; the return toward the centre between 17:00 and 19:00 is heavy. Mid-morning and mid-afternoon are the easy windows.

## On arrival

Van Phuc is compact: a lane of silk showrooms along the river bank, weaving houses in the back streets, and the sound of looms somewhere behind every doorway. The main lane is for walking — park at the lane entrances where the village's attendants watch the bikes, and go in on foot. What you do there is browse silk: scarves, lengths of fabric, ready-made clothes, and accessories, at prices that are genuinely lower than the tourist shops in the Old Quarter for the same craft. Haggling is expected but gentle here; the village is not a tourist trap, and the workshops sell to Hanoi's own tailors.

A visit of one to two hours is enough. If the day has room, combine it with the rest of Ha Dong — the market streets around the metro line, and the wider southwest — for a half-day loop.

## Carrying silk

Silk packs small and weighs nothing, which makes this the ideal shopping ride — everything fits in a backpack or under the seat. If you buy fabric by the metre in quantity, fold it flat and keep it out of the chain side; silk and chain grease do not mix. The luggage guide has the general rules for strapping anything larger.

## When to go

Weekday mornings see the village at its working best; weekends bring local shoppers. The village is busiest around Tet, when Hanoi's tailors stock up — fascinating, but not the day for a quiet browse.
