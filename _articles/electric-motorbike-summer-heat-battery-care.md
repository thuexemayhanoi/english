A Hanoi summer — 35 °C afternoons, asphalt hotter still — is the season that ages an electric motorbike battery faster than any number of kilometres. The chemistry tolerates heat; it does not thrive on it, and the difference between a pack that lasts and one that fades early is mostly a set of parking and charging habits.

## Heat and the pack

The verified Vietnamese machines run LFP (the VinFast class) or high-density lithium-ion (the Dat Bike Weaver 200 class, with its 150,000 km / 15-year battery-life claim and 3-year warranty). Both chemistries degrade faster when stored hot. The practical translation for Hanoi: the two hours your bike spends standing on sun-baked asphalt at 36 °C matter as much to the pack as the ride there.

## The summer habits

1. **Park in shade whenever the choice exists** — tree lines, building shadows, covered parking. The seat thanks you too.
2. **Charge in the evening, not on the hot afternoon** — a pack that comes home heat-soaked and goes straight on the charger is charged at its worst temperature. Let it cool, then charge overnight.
3. **Do not charge in direct sun** — the charger and pack share the sun's attention; shade the socket location if that is where it must happen.
4. **Carry the spare pack like milk, not like a brick** — a removable VinFast-class pack left in the sun on the floor of a shop is being cooked from both sides. Under a seat or in shade.
5. **Expect the honest range dip** — hot-weather riding with a hot pack gives slightly less usable range than the standard-condition claims; budget for it rather than discovering it (see the real-world range guide).

## What heat damage looks like

A pack abused by repeated hot charging does not announce itself; it just holds less. The check is the one in our battery-health guide — how quickly the display falls through the familiar part of your route. A machine that used to arrive home with 40% and now arrives with 25% on the same commute is telling you something about age or treatment.

Winter has its own, opposite set of behaviours — covered in the winter-range companion piece. Between the two seasons, the rule of thumb is constant: the battery hates extremes, and Hanoi supplies both.
