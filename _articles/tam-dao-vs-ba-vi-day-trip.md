Two mountain massifs dominate the day-trip conversation among Hanoi riders: Tam Dao, the former hill station with the ridge-top town, and Ba Vi, the national park with the long forest climb. They are both mountains, and otherwise they are different trips. Here is how they actually compare.

## Distance and time

Tam Dao is about 85 kilometres north — roughly two hours out, plus the hill climb. Ba Vi is closer on paper (60–70 km west, about an hour and a half to the park area), but its own climb inside the park adds a long, slow section before you park. Total saddle time is similar; Ba Vi puts its mountain climbing at the end, Tam Dao spreads it over the last 15 kilometres of switchbacks.

## The riding

Tam Dao’s upper road is tighter and busier with tourist traffic on weekends; its hairpins reward confident throttle-and-brake control. Ba Vi’s park road is wider, quieter and steadier — a long, even gradient through forest that many riders find more enjoyable, and better preparation for real mountain trips. Both are sealed; both get fog quickly.

## The destination

Tam Dao delivers a town: coffee shops with valley views, a colonial stone church, food, atmosphere — a place to linger. Ba Vi delivers nature: pine forest, the upper walking trails, the summit church ruins and temperatures that drop with every bend — a place to walk and look. Tam Dao is a social trip; Ba Vi is an outdoors trip.

## Who should ride which

- **Tam Dao** if you want cafes, photos and a hill-town vibe, and don’t mind weekend traffic on the bends.
- **Ba Vi** if you want forest, walking trails and a more serious mountain feeling, or you’re testing yourself for bigger rides like Ha Giang.
- Fog risk is real on both — check mountain weather, not city weather.
- Both work on a 110 cc bike; neither suits a first-ever ride day.
