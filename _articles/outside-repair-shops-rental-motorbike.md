The short answer: small fixes, yes - with receipts and a phone call first. Real repairs, not without the shop's agreement. Here is the working rule set.

## The call-first principle

A rental bike is the shop's asset, maintained on the shop's side of the deal (oil, chain, tyre pressure, inspection). Before any third party touches it, call the shop and say what happened. Nine times out of ten the answer is a quick "yes, get it done, keep the receipt" or "bring it in, we will handle it" - either way, the shop now knows its bike's history, and you are covered when the return walk-around finds a new part.

## The yes-list (small, standard, receiptable)

- Puncture repair or a new tube at a roadside tyre shop - the puncture article explains the typical payment split; keep the receipt.
- Chain lubrication, a basic adjustment on a long trip.
- A bulb, a mirror, a cable replaced at a provincial workshop mid-tour.
- Anything the shop tells you to get done on the phone.

## The no-list (without agreement)

- Engine, gearbox or electrical work beyond a bulb.
- Anything requiring parts the shop did not approve.
- Cosmetic work on bodywork.
- Modification of any kind, however well-intentioned.

The reason is not bureaucracy: unauthorized work breaks the causal chain for every later dispute. If a third party's repair fails and causes damage, the shop never approved the repair, the deposit conversation becomes a three-way argument, and the renter is in the middle of it. Receipts and a call-first trail keep the story simple.

## Mid-trip reality

On a long-distance tour you may be hours from the shop and next to a perfectly competent mechanic. The same rule scales: call first (the shop's advice is free and it knows the model), keep the receipt, photograph the parts, report at return. The long-distance article folds this into trip planning.

## At return

Hand over any third-party receipts with the bike. A documented small repair, honestly told, is a non-event at the walk-around. An undocumented one is a puzzle, and puzzles are decided against the renter by default.
