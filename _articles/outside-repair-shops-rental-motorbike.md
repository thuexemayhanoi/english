The short answer: small fixes, yes - with receipts and a phone call first. Real repairs, not without the shop's agreement. Here is the working rule set.

That rule set is a way to sort jobs, not a published shop policy. Neither an outside-repair rule nor a reimbursement term is published for Nguyen Tu rentals, so every point below that touches permission or money is a question to put to the shop: at handover if you can, and again on the phone when a problem happens. The guide to [understanding a motorbike rental agreement]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the other terms worth settling out loud at the same time.

## The call-first principle

A rental bike is the shop's asset, maintained on the shop's side of the deal (oil, chain, tyre pressure and inspection may all be part of that). Before any third party touches it, call the shop and say what happened. The shop may answer with a quick "yes, get it done, keep the receipt" or with "bring it in, we will handle it" - either way, the shop now knows its bike's history, and a new part at the return walk-around is no surprise.

Say four things on the call or in the message: what happened, where you are, whether the bike is safe to ride, and what the mechanic in front of you says the job is. A photo of the problem tells a mechanic who knows the model more than a description does.

Other rental problems are sorted by when they happen in [the guide to common rental problems]({{ '/articles/common-motorbike-rental-problems-hanoi/' | relative_url }}); this page covers the narrower question of who may touch the bike.

## When an outside repair may be unavoidable

Call-first assumes that the shop can be reached and that the bike can get to it. Three situations strain that assumption, and none of them changes the order of steps below.

### The bike cannot reach the shop

A flat tyre far from the shop is the plainest case. Riding on a flat risks turning a small repair into a ruined tyre, so a tyre shop near where you stopped may be the sensible next move once the shop knows. [The puncture guide]({{ '/articles/punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers the money side.

### The shop is closed

The shop is open 09:00-21:00 daily. A fault at 21:30 or at 07:00 falls outside those hours, and Nguyen Tu does not promise 24/7 rescue or a guaranteed rescue time; support is primarily for Hanoi and local use. Ask at handover what the shop wants you to do outside opening hours: whether a WhatsApp or Zalo message to +84 942 467 674 will be read when the shop opens, and whether it wants to answer before any repair is done.

If the bike is safe to leave, a repair that can wait until opening time is better waited out than guessed at. If it is not safe to leave or to ride, make it safe first and send the message before anything else.

### The shop does not answer

A message with no reply is not an approval. If the bike is stranded and nobody answers, the lowest-risk outside repair is the smallest one that gets the bike safely moving again, done with the message, the photos and the receipt as your record. Anything larger waits for the shop's answer, even if that costs a night.

## The order of steps

The order matters more than any single step, because each step produces the evidence the next one needs.

1. **Make it safe.** Get yourself and the bike away from traffic. The first ten minutes in [the breakdown guide]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) include quick checks that sometimes make a repair unnecessary.

2. **Photograph before anything is touched.** Take the problem, the part and the bike as it stands. A photo taken before the work is the only record of what the work replaced.

3. **Call the shop, and message if there is no answer.** Give the four points above and attach the photo.

4. **Agree the job and who pays before the work starts.** Ask which job the shop is happy for an outside workshop to do, whether there is a limit above which you must call again, and who pays. With no reimbursement term published, the answer you get is the only term you will have, so put it in a chat message: one line is enough.

5. **Choose a workshop that fits the job, and brief it on that job only.** A tyre shop suits a tyre; a general stand suits a bulb or a cable. Tell the mechanic exactly what the shop has agreed and ask for the price before the work starts. [Getting repairs done by a Vietnamese mechanic]({{ '/articles/talking-to-mechanic-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers describing a symptom and agreeing a price without fluent Vietnamese, and [Hanoi's specialist motorbike streets]({{ '/articles/hanoi-motorbike-shops-streets-guide/' | relative_url }}) describes where repair clusters are found. Whether a brand dealer is worth the trip is a question for the shop, which may name the workshop it prefers.

6. **Watch, and ask for the old part.** Watching tells you what was actually done; the old part shows whether it needed doing, and lets the shop see it if it asks.

7. **Pay, and photograph the receipt and the finished repair.** Ask for a receipt that names the job. A handwritten note with the job and the amount is still a record.

8. **Report the same day, not at return.** Send both photos to the shop so that it knows what was done to its bike while you still remember the details.

## The yes-list (small, standard, receiptable; confirm by phone first)

- Puncture repair or a new tube at a roadside tyre shop - [the puncture article]({{ '/articles/punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains the typical payment split; keep the receipt.
- Chain lubrication, a basic adjustment on a long trip.
- A bulb, a mirror, a cable replaced at a provincial workshop mid-tour.
- Anything the shop tells you to get done on the phone.

These are the jobs least likely to cause trouble, which is not the same as permitted: the shop's answer on the phone is what turns "probably fine" into agreed. A new inner tube or tyre is also a payment question, because inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation; settle who pays on the same call.

Chain work needs one extra distinction. Chain adjustment and tension is among the maintenance the shop may provide, so in Hanoi the first move is to report a loose chain; the [loose chain guide]({{ '/articles/loose-chain-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) lists the symptoms. A roadside adjustment belongs on the yes-list only when you are far from the shop, and even then after a call.

## The no-list (without agreement)

- Engine, gearbox or electrical work beyond a bulb.
- Anything requiring parts the shop did not approve.
- Cosmetic work on bodywork.
- Modification of any kind, however well-intentioned.

The reason is not bureaucracy: unauthorized work breaks the causal chain for every later dispute. If a third party's repair fails and causes damage, the shop never approved the repair, the deposit conversation becomes a three-way argument, and the renter is in the middle of it. Receipts and a call-first trail keep the story simple.

### Why each job is on the list

- **Engine, gearbox or electrical work.** These jobs are hard to check afterwards, and a fault that appears later can be traced back to the work. A bike that has started to feel different belongs with the shop first.
- **Parts the shop did not approve.** A part that fits is not necessarily the part the shop would choose. Ask what the shop wants fitted before the mechanic orders or fetches anything.
- **Cosmetic work.** Paint, filler and replaced panels change how the bike looks, and a handover photo set records how it looked at the start. The [inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) lists what to photograph at handover.
- **Modification.** Anything you cannot undo is a change to someone else's property. [The guide to rental modifications]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-modifications/' | relative_url }}) is written for monthly renters, and the same line applies to a short rental.

## Questions to put to the shop

Because nothing is published, the questions are the useful part. Ask them at handover, when nothing is wrong, in the same conversation as the deposit and the late-return terms.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which jobs may I have done without calling first, if any? | It fixes the line between the yes-list and the no-list for this bike |
| Who pays for a roadside repair, and is any of it reimbursed? | No reimbursement term is published, so an unspoken assumption on either side becomes a dispute at return |
| Is there an amount above which I must call again? | A limit agreed in advance replaces a guess at the roadside |
| Do you want a particular workshop, or will any tyre shop do? | The shop knows the model and may have a preference |
| What should I do outside 09:00-21:00? | The shop is closed then and no 24/7 rescue is offered |
| What do you want me to keep: the receipt, photos, the old part? | You will know what to hand over at return |
| Does outside work affect any warranty or service arrangement on this bike? | Only the shop can say whether one exists |

## What an outside repair can change for the rental

An outside repair can change two things: the parts on the bike and the story at return.

### Parts

A part fitted at the roadside replaces one the shop chose. The replacement may differ from the original in brand, quality or fit, and telling a genuine part from a copy is a skill of its own; [the guide to genuine versus fake parts]({{ '/articles/genuine-vs-fake-parts-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains what to look for. For anything that touches braking or grip, the shop's preference should come before the lowest quote. Ask the mechanic to name the part going in, and keep the receipt that names it.

### The record at return

A repair the shop knew about, with a receipt and photos, becomes part of the bike's history. A repair it never heard about becomes a surprise. How an outside repair is treated when the deposit is settled is not published either, and [how deposit returns work]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the return side.

## Red flags

### At the workshop

- A price that appears only after the work, or no price when you ask for one.
- Extra jobs added "while the bike is here" that the shop never agreed.
- A diagnosis far bigger than the symptom, such as a puncture that suddenly needs more.
- Refusal to show the old part or to write down the job and the amount.
- A job that needs the bike to leave your sight or stay overnight. Call the shop again before you agree.

### On the renter's side

- Riding on after a stop-now symptom to reach a workshop. [The guide to a bike that does not feel normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) separates the symptoms that mean stop from those that can wait for the shop.
- Going back to workshops for the same fault again and again. A repeat fault is the point to go back to the shop with the whole history.
- Saying nothing for days. The shop learns late and the receipt gets harder to find.
- Paying a workshop for something the shop has already said it will handle.

## Four situations compared

| Situation | First move | Outside workshop? |
|---|---|---|
| Puncture far from the shop, shop open | Photograph the cause, call, ask where to go | Can be reasonable once the shop has agreed |
| Puncture after 21:00 | Make the bike safe, message, wait if it is safe to leave | Only the smallest fix that makes the bike safe to move |
| Loose chain inside Hanoi | Report it to the shop | No, the shop's maintenance may cover it |
| A mechanic says the engine or gearbox needs work | Stop, photograph, call the shop | No, that is the shop's decision |

## Mid-trip reality

On a long-distance tour you may be hours from the shop and next to a perfectly competent mechanic. The same rule scales: call first (the shop knows the model), keep the receipt, photograph the parts, and report the same day as well as at return. The guide to [taking a rental motorbike outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) folds this into trip planning.

Because support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, the questions in the table above are best settled before leaving the city, not after the breakdown.

## At return

Hand over any third-party receipts with the bike. A documented small repair, honestly told, is a non-event at the walk-around. An undocumented one is a puzzle that neither side can solve from evidence.

Keep the chat history until the deposit has been settled.

## Questions renters ask

**Can I get a puncture fixed at any roadside tyre shop?** A tyre shop is the standard place for a puncture, and the puncture guide above covers who pays. What is not published is any rule from the shop on outside repair, so tell the shop first and keep the receipt.

**Will the shop pay me back for an outside repair?** No reimbursement term is published, and nothing on this page is a promise of one. Ask before the work and keep the answer in the chat.

**What if the shop is closed and the bike is stuck?** Hours are 09:00-21:00 and there is no 24/7 rescue. Make the bike safe, message the shop with photos, and keep any outside repair to the smallest job that gets the bike moving.

**Does an outside repair affect my deposit?** That is not published, so ask at handover. The records you keep, the receipt, the photos and the messages, are what you would show.

**Can a roadside mechanic adjust the chain?** Chain adjustment is among the maintenance the shop may provide, so report it in Hanoi. Far from the shop it is on the yes-list, after a call.
