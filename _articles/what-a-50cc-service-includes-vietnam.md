Handing a 50cc to a Vietnamese neighbourhood workshop for a service is one of the better deals in vehicle ownership — provided you know what the visit should include, and what no workshop can substitute.

## The golden rule first

Service intervals are set by your model's manufacturer manual — the only authority for when oil, filters and parts are due. No article (including this one), no rule-of-thumb, and no friendly counter estimate replaces that book. If the manual is lost, a mechanic who works on the model can source the schedule; "we usually do it every X" is a habit, not a spec.

## The basic service: what it covers

- **Engine oil and drain.** The item everything else orbits — small engines carry small volumes, so fresh oil at manual intervals is the cheapest life extension available. The level between services is your job (the checking article takes two minutes).
- **Oil strainer or filter screen.** Small engines often run a screen rather than a cartridge; it gets cleaned, not replaced.
- **Air filter.** Dusty-season Hanoi clogs filters fast; our air-filter article covers why it matters more than riders assume.
- **Chain inspection, adjustment and lube** on geared underbones (the chain article covers what good looks like).
- **Cable lube and free-play**: throttle, both brakes, clutch-on-rope models; cables are the steering of a 50cc.
- **Brake adjustment** at both ends, plus shoe wear reported honestly.
- **Tyre pressures set by gauge** and tread condition noted.
- **Lights, horn, brake light** tested; bulbs and connections as needed.
- **Fasteners and steering-head check**: the shake-down that finds loose engine mounts and clicking head bearings.

## The periodic bigger items

Fuel system cleaning (carburettor on older machines, injector service on injected models — our carburettor-versus-injection article explains which you have), wheel bearing checks, valve clearance where the manual calls for it, and spark plug replacement — each on its own manual cadence, not on every visit.

## What no service substitutes

The weekly ten-minute routine in our maintenance checks article. A workshop sees the bike twice a year; the rider sees it twice a day — the two systems work together or not at all.

## Working with the workshop

Ask what was done and what was found, keep the ten-word notebook going between visits, and treat a mechanic who reports "nothing needed" as the asset they are. Workshop rates vary across the city, so collect quotes for anything beyond the basics rather than anchoring on one shop's number.
