Most of a motorbike’s night-time visibility budget is spent forward: the headlight. But being seen is a different problem from seeing — and reflective material is the cheapest, lightest, most passive "be seen" technology on the market.

## How reflectives work

Retro-reflective material bounces light back toward its source — headlights, streetlights, torch beams. It needs no power, weighs nothing, and works at the exact moment it matters: when another vehicle’s lights are pointed at you. Unlike active lighting, it never runs flat, and unlike a hi-vis vest it needs no daylight.

## Where it goes on a bike

Rim tape on wheels turns the spinning circle into a bright ring visible from the side — the classic motorbike application, effective precisely because motion draws the eye. Helmet stickers raise your profile height; pannier and top-box strips widen your apparent width; frame and pannier edges catch light from following cars. The wheel-level and box-level placements matter because most of what threatens a parked or slow motorbike approaches from the side and rear, not the front — where your lights already speak (motorbike-night-lights-vietnam).

## What to buy

Proper retro-reflective tape (sealed microscopic-glass or microprismatic types) dramatically outperforms cheap novelty "shiny" stickers; check that what you buy is rated reflective, not just glossy. Colours: white and fluorescent lime are seen furthest; red on rear-facing edges reads correctly to drivers. Contour-cut conformable tape follows curves and survives washing; bargain sheet stickers peel within a rainy season.

## The honest limits

Reflectives do not light you up in an unlit area with no headlights pointed at you, and they do not substitute for the active visibility and positioning discipline in visibility-riding-motorbike-vietnam and night-riding-motorbike-safety-vietnam. On Vietnam’s unlit rural roads (unlit-rural-roads-night-motorbike-vietnam), reflectives on both you and the bike are part of the layering, not the whole answer.

## A practical start

One roll of quality rim tape, a few helmet and box strips: an afternoon’s work and the smallest money in this entire cluster, for a real gain in the hours when most riders feel least visible.
