Can you flat-foot it? For shorter riders that question matters more than any power figure. Seat height and weight decide whether a bike feels manageable at every red light in Hanoi. All figures below are manufacturer-published (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## The verified seat-height ladder

- Honda Future 125 FI - 756 mm, 104 kg
- Honda Wave RSX - 760 mm, 98 kg
- Honda Blade - 769 mm, 98-99 kg
- Honda Wave Alpha - 770 mm, 96 kg
- Honda CBR150R - 788 mm, 139 kg
- Honda Super Cub C125 - 780 mm, 109 kg
- Honda Winner R - 795 mm, 124 kg
- Honda CT125 - 800 mm, 117 kg
- Yamaha Sirius RC and Exciter 155 VVA - seat heights not published by Yamaha on the retrieved pages.

## The best combinations for shorter riders

The Future 125 pairs the lowest published seat with real torque (10.2 Nm), which matters because launching from a stop with one foot down is where short riders work hardest. The Wave RSX is nearly as low at 760 mm and 6 kg lighter. Both have the foot-shift semi-automatic layout that removes the clutch-lever balancing act entirely - the biggest single friend of a new, shorter rider in traffic.

## Avoid, honestly

The CT125 (800 mm) and Winner R (795 mm) sit tall for their classes, and the CBR150R's 139 kg mass punishes anyone not confident at parking speeds.
