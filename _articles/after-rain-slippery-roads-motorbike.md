The rain stops and the road still tries to kill you for an hour. The most slippery moment on a Vietnamese road is often the first minutes after rain eases: surface oil rises, mud runs onto corners from construction sites, and drivers switch their brains back to dry-road mode while the surface is still wet.

## The first ten minutes are the worst

Light rain lifting moisture out of the asphalt brings a film of old oil and rubber dust to the top. Assume black, shiny asphalt is greasy until you have felt real grip through a couple of corners. Brake earlier, lean less, and let the extra distance do the work.

## Mud and sand wash onto the road

After real downpours, red mud slides from building sites and hillsides onto the road, usually at corners and the bottom of slopes. A strip of fresh mud is as slick as ice: ride through it upright, off the brakes and off the throttle, exactly like riding on gravel: upright, off the brakes, off the throttle — gravel-roads-motorbike-vietnam covers the technique.

## Leaves and debris

Storms and wind leave leaves, branches and fruit on the road. Leaves are treacherous in two ways: they hide what is under them and they slide like grease when wet. Treat any leaf-carpeted corner as unpaved.

## Wet paint and metal stay wet longest

Road markings, manhole covers, steel plates and tiled pavements hold a film of water long after asphalt looks dry. Cross them upright and off the brakes — emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam explains why painted lines cause lock-ups.
