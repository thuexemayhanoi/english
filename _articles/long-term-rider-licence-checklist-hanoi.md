Daily tourists can think about licences once. Long-term riders need a system: documents that stay valid for the whole rental, checked at set-up and tracked across months.

For an expat or any long-stay renter in Hanoi, that system answers three questions: which licence covers the bike, which dates end your right to ride, and when it is worth moving from an International Driving Permit to a Vietnamese licence. The legal points come from Law 36/2024/QH15 in its consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH (as amended by Law 118/2025/QH15) and from Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, which has applied since 1 July 2026 and replaced Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA.

In short, above the moped class you need a Vietnamese licence, a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP carried with an in-term national licence, or a licence recognised under a treaty route (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 57(6)); a resident with a qualifying visa or card can ask to exchange the national licence (Article 24 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA).

## The checklist itself

1. A licence valid for the bike you rent. Licence classes in Vietnam correspond to engine sizes — for example an A1 class covers smaller machines and larger bikes need a higher class. Match your licence class to the bike before signing: [Vietnam Motorbike Licence Classes Explained (A1, A, B1)]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) lists the classes.
2. For foreigners: a Vietnamese driving licence, or an IDP recognised in Vietnam together with the correct home licence, depending on your nationality and situation. The verified rules are in [International Driving Permits in Vietnam: IDP Rules for Motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) — check your own case against them.
3. Your passport with a valid visa or residence basis.
4. A copy of the rental agreement and the shop's contact details, stored somewhere you can find them mid-term.

### Which licence covers which bike

Article 57(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets three motorbike classes, and Article 34(1)(g) defines the moped that needs none. Measure the exact bike you are offered against the table, because a model name on its own does not tell you the class.

| Class | What it covers | Minimum age | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| None (moped) | Two or three wheels and a design speed capped at 50 km/h, with either a combustion engine up to 50 cm3 or an electric motor up to 4 kW | 16 to ride | No licence needed |
| A1 | Two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cm3, or with an electric motor up to 11 kW | 18 to be issued the licence | None |
| A | Two-wheeled motorcycles above 125 cm3 or above 11 kW, plus everything A1 covers | 18 | None |
| B1 | Three-wheeled motorcycles, plus everything A1 covers | 18 | None |

Ages come from Article 59(1) and time limits from Article 57(5)(a). The last column describes a licence gained by test; one obtained by exchange follows the term rule below.

A car licence belongs to different classes: Article 57(1)(d) gives class B to cars and light trucks and names no motorcycle, so [a car licence does not carry over to a 125 cc scooter]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). The moped class is licence-free but not rule-free: Article 56(4) expects a moped rider to understand the road rules, ride competently and satisfy the age and health requirements, as the [50 cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains.

## Set-up and expiry tracking

Write down every expiry date — licence, visa, passport — and check them monthly. [When Your Licence or Visa Expires Mid-Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/licence-expiry-during-long-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers what to do when something expires mid-term; the goal of the checklist is that nothing expires unnoticed.

### The dates to track

Each document ends on its own date, and for riding the first licence date to arrive is the one that counts. Set all of them against the end date of your rental.

| Document | Date that matters | If it lapses | Read more |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign national licence | Its printed expiry date, which also caps the IDP behind it (Article 57(8)(a)) | No longer valid (Article 57(7)(a)), and the IDP no longer fits it | [How long an IDP lasts]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| IDP (1968 Convention) | Three years from issue at most, or the licence date if that comes first | The pair stops counting as a licence | The pair, below |
| Exchanged Vietnamese licence | The term printed on it, which tracks the visa or card behind the exchange and the foreign licence's expiry (Article 24(5)(a)) | Out of term, so no longer valid (Article 57(7)(a)) | [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| Vietnamese A1, A or B1 licence from a test | No printed end (Article 57(5)(a)), but it must still have points (Article 56(1)) | A licence with every point deducted cannot be used to ride (Article 58(3)) | [Licence validity and renewal]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| Visa or temporary residence card | Days left, set against the exchange minimum (Article 24(1)(a)) | Exchange is not open on that paper, and it is one of the dates an exchanged licence follows | Exchange, below |

Passport and visa dates belong on the same list; renewing either is for your embassy and the immigration authorities. Two more dates sit beside them: the end or renewal date in your rental agreement, since a licence date that falls before it needs solving before you extend, and the end of your own insurance policy. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so cover is something you arrange yourself; [insurance for long-term renters]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists questions to put to an insurer.

## Which foreign-licence route you are on

Article 57(6) lists the licences valid in Vietnam: a Vietnamese licence, an IDP with a national licence from a 1968 Vienna Convention state, and two treaty routes.

### The IDP and national licence pair

Under Article 57(6)(b) the permit and the national licence count together when both come from a 1968 Vienna Convention state and both fit the vehicle; [which countries' IDPs count in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how to tell whether your permit's country qualifies. Article 57(8)(b) requires you to carry both, in term, and to follow Vietnamese traffic law, so the permit never replaces the licence behind it. Article 57(8)(a) caps the permit at three years from issue and ties it to the time left on the national licence.

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA also governs the permits Vietnam itself issues (Articles 27 to 29). Article 30(1) is the line a resident needs: a 1968 Convention permit that Vietnam has issued cannot be used inside the country, so it is no substitute for a licence. Article 30(2) leaves a permit recognised under another treaty to that treaty.

### Treaty routes

Article 57(6)(c) and (d) add licences from a country or territory that has a mutual-recognition agreement with Vietnam, and licences recognised through a treaty Vietnam has joined. Check any such route against an official source before building a long stay on it.

### Exchange for a Vietnamese licence

For a stay of months or years, Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA offers exchange as an alternative to the IDP pair. Article 24(1)(a) sets the entry test: the applicant must be in Vietnam as a resident, worker or student, hold a national licence that has not expired, and have one more paper: a permanent residence card that is still valid, a visa or temporary residence card with at least 90 days to run, or a diplomatic or official ID card. An applicant who passes may then ask for the licence to be exchanged for the matching Vietnamese class.

Article 24(1)(c) excludes a temporary foreign licence, an IDP, a foreign licence that is past its term or so erased or torn that it no longer carries enough information, and one not issued by a competent authority.

Article 24(5)(a) is the date rule. In a plain translation of the Vietnamese text, the term of a Vietnamese licence exchanged for a foreigner "is consistent with the term" shown on one of the papers listed in Article 24(1)(a) and "consistent with the term of the foreign licence", but "does not exceed the term set for the Vietnamese licence". The date on the new card is therefore tied to those papers, not to your rental, so read it against the end of your stay.

Article 24(2) lists the dossier: a translation of the foreign licence certified as that paragraph describes, a valid health certificate (not required of a foreigner who exchanges for the term written in the entry visa or temporary residence card) and a copy of the qualifying visa or card.

You file at the Traffic Police Department, at a commune-level police office with a reception point, or online through the National Public Service Portal (Article 24(3)(a)). Article 24(3)(d) gives 1 day from a complete dossier for the Department to pass the data to the Traffic Police Bureau for central management and issue of the licence, electronic version included; a printed licence on request takes up to 2.5 working days. File while the visa or card still has comfortably more than 90 days left. [The expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) compares exchange with the test route, which suits a rider with no motorbike entitlement at home or whose licence cannot be exchanged.

## What to carry while riding

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 asks a driver for a licence that is in term, still has points and fits the vehicle, and for four papers on the road: the registration certificate, the licence, the inspection certificate where the law requires one for the vehicle, and the vehicle owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate. A moped rider is the exception to the licence rule (Article 56(4)); on the IDP route the permit and national licence travel together.

Article 56(3) allows a paper linked to your electronic identification account to be shown through that account. Article 56(3) names only that account, so keep the physical papers with you. The insurance certificate is the vehicle owner's and the registration certificate is the bike's; [what documents a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what a stop involves.

## Why this matters more on long rentals

A tourist riding a week takes a calculated risk once. A resident riding daily for months will eventually meet a police checkpoint, an insurance situation or an accident scene, and every one of those goes better with correct documents. Riding without the right licence carries fines and can escalate to vehicle impoundment — [When Vietnamese Police Can Impound Your Motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers that worst case.

Penalties for licence offences sit in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended from 15 August 2026. This guide quotes no amounts, so read the figures in [the fine for riding without a licence]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [the fine for an expired licence]({{ '/articles/expired-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and confirm them against the decree text in force.

## Practical habit

Set a monthly phone reminder in the first week of each rental month: check licence, check visa, check the agreement dates against your actual plans. Five minutes a month prevents the classic long-term mistakes.

### Before you book

- Name your route: Vietnamese licence, IDP pair, treaty licence or the moped class.
- On the IDP route, read both expiry dates and the motorcycle category on both documents, and measure the earlier date against the day you plan to hand the bike back.
- If an exchange is a real option, check the days left on your visa or card against the minimum given in the exchange section above.

### At handover

- Set the exact bike's engine capacity or motor rating against the class you hold.
- Photograph the date page of each of your documents and keep the pictures away from the originals.
- Enter every date from the tracking table in your phone calendar, with alerts ahead of each.

### Each month

- Look at the licence card itself: a card so erased or torn that it no longer carries enough information cannot be exchanged (Article 24(1)(c)), so keep it undamaged.
- Compare the earliest licence date with the end of the rental, leaving a margin you are comfortable with.
- Compare the agreement's end or renewal date with your real plans, and message the shop before that date if they have changed.

### Before a visa or residence-card change

Before you renew, extend or change status, set the new date beside the licence date.

### Before you leave

Work back from the hand-back date and check that no document lapses before it; once a document lapses, ride no further on it and tell the shop early. To keep the bike longer, get the extension confirmed in writing, and only if every licence date reaches the new end date.

## Quick answers

### Do I still need my IDP after exchanging my licence?

No, not to ride in Vietnam: a Vietnamese licence is itself valid in Vietnam under Article 57(6)(a), so riding on it does not rest on the IDP-and-national-licence pair of Article 57(6)(b). The foreign licence stays with you: Article 24(4) leaves the original dossier, including the foreign licence, with the applicant, apart from the papers listed in Article 26(3).

### Can I start an exchange if my visa has fewer than 90 days left?

Not on that paper: Article 24(1)(a) sets a minimum number of days left, given in the exchange section above, and a visa or card with fewer days does not meet the wording. Check the count before you pay for a certified translation.

### Can I swap to a bigger bike partway through a rental?

Only if the new bike falls inside your class: A1 stops at 125 cm3 for a combustion engine and 11 kW for an electric motor, and above that class A is needed. If a swap is on offer, ask for its engine capacity or motor rating first.
