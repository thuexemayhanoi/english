For expats staying in Hanoi for months or years, a monthly rental sits between daily rentals and buying a bike. It often makes sense — if you set it up properly.

This page covers the monthly arrangement for someone who already lives here: what changes compared with a visitor, how to tie the rental to your lease, probation or contract dates, and the practical details of parking, handover and records. Still settling in? [Motorbike Rental for Expats Moving to Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-expats-moving-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) looks at renting a bike while you settle in.

## Licence reality first

Riding legally in Vietnam requires the right licence for the bike you ride — for foreigners this usually means a Vietnamese licence or a valid IDP paired with the correct home licence, depending on your situation. Do not ride unlicensed; the fines and impound risks are real and are covered in the [law and licences hub]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}). [The Long-Term Rider's Licence Checklist for Hanoi]({{ '/articles/long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the expat-specific paperwork.

### What the law recognises

The rental agreement does not make you legal to ride; your licence does, and Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) recognises a Vietnamese licence, an International Driving Permit carried with a national licence (both issued by a 1968 Vienna Convention state, the licence being appropriate to the vehicle) and licences from countries that have a recognition treaty or agreement with Vietnam, a route to confirm from an official source before you rely on it.

Article 57(8) limits the permit to no more than three years from its issue date, says it has to be consistent with the remaining validity of the national licence, and requires its holder to carry both documents, in date and appropriate to the class of vehicle. A monthly term can outlast either date, so compare both with the end of the term you want, using [the IDP validity guide]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) to check yours.

### Exchanging for a Vietnamese licence

Residents have a second route: under Article 24(1)(a) of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, a foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam and holds a national driving licence that is still valid, together with one of a diplomatic or service identity card, or a temporary or permanent residence card, with a period of three months or more, can be considered for exchange to the matching Vietnamese class. Among others, the Circular excludes an International Driving Permit and a foreign licence that is expired, altered, or so damaged that it no longer shows enough information.

[How Foreigners Exchange a Licence for a Vietnamese One]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the process and [the long-term expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the choice between an IDP and an exchange, so if your stay will outlast the date on your IDP, decide early, because an exchange needs a national licence that is still valid.

## Matching the term to your lease, probation or contract

Nguyen Tu's rental periods are daily, weekly and monthly, and longer-term rental is negotiable with the customer. No longer-term rate is published, so ask what rate and conditions would apply instead of assuming that a longer commitment costs less.

For an expat the useful question is which date limits your stay. A probation period or a lease not yet signed argues for renewing month by month; a contract, lease or course with a known end date is the moment to ask about a longer agreement that runs to that date. The [the monthly rental topic page]({{ '/topics/monthly-rental/' | relative_url }}) lists the guides for three months, six months or longer and a full year, so this page sticks to your own dates.

Put those dates side by side before you agree a term: the end of your probation or contract, the start and end of your lease, the period shown on your residence card, and the dates on your IDP and national licence. A rental that outlasts your lease can leave the bike without a place to sleep.

Two more questions belong here. If a partner or colleague may want to ride too, ask whether the agreement limits who may ride; anyone who does ride needs a valid licence of the right class. And if you expect to ride beyond Hanoi, say so before you rent or renew: support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so ask what happens if the bike needs attention away from the city.

Then send the shop one message that records the start date and the end or renewal date, the rate and deposit for your model, who may ride, where the bike is kept overnight, and the answers you were given on notice and on leaving early.

Renewal timing matters as much as the structure. Agree an extension before the term ends: keeping the bike past the end date without one is a late return, which carries 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged. A message in your final week keeps it simple, and [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows how to phrase it.

## What to arrange at set-up

The standard monthly requirements apply: deposit, documents, agreement terms. [Monthly Motorbike Rental Requirements in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/monthly-motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists them. As an expat you have one extra question: what happens if your visa status changes mid-term? Keep the agreement and the shop's contact details somewhere you can find them months later.

The visa question has a simple core. Your right to ride comes from your licence documents, not from the rental agreement, so if your licence or IDP lapses you cannot ride until you hold a valid document again, and you should ask the shop what happens to the agreement if a document lapses. Your residence documents matter in a different way: the exchange route above needs a card with a period of three months or more, so keep those dates in view too. Tell the shop as soon as you know of any change, because it can affect your end date or your plan to extend.

### Handover and collection

A monthly rental has two handovers, one at each end, and the first is the moment to inspect the bike, settle the deposit and confirm the terms in your written summary. If you want delivery or pickup, raise it when you first enquire; the delivery question below sets out what is and is not published.

If you collect in person, the shop is at 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De, Long Bien, open 09:00–21:00 daily; plan any delivery or pickup within those hours.

### Paying and keeping records

A bank transfer or QR payment leaves a record in your own banking app. Whichever method you use, keep the confirmation together with the message thread that shows the agreed rate and period, and ask when each payment falls due and how a renewal is paid.

A rental that lasts months produces paperwork: the written summary, the deposit and payment confirmations, any repair receipt and your insurance policy all belong in the same place as the agreement.

## Where the bike sleeps: parking at an apartment

A monthly bike needs a fixed overnight place, and in an apartment building that is a question for the building first. Settle it before you sign the lease rather than after the bike arrives, because a bike with nowhere safe to sleep becomes a daily problem. [Apartment Living With a Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/apartment-living-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) separates the building types you will meet (towers with basement parking, older blocks with a caretaker lot, and tube houses or serviced apartments with no building parking) and has a list of questions to put to a landlord.

A rented bike adds questions of its own:

- What does the building ask to see before a bike can park or get a parking card, when the bike is rented rather than owned?
- Who is responsible if the bike is damaged or stolen in the building's lot, and what does your own insurance say about it?
- Does the shop need to know where the bike is kept overnight, and what should you do if that address changes mid-term?

Fees differ between buildings and lots, so get the figure from the one you will use.

## Build the routine

Expats who last on a rental are the ones who park in guarded spots, do the weekly five-minute check, arrange their own insurance — Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, and [Arranging Your Own Insurance as a Long-Term Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains arranging your own — and keep receipts for anything they fix.

Check the policy you already hold before buying another. Some expat health and travel policies exclude motorbike riding or require a valid licence of the class you actually hold, and it is better to find that out before the first ride than after an incident.

### Checks for a bike that sleeps outdoors

A bike parked outdoors or in a shared apartment lot is exposed to knocks and weather, so look over the tyres, chain, brakes and lights before your first ride of the week; [the monthly inspection schedule]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-inspection-schedule-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) gives the weekly routine and the check around 1,500 km. Nguyen Tu's guidance is a periodic check at around 1,500 km or more of use; it is practical guidance, not a manufacturer interval. If something needs fixing, ask the shop how repairs are handled before you pay anyone else.

## Common questions

### Do I need a Vietnamese licence to ride a monthly rental?

Not in every case. As set out above, Article 57 also recognises a 1968-convention IDP carried with a national licence that fits the vehicle, provided both are in date. A Vietnamese licence, which a resident can apply for by exchange under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, replaces that arrangement. Ask the shop which documents it wants to see before you pay.

### Can the bike be delivered to my apartment?

Delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement. No delivery fee or delivery time is published, so ask the shop to confirm the details by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674 before you plan around it, and check with your building that a bike can be brought in on the day.

### Can I pay a monthly rental by card?

Nguyen Tu accepts cash, bank transfer and QR payment. Card payment is not among the published arrangements, so if you need a different method, ask before you plan around it.

### Do I get a refund if I leave Hanoi before the month ends?

There is no automatic refund entitlement for unused time. Give notice as soon as your plans change and agree the return with the shop; [the early-exit guide]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers notice and what to expect on unused time.

### What deposit should I expect on a monthly rental?

The typical range is 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, depending on the vehicle and conditions. Ask for the figure that applies to your bike and for what it is held against, then include both in your written summary of the terms.
