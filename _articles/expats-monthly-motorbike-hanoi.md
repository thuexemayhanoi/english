For expats staying in Hanoi for months or years, a monthly rental sits between daily rentals and buying a bike. It often makes sense — if you set it up properly.

This page covers the monthly arrangement for someone who already lives here: what changes compared with a visitor, the terms worth getting in writing, and the practical details of parking, delivery and payment. Still planning the move? [Motorbike Rental for Expats Moving to Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-expats-moving-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) handles arrival logistics, [Best Motorbike for Expats Living in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-expats-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) helps with choosing a model, and [Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi: How It Works]({{ '/articles/monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the product in general.

The short answer: rent monthly, renew month by month while your plans settle, ask about a longer agreement once they do, and treat your licence and your insurance as jobs the rental does not do for you.

## Licence reality first

Riding legally in Vietnam requires the right licence for the bike you ride — for foreigners this usually means a Vietnamese licence or a valid IDP paired with the correct home licence, depending on your situation. Do not ride unlicensed; the fines and impound risks are real and are covered in the [law and licences hub]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}). [The Long-Term Rider's Licence Checklist for Hanoi]({{ '/articles/long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the expat-specific paperwork.

### What the law recognises

The rental agreement does not make you legal to ride; your licence does. Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) lists the licences valid on Vietnamese territory. Two matter most to an expat on a monthly rental: a licence issued by the Vietnamese authorities, and an International Driving Permit together with a national licence, both issued by a 1968 Vienna Convention state, the licence being appropriate to the vehicle. The law also recognises licences from countries that have a recognition treaty or agreement with Vietnam, so confirm that route from an official source before you rely on it.

Article 57(8) adds two conditions that matter over a long rental. The permit is valid for no more than three years from its issue date and has to fit within the remaining validity of the national licence, and its holder must carry both documents, in date and appropriate to the class of vehicle. A monthly term can outlast either date, so compare both with the end of the term you are asking for.

### Exchanging for a Vietnamese licence

Residents have a second route. Under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, a foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam and holds a national driving licence that is still valid, together with a temporary or permanent residence card valid for three months or more (or a diplomatic or service identity card), can be considered for exchange to the matching Vietnamese class. Among the licences the Circular excludes are an International Driving Permit and a foreign licence that has expired or is altered or damaged.

[How Foreigners Exchange a Licence for a Vietnamese One]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the process, including the certified Vietnamese translation of your foreign licence that the dossier needs. Whether to exchange is a practical decision: if your stay will outlast the date on your IDP, decide early, because an exchange needs a national licence that is still valid.

### Month by month, or a longer agreement

Nguyen Tu's rental periods are daily, weekly and monthly, and longer-term rental is negotiable with the customer. No longer-term rate is published, so ask what rate and conditions would apply instead of assuming that a longer commitment costs less.

| | Month-to-month renewal | A longer agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment | One month at a time | A term agreed in advance |
| Rate | Confirm the monthly rate for your model | Ask directly: no longer-term rate is published |
| Suits | An uncertain stay, a probation period, a lease not yet signed | A contract, lease or course with a known end date |
| Ask about | Notice before each renewal and whether your own bike stays yours | What happens if you leave early or change bike |

Renewal timing matters as much as the structure. Agree an extension before the term ends: keeping the bike past the end date without one is a late return, which carries 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged. A message in your final week keeps it simple, and [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows how to phrase it.

## What to arrange at set-up

The standard monthly requirements apply: deposit, documents, agreement terms. [Monthly Motorbike Rental Requirements in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/monthly-motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists them. As an expat you have one extra question: what happens if your visa status changes mid-term? Keep the agreement and the shop's contact details somewhere you can find them months later.

The visa question has a simple core. Your right to ride comes from your licence documents, not from the rental agreement, so if your licence or IDP lapses the rental carries on but riding has to stop until you hold a valid document again. Your visa matters in a different way: the exchange route above needs a residence card valid for three months or more, so keep those dates in view too. Tell the shop as soon as you know of any change, because it can affect your end date or your plan to extend.

### Terms to get in writing

A monthly term runs for weeks, so each term below applies for far longer than it would on a daily rental. Ask about every row before you pay, then send the shop one message that records the dates, the rate, the deposit and the answers. ## Where the bike sleeps: parking at an apartment

A monthly bike needs a fixed overnight place, and in an apartment building that is a question for the building, not the shop. Settle it before you sign the lease rather than after the bike arrives. [Apartment Living With a Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/apartment-living-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) separates the building types you will meet (towers with basement parking, older blocks with a caretaker lot, and tube houses or serviced apartments with no building parking) and has a list of questions to put to a landlord.

A rented bike adds questions of its own:

- What does the building ask to see before a bike can park or get a parking card, when the bike is rented rather than owned?
- Who is responsible if the bike is damaged or stolen in the building's lot, and what does your own insurance say about it?
- Does the shop need to know where the bike is kept overnight, and what should you do if that address changes mid-term?

Where a building offers nothing, the rules for paid lots, pavement parking and no-parking areas are in [Where to Park a Motorbike in Hanoi: Rules and Etiquette]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Fees differ between buildings and lots, so get the figure from the one you will use.

## Build the routine

Expats who last on a rental are the ones who park in guarded spots, do the weekly five-minute check, arrange their own insurance — Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, and [Arranging Your Own Insurance as a Long-Term Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains arranging your own — and keep receipts for anything they fix.

Check the policy you already hold before buying another. Some expat health and travel policies exclude motorbike riding or require a valid licence of the class you actually hold, and it is better to find that out before the first ride than after an incident.

### A five-minute weekly check

Pick one day a week, ideally before the first ride, and walk around the bike:

- **Tyres:** look for nails, cuts and flat patches, and check the pressure; ask the shop which pressure suits your bike instead of guessing a number.
- **Chain:** listen for dry rattling and look for rust or obvious slack, then report it rather than adjusting it yourself, because chain adjustment is one of the jobs the shop's maintenance may include.
- **Brakes:** both levers, and any pedal, should feel the same as last week; new squealing or grinding is a reason to message the shop.
- **Lights, indicators, horn and mirrors:** all working and none loose.
- **Underneath:** dark stains where the bike sleeps point to a leak.
- **Odometer:** note the reading in your phone so you know the distance since the last visit.

The owner's guidance is a periodic check at around 1,500 km or more of use. That is practical guidance from the owner, not a manufacturer service interval, so ask when your bike is due. If something needs fixing, message the shop before you pay anyone else for more than an emergency repair, and keep the receipt.

## Mistakes that catch expat renters

- **Signing the lease before checking parking.** A bike that has nowhere safe to sleep becomes a daily problem. Put the building questions above to the landlord before you commit.
- **Lending the bike to a friend.** A friend who rides needs the right licence for that bike, and lending to an unlicensed rider has legal consequences that [the lending guide]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out. On a rented bike, ask the shop who may ride it before you hand over the keys.
- **Planning a long trip without telling the shop.** Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use. If you want to ride beyond the city, raise it before you rent or renew, and ask what happens if the bike needs attention away from Hanoi.

## Common questions

### Do I need a Vietnamese licence to ride a monthly rental?

Not in every case. As set out above, Article 57 also recognises a 1968-convention IDP carried with a national licence that fits the vehicle, provided both are in date. A Vietnamese licence, which a resident can apply for by exchange under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, replaces that arrangement. Ask the shop which documents it wants to see before you pay.

### Can the bike be delivered to my apartment?

Delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement. There is no universal fixed delivery fee and no guaranteed delivery time, so confirm the details directly with the shop by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674 before you plan around it.

### Can I pay a monthly rental by card?

Nguyen Tu accepts cash, bank transfer and QR payment. Card payment is not among the published arrangements, so if you need a different method, ask before you plan around it.

### Do I get a refund if I leave Hanoi before the month ends?

There is no automatic refund entitlement for unused time. Give notice as soon as your plans change and agree the return with the shop; [the early-exit guide]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers notice, and [How Returning a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi Works]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the return itself.

### What deposit should I expect on a monthly rental?

The typical range is 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, depending on the vehicle and conditions. Ask for the figure that applies to your bike and for what it is held against, then include both in your written summary of the terms.
