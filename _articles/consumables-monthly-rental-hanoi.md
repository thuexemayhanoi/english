On a short rental, consumables barely matter — a week of riding rarely wears a tyre. Over a month or a season they become real costs, so it helps to know the normal arrangement in Hanoi.

## Who pays for what

Routine maintenance such as oil, chain adjustment and inspection is handled by the shop, but consumables — inner tubes and tyres in particular — may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation. The exact split depends on the rental terms you agree at pickup, so ask the question directly: who pays for a tyre if it wears out during the rental, and who pays if it is punctured on the road?

## Punctures versus wear

These are usually treated differently. A puncture from a nail or road debris is an everyday Hanoi event — punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike covers the practical steps, and tube repairs are cheap and quick at roadside shops. Slow wear from your own mileage is closer to a use cost, which is why it may fall to you on a long rental.

## Replacing fairly

If a tyre needs replacing during your rental, agree with the shop first rather than buying one unilaterally. Outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike explains how small shops interact with rental agreements, and the shop may have preferences about brands or tyre types. Keep the receipt either way.

## Note it in the agreement

Whatever the split, get it written into your rental terms. Maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi outlines the maintenance side of the same conversation.

## The one-line version

Oil, chain and inspection are the shop's side; tubes and tyres may be yours on a long rental — settle who pays before you need the answer.
