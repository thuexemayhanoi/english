Here is the sentence that ruins more Vietnam trips than any road: "I thought my travel insurance covered me on the motorbike." For many travellers it does — conditionally. Travel insurance and motorbikes interact through a set of standard policy clauses that most riders never read, and in Vietnam those clauses matter more than almost anywhere, because the licence question is where most policies quietly escape.

## The four clauses that decide everything

**1. The licence clause.** Insurance guides for the region are direct: coverage depends on whether you hold a licence valid for the vehicle in that country. In Vietnam that means a Vietnamese licence or a 1968-convention IDP endorsed for the bike's class (see our licence overview). Riding on no licence, or on a home-country licence alone (invalid in Vietnam for over 50cc), voids the medical section of most policies — the hospital bills from a crash become entirely yours.

**2. The engine-size cap.** Policies tier their coverage by cc — analysts of Vietnamese motorbike insurance note the market's standard splits at 50cc and 125cc. Many backpacker-oriented policies cover riding up to 125cc with a valid licence, and exclude anything bigger. Riding a 155cc touring bike on a 125cc-capped policy is self-insurance with paperwork attached.

**3. The activity exclusion.** Some insurers exclude two-wheeled travel entirely, and some explicitly exclude "motorcycle touring" as distinct from local transport use — one insurer confirmed in writing that motorbike touring was not covered. Know which activity class your itinerary falls into before you rely on the policy.

**4. The rental versus ownership distinction.** Some policies treat hired vehicles as a separate clause with its own conditions. And note the Vietnam-specific layer: rental bikes are not insured by the rental shop — our article on what rentals include explains that the renter arranges their own cover — while Vietnamese compulsory third-party insurance (a separate, local policy on the bike itself) is not health insurance for you at all; our compulsory-insurance guide explains what that document actually covers.

## How to check your policy in ten minutes

1. Search the policy document for "motorcycle", "moped", "two-wheeled" and "licen[cs]e".
2. Find the cc cap and the licence-validity wording.
3. Compare against what you will actually ride (be honest about the 155cc scooter you are eyeing in Da Nang).
4. Check whether "touring" is excluded while "transport" is covered — a day-trip is transport, a Hanoi-to-Saigon ride is touring.
5. If the answers do not suit the trip, buy a rider-specific policy or an adventure-sports add-on before arrival — insurance sold at the rental counter is rarely what you think it is.

## The boring but decisive rule

The licence and the policy are one system: **get valid riding papers, then match the policy to the papers.** The uninsured rider who crashes is the same person, financially, as the unlicensed rider — and the difference between a covered evacuation and a five-figure hospital bill is usually one paragraph you never read. Ten minutes with the PDF, before the helmet goes on.
