Motorbikes are the budget way to see northern Vietnam — a day trip costs less than a single tourist-bus tour seat. But "cheap" still needs a budget, because the categories are many and small: they add up into the difference between a relaxed day and an awkward one.

## The categories

**The bike.** Rental cost is the fixed line — agree it before the trip, including distance/overnight terms (our rental price guide covers the published daily and monthly rates). Fuel is variable: a typical 110–125 cc bike uses modest volumes per 100 km, and Vietnam prices petrol by the litre at state-set pump prices, so a 200 km day trip’s fuel is a rounding error next to city taxi fares.

**Food.** Rural pho, com and roadside meals cost a fraction of city tourist restaurants; lake and homestay fish dishes are the premium end. Two meals plus water and coffee per person is the honest line item.

**Entries and activities.** Pagodas, national parks, boat trips and lake circuits all charge modest per-person fees — and boats are often the big-ticket item of a day (Quan Son, Tam Coc, Thung Nai all revolve around one). Ferry fares are minor per crossing.

**Lodging** (overnight trips). Homestays and guesthouses in the northern provinces are inexpensive; resort areas (Dai Lai, Tam Dao weekends) price higher. Weekend premiums are real — midweek is cheaper almost everywhere.

**The margin.** The line every rider regrets skipping: cash for a puncture repair, a tow, an unplanned night, or a police paperwork situation. Keep it physically separate from the day wallet.

## What drives the total

- **Distance and bike size**: fuel scales with both, though it stays the smallest major category.
- **Boats and entries**: on nature days, activities can exceed all riding costs.
-**Weekends**: lodging and some lake areas price up.
- **Freight and ferries** for long trips: train-shipping a bike south or ferrying to Cat Ba adds a fixed, plan-able cost.

## A sane planning method

Rather than itemising every dong, budget by day-type: a delta day (fuel + food + entries), a mountain day (fuel + boat/entry + food + lodging), a transfer day (fuel + freight/ferry). Multiply, add the margin, and carry it in cash — rural payments are cash, as the coverage and ATM guide explains.
