You can see Hanoi's postcard sights in a bus, or you can ride them in a day on a motorbike and actually feel the city change between stops. This route strings the classic sights into one loop of roughly 40 kilometres of easy riding, designed for a scooter or light motorbike, with realistic timing and parking for every stop.

## The route at a glance

**Start 07:30, West Lake. → Thanh Nien causeway → Tran Quoc pagoda → Ho Chi Minh complex (Ba Dinh) → Temple of Literature → lunch → Old Quarter and Dong Xuan → Hoan Kiem lake and Ngoc Son temple → ceramic mosaic wall on the dyke → Long Bien bridge → river islands leg → back via West Lake. Finish ~17:30.**

## Morning: the calm sights

Start early — Hanoi's best hours end by nine. Ride the West Lake loop's southern half first, when the water is still misted, stopping at Tran Quoc pagoda (parking inside the temple lot). Continue along Thanh Nien into Ba Dinh for the Ho Chi Minh mausoleum complex area; visit early because entry procedures and closures (afternoons, Mondays, and formal ceremony days) are real — check the current opening pattern before your visit, and note that security here is strict: no bags, no photos inside, respectable dress. A short hop south brings you to the Temple of Literature, the thousand-year-old imperial academy with easy on-site parking.

## Midday: eat like the itinerary means it

Ride into Dong Da or the edge of the Old Quarter for lunch. Pho is a morning dish by local convention — you have already had it or you will have it tomorrow; midday is bun cha or com tam territory. Around the Old Quarter perimeter lots, park once and walk between lunch and the market streets.

## Afternoon: the historic core

Spend two unhurried hours in the Old Quarter and around Hoan Kiem Lake: the thirty-six streets, Hang Ma's decorations, Dong Xuan market, and the bridge to Ngoc Son temple. On weekends remember the walking street: from Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight the lakeside streets are pedestrian-only, so this leg becomes a walking leg anyway. If your day falls on a weekend, flip the plan — do the Old Quarter on foot in the evening and keep the riding legs for the afternoon.

## Late afternoon: the river leg

Ride northeast along the dyke past the ceramic mosaic wall as the light turns golden, then cross the Long Bien bridge — slowly, as its guide insists — for the view over the river islands. On the Long Bien side, ride down through the fruit-market streets to the riverside lanes, then loop back. This final leg is the part of the day that feels least like tourism and most like actually living in Hanoi.

## Practical notes

- **Bike**: any reliable automatic scooter handles the whole day; a manual bike adds nothing except on the dyke legs.
- **Timing cushion**: the plan leaves slack for coffee; Hanoi days should.
- **Parking**: every stop above has attended lots; carry small notes.
- **Rain plan**: the route in reverse puts the indoor-ish stops (museums, temples, market) in the wetter half of the day.
- **Renting**: this route is the standard first-day ride for rental customers; any current model availability is best confirmed with the shop directly, and the guided-vs-self decision has its own comparison if you are unsure about riding Hanoi traffic alone.

One day is not all of Hanoi, but ridden well it is the whole arc: lakes, emperors, empires, markets and the river that made the city — with the wind of a moving bike through all of it.
