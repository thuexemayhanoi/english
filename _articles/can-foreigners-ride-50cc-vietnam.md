The licence-free moped rule applies to everyone on Vietnamese territory, foreign riders included. But "no licence needed" for a 50cc does not mean the rest of the system looks away.

## The legal position

A moped of 50cc or less (or an electric moped up to 4 kW) requires no driving licence; the rider must be at least 16, understand traffic rules and have basic handling skills, wear a standard-compliant helmet, and follow every traffic rule. Foreign nationality changes nothing here — the exemption and the duties are the same.

## Why foreigners look at this route

Visitors without a licence valid for Vietnamese motorbikes — no Vietnamese licence, no 1968 Vienna Convention IDP with motorcycle category — cannot legally ride a 110cc scooter. Some conclude that a 50cc is the loophole. Legally, for the 50cc class only, that conclusion is correct.

## What it does not solve

- **Accidents.** A crash on a moped triggers the same liability, insurance and paperwork as any crash. Riding with no licence entitlement at all (because you hold none anywhere) does not look good in that file.
- **Fines.** Same schedule as motorbikes: red lights 4–6 million VND, helmet 400–600k, alcohol up to 10 million.
- **Availability.** 50cc bikes are a small, informal slice of the Vietnamese market — mostly older models and grey imports. Rental fleets rarely specialise in them. Contact us to confirm current availability before planning around one.
- **Practical speed.** A 50cc that cannot hold 45 km/h is a specific, limited tool; read our honest Hanoi assessment before committing.

## The better question

If you hold a valid motorcycle entitlement from home, the whole question changes: a 1968-convention IDP with the right category plus your national licence opens normal rental scooters. Our tourists article and IDP guide cover the full routes to legal riding.

If the 50cc route still fits — say, a short stay, flat neighbourhood routes, no motorcycle licence — then the renting guide covers what to check before you ride off.
