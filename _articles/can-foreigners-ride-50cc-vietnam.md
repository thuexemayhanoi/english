The licence-free moped rule applies to everyone on Vietnamese territory, foreign riders included. But "no licence needed" for a 50cc does not mean the rest of the system looks away. The Law sets no licence class for the moped category, no nationality condition and a minimum age of 16; what decides whether a bike counts is the legal definition, not the "50cc" on its badge, and [what counts as a moped under Vietnamese law]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) gives the wording.

## The legal position

A moped of 50cc or less (or an electric moped up to 4 kW), designed for no more than 50 km/h, requires no driving licence; the rider must be at least 16, understand the traffic rules and have the skill to control the vehicle, wear a standard-compliant helmet, and follow every traffic rule. Foreign nationality changes nothing here — the exemption and the duties are the same.

Four clauses of Law 36/2024/QH15 carry this. Article 56(1) asks every driver for a licence that suits the vehicle, with an exception for the moped driver described in clause 4. Article 56(4) puts three conditions in its place: knowing the road traffic rules, having the skill to control the vehicle, and meeting the age and health conditions that Article 59(1)(a) and 59(2) set. The age is 16. Article 33(2) adds a helmet for the driver and any passenger.

None of these mentions nationality, so the texts read draw no distinction between a visitor and a resident. Article 56(4) also names no test, course or certificate: it asks for knowledge and skill rather than a card. [The helmet rules for mopeds]({{ '/articles/helmet-rules-50cc-mopeds/' | relative_url }}) cover the helmet duty.

### The test is the definition, not the badge

The word "50cc" describes an engine and nothing else. Article 34(1)(g) defines a moped by a design speed of no more than 50 km/h together with a limit for the motor: 50 cm3 for a combustion engine, 4 kW for an electric one. A badge, a listing title or a seller's description proves none of that. A scooter of about 110 cc is a motorcycle: class A1 covers a two-wheeler up to 125 cm3 (Article 57(1)(a)), and an A1 licence starts at 18 (Article 59(1)(b)).

## Why foreigners look at this route

Visitors without a licence valid for Vietnamese motorbikes — no Vietnamese licence, no 1968 Vienna Convention IDP with motorcycle category — cannot legally ride a 110cc scooter. Some conclude that a 50cc is the loophole. Legally, for the 50cc class only, that conclusion is correct.

A foreign rider usually arrives at the moped class by one of five roads:

- **A car licence only.** Article 57(1) lists the motorbike classes (A1, A and B1) apart from the car classes, so a car licence does not reach a motorbike above the moped class.
- **A licence from outside the 1968 Vienna Convention.** It counts only through a treaty route under Article 57(6)(c) or (d), and this page assumes none applies.
- **A permit without its national licence, or a 1949 Geneva permit.** Neither is the pair Article 57(6)(b) names; see the permit route below.
- **No licence anywhere.** There is nothing to exchange, and a Vietnamese licence comes through training and a test (Articles 60 and 61).
- **A rider of 16 or 17.** The moped opens at 16 (Article 59(1)(a)) and A1 at 18, so a teenage visitor stands where a teenage resident stands.

## What it does not solve

- **Accidents.** A crash on a moped triggers the same liability, insurance and paperwork as any crash. A rider with no licence anywhere breaks no licence rule on a moped; what a crash costs is a separate question, taken up below.
- **Fines.** Same schedule as motorbikes, under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as amended by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026. This page quotes no amounts, so check any figure in the current text, starting with [the guide to fines for 50cc riders]({{ '/articles/50cc-fines-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).
- **Availability.** 50cc bikes are a small, informal slice of the Vietnamese market — mostly older models and grey imports. Rental fleets rarely specialise in them. Contact us to confirm current availability before planning around one.
- **Practical speed.** A 50cc that cannot hold 45 km/h is a specific, limited tool; read our [honest Hanoi assessment]({{ '/articles/is-a-50cc-enough-for-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) before committing.

## Papers a foreign rider still carries

Article 56(1) lists four papers that a driver carries when taking part in road traffic: the registration certificate, a licence that suits the vehicle, the technical-safety and environmental-protection inspection certificate where the law requires one, and the vehicle owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate. The moped carve-out is in the licence requirement only; nothing in the clause waives the other papers. [What a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through the set.

Two points are specific to a visitor. No identity document is on that list, so any duty to carry a passport or a copy would have to come from another text, which this page does not cite. And the registration and insurance certificates belong to the bike, not the rider, so ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop. Article 56(3) lets papers integrated into an electronic identification account be shown through it, but this page does not assume a visitor has one, so plan for paper.

A bike you bring in yourself is different: Article 55(1) requires a foreign motor vehicle brought in by a foreigner for tourism to be licensed by the competent authority, and Article 55(3)(a) keeps it under Vietnamese road traffic law unless a treaty provides otherwise.

## Checking a rented 50cc before you ride

Renting is where a foreign rider meets these rules in practice. Four checks turn the law into questions for the owner of the bike:

1. **Is it a moped in law?** Ask what design speed and what engine capacity or motor power the manufacturer states for the model. A badge proves neither, and a bike that fails the definition is a motorcycle (Article 34(1)(e)).
2. **Does the registration certificate match the bike?** Compare the plate number with the certificate, and check the engine capacity if the certificate shows one.
3. **Is the compulsory insurance certificate in force, and whose is it?** It is the owner's paper (Article 56(1)(d)), so ask to see the owner's compulsory insurance certificate for the bike you are offered, look at its dates and ask how it will be shown if you are stopped.
4. **What about the helmet?** Article 33(2) covers the rider and any passenger. Ask whether a helmet that meets the national technical regulation comes with the bike or should be your own.

## Compulsory insurance and your own cover

The compulsory certificate is narrow. On the 2023 text, Articles 5 and 7(1) of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP put the owner's civil liability on the insured side: damage the vehicle causes in traffic to third parties and passengers. Loss of the bike itself is outside that list. Decree 220/2026/NĐ-CP changed the decree from 1 July 2026, so ask an insurer what the current wording says before relying on a detail.

That cover is for harm to other people, so it does not stand in for your own travel or medical policy. Read your own policy for a licence condition, an engine-size cap or a motorcycling exclusion, and put to the insurer in writing whether riding a moped in Vietnam without a motorcycle licence is covered, and keep the reply.

## After a crash on a moped

The Law defines a road accident as a collision involving people or vehicles in traffic, outside the will of those taking part, that harms the life, health or property of an individual or the property of an organisation (Article 2(12)). Under Article 80(1) the driver and others involved must halt the vehicle immediately, signal the hazard, leave the scene undisturbed, assist anyone hurt and notify the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee, then stay until the police arrive and give identification details and information about the accident to the competent authority. The duty to stay has exceptions: going for emergency care, taking an injured person for it, or a threat to life or health, and even then the person must report at once to the nearest police or People's Committee. [What to do after a motorbike accident]({{ '/articles/motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes the duties step by step.

Who pays is a separate question. Under Article 584(1) of Civil Code 91/2015/QH13, a person who infringes another's life, health, honour, dignity, reputation, property or other lawful rights and interests and causes damage must compensate, unless the Code or another related law provides otherwise, and Article 585(1) provides for actual damage to be compensated in full and promptly, and lets the parties agree the amount, form and method of compensation unless the law provides otherwise. Neither article mentions a licence, and on a moped none is required. Whatever the compulsory certificate does not reach depends on your own policy and the facts. This is a general guide, not legal advice.

If you hold a valid motorcycle entitlement from home, the whole question changes: a 1968-convention IDP with the right category plus your national licence opens normal rental scooters. ### The permit route

"Opens normal rental scooters" holds only when each document covers the bike. Article 57(6)(b) counts an international permit and a national licence together when a 1968 Convention state issued both and each suits the vehicle, and Article 57(8)(b) requires both, in term, to be carried. A scooter of up to 125 cm3 sits in class A1, so the categories on both documents have to reach that size.

### The exchange route for residents

A foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam has a second route. Under Article 24(1)(a) and (c) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA a national licence in term, held with a visa or residence card valid for 90 days or more (or a permanent card), can be considered for exchange; an IDP cannot. Article 18(7)(a) and (b) say a foreigner who needs to drive here and holds a national licence is to exchange it, unless a treaty provides otherwise. Neither article names a deadline for the applicant, so confirm in writing with the traffic police; [the exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the dossier.

## Common questions

### Can a foreigner ride a 50cc in Vietnam without any licence?

Yes, if the bike meets the moped definition and the rider is 16 or over. Article 56(1) leaves the moped driver out of the licence requirement, Article 56(4) asks for knowledge of the rules, skill and the age and health conditions, and none of these clauses mentions nationality.

### Do I need an International Driving Permit for a 50cc?

Not for a bike that meets the moped definition, because no licence applies. A permit matters above the class, and only together with a national licence from a 1968 Convention state (Article 57(6)(b) and 57(8)(b)).

### Does my travel insurance cover a moped?

That depends on the wording of your policy. Look for a licence condition, an engine-size limit and an exclusion for motorcycling, and ask the insurer in writing.

### Can a 16-year-old visitor ride a 50cc?

Yes. Article 59(1)(a) lets a person aged 16 or over drive a moped, with the Article 56(4) skills and a helmet; A1 starts at 18 (Article 59(1)(b)).
