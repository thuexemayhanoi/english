Maintenance is one of the biggest differences between a daily and a monthly rental. A daily bike is back with the shop after a short rental; a monthly bike spends weeks in your hands, so both sides have ongoing responsibilities. Here is how it works.

The short answer: the shop's published wording lists four maintenance items that may be included, and says tubes and tyres may be yours. Everything between those two lines is a question to put to the shop. What a monthly renter can add is a weekly pass built from the checks Honda's owner's manuals ask of a rider, a mid-term check arranged by asking, and a bike kept clean and dry.

## What the shop covers

Maintenance included in the rental may cover oil changes, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection. If something outside that list comes up, contact Nguyen Tu to agree on the right approach before doing anything expensive. Bringing the bike back for a mid-term check is normally straightforward — the shop is open 09:00–21:00 daily, though how and when a check is arranged, and which of the items above it covers, is for the shop to confirm.

### The four published items from the renter's side

The published list is short, and each item looks different from where a renter stands. Nothing published says how often any of them is done, so the last column is what to ask, not what to expect.

| Published item | What you can notice between visits | A question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Oil change | The level on the dipstick, if the shop wants you to read it | When was the oil last changed, and when would you want it changed again? |
| Chain adjustment and tension | Slack you can see, a dry rasp, a clunk when the throttle opens and closes | Who adjusts it, where and when, and what happens if the chain is worn rather than loose? |
| Tyre-pressure checks | A gauge reading, and the look of the tread and sidewalls | What pressure should this bike have, and may I add air myself? |
| Normal inspection | A brake that travels further, a light that has stopped working, a new noise | What does the inspection cover, and can I book one? |

Not every bike has a chain. The Vision is an automatic with no chain to adjust, so on a bike like that ask what the shop's check covers instead; [the breakdowns guide for long rentals]({{ '/articles/breakdowns-during-long-term-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets the Wave Alpha and Vision manuals side by side. [What a Hanoi motorbike rental includes]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the wider set of items worth asking about.

## What stays your responsibility

Consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation. Day-to-day care is yours too: keeping tyre pressure sensible, watching for chain slack, keeping the bike dry where possible, and not ignoring new noises. If the bike needs work, see [Can You Use Outside Repair Shops on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) for how a shop visit during a rental is usually handled.

Two of those care items can be measured, which keeps them from staying vague: sensible tyre pressure is a gauge reading compared with the figure for your bike, and chain slack is something you compare with the chain at handover. Honda's Wave Alpha manual treats the whole job as the rider's, calling the check before riding the rider's responsibility and mandatory for safety, with a flat tyre as its example of a small fault that can cause trouble.

A tyre that wears out is a different case from a puncture. [The consumables guide for long rentals]({{ '/articles/consumables-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) looks at who may pay for what over several weeks.

## A weekly pass with no tools

Honda's Wave Alpha manual asks for its pre-ride checks before every ride. For a monthly renter that splits into a glance at tyres, lights and brakes before each ride, and the fuller pass below once a week. Nothing here needs a tool except a tyre gauge, and nothing changes the bike: you look, listen, feel and write down, and any adjusting is a question for the shop. The lines come from the Wave Alpha (a semi-automatic) and Vision (an automatic) manuals, so ask the shop which model you have before relying on one.

| Check | What Honda's manuals list | What you do on a rental |
|---|---|---|
| Tyres | Both: condition and pressure, adjusted if necessary. Wave Alpha: pressure read with a gauge at least once a month or whenever a tyre looks soft, always cold | Read the gauge before the day's first ride and note the figure; look over tread and sidewalls. A low or uneven reading goes to the shop |
| Brakes | Both: that the brakes work. Wave Alpha: shoe wear and free play. Vision: front fluid level and pad wear, rear shoe wear and free play | With the bike still, work each brake control and note extra travel, a soft feel or a new noise. Adjusting is the shop's job |
| Lights, indicators, horn | Both: that they work | Run through each one, including the brake light as you work the controls. A dead bulb is a small repair, but call first |
| Chain (semi-automatic only) | Wave Alpha: condition and slack, adjusted and lubricated if necessary | Look and listen, and compare with handover. Do not adjust it |
| Throttle | Both: smooth action from fully closed to fully open in every steering position | Turn the bars to each side and open and close the grip. A sticky action is a report |
| Engine oil | Both: level and leaks, read on a dipstick on the filler cap, bike upright on the centre stand on firm, level ground, wiped clean and inserted without screwing it in | Ask the shop whether you should read it, and ask to be shown once. A low level is a message, not a top-up with an oil you cannot name |

Both lists also ask for the fuel level, and for a check that nothing flammable has caught on the bike or in the exhaust area. Both manuals describe a dipstick, not a sight window, so a bike that shows its oil differently is another question for the shop. [What to look for in a loose chain]({{ '/articles/loose-chain-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains the chain line in more depth, including why a renter looks but does not tension.

### Keep the readings

A single gauge reading means little; the change from one week to the next is what a mechanic can use. Write the date, the odometer reading and each figure in a note on your phone. [Keeping a maintenance log on a long-term rental]({{ '/articles/maintenance-log-monthly-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers what else is worth recording, and [a month with a rental motorbike: the check routine]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-inspection-schedule-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out a routine for the same job.

## The high-mileage question

If you ride a lot, the owner historically recommends periodic checks around 1,500 km or more of use. That is practical owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval — but it is a reasonable rhythm for a monthly renter who commutes daily. Tell the shop your expected mileage when you set the rental up; high-mileage use is covered in [High Monthly Mileage on a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

The weekly pass is also where distance gets counted. Write the odometer reading each time and subtract the reading from pickup or from the last check. The guidance is about distance, not weeks, so a light rider may not reach it within a month while a long commute can pass it sooner. The sums are on the high-mileage page above and are not repeated here.

## Arranging a mid-term check

The published wording says the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection. It does not describe how a check is arranged on a monthly rental. The useful move is to ask once, near the start, and keep the reply with your notes.

### Three things to settle

- **Where.** At the shop, 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street in Long Bien, or somewhere else? Delivery and pickup in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement, so ask whether a check could use it; [delivery and pickup for monthly rentals]({{ '/articles/delivery-pickup-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers what is published.
- **When.** The published hours are 09:00–21:00 daily, which a working week may not match, so ask whether a time can be agreed ahead and how long the bike would be away. Ask what you would ride meanwhile: no replacement bike is promised, and current availability is something to confirm by contacting the shop.
- **What.** Which of the four items would be done, whether anything is charged to you, and how a part that wears out, such as a tyre or a tube, is handled. Consumables may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.

### What to bring

Bring the odometer reading, ideally as a photograph of the dashboard, and your dated notes from the weekly pass. Put the changes in order of how much they worry you: a brake that travels further comes before a rattling mirror. Any handover photographs or notes give the shop a baseline for what has changed since week one.

### After the visit

Ask what was done and write it down with the date and the odometer reading. If anything has started to feel wrong in the meantime, do not wait for the check: [the guide to a bike that feels wrong]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) separates the symptoms that mean stopping at once from those to report soon.

## Keeping the bike clean and dry

Honda's manuals give washing advice to owners, and two of its points matter on a rental because water in the wrong place becomes a repair. The steps below follow the Wave Alpha and Vision manuals. Whether the shop wants the bike washed before it comes back is a question for the shop, not something the manuals can answer.

1. **Let it cool.** Both manuals say to let the engine, exhaust, brakes and other hot parts cool completely first.
2. **Rinse gently.** Remove dirt with a low-pressure hose, wipe with a sponge or soft cloth dipped in a mild detergent solution, rinse with clean water and dry with a soft cloth.
3. **Keep water out of the wrong places.** Both manuals say not to use a high-pressure washer and not to spray straight at the exhaust area, under the seat, into the air cleaner or around the headlight. The Wave Alpha manual adds the electrical components.
4. **Dry the brakes.** Water in the brake system reduces braking effectiveness, and both manuals say to apply the brakes intermittently at low speed after washing to dry them. Do it somewhere with no traffic.
5. **Ask before oiling the chain.** The Wave Alpha manual says to lubricate moving parts and the drive chain after washing and drying. On a rental, ask the shop whether it wants you to do that and with what, because lubricant does not take up slack.

### Under cover

For a bike kept outdoors, both manuals advise a full cover and say to take it off once the rain has stopped so the bike can dry. Whether a cover is allowed or provided is a question for the shop. The Vision manual also lists steps for a bike that will not be used for a long period, including removing the battery; none of that is a renter's job without asking. ## What not to do

Do not attempt engine work, electrical changes or modifications on a rental bike. Do not keep riding a bike that feels wrong — see [What to Do If Your Rental Motorbike Does Not Feel Normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) for why small issues get worse over weeks. And do not let a simple puncture turn into a ruined tyre; [Who Pays for a Punctured Tyre on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains the practical steps.

### Look, ask or leave

The jobs on a monthly bike fall into three groups, and the group decides who acts.

| Group | Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Look and note | A gauge reading, brake travel, lights, chain slack you can see, the odometer, a photograph | It gives the shop facts and changes nothing on the bike |
| Ask first | Adding air or oil, a bulb or a puncture repaired at an outside shop, buying a tyre, washing and oiling the chain | Consumables may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so settle who pays before you spend |
| Leave to the shop | Chain and brake adjustment, engine and electrical work, anything that alters the bike | The published list names oil changes and chain adjustment and tension; the Wave Alpha manual warns that fitting accessories or modifying the bike improperly can lead to accidents causing serious injury or death |

## Electric bikes

An electric bike changes the list. The oil and chain items in the published wording have no obvious counterpart on one, so ask which items the shop's maintenance covers on the bike you are offered, and contact the shop to confirm current availability. ## Questions renters ask

### Is a service visit part of a monthly rental?

The published wording says the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection. How a visit is arranged is not published, so ask where, when and what is checked.

### Can I check the oil myself?

Honda's Wave Alpha and Vision manuals both list the engine oil level among an owner's pre-ride checks and describe a dipstick on the filler cap. On a rental, ask the shop whether it wants you to read the level and what to do if it is low; do not add an oil you cannot name.

### How often should I check tyre pressure?

Honda's Wave Alpha manual asks for a gauge reading at least once a month or whenever a tyre looks soft, always with the tyres cold. A weekly reading is a shorter interval than that minimum. The figure to aim for is the one the shop gives for your bike.

### Can I wash the rental bike?

The manuals describe a low-pressure rinse, a soft cloth and no high-pressure washer. Whether the shop expects the bike back washed is not published, so ask.

## The one-line version

The shop's maintenance may include routine items such as oil, chain and inspection; you cover daily care and possibly tubes and tyres — and when in doubt, call the shop before the problem grows.
