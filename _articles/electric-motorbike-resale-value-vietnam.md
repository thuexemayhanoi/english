Resale on an electric motorbike follows one rule everywhere in the world: the buyer is purchasing a battery with a bike attached. In Vietnam the rule is sharpened by the market's structure — swap ecosystems, rentable packs, a fast-moving model cycle — and by buyers who have learned to check.

## The three value drivers

**1. Battery health.** The first question a serious Vietnamese buyer asks is not mileage but battery state. A machine with a documented, healthy pack outsells a newer machine with a tired one. The Weaver 200's published 150,000 km / 15-year battery-life claim and 3-year battery warranty give that machine a transferable story; VinFast-class machines depend more on the pack's rental/ownership structure — a machine on battery rental transfers as "bike minus pack economics", which the buyer will price in.

**2. Ecosystem continuity.** A model tied to a live swap network (V-Green) or a live manufacturer presence is easier to resell than an orphaned model from a brand that exited. Our market overview covers which ecosystems currently look durable.

**3. Documentation.** Registration, plates, service records, charger, both keys and a warranty booklet — the same papers that the registration article explains as legal requirements double as resale proof. A complete folder is worth real money.

## What kills value fastest

- A pack replaced by a non-original, undocumented unit — buyers price the doubt.
- Deep discharge scars: a machine that sat dead for a season (see the storage guide) reads as abused.
- DIY electrical "upgrades" — a soldered alarm or a rewired light is a red flag to every buyer.
- Missing charger — the "lost it" discount is steeper than a charger costs.

## Before you sell

Run the battery-health routine honestly and price against the result. A pack you can demonstrate — a timed charge, a familiar loop, app data where the model has it — turns your asking price into a defensible one.
