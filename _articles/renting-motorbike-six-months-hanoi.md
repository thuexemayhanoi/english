Six months is the duration where rental stops being a convenience and becomes a lifestyle decision — and in Hanoi, it is a decision rental often wins. At this length, long-term motorbike rental in Hanoi is priced and agreed directly with the shop, so the useful preparation is knowing what to put in writing, how far you will ride and which of your documents must still be valid in month six.

## How long-term terms work

Nguyen Tu's rental periods run daily, weekly and monthly, with longer-term arrangements negotiable with the customer — contact us to discuss longer-term rental for your exact dates. The published monthly rates (see [Daily, Weekly or Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi: How to Choose]({{ '/articles/daily-weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) anchor the negotiation: a six-month agreement is typically discussed as a monthly rate plus agreed terms, set directly with the shop rather than extrapolated from any table.

### What to put in your first message

A short first message to the shop does most of the work. Include:

- The start date, and the latest date you might still want the bike.
- The model you have in mind and a second choice, because current availability of any model has to be confirmed with the shop.
- The distance you expect to ride on an average day, which the mileage table below turns into a check schedule.
- Whether you want pickup or delivery for the start; delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement.
- Your licence situation, with the printed expiry date of each document.

## Terms worth getting in writing

Because longer terms are agreed directly, the questions you bring matter as much as the answers you hear. After the conversation, send a short written summary of what was agreed and ask the shop to confirm it. Three terms are worth settling first; the wider checklist is in [Long-Term Motorbike Rental Agreements in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-long-term-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

| Term | Why it matters over six months | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Price for the period | Longer terms are agreed directly rather than read from a table | The total for all six months, and what happens to the price if you extend |
| Leaving early | The term is long enough for plans to change | What, if anything, applies if you leave in month four, and whether it is written down |
| Staying longer | A request to extend works best before the end date | How and by when to ask, and whether the rate stays the same |

### One agreement or monthly renewals

Ask whether the shop would agree the six months as one term or as monthly renewals, and what each would mean for the price, the deposit and an early exit. A fixed finish date, such as the end of a work contract, suggests asking about one term, and a plan that may change suggests asking about renewals.

## What changes at six months

Everything that was minor at one month becomes routine at six:

- Mileage: six months of Hanoi commuting easily adds thousands of kilometres. Nguyen Tu's practical guidance is periodic checks around every ~1,500 km or more of use — owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval — and the inspection rhythm is described in [High-Mileage Rental Riding: When to Bring the Bike Back for Inspection]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-rental-motorbike-inspection/' | relative_url }})
- Consumables: tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation — agree this in writing for a long term ([Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}))
- Maintenance: the maintenance the shop may include (oil change, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks, normal inspection — see [What Is Included in a Hanoi Motorbike Rental?]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) matters more the longer you ride; use it rather than deferring

### How far you ride sets the check schedule

That ~1,500 km figure only becomes a plan once you know your own daily distance, because the odometer matters more than the calendar. The table is illustrative arithmetic for a rider who covers the same distance on each of about 180 days; it is not a schedule set by the shop.

| Average daily distance | Total over about 180 days | Checks at roughly every 1,500 km |
|---|---|---|
| 10 km | 1,800 km | 1 |
| 20 km | 3,600 km | 2 |
| 40 km | 7,200 km | 4 |

Photograph the odometer at handover and again every few weeks, so you know when the first mark is approaching. Telling the shop your realistic daily distance at the start means you can ask the shop to plan the visits instead of improvising them.

## Renting versus buying at this duration

The crossover is real but narrower than people assume. Buying a used underbone looks cheaper on a spreadsheet, then adds: repair responsibility, tyre and battery replacements, paperwork in Vietnamese, resale loss, and the risk of a bad purchase. Long-term rental keeps the maintenance on the shop's side of the ledger and the exit simple. Consumables such as tyres may still be yours, and an early exit is something to agree, not assume. The full comparison method is in [Renting vs Buying a Motorbike in Hanoi: Cost and Convenience]({{ '/articles/renting-vs-buying-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) — decide with numbers for your own mileage, not with the general folklore.

One input only you have is how far you ride, using the table above, since distance drives the upkeep of any bike you own as well as one you rent. The rent-versus-buy questions that depend on your end date are worked through in [the one-year rental guide]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-year-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## Protecting the deposit over a long term

The deposit (typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND — [Motorbike Rental Deposits in Hanoi: Typical Amounts and Return]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) stays held the whole term. Photograph the bike at handover and at agreed intervals; for a long term, agree the return-condition standard at the start, not at the end. How a fair return works is in [How Deposit Returns Work at Hanoi Motorbike Rentals]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

Put three points in the written summary: the amount held, the condition standard for its return, and how it will be paid back. A simple log with the date, the odometer reading, the work done and who paid for it gives you a record at month six that nobody has to rebuild from memory.

## Documents that must outlast the rental

A rental agreement does not give you the right to ride; your documents do, and six months is long enough for one of them to lapse. Before you sign, list the printed expiry date of each document and compare it with the planned end date plus a month of margin:

- National driving licence: its own expiry date, if it has one.
- International driving permit (IDP): the expiry date printed on it.
- Passport: the date its validity ends.
- Visa or permitted stay: the date it ends.

### What the law says about an IDP

Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the IDP rule. An international driving permit issued by a member state of the 1968 Vienna Convention is valid for no more than three years from issue and must be consistent with the remaining validity of the national licence. A rider using one in Vietnam must carry both documents, matching the class of vehicle and still in date. The same article gives Vietnamese A1, A and B1 motorbike licences no time limit, so this checklist matters most to riders on foreign paper.

In practice, the earlier of the two printed dates is the one that counts for your rental. [How Long Can You Ride on an IDP in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the same rule. A stay of this length is also where [the long-term expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) becomes relevant, because it compares the routes beyond an IDP.

### If a date falls inside the term

If a document will lapse before the end date, do not plan to ride past it. Renew or replace it first, or plan to hand the bike back before that date. Tell the shop as soon as you know, because an early conversation leaves more time to ask what applies than a message on the day.

### Visa length: a planning checklist

Immigration rules are outside this guide, so treat this as a checklist and not as advice. Write down the date your permitted stay ends and put it next to the rental end date. If the rental is meant to run longer than your permission to stay, settle how you will extend or leave before agreeing the term. Questions about how long you may stay belong with the immigration authorities or your sponsor.

## The long-term rider's habits

- Learn the shop's preferred channel and response pattern for issues ([What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}))
- Report anything that feels wrong early rather than riding through it — see [What to Do If Your Rental Motorbike Does Not Feel Normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }})
- Keep the licence situation current: an exchange done in month one protects months two to six ([Vietnam Motorbike Licence for Long-Term Expats]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }})). Under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, a foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam can be considered for an exchange if the national licence is still in date and they hold one of the papers it names, such as a temporary or permanent residence card, with a duration of three months or more. Check those conditions before planning around an exchange.

## Ending the term, or staying on

Set a reminder for the start of the final month and decide then whether to extend or return. Late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, so settle any extension before the end date rather than after it. [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains how timing affects an extension, and if you think you may want the bike for twelve months, [Renting a Motorbike for a Year in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-year-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers that arrangement.

When the day comes, keep the handover photos and your written summary where you can find them.

## Who long-term rental suits

Residents who value simplicity over equity, expats on fixed-length contracts, students on year programmes, and anyone who wants to try bike life before buying. If that is you, the next step is one message: contact the shop, state your dates and expected mileage, and negotiate from there.

## Questions about six-month rentals

### Can I multiply the monthly rate by six?

No. A six-month price is agreed with the shop for your dates and is not read from a table, so ask for the total for the full period and for how it changes if you extend.

### Is the deposit held for the whole term?

Expect it to be held while you keep the bike, and confirm that in writing. Deposits typically run 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions.

### What if I leave after four months?

There is no automatic refund for unused time, so ask what, if anything, applies to an early return. That is why the exit question belongs in the written summary before you start, and why it is worth asking whether monthly renewals would keep an early exit simple.

### Does the rental include insurance?

No. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so renters arrange their own cover. For a term this long, settle it before the first long ride rather than after something happens.

### Does an IDP last six months?

Check two printed dates: the IDP's own expiry, which can be no more than three years from issue, and the expiry of the national licence behind it. Plan around the earlier of the two.
