Six months is the duration where rental stops being a convenience and becomes a lifestyle decision — and in Hanoi, it is a decision rental often wins. At this length, long-term motorbike rental in Hanoi is priced and agreed directly with the shop, so the useful preparation is knowing what to put in writing, how far you will ride and which of your documents must still be valid in month six.

## How long-term terms work

Nguyen Tu's rental periods run daily, weekly and monthly, with longer-term arrangements negotiable with the customer — contact us to discuss longer-term rental for your exact dates. The published monthly rates (see [Daily, Weekly or Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi: How to Choose]({{ '/articles/daily-weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) anchor the negotiation: a six-month agreement is typically discussed as a monthly rate plus agreed terms, set directly with the shop rather than extrapolated from any table.

The published monthly rates, and the models they cover, are listed in [Motorbike Rental Monthly Prices in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). No six-month figure is published, so the price for your dates comes out of the conversation with the shop.

### What to put in your first message

A short first message does most of the work, and the [contact page]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) shows how to reach the shop. Include:

- The start date, and the latest date you might still want the bike.
- The model you have in mind and a second choice, because current availability of any model has to be confirmed with the shop.
- The distance you expect to ride on an average day, which the mileage table below turns into a check schedule.
- Whether you want pickup or delivery for the start; delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement.
- Your licence situation, with the printed expiry date of each document.

## Terms worth getting in writing

Because longer terms are agreed directly, the questions you bring matter as much as the answers you hear. After the conversation, send a short written summary of what was agreed and ask the shop to confirm it. The two tables list the terms that carry the most weight over six months.

### Price, payment and upkeep

| Term | Why it matters over six months | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Price for the period | Longer terms are agreed directly rather than read from a table | The total for all six months, and what happens to the price if you extend |
| Payment | Cash, bank transfer and QR payment are the accepted methods | Whether you pay month by month or for the term, and how each payment is confirmed |
| Maintenance | Maintenance by the shop may include an oil change, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection | Where and when the visits happen, and how to book one |
| Consumables | Inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation | Who pays for a worn tyre and who pays after a puncture |

### Scope and exit

| Term | Why it matters over six months | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Where you ride | Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use | What applies if you plan trips outside the city |
| Leaving early | Early return carries no automatic refund entitlement for unused time | What, if anything, applies if you leave in month four |
| Staying longer | A request to extend works best before the end date | How and by when to ask, and whether the rate stays the same |

### One agreement or monthly renewals

Ask whether the shop would agree the six months as one term or as monthly renewals, and what each would mean for the price, the deposit and an early exit. One term gives certainty on dates and cost but makes leaving early a negotiation. Monthly renewals keep the exit simple but leave later months open to whatever terms apply at each renewal. A fixed finish date, such as the end of a work contract, points to one term; a plan that may change points to renewals.

## What changes at six months

Everything that was minor at one month becomes routine at six:

- Mileage: six months of Hanoi commuting easily adds thousands of kilometres. Nguyen Tu's practical guidance is periodic checks around every ~1,500 km or more of use — owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval — and the inspection rhythm is described in [High-Mileage Rental Riding: When to Bring the Bike Back for Inspection]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-rental-motorbike-inspection/' | relative_url }})
- Consumables: tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation — agree this in writing for a long term ([Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}))
- Maintenance: the shop's included maintenance (oil, chain, tyre pressure, inspection — see [What Is Included in a Hanoi Motorbike Rental?]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) matters more the longer you ride; use it rather than deferring

### How far you ride sets the check schedule

The 1,500 km figure only becomes a plan once you know your own daily distance, because the odometer matters more than the calendar. The table is illustrative arithmetic for a rider who covers the same distance on each of about 180 days; it is not a schedule set by the shop.

| Average daily distance | Total over about 180 days | Whole 1,500 km marks passed |
|---|---|---|
| 10 km | 1,800 km | 1 |
| 20 km | 3,600 km | 2 |
| 40 km | 7,200 km | 4 |

Photograph the odometer at handover and again every few weeks, so you know when the first mark is approaching. Telling the shop your realistic daily distance at the start lets both sides plan the visits instead of improvising them.

## Renting versus buying at this duration

The crossover is real but narrower than people assume. Buying a used underbone looks cheaper on a spreadsheet, then adds: repair responsibility, tyre and battery replacements, paperwork in Vietnamese, resale loss, and the risk of a bad purchase. Long-term rental keeps the maintenance on the shop's side of the ledger and the exit simple. The full comparison method is in [Renting vs Buying a Motorbike in Hanoi: Cost and Convenience]({{ '/articles/renting-vs-buying-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) — decide with numbers for your own mileage, not with the general folklore.

Three inputs that only you have settle the comparison:

- How firm your end date is. A date fixed by a contract or a visa makes the sums easy to run; a stay that might stretch changes them.
- How far you ride, using the table above, since distance drives the upkeep of any bike you own as well as one you rent.
- How much time you would have to sell the bike before you leave, because a sale in the final week is a sale in a hurry.

## Protecting the deposit over a long term

The deposit (typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND — [Motorbike Rental Deposits in Hanoi: Typical Amounts and Return]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) stays held the whole term. Photograph the bike at handover and at agreed intervals; for a long term, agree the return-condition standard at the start, not at the end. How a fair return works is in [How Deposit Returns Work at Hanoi Motorbike Rentals]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

Put three points in the written summary: the amount held, the condition standard for its return, and how it will be paid back. A simple log with the date, the odometer reading, the work done and who paid for it gives you a record at month six that nobody has to rebuild from memory.

## Documents that must outlast the rental

A rental agreement does not give you the right to ride; your documents do, and six months is long enough for one of them to lapse. Before you sign, list the printed expiry date of each document and compare it with the planned end date plus a month of margin:

- National driving licence: its own expiry date, if it has one.
- International driving permit (IDP): the expiry date printed on it.
- Passport: the date its validity ends.
- Visa or permitted stay: the date it ends.

### What the law says about an IDP

Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the rule for foreign riders. An international driving permit issued by a member state of the 1968 Vienna Convention is valid for no more than three years from issue and must be consistent with the remaining validity of the national licence. A rider using one in Vietnam must carry both documents, matching the class of vehicle and still in date. The same article gives Vietnamese A1, A and B1 motorbike licences no time limit, so this checklist matters most to riders on foreign paper.

In practice, the earlier of the two printed dates is the one that counts for your rental. [How Long Can You Ride on an IDP in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the same rule. A stay of this length is also where the long-term expat licence guide, [Vietnam Motorbike Licence for Long-Term Expats]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), becomes relevant, because it compares the routes beyond an IDP.

### If a date falls inside the term

If a document will lapse before the end date, do not plan to ride past it. Renew or replace it first, or plan to hand the bike back before that date. Tell the shop as soon as you know, because an early conversation leaves more options than a message on the day. [Fine for an Expired Motorbike Licence in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/expired-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how a lapsed document is treated, so read it rather than assume.

### Visa length: a planning checklist

Immigration rules are outside this guide, so treat this as a checklist and not as advice. Write down the date your permitted stay ends and put it next to the rental end date. If the rental is meant to run longer than your permission to stay, settle how you will extend or leave before agreeing the term. Questions about how long you may stay belong with the immigration authorities or your sponsor.

## Choosing a bike you will live with for six months

Over six months a bike's small annoyances stop being small: a tiring seat or a lack of storage is felt every day. [Choosing the Right Motorbike for a Month in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/choosing-motorbike-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) works through route, body, storage, transmission and mileage in detail. The table only shows how a routine points to a priority.

| If your routine is | Prioritise | Why it matters at six months |
|---|---|---|
| A daily commute across the city | A comfortable seat and a bike that holds its speed | The same long ride repeats every working day |
| Short hops and narrow lanes | A light bike that is easy to manoeuvre and park | Parking and tight turns are daily tasks |
| Shopping, a laptop or rain gear | Under-seat storage or room for a box | You carry the same things every day |
| A regular passenger | A seat and footrests that suit both people | Check it with the passenger present, not alone |

Whatever you choose, ask the shop to confirm current availability of the model. Ask too whether the bike can be changed if it turns out not to suit you, because a poor fit in week two stays a poor fit for the rest of the term.

## Questions about six-month rentals

### Can I multiply the monthly rate by six?

No. A six-month price is agreed with the shop for your dates and is not read from a table, so ask for the total for the full period and for how it changes if you extend.

### Is the deposit held for the whole term?

Expect it to be held while you keep the bike, and confirm that in writing. Deposits typically run 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions.

### What if I leave after four months?

There is no automatic refund for unused time, and what happens instead is settled with the shop. That is why the exit question belongs in the written summary before you start, and why monthly renewals keep an early exit simple.

### Does the rental include insurance?

No. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so renters arrange their own cover. For a term this long, settle it before the first long ride rather than after something happens.

### Does an IDP last six months?

Check two printed dates: the IDP's own expiry, which can be no more than three years from issue, and the expiry of the national licence behind it. Plan around the earlier of the two.
