A monthly rental is won or lost in its first week. Get the start right and the rest of the month is routine; rush it and you spend weeks fixing things the handover should have caught. Starting a monthly motorbike rental in Hanoi comes down to five jobs in week one: a documented handover, a careful learning ride, a parking check, a first weekly inspection and one message to the shop. Here is the first-week playbook.

## Day 0: the handover

Do the full inspection with the checklist in [Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) — brakes, lights, horn, mirrors, tyres, existing scratches photographed, fuel cap located, cold start demonstrated (ask whether you can watch the engine started from cold, because nothing published says the shop will demonstrate it). Sign nothing, pay nothing, until the bike's condition is documented.

Then agree the monthly terms out loud and in a message: rate, deposit (typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND), late-return rule, consumables responsibility, who to call if something breaks. The long-term-specific terms are listed in [Long-Term Motorbike Rental Agreements in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-long-term-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

Deposits vary with the vehicle and conditions, so ask what applies to your bike and have it written in the same message. If the bike is brought to you, the same record applies; delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement, and [delivery and pickup for monthly rentals]({{ '/articles/delivery-pickup-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists what to settle first.

### What to record before you leave

The checklist shows how to examine the bike; this part is about what to keep. A record helps only if it is dated and sits in the same chat as the shop's replies, because in week three you compare the bike against it.

| Record | What to capture | Used again for |
|---|---|---|
| Photos and a short video | Four sides, each existing mark, both tyres | The day-7 check and the return |
| Dashboard photo | Odometer and fuel gauge in one frame | The fuel baseline and the level to return at |
| Keys and extras | How many keys, and anything else handed over | The return |
| Plate | Characters readable in a photo | Which bike was handed over |
| Terms | Rate, deposit and how it is paid, date and hour the term ends | An extension or the return |

Send the photos and the video to the shop's chat on WhatsApp or Zalo (+84 942 467 674) before you leave, so the timestamps match the day. The published late-return charge is 20,000 VND per hour, so the end hour matters as much as the end date, and [extending or renewing the term]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is the page to read before you ask for more time.

### The papers question

Ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop. Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 puts the registration certificate, a licence that fits the vehicle (the moped class is handled separately in Article 56(4)) and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate among the papers a driver carries, and Article 56(3) allows papers held in an electronic identification account to be shown through that account. Carry your own licence; [the guide to documents a rider carries]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the set. Separately, Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so any cover for you is yours to arrange before the first ride.

## Day 1: the learning ride

Ride the bike on routes you know, in daylight, without passengers or cargo. Learn its brakes (every bike grabs differently), its seat height at stops, its turning circle, its fuel gauge behaviour. A month is a long time on one machine — the first hour of acquaintance is the cheapest time to learn it.

"Every bike grabs differently" is a rider's rule of thumb, not a measurement, but bikes do ask for different checks: Honda's Wave Alpha manual lists shoe wear and free play for the front and the rear brake, and the maintenance guide linked near the end of this article sets the Wave Alpha and Vision checks side by side. So squeeze each control gently at walking pace before you ask more of it.

### Before the key turns: a first-ride checklist

Honda's Wave Alpha owner's manual (Vietnam model AFB110MD) makes the check before riding the rider's job. Its list suits a short walk-round, and which items apply depends on the model you are given: the chain line, for example, is for a semi-automatic such as the Wave Alpha.

1. **Fuel.** Read the gauge and note it.
2. **Tyres.** Look at both and read the pressure if you have a gauge. Note the state of the tubes and tyres; who pays for a worn tyre is a question in the table at the end.
3. **Chain, if fitted.** Look at its condition and sag, and report rather than adjust; the table at the end asks who does what.
4. **Brakes.** With the bike still, work each control and note the travel and the feel.

Then the controls and the rest:

5. **Throttle.** Turn the bars each way and open and close the grip; the manual asks for smooth action in every steering position.
6. **Lights, indicators, horn.** Run each one before you move off.
7. **Oil level and leaks.** Ask whether the shop wants you to read the level, and look under the bike for drips.
8. **Exhaust area.** Check that nothing flammable has caught inside the bike or near the exhaust.

### Starting the engine the way the manual describes it

Ask whether the bike was started minutes before you arrived or has stood overnight, because that changes what a cold start shows. Honda's Wave Alpha manual describes an electric starter and a kick lever, and a choke routine for 10 to 35°C: fuel valve ON, ignition on, neutral with the indicator lit, choke fully up, throttle slightly open, then the starter. Straight after the engine fires the choke goes halfway down, and about half a minute later fully down.

The manual says not to use the choke at 35°C or higher, and to restart an already warm engine with those same no-choke steps. If the engine has not started within 5 seconds, it says to switch off and wait 10 seconds so the battery voltage can recover. Those steps are for the Wave Alpha; ask how the bike you are given starts when cold, and read [Cold Starts: Choke, Warm-Up and First Rides on Underbones]({{ '/articles/cold-start-underbone-choke-warmup/' | relative_url }}) for the choke and warm-up habits in more detail.

### Fuel on day one

The Wave Alpha manual asks for unleaded petrol of RON 91 or higher with no more than 10% ethanol, gives the tank as 3.7 L and says not to fill above the lower edge of the filler neck; its fuel valve is ON for riding and OFF for parking, storage or transport. Those are Wave Alpha figures, so ask which grade your bike takes, where the valve should sit when parked and what counts as a full tank at the return. [Rental Motorbike Fuel: What Petrol to Use in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-fuel-what-petrol/' | relative_url }}) explains the pump labels.

### The rules that apply from the first ride

Law 36/2024/QH15 (consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH) sets three rules from the first kilometre; the papers rule is under Day 0. This is information, not legal advice.

| Topic | What the law says | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Lights | Front light on from 18:00 to 06:00 and in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather that limits visibility | 20(1) |
| Helmets | Rider and passenger wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, strap fastened correctly | 33(2) |
| Passengers | One passenger; two only in the listed cases, which include a child under 12 | 33(1) |

A first ride that runs past 18:00 needs the headlight on, so ask whether it comes on with the ignition or needs a switch. Ask too whether a helmet is supplied, and a second one for a passenger, and do not assume either is included; [the helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the standard.

## Days 2-3: the parking reconnaissance

Walk or ride your home-and-work routes and locate the actual parking you will use daily: the attended lot near home, the lot or kerb arrangement at work or campus, the weekend spots you will visit. Daily parking is the hidden infrastructure of a monthly rental — the routine is in [Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), the general rules in [Where to Park a Motorbike in Hanoi: Rules and Etiquette]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### A dry run with the real bike

Do the reconnaissance with the bike, not only on foot: a space can have a ramp too steep to wheel up or a row too tight to turn in, and that shows only with the bike there. Bring it to each place at the hour you will really use it.

- **Where it sleeps:** wheel it in at your usual evening hour, ask whether a rental bike may stay and who watches the space, and note the cost and the hours.
- **Where it stands by day:** arrive at your real time and find the exit at your real finishing time.
- **One weekend spot:** note the parking rule there.

Then tell the shop in the chat where the bike will stay overnight. Run the lock-up once as well: steering lock on, key out, a photograph with the plate in view, and a report to the shop that day if the lock sticks.

## Days 4-7: the rhythm and the baseline

- Set your check routine going: a weekly once-over of tyre pressure, chain look, lights and horn — the schedule is in [A Month with a Rental Motorbike: Inspection and Check Routine]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-inspection-schedule-hanoi/' | relative_url }})
- Note the fuel consumption you actually get, so a sudden change means something
- Save the shop's number, and use it early for anything that feels off — [What to Do If Your Rental Motorbike Does Not Feel Normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) lists what justifies a visit

### The first weekly check

Pick the day of the week now: day 7 suits a bike collected on day 0. Run the first-ride checklist again in the same order, look under the bike for drips and compare it with your handover photographs. A new mark goes into the chat that day, with a photograph, and the date and odometer go in your notes.

### Working out your fuel baseline

A baseline needs two fills, because the first tells you nothing about how full the tank was at handover. Fill to the same point each time (the Wave Alpha manual's limit is the lower edge of the filler neck), write down the odometer at each fill and, at the second, divide the litres the pump shows by the kilometres since the first fill and multiply by 100.

An invented example with round numbers, not a measurement of any bike: fills at 850 km and 910 km with 1.2 L added at the second give 1.2 ÷ 60 × 100 = 2.0 L per 100 km. Use the pump and the odometer, not the needle, and keep the range over two or three fills, because speed, load, traffic and weather all move the figure. Its job is to make a sudden change visible: when it jumps, check the tyre pressure and tell the shop, without guessing the cause.

## The habit that carries the month

One message to the shop in the first week — "settling in well, all good so far" — does two things: it confirms your contact channel works, and it opens the relationship that can make it simpler to ask about maintenance visits and extensions later. The support pattern across the whole term is covered in [Maintenance During a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

No reply time is published, so the message tests the channel rather than any promise of speed. The shop's published hours are 09:00-21:00 daily, and WhatsApp, Zalo and phone are all on +84 942 467 674.

### A first message worth sending

Give the shop the facts it needs and add one question, the one that matters most to you:

> Hello, this is [your name]. I collected the bike with plate [plate number] on [date], odometer [number]. Settling in well so far. Could you tell me [your question]?

### Questions to settle in week one

These are questions, not rules: the published wording covers only part of each, and the rest is for the shop to answer.

| Topic | What to ask |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | Which of the items the shop may include (oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks, normal inspection) apply to this bike, and where and when would they happen? |
| Tubes and tyres | These consumables may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation: who pays if a tyre goes in week two? |
| The 1,500 km check | The shop owner's guidance, practical rather than a manufacturer interval, is a periodic check at around 1,500 km or more of use. When would the shop want to see the bike? |
| When the bike stops | Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use and no rescue service is published, so what should you do first, and whom do you contact outside 09:00-21:00? |

## Questions renters ask in the first week

### What if the bike will not start on day 2?

Check that the fuel valve, if the bike has one, is ON and, on a bike with gears, that it is in neutral. The Wave Alpha manual gives its own steps for a bike that still will not start. If it still does not start after those steps, stop trying, photograph the dashboard and message the shop.

### Is one week enough to know whether the bike suits me?

By day 7 you have ridden the bike, parked it where it will live and seen its fuel use, which is the evidence to bring to the shop if something does not suit. Whether a bike can be swapped is not covered by the published terms, so ask early; [Choosing the Right Motorbike for a Month in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/choosing-motorbike-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows what to test.

## The one-line version

Inspect properly, learn the bike calmly, find your parking, start the check routine, and open the communication channel — that is week one, and it makes the other three quiet.
