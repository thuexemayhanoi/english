A breakdown on a rental splits into two questions: whose problem is it mechanically, and what do you do in the next ten minutes. Answer the second with a playbook, and the first sorts itself out. The playbook is short: get away from traffic, run a few free checks, then call the shop and describe what you see. Who pays is a separate question, covered under whose problem it is.

## The first ten minutes

- Get off the road properly - push the bike to the kerb, a lay-by, anywhere that a following truck cannot make things worse.
- Work out what category it is: no power at all (electrical - battery, main fuse, kill switch), turning over but not starting (fuel or spark), running but wrong (noise, smoke, misfire).
- Try the three zero-skill fixes: kill switch to RUN if the bike has one, fuel valve to ON if it has one, key out and back in.
- If none of it works, call the shop.
- Nguyen Tu: +84 942 467 674, WhatsApp or Zalo, 09:00-21:00.
- Describe the symptom plainly - "pushes freely, lights work, starter clicks" tells a mechanic more than "it's broken."

### While you wait

Stand clear of the traffic line, beside a kerb or behind a barrier rather than next to the bike in the lane. Put the bike on its stand, switch the ignition off and use the hazard lights if it has them. If the bike cannot be moved without stepping into moving traffic, leave it and call from a safe place. Keep your helmet, phone and key with you: a charged phone is the one tool the playbook needs, and [what to carry while riding a motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/what-to-carry-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the rest.

## What you see, and what to check first

Sort the stop into one of the three categories above, then run the checks for it. None of them needs a tool.

| What you see | Check first | Then tell the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing at all: no lights, no horn, no click | Key fully on, kill switch at RUN if fitted, side stand up, the smart-key fob with you if the bike uses one | Which of these you checked and whether any light came on |
| Lights work, but the starter does nothing or only clicks | Side stand up, neutral on a geared bike, brake lever squeezed on a scooter that needs it | The sound it makes. A blown fuse, a loose battery connection and a weak battery are the next checks in Honda's manuals, and they are for the shop to do |
| Starter spins, engine will not catch | Fuel in the tank, fuel valve at ON if fitted, the start routine for that bike | How many attempts, and what the fuel level looked like |
| Runs, but wrong: noise, smoke, misfire, a warning light | Stop riding and switch off. The Vision manual says a flashing fuel-injection warning light means: park somewhere safe away from anything flammable and wait ten minutes for the engine to cool | What changed, and when. The guide to a [bike that does not feel normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) covers the symptoms that come before a failure |

## Starting routines differ by bike

Some stops that look like breakdowns are a start routine the rider did not know. Honda's owner's manuals for the Wave Alpha and the Vision show how far routines differ between a semi-automatic underbone and an automatic scooter. Which version you are given is a question for the shop, and your own bike's manual is the authority, so ask the shop to show you the routine: where the fuel valve is if it has one, which stand and brake conditions apply, and whether it has a choke, a kill switch or an idling-stop switch.

### A semi-automatic: the Wave Alpha

The manual for the [Honda Wave Alpha 110]({{ '/articles/honda-wave-alpha-110-review/' | relative_url }}) describes a two-way fuel valve that controls the flow of fuel from the tank to the carburettor: ON is the normal riding position and OFF is for parking, storage or transport. It shows no reserve position; [the guide to fuel taps and reserve]({{ '/articles/fuel-tap-reserve-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers bikes whose tap does have one.

The start routine is: valve ON, ignition ON, gearbox in neutral with the N indicator lit, then the start button with the throttle slightly open. For air between 10 and 35 degrees Celsius the manual adds a choke lever, pulled fully up to start, moved halfway down once the engine runs and fully off after about half a minute. At 35 degrees or above it says not to use the choke, and it tells the rider to restart a hot engine with that no-choke routine. Any step missed can look like a fault.

### An automatic: the Vision

The manual for the [Honda Vision]({{ '/articles/honda-vision-110-review/' | relative_url }}) describes a side-stand cut-off: the engine will not start with the side stand down, and lowering the stand with the engine running stops it. It also says the starter works only when the brake lever is squeezed and the side stand is up, and that the start switch is pressed with the throttle fully closed, unlike the Wave Alpha's slightly open throttle.

On a version with the Idling Stop system, the engine stops by itself when the bike halts, for example at a red light, and an indicator blinks; turning the throttle restarts it. The manual warns against leaving the bike while that indicator blinks, because the engine can start suddenly if the throttle is turned, and says to switch the ignition off if you step away. If the engine will not restart, its checks run through the side stand, the Idling Stop switch and then the battery. Some versions also use a smart key whose fob has to stay with the rider.

## What to say on the call

A call lets the shop ask questions; a WhatsApp or Zalo message leaves a written record with a time on it. Use both if the signal allows, and keep the chat as the record of what you were told. Give the shop:

- the bike's model and plate number, and where you are: a map pin from the messaging app, or the street name and a landmark;
- the category from the first list: no power, turning over but not starting, or running but wrong;
- what you already tried, and how many attempts, so nobody asks you to repeat them;
- whether the bike is safe where it stands, and whether anything came before the stop: a fall, a knock, a fuel stop, standing water;
- a photo of the dashboard and of any warning light.

Imagine a renter on a scooter who writes: "Lights and horn work, starter spins, engine does not catch, tank about a quarter full, three short tries, side stand up, bike on the kerb outside a cafe, dashboard photo attached." That can be answered in one reply; "it won't go" starts a round of questions.

The further you are from the city, the more the plan rests on the steps above; [the guide to taking a rental outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is the place to settle terms before you leave.

## Whose problem it is

Mechanical failure through normal use is a matter to raise with the shop first: the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, and who pays for a failure, or whether a replacement bike is offered, are questions to put to the shop. No breakdown-cost terms are published, so the call is where they get set; [what a Hanoi rental includes]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the published terms.

Some causes are plainly the rider's doing: running out of fuel, flattening the battery by leaving the ignition on, a crash. Whether the shop treats them as the renter's cost is also a question for the call. What is published is narrower: consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, and [the puncture article]({{ '/articles/punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) splits that.

## The repair-authority rule

Do not take a rental to a random workshop and start a repair on the shop's bike without asking. [The outside-repair article]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers when that is reasonable; the default is: call the shop first, follow its instruction. A rental under repair by a third party without agreement turns every later dispute - scope, price, causation - into a three-way argument the renter usually loses.

## After the call: the possible answers

No breakdown procedure is published beyond the contact details, so these are answers the shop might give, with a follow-up for each.

- **Start it again, with instructions.** Ask whether to ride on or switch off and wait.
- **Ride it in.** Only on the shop's say-so, and gently.
- **Leave it and wait.** Ask where the bike should wait and who will move it; [the lost-key guide]({{ '/articles/lost-key-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) has a plain version of keeping a bike safe overnight.
- **Repair nearby.** Only with the shop's agreement; the repair-authority rule above applies.
- **Swap the bike.** Covered next.

## When it becomes a replacement

If the failure is not fixable roadside, the conversation moves to a swap. This is where renting beats owning outright: a broken rental is an inconvenience, a broken purchased bike is your asset in someone else's workshop. Whether a replacement bike is offered, and whether one is available, is a question for the shop: contact us to confirm current availability. Ask what the replacement would be and whether the rental terms stay the same.

## What not to do while you wait

- **Do not crank and crank.** Both Honda manuals say that if the engine does not start within five seconds, switch the ignition off and wait ten seconds so the battery voltage can recover.
- **Do not jump-start from a car without asking.** The Wave Alpha manual recommends against jump-starting a motorbike from a car battery because it can damage the bike's electrical system.
- **Do not open up the electrics.** The manuals tell an owner to check the fuse and the battery connections when the starter does nothing; on a rental, ask the shop first, because [the outside-repair guide's no-list]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) puts electrical work beyond a bulb on it.

## If another vehicle is involved

A breakdown is not a road accident. If another vehicle hits your stopped bike, or anyone is hurt, the situation changes: [what to do after a motorbike accident]({{ '/articles/motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) summarises the duties of a person involved in a road accident in Vietnam (Article 80 of Law 36/2024/QH15). Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so damage or injury also touches whatever cover you arranged yourself.

## Report early, while the fault is small

Bring the bike back when something feels wrong - [the guide to a rental that does not feel normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) is about exactly that. Small symptoms reported on day two stay small; the same symptoms on day ten are a tow.

## Questions renters ask

### Will the shop come and collect the bike?

No collection or rescue arrangement is published, and Nguyen Tu does not promise 24/7 rescue or a guaranteed rescue time. Ask on the call whether collection is possible and when, and keep the answer in the chat.

### What if the bike stops after the shop has closed?

Switch the ignition off, put the bike somewhere safe if it can be moved, and send a message so the report carries the time it happened. Outside opening hours no after-hours service is published, so the call happens when the shop opens.

### Can I restart it and carry on?

If the cause was plain, such as a side stand left down or an empty tank, and the bike runs normally afterwards, tell the shop anyway: it may want to look, and the message is your record. If it stopped with a noise, a smell, a warning light or a change in the brakes or steering, do not ride on.

### Is it my fault if the starter does nothing?

Not necessarily, and blame is not the first question. Honda's manuals list a blown fuse, a loose battery connection, corroded terminals and a weak battery among the checks for a starter that does nothing. Describe what happens and leave who pays to the terms the shop gives you.
