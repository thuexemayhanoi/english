A breakdown on a rental splits into two questions: whose problem is it mechanically, and what do you do in the next ten minutes. Answer the second with a playbook, and the first sorts itself out.

## The first ten minutes

- Get off the road properly - push the bike to the kerb, a lay-by, anywhere that a following truck cannot make things worse.
- Work out what category it is: no power at all (electrical - battery, main fuse, kill switch), turning over but not starting (fuel or spark), running but wrong (noise, smoke, misfire).
- Try the three zero-skill fixes: kill switch to RUN, fuel tap to ON or RES (underbones), key out and back in.
- If none of it works, call the shop. Nguyen Tu: +84 942 467 674, WhatsApp or Zalo, 09:00-21:00. Describe the symptom plainly - "pushes freely, lights work, starter clicks" tells a mechanic more than "it's broken."

## Whose problem it is

Mechanical failure through normal use is the owner's side of the rental: the bike is the shop's machine, its maintenance (oil, chain, tyre pressure, inspection) is the shop's job, and a gearbox that dies on day two is a conversation about a replacement bike, not a repair bill. What stays on the renter's side: running out of fuel, flattening the battery by leaving the ignition on, crashes, and consumables that the agreement assigns to the renter - inner tubes and tyres can be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation (the puncture article splits that).

## The repair-authority rule

Do not take a rental to a random workshop and start a repair on the shop's bike without asking. The outside-repair article covers when that is reasonable; the default is: call the shop first, follow its instruction. A rental under repair by a third party without agreement turns every later dispute - scope, price, causation - into a three-way argument the renter usually loses.

## When it becomes a replacement

If the failure is not fixable roadside, the conversation moves to a swap. This is where renting beats owning outright: a broken rental is an inconvenience, a broken purchased bike is your asset in someone else's workshop. Availability of a replacement depends on stock - contact the shop and it will tell you what it can do the same day.

## The habit that prevents most breakdowns

Bring the bike back when something feels wrong - the next article is about exactly that. Small symptoms reported on day two stay small; the same symptoms on day ten are a tow.
