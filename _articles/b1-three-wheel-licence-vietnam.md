Three-wheeled motorbikes have their own licence class. Under Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), the B1 class is issued for three-wheeled motorbikes — replacing the old A3 class that the 2024 law retired into the transitional rules.

## What B1 covers

The class covers motorbikes with three wheels, whether the machine is a cargo trike, a passenger three-wheeler, or a motorcycle with a sidecar in the motorbike category. The minimum age is 18, the same as A1. What it does not do is extend two-wheeled coverage: B1 is a separate class, not an upgrade of A1, and a B1 holder riding a two-wheeled 110 cc scooter is riding outside their class — treated by the fine schedule as not meeting licence conditions for that machine.

## Relationship to the old classes

Before 2025, three-wheelers sat in class A3. Article 89 keeps pre-2025 A3 licences valid, and a reissue maps the entitlement onto the new B1. The vehicle categories did not change; only the label did.

## Who actually uses it

In Hanoi, B1 territory is the delivery and small-goods economy: the cargo trikes around Long Bien's wholesale streets, and the three-wheeled machines used by market traders. For visitors, the class is mostly relevant to anyone considering a sidecar or trike rental — ask the operator which licence class their machine requires, because the answer determines whether an A1 covers it.

## Points and validity

B1 follows the same system as the other motorbike classes: no expiry date, 12 points, and the same suspension and deduction mechanics. An unlicensed rider on a three-wheeler faces the same 2–4 million VND band (up to the 125 cc equivalent) as on a two-wheeler.
