An exchange term or a university year in Hanoi is exactly the length where daily rental becomes wasteful and buying is too much commitment. A semester rental fills the gap. For a student, a semester motorbike rental is best planned as monthly periods ending on a date from your own term calendar, with deposit, renewal and return settled before you collect the bike.

This page is about lining a rental up with a semester: which dates to collect from your own calendar, what to settle at the start and at the end, how Tet can land inside a spring term and what the licence has to cover for a student who stays. Choosing a bike is in [Best Motorbike for Students in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-students-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and renting as a newcomer, with the budget and parking questions, is in [Motorbike Rental for International Students in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-international-students-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## Age and licence come first

The minimum riding age and licence rules apply to students like anyone else — riders must meet the legal minimum age, and riding a bike your licence does not cover is a finable offence. Check [Minimum Age to Ride a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and the licence rules in the law and licences hub before arranging anything.

A semester is long enough for the licence to matter at the end as well as the start: it has to cover every day you ride, up to the ride back.

### The moped class and the 18th birthday

At 16 the law opens the moped class and nothing above it: Article 59(1)(a) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lets a person of 16 or over ride a moped. Anything bigger needs a licence valid in Vietnam for the bike's class, and the issuing age for class A1 is 18 under Article 59(1)(b). A student who turns 18 mid-semester becomes old enough to be issued a class A1 licence, but the bigger bike becomes lawful only once the licence exists. Plan around the date the licence is in your hand, not the date on your passport.

### Home licence, Vietnamese licence and the expiry dates

Above the moped class a student needs a licence valid in Vietnam: a Vietnamese licence, or a foreign national licence used under a route the law recognises, such as with a 1968 Convention international driving permit, which the international students guide linked above sets out. What a semester adds is date arithmetic: put the expiry date of every document you ride on beside the date the bike goes back, and treat the earliest as the real end of your riding.

### If you exchange a foreign licence while you are here

Some students stay long enough to exchange a foreign licence for a Vietnamese one. Under Article 24(1)(a) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, in force from 1 July 2026, a foreigner who resides, works or studies in Vietnam and holds a valid national licence may be considered for exchange into the corresponding Vietnamese class if they also hold one of the listed papers, among them a visa or temporary residence card valid for 90 days or more, or a permanent residence card. Article 24(5)(a) then ties the term of the Vietnamese licence to the term on one of those papers and to the term of the foreign licence, and it may not exceed the term set for a Vietnamese licence.

On that wording, a visa or temporary residence card valid for fewer than 90 days is not one of the listed papers, and the licence issued is matched to those dates, so read the printed term instead of assuming it runs past your stay. Whether an exchange is worth it for one term is a question for the licensing office: this guide gives no steps, fees or timings, and [Can Foreign Students Get a Vietnamese Motorbike Licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-student-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the student route.

## Matching the rental to the semester

Rent month by month so the term ends when your study period ends. Where a monthly rate is published for a model, plan around it; otherwise contact us to confirm the current monthly price. Do not rent a semester upfront "for convenience" — plans change, and there is no automatic refund for unused time.

### The dates to collect from your own calendar

A university or programme sets its own term, so a calendar from one school says nothing reliable about another, and this guide names no school and no dates. Write down the ones from your own calendar and paperwork:

| Date | Why it matters | What to ask the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival in Hanoi | The earliest day you could want a bike | Whether the first period starts on collection day |
| First class or orientation | When regular riding begins | Which model suits your route; contact us to confirm current availability |
| Last exam or last teaching day | The earliest sensible end date | What happens if exam dates move |
| Any resit, thesis or results date you may stay for | The latest realistic end date | How a short extension is requested and confirmed |
| Departure flight | A hard stop for the return | Which day and time the bike can come back |
| End of your visa or residence card | The limit on your stay | No question for the shop: check your own end date against it |

Send these dates with your first message, so that the answer you get is about your term and not about a generic month.

### Counting the periods: an illustration

Imagine a term that your calendar puts at four months, from arrival to the last exam. If the shop counts each monthly period from the day you collect the bike, which is something to ask, four periods end on the same day of the month four months later. If your last exam or flight falls after that day, the term has a tail of extra days. If the last exam falls well before it, the final period is partly unused.

Rental periods are daily, weekly and monthly, and longer terms are negotiable, so there is more than one way to cover a tail: ask which the shop would use before you fix the end date. A stay nearer three months fits [Three-Month Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/three-month-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) better, and [Motorbike Rental Monthly Prices in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the published monthly figures.

### Month by month or a negotiated term

Month by month is the cautious default, because it keeps the commitment short. Longer-term rental is negotiable: contact us to discuss it. If you ask for a term-long arrangement, compare it on its terms: what it says about an early return, whether the end date can move with the exam timetable, and what happens to the deposit.

### Exam weeks and breaks

Nothing published says that a rental pauses when you ride less, so treat every day of the term as a rental day unless the shop agrees otherwise in writing. Before the first exam week, ask:

- If I travel for a break, should the bike go back and be rented again afterwards, or stay with me and be parked?
- If the term ends while I am away, who receives the bike, and does the late-return charge keep running?
- Is a message enough to renew while I am out of Hanoi?

A bike that stands unused is also a maintenance question. Business maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, while consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility, so ask which falls where.

## Tet inside the spring semester

If your term runs into early 2027, Tet may fall inside it. The Government portal reported on 22 October 2026, citing Ministry of Home Affairs Notice 11483/TB-BNV, that civil servants and public-sector staff rest from Thursday 4 February to Wednesday 10 February 2027, which it places from the 28th day of the twelfth lunar month to the 5th day of the first. Counting from those figures, the lunar new year itself begins on Saturday 6 February.

Those dates describe public bodies. The same report lets other employers choose one of three five-day patterns, so a shop's Tet days are for the shop to say, and your university's are for the university: ask the academic office or the dormitory which days it closes and whether classes or exams sit around them.

For the rental, three situations come up:

- **A period ends inside or just before the break.** Settle a new end date or an early renewal before 4 February, not during it. [Keeping a Monthly Rental Through Tet in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-through-tet-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows where a term can land against these dates.
- **You go home for Tet.** Ask whether the days you are away count towards the term.
- **You stay and ride.** Ask which days the shop is open, and book routine maintenance before the break.

## Settling the terms at the start

Put these to the shop in the first conversation, and keep the answers in the same chat:

- The end date you are working to, and the date each month's period renews.
- How a renewal is asked for and confirmed, and how far ahead; [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers continuing past a month.

## Settling the ending before it arrives

The last week of a term can be crowded, so decide the return in the first weeks instead.

- Work the return day and time out against your flight and the shop's published hours, 09:00–21:00 daily.
- Know what lateness costs: a late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than six hours late may add a further full rental day, typically 150,000–200,000 VND depending on the bike.
- Treat an early return as a choice to make with the shop, not a right to unused time; [Ending a Monthly Motorbike Rental Early]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the position.
- If you will have left Hanoi by the time the deposit is settled, ask in advance how that will be done.

## Deposits and money

The deposit is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, which is real money on a student budget, so treat the deposit as untouchable savings rather than spendable cash. [Monthly vs Daily Motorbike Rental in Hanoi: Which Makes Sense]({{ '/articles/monthly-vs-daily-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains why monthly wins on cost once you are riding most days, and [Monthly Motorbike Rental Budget in Hanoi: The Full Cost]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-budget-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) helps you total fuel, parking and consumables into an honest monthly figure.

### Spreading the cost over the months

A term budget is a handful of lines, and some of them repeat each month. Sorted by when they fall:

| When | Lines | Where the figure comes from |
|---|---|---|
| At the start | The deposit, the first rental period, your own insurance | The shop and your insurer |
| Each month | The rental period, fuel, parking | The shop's quote, your own distance, the place you park |
| At the end | Any tail of extra days, consumables that fall to you, settlement of the deposit | The shop |

Add the monthly lines for every month, then the start and end lines once. No rate is quoted here, so ask the shop for the rental line for your dates and keep the reply in writing. Ask how long the deposit stays with the shop. If the answer is the whole term, decide at the start where that sum comes from, and ask how the settlement will reach you at the end.

## Where the bike stays for a whole term

Campuses, dormitories and rented rooms each decide their own parking, so ask the place itself; for the full list of questions, use the international students guide. A term adds three. Can parking be arranged for a whole term, or only each time you park? Does the place stay open for the hours you ride, including exam weeks? Is there a limit on how long a bike may stand unmoved while you are away? [Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the everyday routine.

## Sharing is not automatic

A rental agreement may name the rider, so ask what yours says. Letting a flatmate without the right licence borrow the bike puts both the bike and the deposit at risk — the legal side is in [Lending Your Motorbike to an Unlicensed Rider in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and the household option of a second bike is covered in [Renting Two Motorbikes for a Household in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/two-motorbikes-household-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

A friend may ask to take the bike over when you leave. Ask whether the agreement can be moved to someone else, and until the shop says yes in writing, assume it cannot. Whoever rides still needs a licence valid in Vietnam for the bike's class.

## Questions semester renters ask

### Can I rent for exactly my semester?

Monthly is one of the published rental periods and longer terms are negotiable: contact us to discuss. Renting month by month with the end date agreed at the start is the closest fit, and the dates table above is the way to prepare for that conversation.

### Is there a student discount?

Nguyen Tu publishes no student discount. Longer terms are negotiable: contact us to discuss. Compare any arrangement on its early-return and renewal terms.

### What if my exams move after I have agreed an end date?

Tell the shop as soon as you know and ask for the end date to change. A late return carries a charge, so the message belongs before the old end date, not after it.

## The one-line version

Check the age and licence rules, rent month by month to match the semester, protect the deposit, and never lend the bike.
