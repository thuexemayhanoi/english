Manufacturer fuel-economy figures for Vietnamese underbones are impressive — many claim under two litres per 100 km — but those numbers come from gentle test rides. Real Hanoi riding with hard launches, hard braking and under-inflated tyres can use half again as much. The gap between the two is riding habits, and habits are free to change.

## The four habits that matter most

1. **Cruise in the top third of the rev range, not the top of it.** An underbone engine is most efficient at moderate, steady revs with a slightly open throttle. Screaming along at full throttle in a low gear is the single biggest fuel waster.
2. **Shift up early, shift down late.** Upshift as soon as the engine pulls cleanly in the next gear; avoid downshifting merely to accelerate faster than traffic requires. The shift-point guide explains listening to the engine.
3. **Look ahead and coast down to stops.** See the red light 200 metres away, roll off, and arrive at walking pace as it turns green. Every hard brake is fuel already burned to buy speed you then threw away.
4. **Steady throttle beats surging.** Pick your gap in traffic, settle into it, and hold a steady opening. In dense Hanoi flow the surging rider arrives at the same time as the smooth one — and uses noticeably more fuel.

## Maintenance that pays for itself

- **Tyre pressure**: an under-deflated tyre adds rolling resistance and drags fuel economy down within days of neglect. Check weekly by gauge or thumb; the tyre guides cover pressures and wear.
- **Engine oil**: old, thin oil raises friction everywhere. Regular oil changes are the cheapest economy improvement on an old bike.
- **Air filter**: a clogged filter in dusty-season Hanoi richens the mixture and costs fuel; the air-filter guide covers cleaning intervals.
- **Chain**: a dry, tight or misaligned chain wastes power between engine and wheel — clean and lube it, and keep the slack in spec.
- **Brakes dragging**: a stuck caliper or dragging drum is a silent fuel thief. If the bike slows too quickly with the throttle closed, get it checked.

## Drag, load and idling

Luggage changes economy: keep the load low and modest, avoid bulky boxes that sit in the wind, and take the passenger's extra mass into account in your expectations. Long idling — the ten-minute wait outside a school gate — burns fuel for nothing; small engines restart cheaply. And aftermarket parts that promise economy usually do the opposite: wider tyres, louder exhausts and taller screens all cost fuel.
