No underbone has a shift light or a tach worth trusting. Vietnamese riders shift by ear, and after a day of riding you will too. Here is what to listen for.

## The three engine voices

- **Cruising voice:** relaxed, steady, responsive when you open the throttle. You are in the right gear.
- **Working voice:** busy, buzzy, the bike accelerating but the engine note climbing fast. Time to shift up.
- **Struggling voice:** knocking or lugging under load, the bike shaking gently at low revs when you ask for more. Shift down.

Shift up when the working voice appears; shift down when the engine struggles or when you are about to slow into a situation where you will need acceleration — junctions, gaps, hills.

## The habit loop

Every minute of riding runs the same check: engine voice → choose gear → throttle. On a semi-automatic the click costs nothing, so shift freely. On a manual bike each shift costs a clutch squeeze, so plan one gear earlier.

## Why underbones forgive you

The small single-cylinder engines that power this class pull from very low revs — our engine explainer covers why. Their power arrives early and flattens out, so being one gear too high is merely sluggish, never dangerous. The practical rules: shift up early and often in the city; hold a gear longer only when overtaking; and downshift before you think you need to on hills.

## Loading and hills

Two-up or loaded, everything happens one gear lower. Same on slopes: knock down before the climb, not during it, so the engine never lugs. And if the gearbox itself starts refusing smooth shifts, that is maintenance territory — the shifting-technique articles cover the mechanical symptoms separately.
