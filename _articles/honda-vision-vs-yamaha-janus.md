Two light, inexpensive, city-first automatic scooters define the entry class in Vietnam: Honda's Vision and Yamaha's Janus. Here is how they compare using only figures published by Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Vietnam (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## The numbers side by side

| | Honda Vision | Yamaha Janus |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 109.5 cc, eSP, air-cooled | 124.9 cc, Blue Core |
| Max power | 6.59 kW @ 7,500 rpm | 7.0 kW @ 8,000 rpm |
| Max torque | 9.29 Nm @ 6,000 rpm | 9.6 Nm @ 5,500 rpm |
| Kerb weight | 97 kg | 97 kg |
| Seat height | 785 mm | 770 mm |
| Claimed economy | 1.82 L/100 km | 1.98 L/100 km |
| Fuel tank | 4.8 L | 4.2 L |
| Front tyre | 80/90-16 (16-inch wheel) | 80/80-14, tubeless |
| Listed retail price | from 31,506,545 VND | from 29,151,000 VND |

Every row is a manufacturer figure from the product page, not something measured on a road. Power and torque are peak values, economy is a claimed figure, and the prices are "from" list prices, so a higher version of either scooter costs more. This page compares only the rows above. Brakes, underseat volume and other equipment are covered in the single-model reviews: the [Honda Vision 110 review]({{ '/articles/honda-vision-110-review/' | relative_url }}) and the [Yamaha Janus 125 review]({{ '/articles/yamaha-janus-125-review/' | relative_url }}).

## What the numbers mean

- **Performance:** the Janus's larger engine produces slightly more power and torque on paper, with its torque peak arriving at a lower engine speed, as the engine section below shows; the Vision compensates with gearing tuned for the city. Both are city scooters - neither is a tourer.
- **Weight:** identical at 97 kg - the lightest class on the market, which [our light motorbikes for new riders guide]({{ '/articles/lightest-motorbikes-for-new-riders/' | relative_url }}) covers.
- **Economy:** the Vision's published consumption is lower, and its bigger tank gives it a longer range between stops. See the fuel economy comparison for the full class picture.
- **Wheels:** the Vision's 16-inch front wheel gives more stability over potholes (our 16-inch [wheel guide]({{ '/articles/16-inch-wheel-scooters-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains why); the Janus's 14-inch wheels sharpen low-speed agility.
- **Price:** Yamaha lists the Janus from 29,151,000 VND against Honda's Vision listing from 31,506,545 VND - manufacturer suggested retail, retrieved 23 September 2026.

### Engine: a small gap on paper

The Janus has the larger engine, 124.9 cc against 109.5 cc, but the output gap is modest: 7.0 kW against 6.59 kW, and 9.6 Nm against 9.29 Nm. That is about 0.41 kW and 0.31 Nm. Two other details matter more than the headline figures. The Janus reaches peak power at a higher engine speed (8,000 rpm against 7,500 rpm) and peak torque at a lower one (5,500 rpm against 6,000 rpm).

Torque arriving earlier is the more useful trait in stop-start traffic, because an automatic scooter spends most of its life at low and middle engine speeds. A 500 rpm difference is small, though. The gap may be hard to notice on an empty road; it is more likely to show with a passenger on board or on a long gentle climb. Our [power and torque explainer]({{ '/articles/scooter-power-torque-explained/' | relative_url }}) shows how to read these two numbers, and the [110cc vs 125cc guide]({{ '/articles/110cc-vs-125cc-scooters/' | relative_url }}) puts the capacity step in context.

### Licence class: both sit inside A1

The licence guide on this site explains that a new A1 licence covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc (Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15), with a minimum age of 18. Both scooters fall inside that limit. The Janus is the closer call at 124.9 cc, but the law's wording is "up to 125cc", so it does not need a bigger class. That is the legal position as the guide records it; the practical point is that choosing between these two scooters never changes which licence you need. Foreign visitors should read [what the A1 licence covers]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) before riding, because riding with no valid licence of any kind is an offence.

### Seat and weight: a tie and a 15 mm gap

Both scooters are published at 97 kg, so neither is heavier to push off a stand, walk backwards out of a parking row or hold up at a standstill. Seat height differs by 15 mm: 785 mm for the Vision, 770 mm for the Janus. Seat height is only one input to whether your feet reach the ground. The width of the front of the seat, the shape of the floor and your own leg length decide how it feels, which is why the advice to sit on both is more reliable than the figures.

### Wheels and tyres: what the codes tell you

The Vision's front tyre is marked 80/90-16 and the Janus's 80/80-14. In a standard tyre marking the first number is the tyre width in millimetres, the second is the sidewall height as a percentage of that width, and the last is the rim diameter in inches. The same marking is printed on the tyre sidewall, so you can check what is fitted to any specific bike.

A larger wheel rolls over a pothole, a drain cover or a patched edge with less of a jolt, which is the Vision's strength on broken Hanoi streets. A smaller wheel turns in a tighter space and is quicker to change direction, which suits alleys and crowded parking. Yamaha publishes the Janus tyre as tubeless. The comparison table above does not hold a tubeless or tube-type figure for the Vision, so check what is fitted when you look at a specific bike.

### Fuel: lower consumption and a larger tank

The Vision's published consumption is 1.82 L/100 km from a 4.8 L tank, and the Janus's is 1.98 L/100 km from a 4.2 L tank. The consumption gap is 0.16 litres per 100 km. It is real, but small enough that load, traffic and riding style will move your own figure by more than that. The tank difference adds to it, because a bigger tank and lower consumption both lengthen the distance between fuel stops. The [scooter fuel economy comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-fuel-economy-comparison-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works out the arithmetic range of each and ranks them against other models. Claimed figures come from the manufacturer's test method, so treat them as a way to rank two scooters, not as a promise about your own trips.

### Price and what comes with it

At retrieval on 23 September 2026 Yamaha listed the Janus from 29,151,000 VND and Honda listed the Vision from 31,506,545 VND, so the Janus starts lower despite its larger engine. Two cautions apply. A "from" price refers to one version, and the version on a dealer's floor may differ. List prices also leave out registration, plate, insurance, accessories and dealer fees, which our [new scooter price guide]({{ '/articles/new-scooter-prices-vietnam-verified/' | relative_url }}) explains. Warranty is the other half of cost: Yamaha's 3-year/30,000 km Janus warranty is noted in the verdict below, and this comparison does not hold a Vision warranty figure, so ask the Honda dealer for the terms in writing.

## How to test-sit them before you buy

A short, deliberate visit to a dealer tells you more than any table. Take the shoes and helmet you normally ride with.

1. Sit on each scooter and put both feet down. Note whether you are flat-footed or on tiptoe, and how the front of the seat presses on your thighs.
2. Hold the handlebars and check the reach, the mirror view and the position of your wrists and elbows.
3. Walk each scooter backwards and forwards off its stand. Both are published at 97 kg, so a clear difference suggests you are comparing different versions.
4. Open the underseat bay and try your own helmet and a typical load.
5. Ask for a test ride that includes a tight U-turn, a speed bump and a hard stop at low speed.
6. Ask which version the quoted price covers, what registration and plate cost, and what the warranty includes, and get the answers in writing.

## Mistakes buyers make with this pair

- **Treating 125 as much faster than 110.** The published gap is 0.41 kW and 0.31 Nm. Decide on fit, wheels and fuel use, and read the 110cc vs 125cc guide before paying for capacity you may not notice.
- **Comparing list prices across different versions.** A "from" price is a starting point. Ask for the on-the-road price of the exact version you would buy.
- **Reading claimed economy as a promise.** The figures rank the two scooters; your own consumption depends on load, traffic and style.
- **Judging comfort by seat height alone.** A 15 mm difference is easily outweighed by seat shape. Sit on both.
- **Assuming a model is available to rent because it has a page here.** Reviews and comparisons describe scooters; availability is confirmed only by contacting us.

## Common questions

### Is the Yamaha Janus faster than the Honda Vision?

On published figures the Janus is slightly stronger: 7.0 kW and 9.6 Nm against 6.59 kW and 9.29 Nm. The gap is small, neither manufacturer figure here is a top speed, and both scooters are built for city use rather than touring.

### Which one uses less fuel?

The Vision's claimed consumption is 1.82 L/100 km against 1.98 L/100 km for the Janus, and it has the larger tank. Real consumption varies with load, traffic and riding style.

### Do the two need different licences?

No. Both are at or below 125 cc, which the A1 class covers according to our licence guide (Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15). Foreign visitors should check the guide for how a foreign licence and an International Driving Permit are treated.

### Which is cheaper to buy new?

### Which suits a first-time rider?

Both weigh 97 kg and both are built for city use. The Vision offers the larger front wheel and the Janus the lower seat, so the better first scooter is the one you can handle confidently.
