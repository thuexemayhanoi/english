Delivery riding is the hardest test of an electric two-wheeler: hundreds of kilometres a week, mostly stop-start, mostly on a clock. The format can pass that test — but only with the right refuelling model, and the verified numbers make the choice unusually clear.

## The economics that matter at delivery mileage

A delivery rider's machine covers in a week what a commuter covers in a month, so the cost question is refuelling: swap fees (9,000 VND per battery per swap; battery rental 175,000–300,000 VND monthly on the VinFast class) versus home electricity on an owned pack (the Dat Bike class). At high volumes the swap fee scales with kilometres while home charging stays cheap per charge — but the swap rider also never spends off-clock hours waiting on a cable. The swap-vs-charge comparison works the sums; at delivery volumes, both models have honest advocates.

## What the verified machines offer

- **VinFast swap class (Evo 2,450 W, up to 165 km claimed; Feliz II / Viper 3,000 W, up to 156 km)**: licence-free, and the cabinet network (first 4,500 stations installed by mid-January 2026, 45,000 planned) is designed for exactly this duty — a two-minute swap between orders.
- **Dat Bike Weaver 200 (6,000 W, 200 km claimed at 35 km/h, 1 h for the first 100 km of charge)**: A1 licence required, but a working rider can realistically sleep-charge to full and spend the day riding, with the published 150,000 km / 15-year battery-life claim and 48-hour on-site service speaking directly to high-mileage durability.
- **Weaver++ (100 km in 20 minutes at Dat Charge)**: the fast-charge option for mid-shift top-ups on the A1 class.

## The delivery-specific practicalities

1. **Plan the charging clock**: for home-charge machines, the working day must fit between charges — count orders, then range, then charging hours.
2. **Budget real range, not claimed**: stop-start delivery riding is far from the standard conditions of any claim — see the real-world range guide before trusting any figure.
3. **Weight matters**: the Weaver 200's 165 kg max load is the verified figure for cargo-plus-rider; the licence-free class carries less muscle on hills (bridges guide).
4. **Maintain for mileage**: brakes, tyres and bearings wear on kilometres, not years — the high-mileage maintenance guide applies to electric drivetrains too, minus oil changes and clutch work.

## Who should ride what

Swap-capable licence-free machines suit urban food-delivery patterns (short hops, dense cabinet coverage); the A1 charge-at-home class suits long-shift, long-radius work for riders who hold the licence. The honest advice: run the numbers on your own weekly kilometres before switching — the format rewards high mileage best when the refuelling model matches the shift pattern.
