A lost Vietnamese motorbike licence is reissued, not re-earned. Classes A1, A and B1 carry no expiry date (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 57(5)(a)). Article 20(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA lets the holder of a licence still within its period of validity ask for a reissue, using the dossier and steps of Article 21(2) to (4), which list no test. That circular took over from Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA on 1 July 2026.

## Which rules apply

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA was signed on 29 June 2026 and applies from 1 July 2026 (Article 41(1)). Article 41(6) ended Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA from that date, apart from Article 4(1) to (3) and some appendices that stay in force to the end of 28 February 2027; reissue is not among the provisions it names. The Law is quoted from its consolidated text, 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes Law 118/2025/QH15.

Guides written before July 2026 describe the earlier circular; these are the differences for a lost licence:

| Point | Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA (1 March 2025 to 30 June 2026) | Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA (from 1 July 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Application form | A request on the Appendix XII form (Article 21(2)(a)) | No form in the Article 21(2) list |
| Health certificate | Required, except for holders of A1, A and B1 (Article 21(2)(b)) | Required, with no exception stated for any class (Article 21(2)(a)) |
| Data and electronic licence | 1 working day for the data to pass on; electronic licence within 3 working days (Article 21(3)(c)) | 1 day for the data to pass on for central management and issue, electronic version included (Article 21(3)(c)) |
| Printed card | Within 5 working days (Article 21(3)(c)) | Within 2.5 working days, when the applicant asks for one (Article 21(3)(c)) |

## The dossier

Article 20(1) sends a lost licence through the dossier and steps of Article 21(2) to (4). Article 21(2) lists three items. A plain loss needs the first, and the second as well for a foreigner or a Vietnamese settled abroad:

1. A valid health certificate. Article 21(2)(a) asks for the electronic version or, where a technical fault makes it unusable, the paper version issued by a medical facility that meets the standards of the law on medical examination and treatment. The text says only that the certificate must be in force and gives no number of months.
2. A passport still in term, for a foreigner or a Vietnamese settled abroad (Article 21(2)(b)).
3. Papers showing the correct details, for the case in Article 21(1)(c), where the date of birth, name or nationality on the licence differs from the citizen identity card, identity card or passport (Article 21(2)(c)). That is a mismatch exchange, not a loss.

Article 21(2) lists no application form, no police report and no declaration of loss, and it names no identity card among the items, so ask the desk which identity document it wants to see. The applicant declares the details as instructed on the channel used and answers for their accuracy and lawfulness (Article 21(3)(a)). Article 21(5), which Article 20(1) does not name, treats the Article 21(2) papers as the original dossier and leaves it with the driver for safekeeping, so ask the desk whether the certificate is kept or handed back.

The applicant files it at the Traffic Police Department or a commune-level police point that accepts licence dossiers, or through the online public-service portal where available. "Traffic Police Department" here stands for the Phòng Cảnh sát giao thông, the traffic police office of a provincial or city police force; the commune-level point is a police office at which a reception point is arranged. A photograph is taken at the counter. Article 21(3)(a) also allows one white-background portrait of 3 cm by 4 cm to be handed in at the reception point, or the photo held in the identity data to be used.

| Route | Where | When the text points you there |
|---|---|---|
| Online | National Public Service Portal, national identification application or the applications of the People's Public Security forces | The first route named in Article 21(3)(a) |
| In person | Traffic Police Department or a commune-level police reception point | No level-2 electronic identification, no licence data in the licence system, or a portal fault (Article 21(3)(a)) |

The central body, the Traffic Police Bureau (Cục Cảnh sát giao thông), receives the data and issues the licence (Article 21(3)(c)). Article 36(4)(a) tasks it with building a full-process online service for issue, exchange and reissue on the portal, and the clause sets completion before 1 January 2027, so ask the desk which steps the portal handles when you apply.

## Time and cost

The 1-day and 2.5-working-day periods in Article 21(3)(c) run from the receipt of a complete, valid dossier, not from the first visit. The sequence runs like this:

| Stage | What Article 21(3) says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Check at the desk | The Traffic Police Department or commune police check the dossier, look the licence up in the system, check the driver's violations, enter the details electronically and print an appointment slip | (b) |
| A defect in the dossier | The desk says what to add or correct within 2 working days of receiving it | (b) |
| Data to the central office | 1 day from a complete valid dossier to pass the data on for central management and issue, electronic version included | (c) |
| Printed card | On request, within 2.5 working days of a complete valid dossier | (c) |
| Return | At the Traffic Police Department or a commune reception point, or by post on request; an online applicant gets it back personally | (d) |

Article 21(3)(b) treats the appointment slip only as a slip for collecting the result and says nothing that lets it stand in for the licence on the road. The Circular states a duty to pay the licence fee "as prescribed" and to pay other service fees only for services the applicant chooses (Article 21(3)(a)). It gives no amount, so ask the desk what applies on the day.

No retest is involved — the classes on the old licence carry over. For a licence issued before 1 January 2025, Article 89(3) of the Law maps the old class to a current one. An old A1 becomes class A restricted to two-wheeled motorbikes below 175 cm3 or below 14 kW, an old A2 becomes class A and an old A3 becomes B1. [What each old class becomes]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through that mapping. A holder who prefers a lower class registers the wish with the receiving office (Article 21(4)).

## The exceptions

Two situations change the character of the procedure. First, a lost licence that had already expired: the reissue turns into a [validity question]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and licences expired for longer periods can require sitting the theory test again before a new one is issued. Article 20(1) explains why: it opens the reissue to a licence "still within its period of validity" and says nothing about one past its term. The tests come from Article 19(2). For a car licence that expired from 30 days to under 1 year before a complete dossier was filed it sets a theory test, and from 1 year theory, yard and road tests. The paragraph is worded for car licences.

A Vietnamese motorbike licence of class A1, A or B1 has no term to pass (Article 57(5)(a)), but the licence a foreigner receives by exchange carries one, whose term follows the papers behind it (Article 24(5)(a)). Nothing in Articles 19 or 20 says what a lost licence of that kind needs once its term has passed, so ask the Traffic Police Department for an answer in writing.

Second, a lost licence that the records cannot confirm, or whose holder still has an offence to settle: Article 20(2) refuses a reissue in either case.

### A licence the records do not show

Article 20(2) refuses a reissue where the licence's details are not in the licence information system at the Traffic Police Bureau or are not in the register of issued licences (the sổ quản lý). The desk starts by looking the licence up in the system (Article 21(3)(b)). Where the system holds no information yet, the same clause has the desk verify the licence with Form 01 of Appendix X, a written request from the head of the Traffic Police Department to the unit asked to verify.

The form asks for the holder's name, nationality, date of birth and identification number, and for the licence class, number, issuing body and issue date. The reply, Form 02, reports what the test-management records hold. The Circular does not say how that step fits with the refusal in Article 20(2), so ask which applies to an older licence. A photo, a scan or an old paper showing the number and the issue date is worth taking along.

### An offence still open

Article 62(4) of the Law holds back a reissue from a person who has broken the road traffic rules and has not yet finished what the competent state authority requires to resolve the case. Article 20(2) of the Circular lists the same case. The Law's wording is "not yet" (chưa) and ties the bar to the requirement being unfinished. Article 21(3)(b) has the desk check the driver's violations, so settle an open case before applying.

### Zero points

Neither Article 62 of the Law nor Article 20 of the Circular refuses a reissue because points have run out. A reissue keeps the balance the licence had (Law, Article 58(4)). Once all 12 points are deducted, the licence cannot be used to ride; the holder must wait at least 6 months before sitting the road-law test run by the traffic police; passing it restores all 12 points (Article 58(3)). [What happens when all points are lost]({{ '/articles/lose-all-licence-points-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows that path.

## A damaged card

A card that is damaged and no longer usable is not lost. Article 21(1)(a) sends it to an exchange, which uses the dossier and procedure of Article 21(2) and (3), and Article 62(2)(b) of the Law lists damage beside loss as a ground for reissue or exchange.

## The electronic licence

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA recognises two forms of the licence. Article 3(2) defines the electronic licence as a licence in the form of a data message, integrated into the electronic data system or the electronic identification account, and Article 18(2) says a licence is issued either in electronic form or on PET material. On a reissue, Article 21(3)(c) has the licence integrated on the national identification application or the police applications, and a printed card follows only when the applicant asks for one.

For someone who has lost a card, Article 56(3) of the Law lets papers already integrated into the electronic identification account be shown through it. Article 18(8) of the Circular counts a licence issued before 1 July 2026 as valid where its printed form number, issue date and expiry date match the licence management system, or where its details are already integrated into the account or the police applications.

## While you wait

Riding without carrying your licence is handled under the [document-carriage rules]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), which explain what happens at a stop; Article 56(1)(b) of the Law makes the licence one of the documents a driver must carry. [Riding with no valid licence at all]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is a far heavier offence. Those guides quote fine amounts from Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as issued. That decree was amended by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP with effect from 15 August 2026, so check each figure in the amended text before relying on it.

Check your electronic identification account first, since a licence integrated there can be shown through it. If the lost licence cannot be located before you need to ride, the safe answer is to wait for the reissue rather than treat the loss as a technicality.

A renter in this position should ask the rental business what evidence of the licence it will accept and how an electronic licence would be shown at a stop. ## Foreign residents and foreign licences

Foreign residents reissuing a Vietnamese licence follow the same file structure plus passport and residence papers — the same documents the exchange procedure uses. That sentence is wrong about residence papers for a reissue: Article 21(2)(b) lists the passport, which must be in term, and it lists no visa or residence card. A copy of the visa or card belongs to the separate exchange of a foreign licence (Article 24(2)(c)), not to the reissue of a Vietnamese one.

A foreigner who holds a Vietnamese licence may also ask for an exchange before it expires (Article 21(1)(b)). [How foreigners exchange a foreign licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the route from a national licence to a Vietnamese one.

The Circular gives no Vietnamese procedure for replacing a foreign national licence. Article 24 deals with the exchange of a foreign licence still in term, and its dossier opens with a translation of that licence, certified by a notary office in Vietnam or made by a Vietnamese representative office abroad, and stamped across the join with a copy of it (Article 24(2)(a)). This page therefore cannot say how a lost foreign licence is replaced, because that is not a Vietnamese procedure.

## Questions about a lost licence

### Do I have to sit a test to replace a lost licence?

Not for a licence still within its period of validity: Article 21(2) to (4) contains none. [How a first A1 licence is obtained]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the test route for a new applicant.

### How long does a reissue take?

Once a complete, valid dossier is received, the Traffic Police Department has 1 day to pass the data to the Traffic Police Bureau for central management and issue of the licence, electronic version included. A printed card on request takes up to 2.5 working days (Article 21(3)(c)).

### Does a reissue clear my points or an open offence?

Neither. The licence keeps its points (Law, Article 58(4)), and an offence you have not finished dealing with holds the reissue back (Law, Article 62(4); Circular, Article 20(2)).
