Tyres are the electric motorbike's underrated consumable: instant torque, heavy battery packs and regen braking all write their signatures into the rubber faster than on a small petrol bike.

## Why electric wear differs

- **Weight.** Battery packs are dense. The Weaver 200 weighs 120 kg before rider and luggage — more than many 125 cc petrol scooters — and load rides through the contact patches.
- **Instant torque.** Launches stress the rear tyre; if you ride every red light like a grand prix start, the centre of the rear tyre wears first.
- **Regen braking.** Deceleration through the rear wheel adds to rear-tire duty, alongside the front discs doing the hard stops.
- **Silent speed.** Because the bike is quiet, riders tend to carry more speed through corners than they realise — scrubbed tyre edges are common.

## The routine

1. **Pressures weekly** (and always before a long ride), using the manufacturer's specified values — not remembered ones. Use our tyre care guide for the method.
2. **Tread and sidewalls at the same time**: cuts, embedded stones, sidewall bubbles, and the legal wear indicators.
3. **Wheel size honesty.** The Weaver 200 runs 17-inch wheels (17-2.75 front, 17-3.0 rear per its official spec) — underbone-style sizing, not scooter sizing; match replacements exactly.
4. **Age check.** Rubber hardens with years regardless of tread; see our tyre age guide.
5. **After every deep pothole**, re-check pressures — Hanoi's road works are unkind to rims and seals alike.

## What not to do

Do not inflate by eye or feel; do not mix brands front and rear on a torque-heavy machine; and do not stretch worn tyres because the motor still pulls fine — traction, not power, is what fails in the wet. Electric bikes feel effortlessly safe right up to the moment the tyres let go, so let the rubber set the limits.
