One focused hour in a quiet space teaches more than a week of nervous commuting. Work through this checklist in order — do not move to the next item until the current one feels boring.

## Minutes 0–10: sit and touch

Engine off. Squeeze and release the clutch 30 times. Click through the gears with the clutch in and back down again. Squeeze the front brake, press the rear. Find the horn, indicators and lights. Learn where everything is before anything can surprise you.

## Minutes 10–25: friction zone

Engine on, first gear. Creep forward using only the clutch, stop, repeat — at least fifteen launches. Then hold a steady walking pace for ten seconds at a time. No attempts at speed; the goal is lever feel.

## Minutes 25–40: first and second

Launch, accelerate gently to second, ride 50 metres, brake smoothly, downshift, stop. Repeat the loop. Practise stopping with the clutch pulled in before your feet touch the ground, then finding neutral after you stop.

## Minutes 40–55: shapes

Slow circles left and right in first and second, using the friction zone and a steady throttle — the drills in our slow-speed [guide]({{ '/articles/slow-speed-control-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Finish with figure-eights if space allows. This is the hardest and most valuable part of the hour.

## Minutes 55–60: traffic preview

Ride the perimeter of your practice area and practise looking far ahead while riding slowly — head up, eyes distant, controls automatic.

That is the whole hour. If the launches are smooth and the stalls are rare, you are ready for quiet streets at quiet hours.
