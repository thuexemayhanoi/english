Two-up riding is normal in Vietnam, and electric motorbikes handle it well — with a few numbers worth knowing.

## Load limits

The manufacturer's payload figure is the binding one. The Dat Bike Weaver 200, for example, lists a maximum load of 165 kg excluding the vehicle. Two adults plus bags are near that ceiling; check your own model's figure before loading, because exceeding it stresses the frame, brakes and motor.

## What a passenger changes

- **Range.** Add a passenger and real-world range drops noticeably — budget for roughly 15-25 percent less, more in stop-start districts.
- **Braking.** Disc brakes work harder; leave earlier gaps. Regen helps (passengers barely notice regen deceleration), but the front brake does the real stopping.
- **Launch.** Acceleration is slower with a load — plan bigger gaps for crossing arterials.
- **Helmet.** Passengers are required to wear standard-compliant helmets by law, exactly as riders.

## Technique for smooth two-up riding

1. Agree before moving: passenger mounts after you say so, feet on the pegs, no sudden weight shifts in corners.
2. Launch gently — instant electric torque plus a surprised passenger is a wobble.
3. Brake earlier and progressively; use regen for the first half of every deceleration.
4. On longer rides, a backrest or top box gives the passenger something to lean on.

## Licence note

The class does not change with a passenger, but power does set who may ride at all — above 4 kW an A1 licence is required. If your passenger is 15, remember the sub-4 kW class still requires riders, not passengers, to be 16.
