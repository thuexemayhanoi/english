Touring Vietnam on two wheels has always been a petrol discipline — range measured in tanks, refuelled anywhere. Electric touring is now possible, but only on the right machine and only with planning habits that petrol riders never needed. Here is the honest state of it, from verified figures only.

## Which machines can tour

| Machine | Verified distance credentials |
|---|---|
| Dat Bike Weaver 200 | 200 km claimed at 35 km/h; 130 km at 70 km/h; 90 km/h top speed; 1 h restores the first 100 km of range; 165 kg max load for luggage |
| Dat Bike Weaver++ | 200 km claimed; 100 km in 20 minutes at Dat Charge stations |
| VinFast swap class (Evo, Feliz II, Viper) | up to 165/156 km claimed, licence-free — but touring range depends on cabinet coverage along the route |

The A1-class charge-at-home machines are the genuine touring tools: the Weaver 200's 130 km at a real 70 km/h is the figure that matters for highway sections, not the city-cycle 200. The licence-free swap class tours only where the V-Green network reaches — currently 34 provinces and cities per the published plan, with first 4,500 stations installed by mid-January 2026. A route through network gaps becomes a charging-logistics problem.

## The planning discipline

1. **Plan on the pessimistic figure** — real-world range shrinks with load, hills, wind and cold; the range guide covers how much. A 130-km-highway machine plans 100 km hops.
2. **Fix the charge stops before the ride** — know where chargers or cabinets sit on the route and their hours; a rural "charging point" that closes at 18:00 ends the day early.
3. **Budget the charging clock** — a 3-hour full charge on the Weaver 200 is a lunch-and-coffee stop or a hotel decision, not a fuel stop. Fast charging (the Weaver++'s 20-minute Dat Charge) changes the rhythm entirely.
4. **Carry the charger and cables** — touring on electric means the charger rides with you, always.
5. **Ride the speed that maximises range** — the difference between 35 km/h and 70 km/h on the Weaver 200's own published figures is 70 km of range; the touring mind rides the engine's sweet spot.

## Licence and legal notes

A1-class machines require an A1 licence; the licence-free class is capped at 70 km/h machines anyway. Expressways are closed to motorbikes regardless of drive type — touring electric uses the same national and provincial roads as every Vietnamese tour.

## Is it there yet?

For riders who hold the licence, accept planning discipline, and want silence on the passes: yes — the Weaver 200 class makes intercity touring real. For riders who want to ride until a random shop has fuel: not yet. The day-trips-from-Hanoi guide shows what the format already does well close to home.
