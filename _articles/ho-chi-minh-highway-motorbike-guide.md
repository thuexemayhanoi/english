Every country with a famous road eventually gets a debate about which road is the famous one. In Vietnam that debate is over: the Ho Chi Minh Road (Quốc lộ Hồ Chí Minh, QLHCM) — the modern sealed highway built along the line of the legendary wartime supply trail — is the ride, and independent guides describe it simply as one of the most famous roads in the world.

## What it is

About 1,880 kilometres running the length of Vietnam's mountainous western spine, from the northern mountains toward the Central Highlands and on toward the south. It was constructed from the 2000s onward along the corridor of the Ho Chi Minh Trail — the network of paths that carried soldiers and supplies south during the war — and today it is a wide, well-surfaced highway that behaves like a touring route, not a trail.

That length is also the point: the QLHCM and the coastal QL1A give Vietnam two parallel north-south spines, and the mountain one carries a fraction of the traffic. Route guides repeatedly note the near-empty stretches east of the high mountains — riding all day between fuel stops with only forest, karst and the occasional H'Mong or Thai village for company.

## The sections riders talk about

- **The northwest (Hoa Binh toward Son La/Dien Bien):** big mountain scenery near the top of the country, overlapping the classic northern loops.
- **The karst middle (Khe Sanh to Phong Nha and beyond):** perhaps the best-loved stretch — jungle ridges, the old war roads, and the Phong Nha cave region at the end of it.
- **The central highlands (Kon Tum, Buon Ma Thuot, Dalat approach):** coffee country, long climbs and cool air.
- The southern end becomes busier and less dramatic as it funnels toward HCMC; most riders cut to the coast by Nha Trang or via Da Lat.

## Planning the ride

- **Bike:** the road is sealed and in good condition — a standard 110-155cc automatic or underbone is comfortable. The QLHCM is not an off-road route; the "trail" in its name is history, not surface.
- **Distances:** fuel and ATMs thin out for hours at a time; our rural fuel-and-coverage guide is directly relevant. Fill up before the empty stretches rather than on hope.
- **Weather:** the mountain spine is rideable in more months than the coast, but passes still fog over — our mountain-riding and low-visibility guides apply, and the national seasons guide explains the windows.
- **Time:** the full Hanoi-to-Saigon ride on the QLHCM is a 3-4 week project (see our full route guide); the classic sampling is one 4-7 day section — Phong Nha to Khe Sanh and Hue, or Hoa Binh to Son La — which most travellers fit inside a normal trip.

## Why the QLHCM instead of the QL1A?

Traffic, scenery and history, in that order. The QL1A is the nation's working artery — every truck uses it; our QL1A guide is honest about that ride's stress. The Ho Chi Minh Road is the choice when the riding itself is the destination. Many long itineraries use both: QLHCM through the mountains, drop to the coast for Hue and Hoi An, back to the mountains for the highlands, coast into Saigon.
