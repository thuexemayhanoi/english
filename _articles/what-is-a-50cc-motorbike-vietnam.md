A "50cc motorbike" in Vietnam is more than an engine size — it is a legal category with its own rules. Vietnamese law calls these vehicles xe gắn máy, usually translated as "moped", and the definition matters for who may ride one.

## The legal definition

Article 3 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines a moped as a two- or three-wheel engine-driven vehicle designed for a top speed of no more than 50 km/h, with an engine of 50 cc or less — or an electric motor of 4 kW or less. If a bike meets that definition, the licence rules are simple: no driving licence is required, and the rider must be at least 16 years old.

This is the single biggest reason 50cc bikes exist in Vietnam at all. A 16-year-old cannot legally ride a 110cc Honda Wave, but they can legally ride a sub-50cc moped to school. The same rule attracts some visitors who do not hold any licence valid for a motorbike — although a moped does not make the other obligations disappear, as our licence guide explains.

## What the category does not mean

"No licence needed" is not "no rules". Moped riders still follow every traffic rule: helmets, lane discipline, lights, sober riding. Under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, mopeds are fined at the same levels as motorbikes for the same violations.

It also does not mean weak or toy-like. A 50cc bike is a real petrol machine with a real engine, and it demands the same maintenance and the same respect from other traffic.

## Where 50cc bikes come from

There is a practical quirk: Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Vietnam do not currently list 50cc models in their official line-ups, so the 50cc bikes you see are mostly older domestically sold models, grey imports from Japan, or small Chinese-built machines. Our grey-import guide covers that market in detail.

## Engine size in practice

Engine displacement in cubic centimetres describes the volume the piston sweeps. Around 50 cc, an air-cooled single-cylinder engine produces only a few horsepower — enough for city commuting and short trips, not for fast dual-carriageway riding.
