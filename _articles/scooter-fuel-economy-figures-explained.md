Every Vietnamese scooter page leads with a fuel-economy figure - the Gear 125 Hybrid's 1.7 L/100km, the Vario 125's 2.11 - and every rider eventually discovers their own number is higher. Understanding why makes you a better buyer and a calmer owner.

## What the figure is

A manufacturer economy claim is measured under controlled conditions set by the maker. Honda, for instance, states the Vario 125's 5.5-litre tank is good for about 260 km "under normal operating conditions" per Honda's own wording; Yamaha publishes litres/100km figures for the Gear, Latte, Janus and FreeGo. Each figure is a standardised test outcome, not a promise about your commute.

## Why yours will be higher

Real-world consumption varies with load, speed, temperature, terrain and riding style - the honest caveat that belongs beside every claimed figure. Concretely in Hanoi: two-up riding, aggressive throttle from standstill at every light, high-speed ring-road running, luggage weight and idling in jams all push the number up. Expect a real-world figure meaningfully above the claim, and compare models by their claimed figures only against each other's claimed figures - the offset roughly cancels.
