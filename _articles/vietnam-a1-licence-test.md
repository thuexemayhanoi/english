Two exams stand between you and the [A1 licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}): a [computer-based theory paper]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}) and a practical circuit test. Their rules now sit in Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security, which phases in: a 40-question theory paper (36 to pass) from 1 March 2027, and a second practical test from 1 January 2028. Until the end of 28 February 2027, a sitting still follows parts of the earlier Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA.

## The test at a glance

Article 4 of Circular 108 builds the motorbike test from three parts taken in a fixed order: the theory paper, then practical test No. 1, then practical test No. 2 (Article 4(5)(a)). The frame is Article 61 of Law 36/2024/QH15 in its consolidated text: motorbike tests are held at a test centre or at a training yard that meets the standards (Article 61(3)), and the Minister of Public Security sets the form, content and procedure (Article 61(7)).

| Part | What you do | Scoring and pass mark | Applies from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theory paper | Answer 40 questions on a computer in 27 minutes | 1 point a question; 36 or more passes | 1 March 2027 |
| Practical test No. 1 | Ride four figures in the yard, starting with a figure of eight | Out of 100; 80 or more passes | 1 March 2027 |
| Practical test No. 2 | Ride two laps through 11 road situations in the yard | Out of 100; 80 or more passes | 1 January 2028 |
| Earlier procedure | Sit under the parts of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA that stay in force | Set by that circular's appendices | To the end of 28 February 2027 |

The [learner permit and training guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the course, and the [step-by-step route to the licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) shows where the test sits among the other steps.

## Which version applies on your sitting date

Circular 108 took effect on 1 July 2026 (Article 41(1)), but Article 41 holds back the parts a candidate meets:

- **To the end of 28 February 2027:** Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA stays in force for its Article 4(1) to (3), Appendix II and a few other appendices (Article 41(6)).
- **From 1 March 2027:** the theory paper and the yard test content of Appendix I, apart from test No. 2 (Article 41(2)).
- **From 1 January 2028:** the content of practical test No. 2, with the simulated road-situation figure, equipment and software of the yard (Article 41(2) and (3)).

The text gives no separate procedure for sittings between 1 March 2027 and the end of 2027, so ask the centre whether No. 1 alone completes the practical part then. A candidate who sat under the earlier circular and was absent or failed a part re-sits that part under it; from 1 March 2027 the re-sit follows Circular 108 (Article 42(2)).

## Sittings up to the end of February 2027

For a sitting before March 2027, the theory paper and the practical test follow the earlier rules that Article 41(6) keeps: both are set by Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security, and the pass marks are published in its annexes. Article 4 of that circular uses Appendix II for classes A1 and A, and its Article 4(2)(a) has the candidate ride four yard exercises: the figure of eight, the straight line, the road with barrier lines and the bumpy road.

The full text of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA read for this page does not reproduce Appendix II, the test procedure for these classes, so its question count, time and pass marks are not confirmed here. Ask the test centre which format applies on your sitting date. The portal article on the A1 and A procedure listed in the sources describes the earlier theory test as follows.

### The theory test

Twenty-five multiple-choice questions, answered on a computer, drawn from the national question bank. The pass mark for class A1 is 21 of 25 correct answers; class A requires 23 of 25. Dated December 2024 by its web address, the portal article gives these figures for the earlier test; no legal text read for this page confirms them, so check them with the test centre.

That article also describes critical questions, called "điểm liệt" in Vietnamese. Answer a single one of these wrongly and the theory paper is failed regardless of your total score.

## The theory paper from 1 March 2027

### How the paper runs

Appendix I, Part A, sets one computer paper for classes A1 and A together: 40 questions worth 1 point each and 27 minutes to answer. When you finish or time runs out, the computer scores every answer, including questions left blank.

Article 4(1)(a) lists the A1 topics: road traffic law, driving technique, the conduct rules for drivers, traffic culture and the harm done by alcohol and beer while travelling. Construction and routine repair, fire prevention and rescue skills are for class B1, B and above.

### The pass mark

A score of 36 or more out of 40 passes (Appendix I, Part A, section I, item 3.1), so at most four answers may be wrong or blank. Appendix I gives one pass mark for A1 and A together, not a separate mark for each class. It does not mention a question that fails the paper whatever the total, so ask the centre whether the question set keeps one.

### When the theory part changes or falls away

Two provisions change who sits what:

- **A car licence in force.** An A1 or A candidate who still holds a valid car licence skips the theory paper under Article 4(1)(c); Article 4(1)(a) of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA gives the same waiver, so it already applies before 1 March 2027. Neither text says whether a car licence issued abroad counts, and the practical tests are still sat. The [expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the wider route for foreign residents.

- **Ethnic minority candidates who cannot read or write Vietnamese.** Article 14 replaces the computer paper for class A1 candidates in this group with a paper question-and-answer test. Appendix I, Part A, section II, sets 30 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes and 27 correct answers to pass, with one examiner reading the questions, an interpreter translating and a second examiner marking a cross in the box for each answer. The interpreter may not choose the answer.

## The practical test

For A1 the practical part is the in-figure (trong hình) circuit test. Article 4(2)(a) has the candidate ride practical test No. 1 and then practical test No. 2, marked by an examiner directly together with automatic scoring equipment. Article 4(3) applies the road test to car, truck and bus classes only, so A1 and A candidates skip it. The examiner grades against a published manoeuvre list — a standardised course, not a road ride. Appendix I, Part B, is that list, and Article 5 requires the yard to meet the national technical regulation for test centres, including the figures.

You take over the test motorcycle, a helmet, elbow guards and knee guards. In test No. 1 only one candidate rides the figures at a time.

### Test No. 1: four figures

Appendix I, Part B, section III, sets the route:

1. Stop behind the start line with the left indicator on and wait for the examiner's signal.
2. Figure 1: switch the indicator off, turn right into the figure of eight, ride one lap and leave by its exit towards Figure 2.
3. Figure 2: ride over the straight line, switch on the right indicator and ride to Figure 3.
4. Figure 3: switch the right indicator off, ride over the road with barrier lines (vạch cản), switch on the left indicator and ride to Figure 4.
5. Figure 4: switch the left indicator off, ride over the bumpy road and finish.

You must keep to the sequence, keep the wheels off the boundary lines and barrier lines, finish within 3 minutes, avoid stalling, and ride with both hands on the handlebar and feet on the footrests or footboard.

### How test No. 1 is scored

The score starts at 100. Each of these faults costs 5 points: not adjusting the mirror before setting off; not switching an indicator on or off where required; a wheel on a boundary line or a barrier line; a foot touching the ground; stalling; a foot off its place or riding with one hand; and each 15 seconds beyond the 3 minutes. Most count every time they happen; the mirror check and the foot or one-hand fault count once.

Other faults end the test: not looking over both shoulders before starting; no helmet, or a strap fastened wrongly; the wrong sequence; both wheels outside the figure; the motorcycle falling; and not finishing. The pass mark is 80 of 100, so four deductions still leave 80 and a fifth takes the score to 75.

### Test No. 2: eleven road situations from 2028

From 1 January 2028 (Article 41(2)), section IV of Part B adds a second test: two laps of a course of 11 situations that need not come in a fixed order. They are the start; joining a main road from a side road or a road without priority; a right turn; a left turn; a red light; a U-turn; giving way to pedestrians crossing; overtaking; a hazard; a railway crossing; and the finish.

The appendix sets measurable standards. At the red light, the pedestrian situation and the railway crossing the front wheel must stop no more than 500 mm short of the stop line. In the hazard situation you must be at 20 km/h or more where braking starts and stop with the front wheel within 2000 mm of the line; overtaking must happen above 20 km/h.

Scoring follows test No. 1: 100 points and 80 to pass, with 5 points off each time for a missing or uncancelled indicator, a missed slowing, speed below the 20 km/h minimum where one applies, a stall or engine revving, a foot down while moving outside the permitted stops, and for each 30 seconds over 5 minutes. The test ends on no helmet, no elbow and knee guards, no shoulder check before starting, not stopping (or stopping past the marked position) at the junction, red light, pedestrians, hazard or railway crossing, a fall or a collision with an obstacle, the wrong sequence, or a wheel on a boundary line, which unlike in test No. 1 ends this test.

## If you fail

Fail either part and you re-sit that part. Failing the theory paper keeps you out of No. 1, and failing No. 1 keeps you out of No. 2 (Article 12(1)(b)). Article 12(1)(c) keeps a theory pass for one year from the day it was earned, and a No. 1 pass for whatever time remains on the theory pass.

You register again through the unit that takes dossiers under Article 9(1) (Article 12(1)(d) and (5)); the dossier adds the record of your earlier results (Article 16(2)(d)).

Bringing documents, a phone or a technical device into the theory room or onto the test motorcycle, or any other deception that distorts the result, gets you suspended and the result of the sitting cancelled (Article 12(4)). If the scoring equipment fails through no fault of the candidate, the result is cancelled and the candidate sits again.

## Practical preparation notes

Practice the same circuit your test centre uses. The fault lists for test No. 1 double as a checklist: set the mirror before moving off, look over both shoulders, fasten the helmet strap, switch indicators on and off at the marked places, keep your feet on the footrests and ride with both hands on the handlebar. For the paper from 1 March 2027 a blank question earns no point, so answer every question before the 27 minutes end.

## Foreign candidates and the language of the test

Foreigners should note again: the exam is in Vietnamese only, which is how the format reads rather than a stated rule, because no article of Circular 108 states the language of the test in as many words. Candidates who cannot read the language reliably usually route through a licence exchange instead of the fresh-test path. Article 18(7) of Circular 108 sends a foreign licence holder who needs to drive in Vietnam to the exchange, with a treaty exception. The [exchange procedure]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) shows who qualifies.

The question of [whether the test can be taken in English]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}) has its own guide.

## Common questions

### How many questions are on the A1 theory test?

For sittings from 1 March 2027, Appendix I sets 40 questions in 27 minutes. Before that date the earlier appendix applies, so confirm the count with the test centre for your sitting date.

### Does putting a foot down fail test No. 1?

Not by itself under Appendix I (from 1 March 2027): a foot on the ground costs 5 points each time, and the pass mark is 80 of 100. The test ends on the faults that Appendix I lists as ending it, such as a helmet strap fastened wrongly or a fall.

### Is the test the same for class A?

Appendix I covers A1 and A together, with the same paper, figures and pass marks. The classes differ in what they let you ride, which the [guide to what an A1 licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) sets out.

### Does a moped need this test?

The test belongs to the licence classes. Article 59(1)(a) lets a person aged 16 or over ride a moped, while class A1 needs 18 (Article 59(1)(b)); the [50cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the moped rule.

### What happens after I pass?

Article 12(3) gives the Traffic Police Division 2 hours after the sitting ends to review the results and send the data of those who passed to the Traffic Police Department, so the electronic licence can be issued.
