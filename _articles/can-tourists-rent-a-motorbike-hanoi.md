Tourists rent motorbikes in Hanoi constantly. Whether a tourist can ride one legally is a different question with a clean answer.

## Renting as a tourist: no barriers

There is no tourist-specific restriction on renting. A passport, a cash deposit (typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND) and the rental price get you a bike. No visa type, return ticket or hotel booking is checked. The transaction is commercial; Vietnam treats renting and riding as separate matters.

## Riding as a tourist: the licence rule

The traffic law does not care that you are a tourist. What counts is your licence:

- Home motorcycle licence + 1968-convention IDP: legal for the classes the licence covers.
- Vietnamese licence: legal for its classes.
- Nothing, or an IDP alone, or a non-1968 permit: illegal on anything above 50 cc. The fine for riding unlicensed is 2-4 million VND up to 125 cc, 6-8 million above, and police can hold the bike.
- 50 cc or less: no driving licence required, but the rider must be 16 or older.

That last line is the honest tourist option: a licence-free 50 cc bike or an e-bike keeps a visitor with no motorcycle licence fully within the law at city speeds.

## The insurance point

Compulsory third-party insurance attaches to the vehicle and is carried by the owner. Rental shops do not insure the rider - injuries to yourself and damage you cause beyond the vehicle's cover are your own exposure. Travel insurance that covers motorbike riding is worth checking before you fly; many policies exclude it or exclude unlicensed riding.

## What tourists should ask the shop

Confirm the model is available on your dates, confirm the deposit and the return conditions, and make sure the registration certificate rides with the bike - unable to produce papers at a stop, the bike can be held until they are shown. Photograph the bike at handover.
