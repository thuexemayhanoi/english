Camshaft count is the quiet spec that shapes an engine's character. Vietnam's underbones split into SOHC and DOHC camps, and the manufacturers name their layouts (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## Who uses what (verified)

DOHC: Honda's CBR150R page lists a DOHC four-valve liquid-cooled engine - the most sophisticated valvetrain in the class. SOHC: Yamaha lists single-overhead-cam engines for the Sirius RC 110 and the Exciter 155 VVA (the Exciter with four valves under that single cam). Honda's Wave/Blade/RSX/Future/Cub/CT/Winner pages publish valve counts without cam-layout claims - those cells stay empty in our model database, as no claim should be invented.

## What the difference means

A camshaft pushes the valves that breathe the engine. SOHC uses one cam per cylinder, operating valves often via rockers - simpler, cheaper, compact, and entirely sufficient at underbone power levels. DOHC uses two cams, allowing better valve angle, direct actuation and high-rpm breathing - which is why the CBR150R safely spins out 12.6 kW at 9,000 rpm, the highest specific output in this comparison.

## Does it matter to you?

As a rider: DOHC buys top-end performance and costs complexity at service time. As an owner: SOHC underbones are the cheaper long-term proposition - fewer components, simpler clearances. Our valve-clearance guide covers the maintenance both layouts share. For 99 percent of Vietnamese riding, the SOHC 155 of the Exciter - with its VVA variable timing - delivers more usable performance than the layout debate suggests.
