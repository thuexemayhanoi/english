Train Street — the section of Phung Hung where houses stand a metre or two from the live railway line — became Hanoi's most photographed street almost overnight, and the attention brought restrictions. If you ride there in 2026, the visit works differently from the early Instagram years: the street is open to independent visitors, but with access rules that vary by section, and cafe patronage has become the practical way past the barriers.

## Where it is and how to ride there

Train Street runs behind Phung Hung street in the Old Quarter's northwest corner, parallel to it. The classic approach  is from Hoan Kiem: west along the old streets, then north — the whole ride is a few minutes, though narrow and busy. This is dense Old Quarter riding: one-way lanes, loading bikes, pedestrians everywhere. Ride it slow, lights on, and expect to stop.

## The access situation

The current rules reflect railway-safety enforcement that tightened after crowds and incidents on the tracks. Security checkpoints and barriers sit at the main entrances, most visibly on the Phung Hung side. The working method for visitors is simple: the cafes along the lane act as the entry points — buying a coffee or a drink is how you legitimately sit by the tracks, and it is the only way past the barriers at the restricted sections. The rules and enforcement level vary along the street and change over time, so check the current situation on the day rather than trusting any single post.

## What not to do

Do not step onto the tracks, ever — this is a live line, and enforcement exists because people did. Do not park in the lane itself. Do not ride into the narrow residential sections looking for a photo; the lane is walking space.

## Parking

This is the real rider question, because Train Street has no parking of its own. The workable pattern: park at an attended lot or cafe kerb on the Hoan Kiem side or along Phung Hung's parallel streets, and walk in. Expect to pay a few thousand dong for attended kerbside parking; the conventions are in the general parking guide. Do not leave the bike unattended on a random kerb in this neighbourhood — it is one of the busiest pedestrian zones in the city.

## Making the visit work

Go in the hour before a train is due — the cafes know the schedule — and go in the golden light if you want photographs. Trains pass a couple of times a day in each direction; the timing is not a secret and the cafes post it. Combine the visit with the wider Old Quarter loop, and keep the ride slow: this corner of the city rewards walking pace.
