European emissions labels appear across Vietnamese model pages, and they signal more than environmental intent - they mark where a bike sits in Honda and Yamaha's technology generations. Only manufacturer-published claims below (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## What the manufacturers actually publish

Honda's Blade page lists Euro 3. Honda's SH Mode 125 page lists EURO 4 (a scooter, but the clearest label in the range). Most underbone pages - Wave Alpha, Wave RSX, Future 125, Super Cub, CT125, Winner R, CBR150R - publish no emissions-class claim at all on the retrieved pages, and no claim should be invented for them. Yamaha's Sirius and Exciter pages likewise publish no emissions class.

## Why labels lag the market

Euro 3 machines are typically earlier-engine-generation products still on sale because they remain cheap, simple and demand-proven - the Blade being the honest example. Euro 4 generally implies injection, tighter fuelling control and updated engine management, which is part of why the modern injected machines (like the Wave RSX with PGM-FI) post better economy figures.

## What it means for a buyer

Emissions class is a proxy, not a verdict: a Euro 3 Blade still runs reliably and cheaply, while an unlabelled modern bike may be cleaner still.
