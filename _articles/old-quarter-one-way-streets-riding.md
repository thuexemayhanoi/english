The Old Quarter's "36 streets" — the ancient guild streets with their Hang-names — form the densest, most one-way-warped grid a rider will meet in Vietnam. Riding it well is not about speed; it is about understanding a system that predates the motorbike by centuries and has never fully agreed to accommodate it.

## How the system works

The Old Quarter is built on a diagonal grid: a set of streets running roughly toward the river, crossed by another set running roughly with it. Most of the narrower streets are one-way, and the directions form a rough alternation — if a street refuses you, its neighbour one block over usually flows your way. The classic spine streets (Hang Gai–Hang Bai, Hang Dao–Hang Duong and the others toward Dong Xuan
 
market) carry the through-traffic; the cross streets feed them.

The one-way logic is signed, but weakly: look for the arrow plates at junction mouths and, more reliably, at the direction the parked bikes face — parked motorbikes are Hanoi's most reliable signage. If the parked bikes on both sides face you, you are almost certainly wrong-way, which is both illegal and the most common tourist mistake in the district. The fine for wrong-way riding is its own article; here the point is avoidance.

## Riding strategy

Ride the grid, not the destination. In the Old Quarter, a straight-line approach to a specific address usually fails; the working method is to flow with the street you are on until a legal turn appears, then accept the detour. Local riders plan a two-step approach — get within a block, then circle. Loops of two or three streets are normal here; fighting the system is not.

Speed is walking-pace-plus. The streets mix pedestrians, shop goods, food stalls, delivery bikes and tourists walking four-abreast. Use the horn as information, not aggression — a light tap announces you to people facing away. The horn etiquette guide covers the cultural side.

## Getting stuck and unstuck

You will end up going the wrong way sometimes anyway — everyone does. The graceful fix is the immediate, slow, safe exit: first side gap or kerbside, dismount if necessary, and walk the bike a few metres. Never reverse into moving traffic and never push on against a wall of oncoming bikes.

## The reward

The maze is the attraction. Ridden at the right speed — early morning, lights on, patient — the Old Quarter is the best urban riding in Asia: temple gates, silk shops, bread vendors, all at arm's length. Ride it to get lost on purpose, and use the main spines when you actually need to be somewhere.
