A brake that will not let go is stranger than one that will not bite: the bike feels sluggish, the wheel drags, the rim stays hot after a short ride, and fuel economy quietly worsens. Sticking brakes are common in Vietnam's humidity because disc brakes expose their sliding parts to exactly the wet, gritty conditions that seize them.

## Symptoms: how a drag announces itself

- The bike creeps forward or decelerates sharply with the throttle closed.
- One wheel or disc is noticeably hot after a normal ride — careful, a badly dragging disc can burn skin; hover a hand, do not touch.
- The bike pulls to one side under gentle braking.
- The brake lever or pedal feels the first bite almost immediately, as if the pads never backed off.
- A smell of hot friction material after uneventful riding.

## Why calipers stick

A disc caliper must both squeeze and release. It slides on pins or slides so both pads can float off the disc, and a piston retracts a fraction of a millimetre when pressure drops. Sticking happens when:

- **Sliding pins are dry or corroded.** The pins need clean, grease-lubricated bores; once corroded or packed with gritty sludge, the caliper stops floating and one pad drags constantly. This is the most common and most fixable cause.
- **The piston is gummed or corroded.** Dust and moisture past a torn dust seal rust the piston, and it stops retracting.
- **The pads are seized in the caliper bracket.** Pads must slide microscopically in their seats; rust-welded pad ears hold them against the disc forever.
- **The lever-side is mechanical, not hydraulic.** a bent bracket or a corroded return spring on drum brakes or the rear pedal linkage. Dragging brakes can also originate from cable and linkage problems on the rear.

## The cleaning job

On many bikes the pads can come out and the pins can be withdrawn with basic hand tools: clean the pins and bores with brake cleaner and a nylon brush, inspect the pin rubber boots, regrease with high-temperature brake grease only (not engine oil, not chain lube — both attack rubber), clean the pad seats to bright metal, and push the piston back gently with a spreader or a flat lever against the old pads. If the piston resists retreating or the dust boot is torn, that caliper needs a mechanic or a rebuild kit — see brake-pads-replacement-motorbike-vietnam for the pad-side procedure.

## Safety lines you do not cross

Never disconnect hydraulic lines for a cleaning job you can do with them attached. Never use petrol or engine degreaser on rubber seals. Never ride with pads that have been cooked blue by constant dragging — glazed, heat-faded pads do not recover; replace them, and inspect the disc for the same treatment per inspecting-brake-discs-motorbike.

## Prevention

Wash-season grit and flooding are the accelerators; the rainy-season routine in motorbike-maintenance-rainy-season covers the general habits. The single best preventive is a monthly wheel spin with the bike on its stand: each wheel should turn freely and stop on its own weight.
