Northern Vietnam has four riding seasons, and they are not equal. The city commuting calendar (see the Hanoi riding-seasons guide) matters less out here — what matters is the monsoon, the mountain cold, and the two golden windows everyone plans around.

## Spring (March–April)

The first of the two prime windows. The rainy season has finished, skies are clear, temperatures are moderate, and the northern mountains — Ha Giang, Cao Ban
g, the n
orthwest loops — are at their most rideable. Expect the odd drizzle and cool mornings in the highlands. This is when long-distance riders do the big loops.

## Summer (May–August)

Hot and increasingly wet — the monsoon builds from May, and July–August afternoons bring thunderstorms that can close mountain roads with water, mud and rockfall. Riding is still possible, and the terraces of Mu Cang Chai are green from July, but the discipline is strict: ride mornings, shelter early afternoons, never gamble with storm fronts on passes. Delta day trips (pagodas, craft villages, lakes) are the better summer plan, with swimming stops built in.

## Autumn (September–early November)

The second prime window, and for scenery the best one. Rice harvests in the northern uplands run from September into October — the Mu Cang Chai terraces turn gold, Ha Giang dries out under clear skies, and the first half of November still rides well before real cold arrives. Book accommodation ahead in the terraced districts; this is everyone’s season.

## Winter (December–February)

Dry but cold, and in the mountains genuinely cold — the high passes around Ha Giang and Sapa can hover near freezing, with ice and fog. The winter trade is simple: ride the lowlands and the near hills (the Perfume Pagoda pilgrimage season opens, Ninh Binh, the craft villages) and keep the high loops for another season. Around Tet, the whole country moves — see the Tet traffic guide before planning anything in the lunar new year fortnight.

## Quick planning rules

- **Best long-distance windows:** March–April and September–early November.
- **Terrace gold in the northwest:** September–October.
- **Avoid high passes:** rainy-season afternoons (June–September) and icy mornings (December–January).
- **Fog risk on any mountain ride:** year-round — always check mountain weather, not the city forecast.
