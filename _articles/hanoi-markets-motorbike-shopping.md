Hanoi's shopping geography falls into three layers — the wet markets, the specialist trade streets and the shopping malls — and each one has its own motorbike logic. Learning all three is a genuine riding skill, because the difference between them is mostly the difference between parking options.

## The wet markets

The neighbourhood wet markets — one per district, always busy before 09:00 — are where the city buys food: vegetables on the ground, meat on hooks, fish in basins, and aisles built for pedestrians carrying baskets. On a motorbike, the wet-market drill is fixed: park at the market's bike area or the attendant kerb at the nearest corner, shop on foot with a backpack or the bike's under-seat box, and make two trips rather than one overloading gamble. Wet markets run all day but thin by mid-afternoon; go in the morning for the full spectacle and the best produce.

## The trade streets

Hanoi's old guild logic survives in the specialist trade streets: fabric streets, hardware streets, the lighting quarter, the toy quarter, the packaging streets. Shopping here means street-level browsing on roads with heavy bike flow. The local method: ride the street once at low speed to see who has what, then park at an attendant kerb for the actual buying. For anything long or awkward — a pipe, a shelf board — the cargo-carrying guide matters more than usual: strap it flat along the rack, flag the ends with a cloth, and take the side streets home.

## The malls

The malls and big-box stores are the easy layer: every one has a large attended motorbike garage, and the garage-to-escalator run is the safest cargo flow in the city. This is where the weekly shop belongs: park in the basement, buy the heavy things, and let the garage attendants help you load. For big hauls, a top box or a rear rack turns the trip from risky to trivial.

## Carrying it home

The principles live in the luggage and cargo guides, and the market context adds three of its own. Keep raw meat separate from everything else — a sealed bag in the under-seat storage, never loose. Do not hang bags from the handlebars; weight on the bars is a steering problem at exactly the moment a market street needs your steering. And resist the one-big-shop temptation: two light trips beat one overloaded one every time.

## A weekly pattern

Many expat riders settle into a rhythm: wet market for produce two or three mornings a week, trade streets for household needs, a mall run monthly.
