50cc motorbike rental in Hanoi is a niche request, and it deserves a straight answer about both the law and the market. The [50cc guides]({{ '/topics/50cc/' | relative_url }}) cover the class in depth; this one is about renting. The short version: a true 50cc moped needs no driving licence, 50cc availability and price are quoted on enquiry only, and a rider who holds a valid licence will usually be better served by a standard automatic.

## Why renters ask for the class

The usual reasons are legal and practical: a rider who is 16 or 17, for whom a moped is the only legal motorised option; or a visitor with no licence valid for Vietnamese motorbikes, for whom the licence-free 50cc class is the only fully legal self-ride option. Both reasons are real — [can foreigners ride a 50cc without a licence?]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-ride-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the exact position.

### Who realistically asks

- **A 16- or 17-year-old.** A shop's own conditions for young renters are something to ask about, not assume; the age rules are in the legal section below.

- **A visitor with no valid licence.** The pedal-assist e-bike is the other licence-free route, compared in [50cc petrol bike vs e-bike]({{ '/articles/50cc-vs-e-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

- **A short-stay rider with modest plans.** Short hops inside the older central districts may suit a small bike; [is a 50cc enough for Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/is-a-50cc-enough-for-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) draws the line between those streets and the rest of the city.

- **A licensed beginner who wants the smallest bike.** The better question is which bike is simplest to ride, and an automatic scooter has no gears to manage.

## What the law says about the class

Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines a moped (xe gắn máy) as a two- or three-wheel engine-driven vehicle designed and built for road use, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. If the engine is a combustion engine, its displacement must not exceed 50 cm3; if it is an electric motor, its power must not exceed 4 kW. The definition expressly excludes the motorised pedal bike (xe đạp máy).

Article 59(1)(a) allows anyone aged 16 or over to ride a moped, and the site's [licence guide for 50cc bikes]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out why no driving licence is required for the class.

Article 33(2) requires riders and passengers on motorcycles and mopeds to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the chin strap fastened. The guide to [helmet rules for 50cc mopeds]({{ '/articles/helmet-rules-50cc-mopeds/' | relative_url }}) covers the duty, and the [50cc law myths guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-motorbike-myths-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains that mopeds are fined at the same levels as motorbikes (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 7).

The same law lets people aged 18 or over be granted licences such as A1 (Article 59(1)(b)), which is the step needed for a 110cc bike; the [A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) lists what it covers. A 16- or 17-year-old therefore cannot legally ride a standard rental scooter, however experienced.

*Information, not legal advice. Articles 33(2), 34(1)(g) and 59(1)(a)-(b) checked against the full text of Law 36/2024/QH15 on 2026-10-25. The Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP reference is taken from the site's verified myths guide and was not re-read here.*

## Why fleets rarely specialise in 50s

Rental fleets run on mainstream 110–125cc machines with known parts and support, so they rarely hold sub-50cc stock. The bikes a typical Hanoi rental shop offers are 110–125cc scooters and underbones, all within the A1 class. Where a 50cc is offered it is usually a single informal-market bike rather than a fleet class. That means one practical rule above all others: **confirm availability before planning around it.** [Contact us]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) to confirm current availability of 50cc bikes — and their rental price, which is quoted directly, never published as a fixed rate for this class.

The practical effect for a renter is that each bike is an individual. Its age, papers and condition can differ far more than two scooters from the same model line, so judge the bike in front of you rather than the class it belongs to.

## What to check before riding off

The standard rental checks apply doubly to a small, often older machine, because age is where 50s fail. The two-minute version of the mechanical checks lives in the [50cc pre-ride checklist]({{ '/articles/50cc-pre-ride-checklist/' | relative_url }}), and the paperwork is described in our first-time [rental guide]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). The points below add what is specific to renting a 50cc, and, as the legal section above explains, a helmet for every rider is part of the list.

### Brakes first

Roll the bike forward a few metres and stop with the front brake alone, then with the rear alone. Each should slow the bike in a straight line without grabbing, juddering or needing the lever pulled almost to the grip. A bike that pulls to one side under braking is a reason to ask for another bike rather than a reason to adapt.

### Tyres and wheels

Rubber ages as well as wears, so a tyre with tread left can still be cracked. Spin each wheel and watch for a wobble. Ask in advance who pays if a tyre or inner tube fails: Nguyen Tu's policy is that consumables such as inner tubes or tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so settle it before you ride, not after a puncture.

### Lights, horn and electrics

Check the low and high beam, the brake light under each lever, the indicators and the horn. The electrics are often the first system to show age on a small bike. Confirm that both mirrors are present and adjustable.

### Starting, idling and sounds

Start the engine from cold if you can. It should catch without a long struggle, settle into a steady idle and return to idle when you release the throttle. Look under the bike for fresh leaks and listen for knocking or rattling. Persistent exhaust smoke or a throttle that sticks open are faults to report before leaving, not quirks to live with.

### Papers, and the question of whether it really is a 50

Ask to see the registration certificate and keep a photo of it. Pay particular attention to any claim that a larger-engined bike has been "limited" to behave like a 50cc. The legal definition above turns on the engine's displacement and the vehicle's design, so a seller's description of a limiter is a sales statement and does not change what the bike is. If the shop cannot say what the engine size is and how it knows, treat that as a warning sign.

### Helmet and photographs

Ask whether a helmet comes with the bike, and check the shell, the strap and the fit before leaving. Then photograph the bike from several angles, including any existing scratches, and note any faults on the contract.

## Deposit and responsibilities

Normal rental practice applies: a deposit against the bike, your responsibility for the machine's condition, and insurance duties that follow the vehicle — the renting basics are covered in [what documents you need to rent a motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### The deposit

A typical deposit is 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, depending on the vehicle and conditions. The figure for a particular 50cc is agreed when you enquire, because the class has no published rate. The [rental deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains what a deposit covers and how to protect it. Payment is by cash, bank transfer or QR payment.

### Late and early return

A late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour. If you are more than six hours late, a full additional rental day may be charged. Early return carries no automatic refund entitlement, so agree the return date and time realistically when you book.

### Insurance and support

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own cover. Compulsory civil-liability insurance attaches to the vehicle, and the [50cc insurance guide]({{ '/articles/do-you-need-insurance-for-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the rule and the fine for being unable to produce a valid certificate.

Business maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks and a normal inspection. Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so plan any rental, including a 50cc, as a city bike.

### If you are... choose...

- **Aged 16 or 17:** a moped or a licence-free electric option only; no standard rental scooter is open to you.
- **Aged 16 or over with no licence valid in Vietnam:** a 50cc if one is available, or an e-bike once you have confirmed its rated power and class.

## Common questions

### Can I rent a 50cc in Hanoi without a driving licence?

The law requires no driving licence for a true moped, and the rider must be at least 16. A shop's own conditions are confirmed when you enquire.

### Does Nguyen Tu have 50cc bikes, and what do they cost?

Availability is a contact-first question, so ask for your dates before planning around a 50cc. There is no fixed or published price for 50cc motorcycles; the rate is quoted directly.

### How old do I need to be?

Sixteen for a moped, and 18 for the A1 licence that a 110cc bike needs; see the [A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}).

### Are helmets and insurance still needed on a 50cc?

Yes to both: the helmet duty is in the legal section above, and the [50cc insurance guide]({{ '/articles/do-you-need-insurance-for-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own cover.

### Is a 50cc enough for Hanoi's bridges?

It is workable for short, flat, central routes but marginal on long bridge ramps, especially with a passenger. The decision rule is in the traffic section above.
