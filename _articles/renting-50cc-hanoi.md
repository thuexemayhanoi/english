50cc motorbike rental in Hanoi is a niche request, and it deserves a straight answer about both the law and the market. The [50cc guides]({{ '/topics/50cc/' | relative_url }}) cover the class in depth; this one is about renting. The short version: a true 50cc moped needs no driving licence, 50cc availability and price are quoted on enquiry only, and a rider who holds a valid licence will usually be better served by a standard automatic.

## Why renters ask for the class

The usual reasons are legal and practical: a rider who is 16 or 17, for whom a moped is the only legal motorised option; or a visitor with no licence valid for Vietnamese motorbikes, for whom the licence-free 50cc class is the only fully legal self-ride option. Both reasons are real — [can foreigners ride a 50cc without a licence?]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-ride-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the exact position.

### Who realistically asks

- **A 16- or 17-year-old.** Sixteen is the minimum age for a moped, and a 110cc bike is not open until 18 (see the legal section below). Any shop's own conditions for young renters are something to ask about, not assume.
- **A visitor with no valid licence.** Without a licence Vietnam recognises, the 50cc moped class is the only petrol option the law leaves open. The pedal-assist e-bike is the other licence-free route, compared in [50cc petrol bike vs e-bike]({{ '/articles/50cc-vs-e-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).
- **A short-stay rider with modest plans.** Someone who only wants short hops inside the older central districts may find a small bike enough; [is a 50cc enough for Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/is-a-50cc-enough-for-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) draws the line between those streets and the rest of the city.
- **A licensed beginner who wants the smallest bike.** The better question for a nervous beginner is which bike is simplest to ride, and an automatic scooter has no gears to manage. The [first-time rental guide]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains why it recommends an automatic.

A moped does not make the other duties disappear: the [guide for tourists riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains why insurance and accident questions remain whatever the engine size.

## What the law says about the class

Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines a moped (xe gắn máy) as a two- or three-wheel engine-driven vehicle designed and built for road use, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. If the engine is a combustion engine, its displacement must not exceed 50 cm3; if it is an electric motor, its power must not exceed 4 kW. The definition expressly excludes the motorised pedal bike (xe đạp máy).

Article 59(1)(a) allows anyone aged 16 or over to ride a moped, and the site's [licence guide for 50cc bikes]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out why no driving licence is required for the class. Helmets are compulsory for moped riders and passengers (Article 33(2)), at the same fine levels as motorbikes, as the [moped helmet guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-rules-50cc-mopeds/' | relative_url }}) explains.

The same law lets people aged 18 or over be granted licences such as A1 (Article 59(1)(b)), which is the step needed for a 110cc bike. A 16- or 17-year-old therefore cannot legally ride a standard rental scooter, however experienced.

*Information, not legal advice. Article 34(1)(g) and Article 59 checked against the full text of Law 36/2024/QH15 on 2026-10-25.*

## Why fleets rarely specialise in 50s

Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Vietnam do not list 50cc models in their official line-ups, so rental fleets, which run on mainstream 110–125cc machines with known parts and support, rarely hold sub-50cc stock. Where a 50cc is offered it is usually a single informal-market bike rather than a fleet class. That means one practical rule above all others: **confirm availability before planning around it.** [Contact us]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) to confirm current availability of 50cc bikes — and their rental price, which is quoted directly, never published as a fixed rate for this class.

The 50cc bikes that do circulate in Vietnam are mostly older domestic models, grey imports or small imported machines, which is covered in the [grey-import 50cc guide]({{ '/articles/grey-import-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). The practical effect for a renter is that each bike is an individual: its age, papers and condition can differ far more than two scooters from the same model line. Judge the bike in front of you rather than the class it belongs to.

## What to check on a 50cc before riding off

The standard handover checks apply to any rental. They matter more on a small bike that may be old and individually sourced, so give each of these a deliberate minute rather than a glance.

### Brakes first

Roll the bike forward a few metres and stop with the front brake alone, then with the rear alone. Each should slow the bike in a straight line without grabbing, juddering or needing the lever pulled almost to the grip. A bike that pulls to one side under braking, or whose lever feels different from one try to the next, is a reason to ask for another bike rather than a reason to adapt.

### Tyres and wheels

Look for visible tread, cracking in the sidewall and embedded glass or nails. Rubber ages as well as wears, so a tyre with tread left can still be cracked. Spin each wheel and watch for a wobble. Ask in advance who pays if a tyre or inner tube fails: the owner's approved policy is that consumables such as inner tubes or tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so settle it before you ride, not after a puncture.

### Lights, horn and electrics

Check the headlight on low and high beam, the taillight, the brake light under each lever, the indicators and the horn. Age is where small bikes tend to fail, and the electrics are often the first system to show it. Also confirm both mirrors are present and adjustable.

### Starting, idling and sounds

Start the engine from cold if you can. It should catch without a long struggle, settle into a steady idle and return to idle when you release the throttle. Look under the bike for fresh leaks and listen for knocking or rattling. Persistent smoke from the exhaust or a throttle that sticks open are faults to report before leaving, not quirks to live with.

### Papers, and the question of whether it really is a 50

Ask to see the registration certificate and keep a photo of it. The [documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains why registration papers should travel with the bike.

Pay particular attention to any claim that a larger-engined bike has been "limited" to behave like a 50cc. The legal definition above turns on the engine's displacement and the vehicle's design, so a seller's description of a limiter is a sales statement and does not change what the bike is. If the shop cannot say what the engine size of the bike is and how it knows, treat that as a warning sign.

### Helmet and photographs

Ask whether a helmet comes with the bike, and check the shell, the strap and the fit before leaving. Then photograph the bike from several angles, including any existing scratches, and note any faults on the contract. The [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) covers the general walk-around, and the [50cc pre-ride checklist]({{ '/articles/50cc-pre-ride-checklist/' | relative_url }}) is the two-minute routine to repeat before each ride.

## Documents, deposit and insurance

The rental paperwork described in our first-time [rental guide]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) still applies: for a moped no driving licence is required, but the booking and contract work as they do for any bike. ### The deposit

The owner's typical deposit range is 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, depending on the vehicle and conditions. The figure for a particular 50cc is agreed when you enquire, because the class has no published rate. The [rental deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains what a deposit covers and how to protect it. Payment is by cash, bank transfer or QR payment.

### Late and early return

A late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour. If you are more than six hours late, a full additional rental day may be charged. Early return carries no automatic refund entitlement, so agree the return date and time realistically when you book.

### Insurance and support

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own cover. Separately, compulsory civil-liability insurance attaches to the vehicle, and the [50cc insurance guide]({{ '/articles/do-you-need-insurance-for-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the rule and the fine for being unable to produce a valid certificate. Ask what insurance papers exist for the specific bike before you leave, and arrange your own cover for the rest.

Business maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks and a normal inspection. Support is aimed at Hanoi and local use, so a 50cc is a city bike on this basis, not one for a long loop out of town.

### If you are... choose...

- **Aged 16 or 17:** a moped or a licence-free electric option only; no standard rental scooter is open to you.
- **Over 16 with no licence valid in Vietnam:** a 50cc if one is available, or an e-bike once you have confirmed its rated power and class. The [e-bike comparison]({{ '/articles/50cc-vs-e-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the trade-offs.
- **Holding a valid licence:** an automatic scooter, because there are no gears to manage while you learn how the city moves. The semi-automatic Wave and Sirius add a foot shift that first-timers must think about on day one.
- **Staying a short time, riding central streets only:** either class can work, which makes price and availability the deciding factors.

## Common questions

### Can I rent a 50cc in Hanoi without a driving licence?

The law requires no driving licence for a true moped, and the rider must be at least 16. Whether a 50cc is available for your dates is a contact-first question, and any shop's own conditions are confirmed when you enquire.

### Does Nguyen Tu have 50cc bikes, and what do they cost?

Availability is contact only, and nothing is guaranteed in advance. There is no fixed or published price for 50cc motorcycles; the rate is quoted directly.

### How old do I need to be?

The law sets a minimum age of 16 for riding a moped. A 16- or 17-year-old cannot legally ride a 110cc bike, as the licence for it starts at 18.

### Are helmets and insurance still needed on a 50cc?

Yes. Helmets are compulsory for moped riders and passengers, and compulsory civil-liability insurance attaches to the vehicle. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own cover.

### Is a 50cc enough for Hanoi's bridges?

It is workable for short, flat, central routes but marginal on long bridge ramps, especially with a passenger. The decision rule is in the traffic section above.
