Short answer: leaving the scene of a crash you were in is one of the heaviest riding violations in the administrative schedule. Article 7(9)(c) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP prices failing to stop, failing to preserve the scene, failing to help the injured, or failing to report — 8,000,000–10,000,000 VND, six points off the licence, and the bike subject to being held. Where the accident caused injury or death, the same conduct becomes a criminal matter, not a fine matter.

## What the law demands at the scene

Article 80 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) sets the sequence every rider is inside from the moment of impact: stop immediately, keep the scene as it is, help anyone injured, and report to the nearest police or people's committee. The article also protects the helper — a rider who takes the injured to hospital and returns to the scene has still met the duty. The decree then prices the two ways of failing it: a mild version and a severe one.

The mild version (Article 7(5)(a)) catches riders involved in a crash who fail to stop or preserve the scene where no serious consequence followed — 1,000,000–2,000,000 VND with two points. The severe version (Article 7(9)(c)) is the full abandonment: not stopping, not staying, not helping, not reporting — 8,000,000–10,000,000 VND, six points, and the vehicle can be held on the spot under the decree's holding rules.

## Why fleeing converts the case

The administrative fine is the ceiling of what leaving the scene costs when nobody was much hurt. But Vietnamese criminal law treats fleeing after a crash that caused damage as an aggravating, sometimes defining, element of the road-traffic offences — and in the worst cases, leaving an injured person unhelped is punished as its own wrong, beyond the driving violation. Practically: the accident that would have been a 10–14 million VND accident-band fine if you stayed becomes a criminal file if you ride away. Insurance moves the same direction — a compulsory third-party policy expects the rider to have reported; a rider who vanishes from the scene of their own crash finds the insurer's obligations narrowing fast.

## The tourist version of this mistake

The common scenario: a clip in traffic, both bikes upright, some scratches, the tourist's instinct is to wave apologetically and ride on. The other rider's instinct — sometimes — is to call the police and report a hit-and-run. A small, cheap incident becomes an 8-figure case with an impounded passport in the middle. The rule that works in Hanoi: if it touched, stop; if anyone is hurt or the machines are down, call the police (113); photograph everything before the bikes move; and never let a bystander's "không sao, đi đi" decide your legal position for you.
