Choosing a bike for a month is a different decision from choosing one for a day. Small annoyances do not have time to matter in 24 hours; over thirty days they define the rental.

The short answer: an automatic scooter suits a rider who wants storage and no gear changes, and a semi-automatic underbone suits a rider who wants a simpler machine and does not mind changing gear with a foot lever. What decides the month is everything around that choice: your route, your body, what you carry, the weather, where the bike sleeps at night and how far you ride. There is no single best motorbike for a monthly rental in Hanoi; the best one fits your route, body, storage needs and mileage, and the table below sets the verified manufacturer figures against each need. The sections below take those in turn, with manufacturer figures where a figure has been verified and plain advice where it has not.

## Start with the route, then the body

What does your average day look like — a 5 km commute, a 20 km cross-city loop, weekend trips? High daily mileage points toward comfort and a bike that holds up at speed; short urban hops point toward something light and easy to park. Then fit: seat height, reach, and whether you carry a passenger. Tall riders have different needs again — [Best Motorbikes for Tall Foreign Riders in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/best-motorbikes-for-tall-riders-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers that case.

### What your route asks of the bike

Think about the worst ten minutes of your day rather than the average. If those minutes are traffic lights and narrow lanes, the bike that is easy to hold at walking pace and easy to turn in a small space is the one you will still like once the novelty has gone. If they are a long stretch at speed, a seat and a riding position you can hold for the whole trip count for more than agility.

Stop-start traffic is also where an automatic and a semi-automatic feel most different. On the Vision, Honda's page describes an idling stop: the engine cuts after more than three seconds stationary and restarts on a twist of the throttle. Whether you like that in a queue is personal, so ask whether the bike you are given has the feature and try the restart on a quiet stretch before you depend on it.

### What your body asks of the bike

Seat height is an easy number to look up, and the manufacturer pages show how little separates typical models: 785 mm on the Honda Vision and 770 mm on the Honda Wave Alpha 110, both from Honda Vietnam's own pages. A gap of 15 mm is not a reason to choose either. Sit on the bike in the shoes you will ride in, put both feet down, then wheel it backwards for a couple of metres. If that takes effort, it will take effort every time you park, whatever the spec sheet says.

Posture matters more across weeks than across days. Honda says the Vision's large 16-inch front wheel gives a high, upright riding position and a wide view ahead. Whether that suits your back, wrists and knees after an hour is something only a ride can tell you, which is why the trial section below matters.

### Carrying a passenger

If a partner or friend will ride with you regularly, test the bike with both of you on it. Pulling away, braking and wheeling the bike into a parking row all feel different when it is loaded, and the passenger's seat and footrests decide whether they will still agree to ride in week three. ## Transmission and storage

The automatic-versus-semi-automatic choice over a month is a comfort and cost decision, covered in [Automatic or Semi-Automatic for a Monthly Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/automatic-or-semi-automatic-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Storage matters more than renters expect: a month of groceries, rain jackets, helmets and chargers lives somewhere, and under-seat space or a box changes daily life.

### The test that settles transmission

That guide sets out the trade-offs; what it cannot do is tell you how your left foot and your route will get on. On a semi-automatic you change gear with a foot lever and have no hand clutch. Honda's Wave Alpha 110 page lists a mechanical four-speed rotary gearbox, and Yamaha Vietnam's Sirius RC 110 page, as read on 23 September 2026, lists a four-speed rotary gearbox with a centrifugal clutch. Ride your real route on each type through a stretch of genuine traffic and notice which one you stop thinking about.

Two things catch out a renter who picks the semi-automatic for the month: how often a junction or a slope asks for a gear change, and how little carrying space the bike offers. If either worries you, the automatic is the safer choice. If you like the mechanical feel and carry little, the simplicity of the semi-automatic is a real advantage.

### Storage you can check

Honda's Air Blade 125 page lists a 23.2 L under-seat bay that takes two half-helmets, plus a USB-C charging port (Honda Vietnam page, read 23 September 2026). The Vision page describes an under-seat bay for a helmet and personal items and a covered front compartment with a USB-C port, but it gives no litre figure. The Wave Alpha 110 page and the Sirius RC 110 page publish no storage capacity at all, so on those bikes plan for a backpack and ask whether a rack or box is an option.

Bring what you actually carry to the handover: your own helmet, the laptop bag, a folded rain jacket. A full-face helmet that will not fit under the seat is a small irritation on day one and a daily one by week three. [The verified storage comparison]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-cargo-storage-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the capacities verified from Honda and Yamaha Vietnam pages for other scooters.

### Fuel use on paper

The consumption figures in the table are manufacturer claims under the maker's test conditions, and real figures vary with speed, temperature, load and terrain. Taking the two Honda claims, 1.72 L/100 km for the Wave Alpha 110 and 1.82 L/100 km for the Vision differ by 0.10 L per 100 km. That is small enough that storage, fit and parking should decide first. The Sirius RC 110 figure comes from Yamaha, so it is not compared with the Honda figures. ## Price reality

Published monthly rates exist only for some models — [Which Motorbikes Have Published Monthly Rates in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/which-motorbikes-have-monthly-rates-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists exactly which ones. For any model without a published monthly rate, contact us to confirm the current monthly price. Never assume the daily rate multiplied by thirty is the answer.

What you can plan with is the published daily rate: 150,000 VND for the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio, and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade. A daily rate is the price of a day, useful for costing a short trial, not for estimating a month. The deposit is typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions. Ask for the figure for the specific bike at the same time as the quote.

## Mileage and wear

A month of heavy riding makes you a high-mileage renter — [High Monthly Mileage on a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the check rhythm that implies. If you expect to ride a lot, say so at set-up: it affects which bike suits you and how the shop plans maintenance.

Ask who pays for what before the month begins. Maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, while consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation. Yamaha lists the Sirius RC 110 with tyres that use inner tubes, so on a bike like that a puncture is a repair worth understanding in advance. Ask who covers it, and keep the answer alongside your odometer note.

## Rain, darkness and the weeks in between

Depending on the month you rent, you may ride in heavy rain, cold drizzle or heat. Choose with the worst of those in mind rather than the best.

- **Brakes.** Ask what the bike you would receive has: ABS, combined braking, a disc or a drum at the front. Yamaha lists the Sirius RC 110 with a front hydraulic disc and a rear drum, and notes that drum and disc versions exist.

- **Lights.** Honda's pages for the Vision and the Wave Alpha 110 both list a headlight that stays lit while the bike is running. Whatever the model, ride a short evening stretch in the first days to see the beam on your usual road, and read [Motorbike Light Rules in Vietnam: Headlights After Dark]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for the rules.

- **Rain gear.** A jacket fits under a scooter seat; on an underbone it travels in a bag. [Riding a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi Rain: What to Know]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) covers how to ride when the rain starts.

## Parking and keeping the bike overnight

A rental you cannot park comfortably is a rental you will use less. Before choosing, picture the place it will sleep and the place it will stand during the working day: a building basement or ramp, a guarded row of bikes, a narrow hallway. A bike you can wheel in and out easily is a kinder choice in a crowded row than one that needs a lot of room to manoeuvre. [Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the daily options.

Locks are part of the choice too. Honda lists a Smart Key system among the Vision's features, with remote location and unlocking, and a three-in-one lock (ignition, steering and magnetic) on the Wave Alpha 110. Which of these the bike you are given actually has is a question for the handover, since pages describe a model range rather than one particular bike.

## Try before committing

A short test or a daily rental before converting to monthly is the honest way to know a bike fits. [Trying a Motorbike Before Committing to a Monthly Rental]({{ '/articles/test-riding-before-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers how that works in practice.

Make the trial look like your month rather than like a test ride around the block:

- Load it as you would on a normal day, with the bag, the helmet and a passenger if you carry one.
- Park it where it will actually live, and push it out again.
- Ride part of the route after dark and part in traffic.
- Run the [handover inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}), because a bike you may keep for a month deserves a stricter look than a day-tripper's.
- Ask whether the trial days count toward the month and whether the same bike can stay with you. Contact us to ask about both.

## Mistakes that cost a month

- **Choosing the biggest or newest.** More machine means more weight to park and push, and a short commute may never use the extra.

- **Staying on the wrong bike out of politeness.** If something is wrong in week one, raise it then.

## Questions renters ask

### Can I swap bikes if the first one does not suit me?

The published terms do not say, so ask before the month starts whether a change is possible and on what basis. Contact us to discuss it.

### Is a month of commuting a lot of mileage for a rental?

1,500 km over a 30-day month is 50 km a day, so a 20 km loop each day (about 600 km) stays well below the owner's guidance of periodic checks around 1,500 km or more of use; long daily rides or regular weekend trips can pass it. This is practical owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval, and it is why mileage is worth mentioning at set-up.
