Whether you can take a rental motorbike beyond Hanoi is a rental-terms question first and a riding question second. The national road law applies on every road in the country, but the rental agreement may set limits of its own, some roads are closed to motorbikes, and distances change the risk calculation. Nguyen Tu's support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so for any ride into the provinces the short answer is to ask the shop about the trip before you book it.

## Ask the shop first

Does the rental agreement limit how far a bike may travel? That is the first question to put to the shop, because a breakdown, say 100 km away, is a different problem from one in Hanoi. Support arrangements are primarily for local use. Ask before you plan the trip, agree it in the terms (see [Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), and be honest about the distance. A shop that knows your itinerary can also tell you whether the bike you chose suits it — underbones are fine for pagoda runs, less comfortable for mountain passes (see [Best Motorbike for Long-Distance Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-long-distance-vietnam/' | relative_url }})).

No distance limit and no list of allowed provinces is published for Nguyen Tu rentals. That is the reason to ask rather than assume: nothing on this page says that a route is allowed or forbidden, and the shop's answer is the only term you will have. The shop is reachable on +84 942 467 674 by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo, and is open 09:00-21:00 daily.

### What counts as local use

Nothing published says where local use ends, so name the destination rather than a direction and ask whether the shop treats it as local. A ride that stays on the Hanoi map can still be a long way from the shop at 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street in Long Bien.

### What to settle, by kind of trip

| Kind of trip | Settle with the shop | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Same-day ride out and back | The destination and the time you expect to return | A return after the shop has closed is a conversation to have in advance |
| Overnight trip | The number of rental days, the day and time of return, and who to message from the road | An unplanned night away changes the return, and late return is charged by the hour |
| Several days or provinces | The whole route, a spare day in the plan, and when the bike is due a check | Distance adds wear; the [guide to high-mileage rental riding]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-rental-motorbike-inspection/' | relative_url }}) explains the check |
| Mountain or remote roads | Which bike suits the route, and how you will reach the shop if the phone signal drops | A bike chosen for city streets may suit a pass less well |
| One-way trip or leaving the bike elsewhere | Nothing is published for it, so plan a round trip that ends back in Hanoi | No one-way rental, drop-off in another city or touring package is described for Nguyen Tu rentals |

### What does not change with distance

A trip is also a plan for the return, and the published return terms do not mention distance. Late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged (typically 150,000-200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle). Whether a delay caused by a breakdown is treated differently is a question for the shop, not something to assume. Early return carries no automatic refund entitlement, so a trip cut short by weather or a fault should not be planned around a refund for unused time.

## Roads that do not allow motorbikes

The practical barrier most riders hit: expressways, though that is a general impression, not a counted figure. Motorbikes are banned from Vietnamese expressways (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 25(3)), and the penalties for entering one are set separately — see [Are Motorbikes Allowed on Vietnamese Expressways]({{ '/articles/motorbikes-on-expressway-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Your route out of Hanoi therefore uses national roads and provincial roads, which are slower but the ones everyone rides, or at least the ones whose signs leave them open to motorbikes. Plan distances accordingly: a "150 km trip" on a map can be a longer ride in real conditions than the map suggests, so build the day around the stops and the slow sections rather than the distance alone.

### What the law says

Article 25(3) of Law 36/2024/QH15 names mopeds as well as motorbikes, and its only exception is for people, vehicles and equipment working on the management and maintenance of the expressway. Nothing in the paragraph depends on engine size or on who owns the bike, so a rental agreement cannot make an expressway ramp open to a motorbike. The guide linked above is the place to check what a rider risks by entering, because penalty rules are separate from the traffic law and a figure remembered from elsewhere may be out of date.

### Keeping a route on motorbike-legal roads

A map app left in car mode can offer an expressway as the fastest route. Set the app to a motorbike or two-wheeler option where it has one, check the first stretch against the road signs, and treat any ramp as a reason to stop and look before you enter. The guide to [navigation and offline maps for motorbike trips]({{ '/articles/motorbike-trip-navigation-offline-maps-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the car-mode expressway trap and following the national highways (quốc lộ) instead.

## Speed limits and following distance outside the city

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets default maximum speeds for stretches that have no speed sign (Article 4(2)). A provincial ride passes through towns and open country, so the figure that applies changes along the way; read the signs rather than carry one number in your head. The defaults for the two classes that matter to a renter are:

| Road | Motorbike | Moped |
|---|---|---|
| Built-up area, dual carriageway or one-way road with two or more lanes | 60 km/h | 40 km/h |
| Built-up area, two-way road or one-way road with one lane | 50 km/h | 40 km/h |
| Outside a built-up area, dual carriageway or one-way road with two or more lanes | 70 km/h | 40 km/h |
| Outside a built-up area, two-way road or one-way road with one lane | 60 km/h | 40 km/h |

A built-up area is marked on the road by a start sign and an end sign (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, Article 3(1)), so the first two rows apply between those signs and the last two outside them. Posted limits override these defaults, and the [guide to speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-speed-limits-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how the limits work and what exceeding them can cost.

Under Article 34(1) of the Law, a moped is a two- or three-wheeled vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, with a combustion engine, a capacity of 50 cm3 or less, or, with an electric motor, a power of 4 kW or less. A two-wheeled motor vehicle that is not a moped counts as a motorbike. The moped default of 40 km/h is the same inside and outside built-up areas.

Article 11 of the Circular adds a minimum gap to the vehicle ahead. On a dry, straight, flat road with good visibility the table gives 35 m at 60 km/h and 55 m above 60 up to 80 km/h, a band that includes the 70 km/h default of a motorbike on a dual carriageway outside a built-up area; below 60 km/h the rider keeps a gap suited to the traffic. In rain, fog, on slippery surfaces, on winding or hilly roads or where visibility is limited, the gap must be larger than the table value.

## Papers to carry on a provincial road

Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the papers a driver carries, and the duty is the same on every road in the country, so nothing in it changes outside Hanoi. Article 56(3) lets papers that are integrated into an electronic identification account be shown through that account. Moped riders are excepted from the licence item (Article 56(1) and (4)); the definition of a moped is in the speed section above. The [guide to the documents a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through the full set.

Article 56 is a duty to carry the papers. It does not say how the papers of a rented bike reach the renter, so ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop.

## Checks before you leave the city

- Fuel planning: stations thin out in rural stretches; top up before leaving town
- Toolkit and spares: what is under the seat, and does the shop want you back for a check around high mileage? The [guide to bringing a high-mileage rental back]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-rental-motorbike-inspection/' | relative_url }}) explains what that check covers.
- Phone: the shop's number, a power bank, and a paper note of your accommodation address
- Documents: licence and the rental paperwork, as set out under the papers to carry above
- Tyres and chain: worth sixty seconds of inspection before a long day — see [Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}).

### Shaping the day

Decide the turnaround time before you leave: the hour at which you head back wherever you are, whatever is left on the list. [How far you can ride in a day from Hanoi]({{ '/articles/how-far-motorbike-day-trip-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) works through realistic distances, and the same rule keeps the return ride out of the dark, when tiredness and poor light combine badly on unlit roads.

Fuel is the next thing to settle. Ask which petrol the bike takes and what the shop expects at return; the [guide to fuel policy when renting in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-fuel-policy-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the questions.

## Breakdown far from home

The honest reality: a rental shop's practical support is Hanoi-focused. Out in the provinces the nearest help may be a small roadside mechanic for a puncture or minor work, but on a rental the question of which outside repair is acceptable is one to settle with the shop, so call before serious work is done. The rules of thumb are in [Can You Use Outside Repair Shops on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) and [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

Nothing published promises roadside rescue, recovery or a replacement bike for Nguyen Tu rentals, and the business does not promise 24/7 or nationwide rescue or a rescue time. A trip is best planned so that it does not depend on help reaching you. If the bike cannot be ridden home, the useful question is what the shop wants you to do, and the answer is better agreed before you leave than argued at the roadside.

### What distance changes

- **Time.** The shop's published hours are 09:00-21:00 daily, so a message sent outside them may wait until opening. A fault at night needs a plan that does not depend on an answer: a safe place for the bike and somewhere to stay.
- **Place and records.** What to send the shop before work starts, and what to keep afterwards, is covered in the [outside repair shop guide]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).
- **Your own way home.** If the bike cannot be ridden, you may need to get back to Hanoi while it is sorted out; find out where onward transport leaves from before you commit to a remote route.

### If there is an accident

A road accident brings legal duties of its own, which are listed in [what to do after a motorbike accident in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance and customers arrange their own, so check before you leave whether your own cover applies to a rented motorcycle away from Hanoi.

## The reward

Done with the shop's blessing and a suitable bike, riding out of Hanoi is the single best use of a rental: Bat Trang, Duong Lam, Perfume Pagoda roads, Ba Vi, or the long run toward Ninh Binh, though "best" is a matter of taste rather than a ranking, and support is primarily for Hanoi and local use. Several of these lie inside Hanoi's boundary, so ask whether the shop counts a given one as local use; Ninh Binh lies beyond it. Start with the suggestions and distance realities in [Best Motorbike for Day Trips from Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-day-trips-from-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and work up from there.

## Questions riders ask about leaving Hanoi

### Can I take a rental motorbike to another province?

That depends on the shop agreeing to the trip, and nothing published sets a limit either way. Give the destination, the number of days and the return day when you ask, and keep the reply in writing.

### Can I use the expressway to get out of Hanoi faster?

No. Article 25(3) of Law 36/2024/QH15 bars motorbikes and mopeds from expressways, whatever the engine size. Use the national and provincial roads.

### Will the shop collect the bike if it breaks down far away?

Nothing published promises collection, recovery or rescue; support is primarily for Hanoi and local use. Message the shop with your location, the symptom and a photo, and ask what it wants you to do before anyone repairs the bike.

### Can I leave the bike in another city?

No one-way rental or drop-off elsewhere is published for Nguyen Tu rentals. Plan every trip as a round trip that ends with the bike back at the shop in Hanoi.
