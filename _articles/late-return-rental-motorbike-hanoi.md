Life slips; flights move; the return runs late. The terms are simple, and so is the way to minimise them.

At Nguyen Tu, a late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and a return more than six hours late may be charged a full additional rental day - typically 150,000-200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle. A rental that ends a month or more in has its own page: [late return at the end of a long-term rental]({{ '/articles/late-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## The terms

Returns happen inside opening hours (09:00-21:00 daily); a return you plan for after closing needs to be agreed in advance, not improvised at 22:00.

No return outside opening hours is published, so read "agreed in advance" as something to ask the shop for, not as a service to count on.

### What the published figures come to

The sums below are arithmetic on the published terms, not a quote from the shop. The hourly figure is 20,000 VND. The published day rates are 150,000 VND for the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade, which matches the range quoted for an extra day; [the rental price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) has the wider price picture.

| How late | What the published terms give |
|---|---|
| 1 hour | 1 × 20,000 VND = 20,000 VND |
| 2 hours | 2 × 20,000 VND = 40,000 VND |
| 3 hours | 3 × 20,000 VND = 60,000 VND |
| 6 hours | 6 × 20,000 VND = 120,000 VND |
| More than 6 hours | A full additional rental day may be charged, for example 150,000 VND on a Honda Wave or 200,000 VND on a Honda Vision at their published day rates |

Two things follow from the table alone. For a delay of six hours or less the hourly sum is the smaller figure: three hours late is 60,000 VND against 150,000 VND for a full day on a Honda Wave. And the terms do not say whether the hours already counted are added to the day charge or replaced by it, so a total such as 120,000 VND plus 150,000 VND is not something to assume. Put that to the shop before you need the answer.

### What the terms do not say

The published terms stop at those figures. Each point below can change the final number, and each is a question to ask, not a rule to assume:

- Grace period: whether any time is allowed before the hourly charge starts, and how a part of an hour is counted.
- The clock: whether lateness stops when you reach the shop, when the key is handed over or when the check is finished.
- The six hours: whether they stack with the day charge, as above.
- Extension: whether a delay you reported in advance is treated as an extension at an agreed rate or as a late return.
- Settlement: whether a charge is taken from the deposit or paid on its own.
- Collection from your address, if one was arranged: whether the rental ends at the agreed collection time or when the bike is back at the shop.

Send these as one message when you book and keep the reply in the same thread, as [the booking guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) suggests for the rest of the terms.

## How the charge is applied

Nothing published says when or how an hourly charge is settled, so ask how the shop adds it up. If you are more than six hours late, the day charge may apply, and the arithmetic becomes: one extra day on your model's rate (150,000-200,000 VND on the published day rates).

Ask whether a late charge comes out of the deposit, and ask for the total as a list (each hour counted, the rate and the sum) before you pay. The accepted payment forms are cash, bank transfer and QR payment.

## What the Civil Code says about a late return

A motorbike rental fits the lease of property in Vietnam's Civil Code (Law 91/2015/QH13, Article 472), and Article 482 covers giving the property back. This is a general guide to what these articles say, not legal advice: the official text is in Vietnamese, and the terms you agreed with the shop govern your rental.

Three points matter when a bike comes back late:

- **The delay (Article 482(4)).** The lessor may ask for the property back, for rent covering the time of the delay and for compensation for damage; a penalty for the late return is owed only if the parties agreed one.
- **The risk (Article 482(5)).** The lessee bears the risk to the property during the delay.
- **Penalties (Article 418).** A penalty is an agreed sum, at whatever amount the parties choose, unless a related law says otherwise. Where the parties agreed a penalty but did not also agree that damages are payable on top, only the penalty is owed (Article 418(3)).

Neither article sets an amount. The 20,000 VND per hour is the shop's published figure, so the useful questions are about the terms you agreed: whether the hourly figure counts as rent for the overrun or as an agreed penalty, and whether it is the whole consequence of being late or the shop could also claim for loss the delay caused. The lease articles as a whole, with the other clauses worth reading, are in [the rental agreement guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## The move that saves the money

Message ahead the moment you know. Compare two versions of the same delay, an illustration and not a record of any renter. A shop that hears "my flight moved, I will be three hours late" at 10:00 may have room to look at its handovers and to say what it can offer. A shop that discovers it at 21:30 with tomorrow's renter waiting has no such room. WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674; the earlier, the more options you have.

### What to put in the message

- the bike (model and plate) and the return time you agreed;
- the time you now expect to arrive, and why;
- two questions: how the shop will count the delay, and whether it would treat the time as an extension;
- a request to confirm the answer in the same thread.

An illustration of the wording: "Hello, I am renting the Honda Vision, plate [plate], due back at 18:00 today. A delay means I will arrive at about 20:00, before you close. Will that be counted as a late return or can it be agreed as an extension, and what will it cost?" On the published terms, 18:00 to 20:00 is two hours, 2 × 20,000 VND = 40,000 VND, so ask what figure the shop would write.

## Extending instead of returning late

Some late returns are really unplanned extensions. If you want the bike longer, say so, and ask what an agreed extra day would cost: no extension rate is published, and the published day rate for your model is only the reference point. Availability permitting, extensions are the better path for everyone. Better means agreed in advance, not automatically cheaper: ask the shop to price both routes. For multi-day extensions [the period article]({{ '/articles/daily-weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows where the weekly rate takes over.

[The extending guide]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is written for monthly rentals, but its warning holds for a shorter one: asking for more time only after the agreed end has passed puts you on the late-return terms.

## Plan the return around opening hours

The shop is open 09:00-21:00 daily, so the plan starts inside that window. Put the return early in the window rather than late. A bike due back at 20:30 has half an hour before the shop closes at 21:00; one due at 14:00 has seven hours. An afternoon return can absorb a hold-up that an evening return cannot. [How returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi works]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) has the day's timeline for a flight or a check-out; the point specific to lateness is the margin.

If you arranged collection from your address instead of riding back, agree the place, the window and which moment ends the rental in the thread. Collection may be available depending on location and arrangement, inside opening hours, and [shop pickup versus delivery]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-pickup-or-delivery-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) compares the two routes.

### Traffic and distance

Plan the ride too. The bike has to reach 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street in Long Bien, and peak traffic can slow a journey, so a return that only works on a clear road may run late; [the rush-hour guide]({{ '/articles/traffic-at-rush-hour-riding-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers riding in peak traffic. A charge counted in hours is never worth a risk taken in minutes: speeding up in traffic to save one hour of 20,000 VND is a poor trade.

### Rain

If rain makes the road unsafe, stopping is the safer choice, and [the guide to riding in Hanoi rain]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) lists the signs that tell you to pull over and wait. Message the shop that you are waiting, so the delay is on record from its start.

### A breakdown

A bike that stops is a different case from a bike that comes back late. Follow [the breakdown guide]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}): get safe, call the shop and describe where you are. Then ask whether the time off the road counts as rental time; no rule for that is published, so get the answer in the thread and keep the times of your calls and messages.

### Petrol

A stop at the pump belongs in the plan. If the level agreed at handover has to be matched, build the stop in before the deadline, not into it; [the fuel policy guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-fuel-policy-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the conventions you may be offered and what to ask.

## What never to do

Return after closing without notice, or leave the bike unattended at the shutter with the keys on the seat. The bike is on your deposit until the shop has it back in hand. An unattended handover is a theft risk you own.

### If the shop is already closed

Nothing published covers an after-hours return, so do not assume the shop can take the bike back tonight. Keep the bike somewhere secure, message the shop with the time and where the bike is, and ask what it wants done; nothing is agreed until the shop confirms it in the thread.

## What to record at return

A late return turns the return time into evidence, so record it as deliberately as the bike. In order:

1. Before you set off: the message to the shop, with its time.
2. On arrival: photographs of the bike at the shop, plus the odometer and the fuel gauge, from the same angles as your handover set. [The inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) has a section on running the handover backwards at the return.
3. At the counter: ask the shop to say, and write in the thread, the time it records for the return, how many hours it counts and what that comes to.
4. Before you pay: compare the total with the arithmetic above and keep the payment confirmation; for a cash payment, ask the shop to write the amount down or give a receipt.

The photographs and readings show the condition on arrival, so the open question is the time. If an amount does not match your record, ask where each figure comes from before you agree to it.

## Quick answers on late returns

### Does a message stop the charge?

Nothing published says it does. A message gives you a time-stamped record and a chance to agree something else; it does not change the published figures by itself. Ask the shop to confirm in the thread what it has agreed.

### What if I return early instead?

You can hand the bike back early, but there is no automatic refund entitlement for unused time. [The early-return guide]({{ '/articles/early-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers that side.

## The short version

Late is a solvable problem with a phone in your hand. Early notice, agreed extension, settled at the counter. How the delay is counted and how the charge is settled are the parts to confirm with the shop first.
