Punctures are the most common rental-bike event after refuelling, and the money question is genuinely situational. Here is the honest split.

## The rule of thumb

Inner tubes and tyres can be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation. The distinction that matters:

- A puncture from road debris - a nail, glass, a pothole strike - is typically the renter's to pay: it happened while the bike was on your watch, and the tube is a consumable.
- A tyre that was bald or damaged at handover, or a failure that comes from the bike's own condition, is the shop's: that is maintenance, and maintenance is the owner's side of the rental.

This is why the handover checklist includes looking at the tyres: tread visible, no cracks, and photograph them. The tyre's handover state is the evidence for whose failure a later flat was.

## What a puncture actually costs

A tube repair or replacement at a Vietnamese roadside tyre shop is a small bill - tens of thousands of dong for a patch, more for a new tube, more again for a tyre. It is paid in cash on the spot, and it is not worth a negotiation with a rental shop over amounts this size; the deposit-return conversation matters, this does not.

## What to do when it happens

- Stop quickly - riding on a flat shreds the tube and can wreck the tyre, turning the cheap repair into the expensive one.
- Any tyre shop (vá xe) fixes it in minutes; they are everywhere in Hanoi, marked by a tub of water and an air pump.
- Keep the receipt, photograph the cause if you can (the nail in the tube is a good photo), and tell the shop what happened - especially on longer rentals where the shop tracks its bikes' condition.

## Prevention, in one habit

Check the tyres at handover, then once a week by feel and at any fuel-station gauge. Underinflated tyres puncture more, handle worse, and wear faster - five seconds of attention prevents most of this article.
