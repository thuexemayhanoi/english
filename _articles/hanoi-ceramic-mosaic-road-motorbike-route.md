The ceramic mosaic wall along the Nghi Tam–Yen Phu dyke is one of Hanoi's most photographed kilometres — a continuous relief of glazed tiles, batiks and painted ceramics running along the Red River flood embankment, decorated in the run-up to the city's 2010 millennium celebrations and recognised as the world's longest ceramic mosaic. For riders, it is also a genuinely useful piece of road: the dyke stretch connects the northeast corner of West Lake with the Long Bien area, and it turns a commute into a gallery visit.

## Where it runs

The wall follows the dyke road from the Nghi Tam area (the West Lake / Truc Bach corner, near the spillover) eastward along Yen Phu and down toward the Nhat Tan direction, parallel to the river and behind the thin strip of old houses facing the lake-side streets. The most decorated, most photographed section is around Nghi Tam and the start of Yen Phu. Access is simple: from the northeast corner of the West Lake loop, take the dyke ramp and ride east.

## Riding the dyke road

Dyke roads have their own character — long sight lines, few junctions, and surfaces that mix smooth new asphalt with patched concrete. Riding notes:

- The road is a favourite morning-cycling and running route; before 07:30 expect pedestrians and spandex in both directions.
- The wall side has no kerb; the riding line is close to the tiles, which is fine at dyke-road speeds but demands attention when photographers are stopped.
- After heavy rain, the low ramp sections at either end can hold runoff from the dyke itself; treat the first and last fifty metres as wet zones.
- On summer evenings the sun sits over the river side and blinds westbound riders — the glare guide applies.

## Photo stops that don't block traffic

The wall is generous with stopping space if you use it honestly: several widened shoulder areas near the decorated section serve exactly this purpose, and there is almost always a coconut vendor or a security guard near the famous panels who will watch your bike for the price of a drink. Stop fully off the riding line, on the stand, before lifting a camera.

## Fitting it into a ride

The mosaic wall is the natural east leg of the classic West Lake loop: ride the lake counterclockwise, finish at Nghi Tam, drop onto the dyke, ride the wall toward Yen Phu, and either loop back to the lake or continue down toward Long Bien and the bridge. Do it at 08:00 with the light on the tiles, or at 17:00 when the wall glows and the river reflects the sunset. Either way it is the city's easiest art gallery — one you can ride through at thirty kilometres an hour with the river on your left hand.
