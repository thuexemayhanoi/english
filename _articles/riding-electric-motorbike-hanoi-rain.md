Hanoi's rain season puts every two-wheeler under water — painted arrows, hidden potholes and flooded underpasses. Electric bikes raise one extra question: is the electrics-in-the-rain thing safe? Yes, with the right understanding.

## What the waterproof ratings mean

Manufacturers certify water resistance with IP codes, and Vietnam's makers publish them. The Dat Bike Weaver 200 carries IP65 — protected against water jets from any direction; the Weaver++ is rated IP67 — rated for temporary immersion. In rain terms: normal riding through downpours is inside the design envelope of these machines. What IP ratings do not cover is deep standing water; flood water that reaches controller or battery compartments is a different problem, submerged electrical connectors being the risk even when the bike is nominally sealed.

## Wet-road technique on electric

- **Regen changes braking feel.** On throttle-controlled regen (as on t
he Weaver 200), the same grip movement that normally slows you also does double duty in the wet — release progressively, because regen acts on the rear wheel and can slip on slick paint or mud.
- **Silence cuts both ways.** Rain noise masks your already-quiet motor further; ride as if invisible.
- **Budget for less regen.** On very slippery surfaces the system recovers less energy — and range drops slightly in heavy rain with lights on.
- **Avoid flood lines.** If water hides the road surface, do not cross at speed; our flooded-street riding guide covers the technique for every bike type.

## After the rain

Dry the bike, check connectors and lights, and if the bike sat in water above ankle depth, do not charge it until it has been inspected. Charging a wet battery connection is the real hazard, not the rain itself. See our washing guide for cleaning rules and the after-rain surfaces guide for grip.
