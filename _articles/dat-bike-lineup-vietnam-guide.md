Dat Bike builds the A1-licence end of Vietnam's electric market: machines above the 4 kW licence-free line, for riders who treat an electric as a real motorcycle. The three verified machines form a clean ladder; figures below are from the official product pages.

## The verified ladder

| Model | Motor | Top speed | Claimed range | Charging | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weaver | 5,000 W hub | 80 km/h | 100 km | ~3 h | A1 |
| Weaver 200 | 6,000 W hub | 90 km/h | 200 km at 35 km/h; 130 km at 70 km/h | 1 h for first 100 km; 3 h full | A1 |
| Weaver++ | mid-motor | 90 km/h (33% higher acceleration than Weaver 200) | 200 km | 100 km in 20 min at Dat Charge | A1 |

All three are A1-class machines under Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 (above 4 kW, within the 11 kW A1 band) — licence-free riding ends at 4 kW, and nothing in this line-up pretends otherwise.

## What each machine is for

- **Weaver**: the entry — city commuting distances, the familiar underbone shape, the simplest version of the idea. Review.
- **Weaver 200**: the distance machine — 200 km claimed at 35 km/h, 120 kg, 165 kg max load, IP65 sealing, hydraulic discs, throttle-controlled regen, and a published battery-life claim of 150,000 km / 15 years with a 3-year battery warranty and 48-hour nationwide on-site service. Review.
- **Weaver++**: the technology flagship — mid-motor layout, IP67, monoshock, tubeless tyres on alloy wheels, adjustable chainring, app connectivity, and fast charging that turns long days into short stops. Review.

## The wider names

Dat Bike's range also lists the Quantum family (Quantum, Quantum S1/S2/S3) and the Era. Those product pages were not retrieved in detail for our verified database, so no specifications appear here — this guide does not guess. When those rows are verified, they will be added to the same ladder.

## Choosing inside the brand

The ladder is honest: distance (Weaver 200) versus response and charging speed (Weaver++) versus simplicity (Weaver).
