Vietnam divides motorbikes into xe so - twist-and-shift semi-automatics - and xe con tay - manual-clutch bikes. This comparison is that divide in one table: Honda's Winner R sport underbone against Yamaha's Sirius workhorse. Both manufacturers publish full specs (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## The core difference

The Sirius RC 110 is a 110.3 cc semi-automatic: 5.9 kW at 8,000 rpm, four-speed rotary gearbox, no clutch lever, 100 kg wet. The Winner R is a 149.2 cc liquid-cooled manual-clutch machine: 11.5 kW at 9,000 rpm, 13.5 Nm at 7,000 rpm, 124 kg, with a big-tyre 90/80-17 front and 120/70-17 rear.

## The verified numbers

| Spec | Sirius RC 110 | Winner R |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 110.3 cc, air-cooled | 149.2 cc, liquid-cooled KC46E |
| Max power | 5.9 kW @ 8,000 rpm | 11.5 kW @ 9,000 rpm |
| Max torque | 9.0 Nm @ 5,000 rpm | 13.5 Nm @ 7,000 rpm |
| Gearbox | 4-speed rotary | manual clutch |
| Fuel tank | 4.2 L | 4.5 L |
| Claimed economy | 2.08 L/100 km | 2.59 L/100 km (UDC method) |
| Kerb weight | 100 kg wet | 124 kg |
| List price | 21,993,000 VND (RC) | 46,360,000 VND |

Real-world fuel economy varies with speed, load and conditions; Honda notes a changed UDC test method for the Winner's figure.

## What it means on the road

The Sirius does everything a Hanoi day asks: light, cheap, clutch-free, and easy to park anywhere. The Winner R exists for riders who have decided the clutch is the point - 9,000-rpm power peaks, wider tyres, and the closest thing to a race replica underbone Honda sells below the CBR150R.

Note the practical asymmetry: the Sirius is a bike anyone can ride immediately; the Winner R assumes you can already operate a manual clutch in dense traffic.

## Which one for you?

If you are new to Vietnam, ride daily in the city, or carry passengers and shopping, the Sirius is the correct answer - and it is one of our published rental classes. If you already ride manuals and want a sport underbone for weekends, the Winner R is Honda's entry ticket.
