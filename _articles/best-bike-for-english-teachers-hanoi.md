A teaching contract in Hanoi means a fixed commute, an income in dong, and a bike that is a tool rather than a toy. The maths and the machine are both simple. The best motorbike for English teachers in Hanoi is an automatic scooter in the Vision, Click or Mio class; try the Honda Air Blade 125 first if you carry a laptop and materials, because among those four it is the only one with a published under-seat storage figure.

## The machine

An [automatic scooter]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-to-rent-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) in the Honda Click / Yamaha Mio / Honda Vision class. Why this class wins the teacher's use-case:

- The daily school run is stop-start district riding; automatics own it.
- Under-seat storage takes a helmet, a laptop and a change of clothes. How much a given bike takes varies, so test the bay with your own bag before you agree to a term; the bag section below shows which figures exist.
- Running costs are modest for a small-engine scooter, and a manufacturer's consumption figure for the exact bike, where one is published, lets you check that instead of taking it on trust.
- Scooters of this class are common on Hanoi's roads, which generally makes a mechanic easy to find; the shop's own maintenance is covered further down.

[Published monthly rates]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}): Click and Mio at 1,000,000-1,200,000 VND, Vision at 1,800,000-2,000,000 VND. ## Start from your timetable, not the spec sheet

A spec sheet describes a bike; a timetable describes a job. Five questions about your own week point at five different features, so answer them before you compare models.

- **How many sites in a day?** Every extra site is another stop, another parking row and another moment of steadying the bike, so weight and parking count for more than top speed.
- **What is in the bag?** A laptop, printed materials and a spare shirt need a bay that closes over them, which makes storage the first test.
- **When does your last class end?** A late finish means riding home in the dark, so lights, brakes and tiredness matter, and so do the shop's opening hours of 09:00-21:00 daily.
- **Will you ride in the rain?** A class starts when it starts whatever the weather, so the bike has to carry wet-weather gear and brake predictably in the wet.
- **Will anyone ride behind you?** A passenger now and then is a question of fit and rules, not a reason to choose a different class of bike.

[The commuting guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-commuting-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) works through fuel, storage, seat height and rain for a single daily route. This guide adds what changes when the route is several sites and the bag is a teacher's.

## The bag: what the manufacturers publish

The figures in this guide come from the Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Motor Vietnam model pages as last checked in September 2026; where none is quoted, the text says so.

### Under-seat storage

Honda lists the Air Blade 125's under-seat bay at 23.2 L and words it as two half-helmets; its page also lists a USB-C charge port. No litre figure is quoted here for the Vision, Click or Mio, so a bay you have not tested is an unknown. The Air Blade 160 page lists a USB-C charge port too, but this guide quotes no storage figure for it.

A litre figure is a start, not a test. Two half-helmets is a different job from a full-face helmet plus a laptop bag, so put in the helmet you will wear, the bag you will carry and the rain jacket, and check that the seat closes without you leaning on it. If your materials will not fit, ask whether a bigger-bay scooter is on offer: Honda lists 37 L, worded as two full helmets, for the Lead 125, but no day rate is published for it, so treat that as a question for the shop and not an expectation.

### Without a bay

Honda and Yamaha publish no under-seat storage for the Wave Alpha 110 or the Sirius RC 110, so an underbone rider plans on a backpack or a rack bag. For a teacher that means carrying the laptop on the shoulders, or strapped behind, through the whole day. [The scooter and semi-automatic comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-vs-semi-automatic-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets storage beside the other trade-offs between the two classes.

### Dry and safe

In the wet season, line the bag with a waterproof liner and keep a wet jacket in its own bag so it does not soak the rest of the bay. A laptop left in the seat of a bike parked for hours is an avoidable risk, so take it inside with you.

## Several sites in a day

### Weight at every stop

Honda publishes kerb weights of 97 kg for the Vision, 110 kg for the Air Blade 125, 113 kg for the Air Blade 160 and 96 kg for the Wave Alpha 110; Yamaha publishes a wet weight of 100 kg for the Sirius RC 110, so compare across the brands loosely. The Vision, Wave Alpha 110 and Sirius RC 110 sit within a few kilograms of each other, too close to choose between. The Air Blade 160 is 16 kg heavier than the Vision, and a teacher who steadies the bike at every school gate and parking row feels a gap like that more than a rider who parks once a day.

### Parking and fuel along the way

Ask each employer where staff bikes go before the first class, because arrangements differ from site to site and a bike with nowhere to stand is a daily problem. [The parking guide]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the rules and etiquette.

Fuel is a planning item more than a deciding one. Honda publishes 1.72 L/100 km and a 3.7 L tank for the Wave Alpha 110, and Yamaha 2.08 L/100 km and a 4.2 L tank for the Sirius RC 110; no consumption or tank figure is quoted here for the Vision, Air Blade, Click or Mio. These are manufacturer test results, and speed, load and traffic move them, so ask what fuel level the bike is handed over with and fill up in daylight before a late class, not at 21:30 on the way home.

## Evening classes and wet afternoons

### Riding home after dark

Before you take the bike for a term, check that the headlight, tail light, brake light and indicators all work; [the rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) gives the full walk-round. A late class also means a tired ride home, which is one more reason to prefer an automatic if you do not already shift gears with a foot.

The shop is open 09:00-21:00 daily, so a teacher whose classes fill those hours should ask early how a service visit can be fitted around the timetable.

### Rain between sites

In the wet season a shower can catch you between two sites. Brakes decide how that goes: Yamaha lists the Sirius RC 110 with a front hydraulic disc and a rear drum, Honda's page for the Wave Alpha 110 lists no disc version, and no brake information is quoted here for the Vision, Air Blade, Click or Mio. Ask what brakes the bike has and whether it has ABS, and read [how to ride a rental motorbike in Hanoi rain]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) before the first storm, not during it.

## The licence, before anything else

Teachers staying months have time to do this properly: a home motorcycle licence plus a 1968-convention IDP, or an exchange to a Vietnamese licence for longer stays. How long an exchange takes is a question for the issuing police office, and it needs a full dossier - the [licence exchange dossier]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is listed in its own guide - but a teacher commuting daily for a year should not spend that year on a licence gap.

### Which licence fits which bike

Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines a moped as a machine with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and either an engine of 50 cc or less or an electric motor of 4 kW or less. A moped needs no licence from age 16 under Article 59. The Vision, Click, Mio and Air Blade are bigger than that, so a teacher on any of them needs a licence that covers the bike.

A new A1 licence covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc under Article 57, which takes in the Vision and the 125 cc Air Blade. A 160 cc bike such as the Air Blade 160 is above that line on a new licence, while A1 licences issued before 1 January 2025 keep their earlier scope under Article 89; [the A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) sets out what each class can ride. The published Air Blade rate does not name a version, so ask whether you would be given the 125 or the 160, and check that your licence covers it before you accept the bike.

A foreign licence is judged the same way: Article 57(8) recognises a national licence with a 1968-convention IDP only when the licence is appropriate to the vehicle, and an IDP cannot add a category your national licence lacks.

Residents have a second route. Under Article 24(1) of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, a foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam and holds a valid national licence, with a diplomatic or service identity card or a temporary or permanent residence card valid for three months or more, can be considered for exchange to the matching Vietnamese class. An IDP cannot be exchanged, and neither can an expired national licence, so check the date on your home licence before you arrive.

A Vietnamese licence exchanged for a foreigner is valid only for the term on the residence card and on the foreign licence (Article 24(5)(a)), so note both dates when you plan a second year.

## Questions teachers ask

### Which bike is best for an English teacher in Hanoi?

An automatic scooter. Start with the Vision for one or two sites and a light bag, and with the Air Blade 125 for a laptop and materials; the table above sets out the other cases.

### Can I try a bike by the day before committing to a term?

The published day rates are 150,000 VND for the Honda Click and the Yamaha Mio and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and the Honda Air Blade, so a few days is a way to test a bay and a route before a term starts. Whether a day rental can move to a monthly one is a question for the shop, not something to assume.

### Is a semi-automatic a bad choice for a teacher?

Not if you already shift gears with a foot. The Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius are published at 150,000 VND per day, but neither the Wave Alpha 110 nor the Sirius RC 110 has a published storage figure, so the laptop rides on your back or a rack. An automatic takes one task off a rider who does not shift gears already.

### Can I ride without a motorbike licence while I settle in?

Only in the licence-free moped class described in the licence section above. A machine counts as a moped only if it meets the definition on every limit, so ask for its registration category, and remember that a design speed capped at 50 km/h makes cross-city hops slower. The shop publishes no fixed 50 cc price, so contact the shop for 50 cc availability and rates.
