Yes, you can hand the bike back early. No, the unused days do not automatically become cash. Here is the honest position.

## The principle

A rental is priced for the period booked. If you booked a week at 600,000-700,000 VND and return on day four, the week was still the price of the bike being unavailable to anyone else for those days. Standard practice in Hanoi is: no automatic refund entitlement for early return, and any adjustment is the shop's discretion, not the renter's right. This is also the honest way around - a renter who wants to leave early has no obligation to explain, and the shop has no obligation to re-price.

## What early return does and does not change

- The deposit: returned in full, on the spot, after the walk-around - early return does not touch it.
- The condition checks: identical to any return - photos, fuel, balance settled.
- The price: the agreed rate for the agreed period stands unless the shop chooses otherwise.

## How to leave money on the table less often

- Book the period you actually believe, not the period you hope. The period article shows the rate breakpoints - if you are unsure between daily and weekly, the arithmetic of a couple of extra days usually answers it.
- If your dates might move, say so when booking; shops plan their fleets around dates, and a flagged uncertainty gets handled like a normal booking question.
- If you know mid-rental that you will finish early, tell the shop then - an early notice sometimes lets the bike go to another booking, which is the situation where a goodwill adjustment is realistic.

## What to never assume

Never assume the "unused" days are refundable at the counter, and never hand the bike back early as a surprise. The return itself is simple - the terms are what they are, and both sides do better when the shop can plan.

The short answer: return early freely, expect the booked price, treat any adjustment as a bonus rather than a right.
