Vietnamese rain does not schedule itself. The storm that soaks a rider in five minutes also soaks the registration card in the underseat box, and a waterlogged licence at a checkpoint is a bad afternoon made worse. Documents are small, irreplaceable and made of paper — they need more waterproofing than anything else on the bike.

## What actually gets wet

Two failure modes, and riders plan only for the first. Sudden soaking: rain driven into every opening, wet gloves handling papers at a checkpoint, a backpack let open in the rush. Slow soaking: the underseat box of a scooter in the wet season is humid, not sealed — a registration card living loose in the toolbox goes limp, then mouldy, then unreadable over a season. Documents should ride in a sealed pouch whether it rains today or not; the full list of what you are required to carry while riding is in documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam.

## Pouch options

Ziplock-style bags are the honest baseline: genuinely waterproof, replaceable, free. Their weaknesses are punctures and closures that wear out, so they need periodic replacement. Proper document pouches with welded seams and zip or roll closures last longer, and roll-top pouches (the same dry-bag construction scaled down, as in dry-bags-waterproof-luggage-riders) survive total immersion. Look for: welded seams rather than stitched, a closure you can operate one-handed, and a size that fits papers unfolded — folding weakens laminated cards at the crease.

## The copy strategy

Ride with originals only when you must; carry clear photos on your phone as well. A photo does not replace the physical card at a checkpoint, but it makes replacing a destroyed card far easier, and it backs you up while paperwork is being reissued. Keep originals in the sealed pouch, in an inner pocket or the driest part of the luggage — load placement logic is in carrying-luggage-motorbike-safely-vietnam — not loose in the underseat box next to the tool roll (spare-parts-kit-underseat-motorbike covers what belongs there).

## After a soaking

Dry documents flat, away from direct sun — heat warps laminates. A pouch that took water with documents inside was a failed test: replace the closure or the pouch, not just the documents.
