Hanoi's museums are spread across the city, which makes them — more than temples or parks — a natural motorbike project: each one sits in a different district, and the rides between them are a tour of Hanoi's street geography in themselves.

## The map, roughly

The central band holds the history museums: the National Museum of History by the Old Quarter's southeast corner, and the Vietnam Military History Museum in the Ba Dinh government quarter. The west holds the two big easy-riding destinations: the **Vietnam Museum of Ethnology** on Nguyen Van Huyen street in the Cau Giay area — with its famous outdoor exhibits of Vietnam's ethnic house architectures — and the **Hanoi Museum** in Nam Tu Liem, an inverted-pyramid building that tells the capital'
s story. The Fine Arts Museum sits near the Temple of Literature.

## The rides

The two western museums are the pleasant ones: wide streets, boulevard riding, easy parking. The Ethnology Museum from the centre is twenty to thirty minutes through the Cau Giay arteries; the Hanoi Museum adds ten more along the ring roads. The central museums are short rides through dense districts — easy distance, busy riding.

Museum days reward the geography: do a central museum in the morning while the Old Quarter streets are at their freshest, then ride west after lunch when the boulevards open up. Two museums a day is the comfortable maximum with riding in between.

## Parking

This is the quiet advantage of the museum-by-bike plan: every major museum has attended motorbike parking at its gate — among the cheapest, safest stops in the city, and the Ethnology Museum's lot is famously calm compared with Old Quarter kerbs. Fees are a few thousand dong; helmets come off at the gate.

## Practical notes

Check opening days before riding out — most Hanoi museums close one weekday, and a few shift hours seasonally. Budget half a day for the Ethnology Museum alone; its outdoor exhibits are worth the trip on their own. Rain days are museum days by design: a wet Hanoi afternoon plus the museum circuit is the classic fallback plan, and the rain-gear guide covers getting there dry.
