Parking is a bigger daily decision in Hanoi than riding is. The city runs on motorbikes, and every street corner reflects it: paid lots, sidewalk edges, and a clear line between tolerated parking and ticketed parking. Here is how it works.

## Use paid lots whenever they exist

Train stations, markets, shopping malls, hospitals, parks, and larger restaurants operate attended motorbike lots (bãi gửi xe). A few thousand dong per session is normal — small coins are useful. You get a numbered ticket; keep it, because the attendant will want it back when you leave. If you lose it, expect to describe the bike and wait while the staff confirm it is yours.

## Sidewalk parking: read the signals

Parking on the sidewalk is tolerated on most residential and shop streets, but not everywhere. Indicators that parking is acceptable: motorbikes already parked in neat rows, a painted outline on the pavement, or an attendant collecting money. Indicators that it is not: red-and-white curbs, no-stopping signs, rows of bollards, storefronts with clear space in front, or a street that has clearly been swept of parked bikes. Where in doubt,
 use the nearest paid lot.

Riding on the sidewalk is a different matter from parking on it — riding to reach a parking spot a few metres in is one thing, using the sidewalk to bypass traffic is a fined violation. See riding-on-sidewalk-vietnam for the legal line.

## Do not block

The etiquette everyone local follows: leave the shop entrance clear, leave the bike lane passage walkable, park nose-to-kerb, and squeeze in rather than spreading out. A bike that blocks a doorway or a fire exit will get moved — sometimes roughly. Fold your mirror in on tight rows; it is a cheap habit that saves parts on a rental.

## No-parking zones to respect absolutely

Never park in front of government offices, embassies, hospitals' emergency entrances, bus stops, or on bridges and bridge approaches. These spots get enforcement attention, and a rental bike being towed or ticketed becomes your problem for the day: the fine process is described in motorbike-fines-vietnam-overview, and recovering a towed bike costs hours.

## Overnight parking

Most street lots close in the evening. If your rental stretches overnight, ask your rental shop where they recommend parking near your accommodation, or choose accommodation with its own motorbike space. A bike left on the street overnight in an unattended spot is a theft target in any big city, and theft responsibility on a rental is a conversation you do not want to have — see theft-loss-responsibility-rental-motorbike-hanoi.

## Before you walk away

Glance back once: steering lock on, key with you, helmet taken or locked to the bike, registration papers with you (never leave papers in the bike — see documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam). Thirty seconds of habit prevents most parking-related losses.
