Parking is a bigger daily decision in Hanoi than riding is. The city runs on motorbikes, and every street corner reflects it: paid lots, sidewalk edges, and a clear line between tolerated parking and ticketed parking. Here is how it works.

Short answer on where to park a motorbike in Hanoi: leave it in an attended lot (bãi gửi xe) whenever one is close, keep the ticket, and use the kerb or pavement only where no sign or marking forbids it and the bike blocks nobody. If you cannot tell which applies, use the lot.

## Use paid lots whenever they exist

Train stations, markets, shopping malls, hospitals, parks, and larger restaurants operate attended motorbike lots (bãi gửi xe). Lots charge a fee, so carry small notes or coins, and if no price is displayed, ask what it costs before you leave the bike. You get a numbered ticket; keep it, because the attendant will want it back when you leave. If you lose it, expect to describe the bike and wait while the staff confirm it is yours.

### What the ticket is for

The ticket is what lets the lot match a bike to the person who left it, so a few habits are worth the effort.

- Read it before you walk away. If the attendant writes your plate number or a code on it, check that it is right.
- Keep it on you, not in the under-seat box. If the bike goes missing, the ticket is your record that you left it there.
- Photograph it, the lot's entrance and the bike with its plate and a sign or shopfront in view. If you do lose the ticket, the photos help you describe the bike and show that it is yours, and they settle, two hours later, whether this is the right corner.

## Where you leave the bike, compared

The options differ in who is watching and who sets the rules, so compare them on those two points.

| Place | What to expect | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Attended lot | An attendant and a numbered ticket | The ticket matches your bike; you know the price |
| Kerbside attendant | A person managing a row of bikes at the street edge | No sign or marking forbids the spot; what you will pay |
| Hotel or building space | Set by the hotel or building: inside, a partner lot, or the street | Whether it is watched overnight; whether staff move bikes |
| Open kerb, no attendant | Nobody is watching or arranging the row | No sign, no marking, nothing blocked; lock the bike, take the helmet |

## Sidewalk parking: read the signals

Parking on the sidewalk is tolerated on some residential and shop streets, but not everywhere, and tolerated is not the same as permitted. Indicators that parking is acceptable: motorbikes already parked in neat rows, a painted outline on the pavement, or an attendant collecting money. None of these proves that the spot is lawful. Indicators that it is not: no-stopping and no-parking signs, painted kerb markings, rows of bollards, storefronts with clear space in front, or a street that has clearly been swept of parked bikes. Where in doubt,
 use the nearest paid lot.

Riding on the sidewalk is a different matter from parking on it, and it is the wrong way to reach a parking spot: using the sidewalk to bypass traffic is a fined violation. If the spot you want is a few metres up the pavement, stop at the kerb, dismount and push the bike the rest of the way. See [Riding on the Sidewalk in Vietnam: What the Law Says]({{ '/articles/riding-on-sidewalk-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for the legal line.

### What the law says about the sidewalk

- The road law bars stopping and parking "on the roadway or sidewalk against the provisions of law" (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 18(4)(o)), and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP lists parking or leaving a vehicle on the roadway or sidewalk unlawfully (trái phép) as a violation (Article 7(2)(d)).
- Article 18 does not itself say which sidewalks are open to bikes, so the answer for a street depends on the local rules and permissions that apply to it.
- Hanoi runs a permission system: its People's Committee approved a list of streets eligible for temporary use of part of the roadway for vehicle parking (Decision 6440/QĐ-UBND, reported by VOV on 18 December 2024), and fewer streets were licensed than were listed. An attendant collecting money does not by itself prove that a spot is licensed.

Riding along a sidewalk is a separate violation (Article 7(7)(a)).

### Five questions before you leave the bike at a kerb

1. Is there a sign or a marking? If there is one and you cannot read it, treat it as a restriction and move on.
2. Is there an attendant? If so, follow where they point, and still look for a sign.
3. Does the bike block a doorway, a gate, a crossing, a bus stop, a ramp or the width a pedestrian needs?
4. Could the bikes beside you leave without moving yours, and could yours leave without moving theirs?
5. Will you be gone for hours, or until night? If so, the kerb is the wrong place.

A single "no" or "not sure" is enough to choose the lot.

## Do not block

The etiquette everyone local follows: leave the shop entrance clear, leave the bike lane passage walkable, park nose-to-kerb, and squeeze in rather than spreading out. A bike that blocks a doorway or a fire exit will get moved — sometimes roughly. Fold your mirror in on tight rows; it is a cheap habit that saves parts on a rental.

Nose-to-kerb describes a row that someone is arranging. For a bike you park yourself on a city street, Article 18(6) of the road law says to stop or park close alongside the kerb or sidewalk on the right-hand side in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from it and without obstructing or endangering anyone.

In a packed row bikes touch handlebar to handlebar. Photographs taken at handover, as in the [rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}), let you and the shop tell an old mark from a new one.

## No-parking zones to respect absolutely

Never park in front of government offices, embassies, hospitals' emergency entrances, bus stops, or on bridges and bridge approaches. These spots get enforcement attention, and if a rental bike is fined it becomes your problem for the day: the fine process is described in [Motorbike Fines in Vietnam: Current Overview (Decree 168/2024)]({{ '/articles/motorbike-fines-vietnam-overview/' | relative_url }}), and [When Vietnamese Police Can Impound Your Motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how a held bike is released. Treat this list as cautious advice: at any spot, the rule that applies is the one on the sign or marking beside it. The law's own list follows.

### Where the road law bars stopping and parking

Article 18(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the positions where a rider may not stop or park. These are some of the positions a motorbike rider in Hanoi may meet.

| Position | Item in Article 18(4) |
|---|---|
| On the left-hand side of a one-way street | (a) |
| On a bridge, unless traffic organisation allows it | (c) |
| On the part of the road where pedestrians cross | (g) |
| At a junction and within 5 metres of the junction's edge | (h) |
| At a passenger pick-up or drop-off point, such as a bus stop | (i) |
| In front of, and within 5 metres either side of, the gate of an office or organisation that has a way in and out for vehicles | (k) |
| Covering a road sign or a traffic light | (n) |

The law names bridges (item (c)) and the space under flyovers (item (d)), not bridge approaches, so treat the approaches as cautious advice. The one-way rule matters in the Old Quarter, where [Riding the Old Quarter's One-Way Street Maze]({{ '/articles/old-quarter-one-way-streets-riding/' | relative_url }}) explains the layout.

Decree 168/2024 attaches penalties to several of these for motorbikes: stopping or parking on the roadway so that it obstructs traffic, or parking or leaving a bike on the roadway or sidewalk unlawfully (Article 7(2)(d)); stopping or parking at a pick-up or drop-off point, a junction or a pedestrian crossing, stopping where a "no stopping and parking" sign stands, or parking at a "no parking" sign (Article 7(2)(e)); and stopping or parking on a bridge (Article 7(3)(c)). This guide gives no fine amounts: Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, in force from 15 August 2026, amends Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP and its amended text was not read, so the Decree 168 items cited here are as issued; check them against the current text.

### Towing and holding

Holding a vehicle is separate from fining it; the impound guide linked above covers when police may do it. This guide cannot say whether a wrongly parked bike is also moved and held.

Article 48 of Decree 168/2024 does say who pays for moving a held vehicle. If the driver or owner is absent or does not comply, the authority moves it to the holding place, may hire someone if it cannot do so itself, and the driver or owner pays that hire. If an officer tells you to move the bike, do it; [Stopped by Traffic Police in Vietnam: What to Do]({{ '/articles/stopped-by-traffic-police-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the stop itself.

### Friday to Sunday evenings near Hoan Kiem

Near Hoan Kiem, a 2026 pilot bars road vehicles, motorcycles included, from the Zone 1 streets named in the official notice from 19:00 to 24:00 on each Friday, Saturday and Sunday; park outside them.

## Overnight parking

Street lots can close in the evening, so check a lot's closing time before you rely on it for the night. If your rental stretches overnight, ask your rental shop where they recommend parking near your accommodation, or choose accommodation with its own motorbike space. A bike left on the street overnight in an unattended spot is a theft target in any big city, and theft responsibility on a rental is a conversation you do not want to have — see [Theft and Loss Responsibility on Rental Motorbikes in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/theft-loss-responsibility-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

For a hotel stay, [Choosing a Hotel With Motorbike Parking in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/hotel-parking-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) gives the questions to ask before you book. Wherever the bike sleeps, use the steering lock, and add a disc lock or chain if the bike has one; [Motorbike Security: Disc Locks, Chains and What Actually Deters]({{ '/articles/disc-locks-chains-motorbike-security/' | relative_url }}) compares the options.

### Parking at home for a monthly rental

On a rental of weeks or months, the overnight spot becomes a daily routine, so ask your building or landlord before the bike arrives where it can park, whether the space is watched, and what it costs. Then read what your rental agreement says about where the bike is kept, and tell the shop where it will sleep; [Apartment Living With a Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/apartment-living-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) describes the building types you may meet, and [Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the day-to-day side.

## Before you walk away

Glance back once: steering lock on, key with you, helmet taken or locked to the bike, registration papers with you (never leave papers in the bike — see [What Documents Must You Carry While Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }})). Thirty seconds of habit removes the easiest chances for a parking-related loss.

## If the bike is not where you left it

Do not assume theft straight away. Work through these in order.

1. Check that you are at the right place: the same side of the street, the same lot entrance, the scene in your photograph.
2. Look along the row and the next street, then ask the attendant or nearby shop staff whether bikes were moved, and show the ticket and photographs.
3. If the police or officials took the bike, ask where it is kept and what to bring to collect it.
4. If it is gone, call the shop, report it to the police and ask for the written report (biên bản). [Common Motorbike Rental Problems in Hanoi and How to Avoid Them]({{ '/articles/common-motorbike-rental-problems-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows where a theft sits among the other rental problems.

Nguyen Tu's published opening hours are 09:00-21:00 daily, and the number for WhatsApp and Zalo is +84 942 467 674. Message the shop in writing with your photographs as soon as you know, so there is a dated record, and do the police report without waiting for a reply.

## Common questions

### Can I park a motorbike on the sidewalk in Hanoi?

Not as a blanket rule: which pavements are lawful for bikes depends on local rules and permissions that you cannot check from a map. Use an attended lot, and never ride along the pavement to reach a spot.

### How much does it cost to park a motorbike in Hanoi?

Attended lots charge a fee. If no price is shown, ask before leaving the bike.

### Does the parking ticket cover me if the bike is stolen?

The ticket shows where you left the bike and lets you collect it. What a lot owes if a bike goes missing depends on the operator. Who bears a theft loss depends on the rental contract, as the [theft and loss responsibility guide]({{ '/articles/theft-loss-responsibility-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains, and Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so any cover for theft is something you arrange yourself.
