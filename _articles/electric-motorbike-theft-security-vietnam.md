Electric motorbikes are attractive to thieves for one reason: the most valuable part unplugs. Security for an electric is partly bike security and partly battery security.

## What modern electrics give you built-in

The connected features are genuinely useful here. VinFast's Viper, for instance, carries a Smart Key system with vehicle tracking and remote vehicle-finding, plus remote on/off control for anti-theft support. Dat Bike's app adds smart unlock and remote software features. These raise the difficulty of a silent theft — but they are layers, not substitutes for physical locks.

## The physical layer still matters

- **A disc lock or chain at every stop.** Electronic deterrence can be relayed around; a hardened lock cannot be texted away. Our lock guide covers the trade-offs.
- **Steering lock engaged** — the base layer every bike already has; use it.
- **Battery out of sight, or out of the bike.** Removable packs should come with you into buildings where parking is unattended. On swap-models, an empty battery bay is the cheapest security there is.
- **Park in monitored spots.** Building basements, attended lots and camera-covered rows beat convenience parking. Overnight rules apply to every bike type.

## Charging security

A bike charging in a corridor with its charger cable visible is advertising both its value and its location. Charge in garages or behind locked doors, and never run cables across shared walkways — it flags the bike and trips people.

## If the worst happens

Report with the bike's serial and the app-account details; connected bikes are trackable while powered. For renters: the security routine you keep for a rental protects your deposit too — see our rental [security guide]({{ '/articles/disc-locks-chains-motorbike-security/' | relative_url }}).
