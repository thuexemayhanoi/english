On a manual-clutch underbone, the lever is the main control surface you own. Two settings decide how it behaves: reach and free play.

## Reach

Lever reach — how far the lever sits from the grip — is adjustable on many bikes, either with a dial at the lever perch or by moving the perch along the bar. Small hands need the lever closer, so the fingers can hold it without stretching. Test: with your hand relaxed on the grip, you should reach the lever without repositioning.

## Free play

Free play is the small amount of lever movement before the clutch begins to disengage — typically a few millimetres at the lever end. Too much free play means the clutch never fully disengages: shifting gets clunky and the bike creeps in gear. Too little means the clutch slips constantly, revs rising without speed. On most underbones the adjuster is a knurled collar where the cable meets the lever or the engine cover; small turns make big differences, so adjust in fractions and test between turns.

## Hand position

- Four fingers for full squeezes: launches, shifts, stops.
- Two fingers covering the lever while creeping in traffic — the outer fingers keep grip strength on the bar.
- Release always to the fully home position; a lever held a few millimetres out is a clutch that never quite engages.

## What belongs to a mechanic

Free play at the lever corrects cable stretch, but if the clutch still bites late, drags, or the lever pulls to the grip with no resistance, the cable or plates need attention — the clutch slipping and broken cable guides cover the symptoms. Rental riders should not rebuild anything: photograph the lever position at pickup, ride with it as delivered, and report problems to the provider instead of adjusting a bike that is not yours to repair.
