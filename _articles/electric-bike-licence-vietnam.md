Vietnam classifies electric two-wheelers by construction and power, and the boundaries decide whether you need a licence at all. The definitions come from Article 34 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15). In short, a pedal-assist electric bicycle and an electric moped need no licence class, a two-wheeled electric motorbike of up to 11 kW needs an A1 licence, and one above 11 kW needs class A (Article 57(1)).

Article numbers follow the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH of the Law, which includes Law 118/2025/QH15 (in force from 1 July 2026). Of the clauses cited here only Article 34(6) was amended; it now leaves the detail of Article 34 to the Minister of Construction.

## The three groups

**Pedal-type electric bicycles (xe đạp điện).** The law defines these as bicycles with motor assistance whose power cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or when the bike reaches 25 km/h. Article 34(2)(b) lists them among the non-motorised vehicles (xe thô sơ), apart from the motor vehicles of Article 34(1). Article 56(1) puts the licence duty on drivers (người lái xe), a term Article 2(9) defines as the person controlling a motor vehicle, and Article 59(1) names no minimum age for riding a motorised pedal bicycle. Riders and passengers must still wear standard-compliant helmets (Article 31(3)).

**Electric mopeds (xe gắn máy chạy điện), 4 kW and under.** Scooter-style electric two-wheelers of 4 kW or less, designed for no more than 50 km/h, fall in the moped class: no driving licence, rider must be at least 16. Article 34(1)(g) sets both limits, Article 56(1) exempts moped riders from the licence requirement, and Article 56(4) asks them instead to know the road traffic rules and to have the skill to control the vehicle. Article 59(1)(a) sets the age of 16 and Article 59(2) asks for health suited to the vehicle.

This is where many popular city e-bikes and small electric scooters sit. Whether a particular model sits there depends on its own power and design-speed figures, and the guide to [what counts as a moped under Vietnamese law]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) works through the definition.

**Electric motorcycles, above 4 kW or faster than 50 km/h.** These need a licence, matched to power under Article 57 (a machine of 4 kW or less that is designed for more than 50 km/h, such as a 70 km/h scooter, is not a moped and needs an A1):

| Power | Licence class |
|---|---|
| Above 4 kW, up to and including 11 kW | A1 |
| Above 11 kW | A |

Class A covers every electric two-wheeler above 11 kW — there is no higher separate class in the new law. Electric A1 licences issued before 1 January 2025 keep transitional coverage from 4 kW up to (but not including) 14 kW under Article 89.

Article 89(2)(a) lets an old A1 licence go on covering electric motors from 4 kW to below 14 kW until it is exchanged (the Law took effect on 1 January 2025, Article 88(1)), so such a licence can cover a 12 kW machine that a new A1 does not. On exchange or reissue Article 89(3)(a) turns it into class A restricted to two-wheeled motorbikes below 175 cm3 or below 14 kW, and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA prints that restriction on the licence (Appendix IX, point 4(g)). The guide to [old licence classes after 2025]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the other old classes.

## Why the distinction matters

Riders sometimes assume every electric two-wheeler is "just a bicycle". A scooter-style electric machine that looks small can still exceed 4 kW and require an A1 licence. Check the rated power and the top speed, not the size of the bike. Article 34(1)(g) draws its line on two figures at once, the power of the motor and the design speed, so a size, or a label such as e-bike or scooter, settles nothing.

## Four questions that place any electric two-wheeler

Take the machine's own figures and put four questions to it, in this order.

1. Is it a bicycle worked by pedals or hand cranks, with motor assistance that cuts out when pedalling stops or at 25 km/h (Article 34(2)(a) and (b))? Then it is a motorised pedal bicycle with no licence class.
2. Is it a two- or three-wheeled vehicle driven by a motor? Then it is a motor vehicle (Article 34(1)(e) and (g)), which needs a registration certificate and a plate (Article 35(1)(a)).
3. Is its design speed 50 km/h or less and its motor 4 kW or less (the limits of Article 34(1)(g))? Then it is a moped: no licence class, minimum age 16.
4. If it misses either limit, it is a motorbike (Article 34(1)(e)): class A1 up to 11 kW, class A above, minimum age 18 (Article 57(1) and Article 59(1)(b)). A three-wheeled motorbike takes class B1 (Article 57(1)(c)).

The two limits in step 3 are tested together, so a machine of modest power but a high design speed lands in step 4. [Electric motorbike power and licence classes]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-power-kw-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lays the bands out by kilowatts.

## What each licence class says about electric machines

### Class A1: up to 11 kW

Article 57(1)(a) gives class A1 to drivers of two-wheeled motorbikes with a cylinder capacity of up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor of up to 11 kW. One clause covers petrol and electric machines, so the Law has no electric-only class. The minimum age is 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), and an A1 licence has no time limit (Article 57(5)(a)). A licence issued to a foreigner on exchange is different: under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a), its term follows the visa, residence card or other paper in Article 24(1)(a) and the foreign licence, so read the expiry date on the card. [How to get an A1 licence in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the route, and [what an A1 licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) the scope.

### Class A: above 11 kW

Class A is written for two-wheeled motorbikes above 125 cm3 or with an electric motor above 11 kW, together with the vehicles named for class A1 (Article 57(1)(b)). A class A holder may therefore ride an electric two-wheeler of any power. One exception is a class A issued on exchange or reissue of an old A1 licence, which stays restricted to two-wheeled motorbikes below 175 cm3 or below 14 kW (Article 89(3)(a)). The [licence for fast electric motorbikes]({{ '/articles/fast-electric-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes up the machines at the top of the range.

### Three wheels: class B1

For a three-wheeled motorbike Article 57(1)(c) names class B1, which also covers the vehicles named for class A1. Article 34(1)(e) defines such a motorbike by an own weight of no more than 400 kg.

### What a licence check looks at

Article 56(1) asks a driver for a licence that still has points, is in force and suits the type of vehicle being driven, and the driver must carry it. A car class does not answer that test for a two-wheeler, as [a car licence and electric motorbikes]({{ '/articles/can-you-ride-electric-motorbike-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(4), lets a holder drive the classes stated on the licence, so it is the class printed on the licence that has to cover the machine.

## What else changes with the category

The same clauses decide several duties besides the licence, and they differ between the three groups.

| Duty | Pedal-assist e-bike | Electric moped | Electric motorbike |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licence | No class in Article 57 | None; rider conditions in Article 56(4) | A1 or A (Article 57(1)) |
| Minimum age | None named in Article 59(1) | 16 (Article 59(1)(a)) | 18 (Article 59(1)(b)) |
| Helmet | Article 31(3) | Article 33(2) | Article 33(2) |
| Passengers | One person, or up to two when the extra one is a child under 7 (Article 31(1)) | One person, or two in the cases of Article 33(1) | Same as the moped for a two-wheeler (Article 33(1)) |
| Registration certificate and plate | Article 35(1) covers motor vehicles; conditions for non-motorised vehicles are left to the Government (Article 35(5)) | Required (Article 35(1)(a)) | Required (Article 35(1)(a)) |

The two-passenger cases of Article 33(1) are a patient taken to emergency care, a person being escorted for breaking the law, a child under 12, and a frail or disabled person.

None of the three groups may use an expressway: Article 25(3) bars motorbikes, mopeds, similar vehicles and non-motorised vehicles, apart from people, vehicles and equipment serving the management and maintenance of the expressway. The [helmet rules for e-bikes and electric scooters]({{ '/articles/e-bike-helmet-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cover the helmet duty, and the [rules for motorised pedal bikes]({{ '/articles/xe-dap-mai-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cover the first group on its own.

## Worked examples

The examples are illustrative: the figures are invented and are not taken from any model's specification.

Imagine an electric scooter with a 2 kW motor and a stated design speed of 70 km/h, and test it against Article 34(1)(g). The motor is under the 4 kW line but the speed is above 50 km/h, so the machine is not a moped under Article 34(1)(g). It is a motorbike under Article 34(1)(e), it needs class A1 (Article 57(1)(a)) and its rider must be 18 or older (Article 59(1)(b)).

Imagine two electric motorbikes that differ only in motor power, one at 9 kW and one at 12 kW. The first is an A1 machine. The second needs class A, because Article 57(1)(b) starts class A above 11 kW.

## When a machine does not fit the boxes

### Throttle-only machines sold as bicycles

Article 34(2)(a) defines a bicycle as a vehicle with at least two wheels operated by human power through pedals or hand cranks, and clause 2(b) builds the motorised pedal bicycle on that base. A machine that runs on a throttle alone is not what that wording describes, so it is tested against the moped and motorbike definitions. A seller's label of "e-bike" does not change the test.

### Power figures that disagree

Article 34(1)(g) refers to the power of the motor and does not say whether rated or peak output is meant, and a maker can publish both. If one figure is under 4 kW and the other above it, the clause does not settle which counts, and Article 34(6) leaves the detail to the Minister of Construction. Ask the traffic police for a written answer before relying on the lower figure.

### Modified machines

Article 2(7) defines modification (cải tạo) as a change to the characteristics of a registered vehicle that changes its vehicle type as the competent authority prescribes, and Article 9(11) lists unauthorised modification among the prohibited acts. Whether a change that moves a registered electric machine across the 4 kW or 50 km/h line of Article 34(1)(g) counts is for the authority.

### Devices the Law does not name

Article 34 names no stand-up kick scooter, hoverboard or similar personal device. Clauses 1(h) and 2(e) add similar vehicles to the lists and clause 5 says a similar vehicle is managed under the rules for the type it resembles, but the text does not say which type a particular device resembles. Ask the traffic police before riding one on a public road.

## Foreign licences and electric machines

Article 57(6) lists four kinds of licence valid in Vietnam, and clauses (b), (c) and (d) ask for a licence suited to the type of vehicle it permits. The clause gives no conversion between a foreign category and the 4 kW and 11 kW lines, so get the traffic police's view in writing before riding an electric machine near either line. [How foreigners exchange a licence for a Vietnamese one]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and the [motorbike IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) cover the two routes.

## Penalties and points after the 2026 decree change

The Law sets the duties; fines and points for each offence are set by Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended from 15 August 2026, and the amounts in the [electric motorbike fines guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-fines-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) need checking against the amended text. The Law itself gives the points frame: a licence carries 12 points (Article 58(1)), and a driver whose points reach zero may not drive on that licence (Article 58(3)).

## Questions riders ask

### Do I need a licence for an electric scooter in Vietnam?

It depends on the machine, not on the word scooter. With a motor of 4 kW or less and a design speed of 50 km/h or less it is a moped and needs no licence class (Article 34(1)(g) and Article 56(1)). A machine above either line is a motorbike: A1 up to 11 kW and A above (Article 57(1)).

### Is an A1 licence enough for every electric bike?

No. Class A1 stops at an electric motor of 11 kW (Article 57(1)(a)), and above that the rider needs class A (Article 57(1)(b)). A three-wheeled motorbike needs class B1 (Article 57(1)(c)).

### Can a 16-year-old ride an electric bike?

On a moped-class machine, yes (Article 59(1)(a)), with the rider conditions of Article 56(4). A licence class starts at 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), so a faster or stronger machine has to wait; [riding an electric moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-electric-moped-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what the younger rider can do. For a pedal-assist bicycle Article 59(1) names no age.

### Does a licence-free electric need a plate?

A moped is a motor vehicle (Article 34(1)(g)), and Article 35(1)(a) asks motor vehicles for a registration certificate and a plate, so the licence exemption does not remove that condition. A pedal-assist bicycle is listed with the non-motorised vehicles (Article 34(2)(b)), outside the motor vehicles that Article 35(1) covers.
