Ask a Vietnamese commuter what sells a scooter and the answer is often not horsepower - it is the flat floor, the level footboard that turns a scooter into a load carrier. Here is what the verified pages show and why it matters.

## What the flat floor does

A flat footboard accepts what no underseat bay can: a 20-litre water crate, a bag of groceries, a boxed food order, a small suitcase standing between your feet. No rack, no top box, no straps. Honda quantifies the Vario 160's flat floor at 422 mm of usable width - the only manufacturer width figure published in our verified data (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## Verified flat-floor models

- Honda Vario 160 - flat floor, 422 mm (manufacturer figure)
- Yamaha FreeGo 125 - flat floor alongside the 25-litre bay
- Yamaha Latte 125 - flat floor with the 37-litre underseat bay

The classic contrast is the sporty scooter with a tunnel - the NMAX, NVX and SH shapes trade the floor for frame rigidity, styling and a central spine, which is exactly why the cargo comparison in our storage [guide]({{ '/articles/scooter-underseat-storage-comparison/' | relative_url }}) rarely features them.

## Flat floor versus litres

For a delivery rider, a 422 mm floor often beats a 25-litre bay: the bay holds your helmet and jacket, the floor holds the order. For a family, the floor takes the school bag while the bay takes the rain coats. Our delivery guide ranks the practicality numbers together - flat floors, bays, and economy.

## Cautions

Load on the floor changes handling - keep weight low and centred, and mind payload limits (our payload [guide]({{ '/articles/scooter-payload-load-limits-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the maths). A floor load also blocks quick dismounts, so keep it under control height. Renters: if a flat floor matters to your routine, confirm the model - contact us to confirm current availability.
