New clutch riders make the same handful of mistakes everywhere in the world. Here are the big five, and the fix for each.

## 1. Dumping the lever

Releasing the clutch in one motion stalls the engine or lurches the bike. Fix: pause in the friction zone. The pause is the technique — a half-second where the bike takes the load gently. The launch drill builds it in quickly.

## 2. Riding the clutch on the move

Once you are rolling, the lever should be fully out. Holding it half-in all the time glazes the plates and keeps your control vague. Fix: complete every release, and use the friction zone only below walking pace or when creeping in traffic.

## 3. Coasting with the clutch pulled in

Rolling downhill or toward a junction with the lever in gives up engine braking exactly when you want controlled slowing. Fix: keep the gearbox engaged and brake in gear; pull the clutch only in the last metres of a stop. Our engine-braking guide explains why this matters on descents.

## 4. Launching in the wrong gear

Trying to move off in second (or higher) feels like a weak, stalling engine. Fix: click all the way down to first before every launch, then shift up early — second comes almost immediately on an underbone.

## 5. Four fingers on the clutch in traffic

Winding all four fingers around the lever at every bump can cause accidental clutch pulls and slow reaction to the front brake. Fix: cover the lever with two fingers when creeping, four only for full squeezes and launches.

One bonus habit: practise one emergency stop on day one, from a low speed, in a safe place. The technique in our emergency [braking guide]({{ '/articles/emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) — both brakes, clutch in at the end — removes the fear of the front brake before traffic adds pressure. None of these mistakes is about talent; each is just a habit waiting to be replaced.
