A motorbike falling over at a standstill is a cheap accident. The same fall becomes expensive when the engine cover hits the pavement first — cases crack, oil drains onto the road, and a R200 repair becomes an engine rebuild. Frame sliders exist to put a sacrificial lump of plastic between the bike and the ground in exactly that moment.

## What they are

A frame slider is a hardened polymer puck mounted on a bracket that bolts to a reinforced frame or engine mounting point, positioned so that in a tip-over or low-side slide it contacts the ground before the frame spars, fairings and engine covers. In a stationary drop it typically saves the lever, mirror, fairing edge and case. In a sliding crash it can turn a written-off bike into a rideable one — or, if badly mounted, punch through something more expensive.

## The fitment mistakes that matter

Mounting to the wrong point is the classic failure: sliders that attach to thin cast brackets or fairing mounts bend the mounting into the frame or crack engine cases when loaded. Proper kits mount through existing frame or engine-bolt positions with a machined spacer. Second mistake: over-tightening into alloy cases, or guessing torque — bolt specs matter here, and they come from the kit or the manual, not instinct. If the kit instructions do not name your bike's mounting point and torque precisely, this is a shop job: installing-accessories-diy-vs-shop.

## How they relate to the other protection parts

Frame sliders are one layer in a system. Crash bars wrap a cage around the whole bike and are the standard answer for manual bikes and bigger machines that carry luggage or ride rough roads — the trade-offs are in crash-bars-engine-guards-motorbike. Skid plates defend the underside from below, off-road and speed-bump style — skid-plates-underbody-protection. Sliders defend the side in a drop; they are the most discreet of the three and the least obtrusive on a city bike.

## After any fall

Sliders are sacrificial: a slider that has taken a hit is done, and so is its mounting bolt — inspect both after every fall. And a fallen bike needs more than a glance, slider or not: checking-motorbike-after-fall-accident covers what to check before riding away. Sliders reduce the bill; they do not cancel the inspection.
