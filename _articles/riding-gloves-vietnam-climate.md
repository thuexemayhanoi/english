Hands are what hit the road first in most falls, and they are what you use to hold on, work the brakes and signal. Yet in Vietnam's heat, gloves are the piece of gear riders skip most. The answer is not "wear more" — it is "wear the right thin pair."

## Why gloves matter more in traffic than on highways

City falls happen at low speed, but they happen to hands that are about to meet tarmac, gravel or a hot exhaust. A simple layer of leather or textile turns a week of bandaged palms into a half-hour of "that was close." Gloves also protect from exhaust burns at traffic lights and keep grip reliable when your hands are sweaty or soaked.

## What works in the heat

Short-cuffed, ventilated gloves: mesh backs, perforated palms, snug but not tight. Thin leather palms grip better and last longer than fabric. Full textile summer gloves are fine for city riding. What does not work: thick winter gloves in 35°C — hands sweat, grip slips, and you take them off, which defeats the purpose.

## Rain season, second pair

Wet gloves lose grip and shape. A cheap waterproof pair for the rainy months keeps the summer pair dry at home. If you ride daily, two thin pairs beat one expensive do-everything pair — what-to-wear-riding-motorbike-vietnam breaks the whole outfit down.

## Fit and feel on Vietnamese controls

Try gloves with your hand around a grip-sized object. The palm must not bunch at the fingers; the seam must not sit under the brake fingers. You should be able to squeeze the lever firmly without the glove pulling your fingertips. ## A first-aid note

Road rash on palms heals slowly and badly, because you use them for everything. If you do fall, clean palm wounds thoroughly — first-aid-kit-motorbike-riders-vietnam covers the kit to carry. Better still, wear the gloves.
