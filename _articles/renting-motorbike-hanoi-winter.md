Winter motorbike rental in Hanoi works well if you plan for three things: layers against the wind, the short days and a cold start at handover. Hanoi's winter — roughly November through March — surprises visitors who expect tropical heat: mornings can sit near 10–15°C and feel colder at speed on a motorbike. Riding through it is routine for locals; being equipped for it is what separates a good winter rental from a miserable one.

## What the season is actually like

Cool, often dry and grey at times; December and January bring the coldest spells and occasional drizzle with poor visibility. The monthly averages below are the 1991-2020 climate figures for Hanoi that Viet Nam's national meteorological and hydrological service supplies to the World Meteorological Organization.

| Month | Mean daily minimum | Mean daily maximum | Days with rain |
|---|---|---|---|
| November | 19.2°C | 26.3°C | 7 |
| December | 15.4°C | 22.1°C | 7 |
| January | 14.6°C | 20.0°C | 11 |
| February | 16.2°C | 21.3°C | 12 |
| March | 18.7°C | 23.5°C | 18 |

A rain day is any day with at least 0.1 mm, so a day of light drizzle counts the same as a downpour. The mean daily minimum is under 20°C from November to March and above it in every other month, which is why "roughly November through March" fits the cool season, and January is the coolest month on average.

Averages hide cold spells. On 10 January 2021 the national forecasting centre put Hanoi at 10°C that morning and forecast lows of 7–9°C for the next, the Government news portal reported, along with health advice to wear several thin layers and keep the head, neck, chest, hands and feet warm. That is the logic of the kit below.

Rainfall adds a second pattern. December, January and February are the three driest months of the year (24, 28 and 19 mm, against 300 mm in July), yet March has 18 rain days, as many as July, with far less water (55 mm). The cool season is dry at its core and drizzly at its tail.

Air quality episodes can occur in this period, and long outdoor rides are the moments when that matters most; the air-quality section below gives the official advice. Daylight is shorter: dusk arrives before 18:00, and lights are a legal requirement between 18:00 and 06:00 anyway — see [Night Riding: Motorbike Light Rules in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

On timeanddate's table for Hanoi in December 2026, sunset falls between 17:14 on 1 December and 17:25 on 31 December. Against the shop's published hours of 09:00 to 21:00, that leaves more than three and a half hours of every December day after dark, so a pickup late in the day means riding the first kilometres by headlight.

## What to wear

The wind-chill on a moving bike is real. The working kit:

- A windproof outer layer over something warm — more important than heavy insulation
- Gloves; hands take the worst of it
- Closed shoes, and a scarf or face cover for the coldest mornings
- A clear visor or glasses for the eyes; winter wind and grit at speed sting

Everything stows under the seat or in a backpack when parked — see [What to Carry While Riding a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/what-to-carry-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Details that matter on a bike

Gloves must leave enough feel to work the brake and throttle smoothly, so try them on the bike's own controls rather than choosing the thickest pair. A neck tube or thin balaclava under the helmet closes the gap at the collar where the wind gets in, and any scarf should be tucked in so that no loose end can trail towards the wheel or chain.

A thin waterproof shell doubles as a windproof layer on the drizzly days that the table shows late in the season. Wet-road technique is in [Riding a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi Rain: What to Know]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}), and the wet-season kit and planning are in [Renting a Motorbike in Hanoi in the Rainy Season]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-hanoi-rainy-season/' | relative_url }}). Plan to bring or buy your own warm clothing.

## How winter changes the riding

- Tyres and brakes have not warmed up in the first minutes, and the road may be damp; ride gently until the bike feels settled
- Fog can lie thicker near water, so slow down on river crossings and bridges in the early morning
- Cold hands brake worse; budget extra distance on every stop
- Equipment still matters legally — lights, horn, mirrors, brakes — see [Required Equipment on a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-required-equipment-vietnam/' | relative_url }})

This guide stays on the rental side. Technique on damp winter roads, with numb hands, slick painted lines and early darkness, is in [Winter Riding on a Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/winter-riding-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### Fog and lights

Fog and haze make the bike's lights part of the rental check, because lights are how a rider is seen. Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 20(1), requires the front light to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility, so a grey winter afternoon can call for the light as much as dusk does. The fine bands for riding unlit are in [the light-use fines and tunnel rule]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

At handover, test the front light with the lights step of [the handover checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}). The riding side of fog, including how fast to go when you cannot see far, is in [Riding a Motorbike in Fog and Low Visibility]({{ '/articles/fog-and-low-visibility-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Air-quality days

The Government news portal reported on 11 December 2025 that Hanoi People's Committee Directive 19/CT-UBND put dense cloud and mist, low temperature and temperature inversion behind air-quality readings in the bad and very bad bands in the days before. A rider is outdoors, in traffic, for the whole ride, so the Ministry of Health's advice by band, as reported on 4 December 2025, is worth knowing before you plan a long day.

| VN_AQI band | Reading | Advice for an ordinary adult |
|---|---|---|
| Medium | 51–100 | Outdoor activity is not restricted |
| Poor | 101–150 | Spend less time outdoors, especially with sore eyes, cough or sore throat; limit or avoid activity in high-risk areas such as streets, junctions and construction sites |
| Bad | 151–200 | Limit outdoor activity and strenuous effort; if you must be outside, choose the less polluted time of day, rest more and avoid high-risk areas |

VN_AQI is the Vietnamese index, and a phone app can show a different scale, so check which one a number belongs to before applying the bands. The same report tells people to check the published air-quality figures regularly. On a bad day it is reasonable to ride less, and the questions below cover what to ask the shop about unused time.

## Rental-side differences

Handover inspection is unchanged ([Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }})), but add one check: does the bike start easily cold? Ask the shop to demonstrate a cold start. Battery issues and gummed-up fuel systems are the classic winter rental problems; if anything electrical feels off, report it — [What to Do If Your Rental Motorbike Does Not Feel Normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) lists the symptoms worth taking back.

### Cold starts by bike

What "starts easily" means depends on the bike. Honda's owner's manuals for the Wave Alpha, a carburettor underbone, and the Vision, a fuel-injected scooter, show the difference: the Wave Alpha's routine depends on the air temperature, while the Vision has no choke. The Wave Alpha manual gives three cases:

- From 10°C to 35°C: choke lever fully up to start, halfway down as soon as the engine runs, fully off about half a minute later
- At 35°C or above: no choke; the throttle opened about one-eighth to one-quarter, then the start button, and the same routine restarts a hot engine
- At 10°C or below: the middle-band steps up to the warm-up, then keep warming the engine until it runs evenly and answers the throttle with the choke fully off

Every monthly average in the table above sits inside the middle band, so that is the routine on an ordinary winter morning; the last case matters only if a cold spell takes the air down to 10°C. The Vision manual gives a single routine whether the engine is hot or cold: no choke, throttle fully closed, and the start switch released as soon as the engine starts.

Which model and version you are given is a question for the shop, and other models have their own routines, so ask to see the routine on your bike before you leave. A bike that needs repeated cranking to start, or that dies as soon as it is left to idle, is worth reporting at the counter rather than on the road. The general cold-morning habits are in [Cold Mornings: Starting a Motorbike in Hanoi's Winter]({{ '/articles/cold-start-motorbike-winter-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### What to settle before you ride away

- **Helmet and visor.** Ask which helmet comes with the bike and whether it has a visor.
- **Return time.** With December sunsets between 17:14 and 17:25, plan to be back with margin. The published late-return charge is 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged (typically 150,000–200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle).
- **Unused time.** Early return carries no automatic refund entitlement, so ask what, if anything, can be adjusted if a bad-air or drizzly day keeps you off the road.
- **Cover.** Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance; customers arrange their own, and a slip on a damp road is the case to check your own cover for.

## Questions riders ask about winter rentals

### Will a rental motorbike start on a cold Hanoi morning?

An average winter morning needs nothing beyond the bike's normal routine, as the cold-start section above shows. If the engine does not start within about five seconds, both manuals say to turn the ignition off and wait ten seconds before trying again so that the battery voltage can recover. Long cranking drains a battery when it is weakest. If the bike still will not start, the steps are in [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### Can I ride when the air quality is bad?

The Ministry of Health's text is health advice, not a traffic rule. For the bad band (VN_AQI 151–200), the report says that ordinary people who must travel should use public transport more and limit motorbikes and bicycles, to reduce exposure. Treat that as a reason to keep a plan B for the day and to ask the shop what can be adjusted if you leave the bike parked.

### Does a neck tube count as a mask?

A neck tube or balaclava keeps wind off the face and neck but is not a filter: [Neck Gaiters and Dust Masks for Riders]({{ '/articles/neck-gaiters-dust-masks-riders/' | relative_url }}) says no fabric mask filters at the level of certified respiratory protection. The Ministry's report advises a mask of assured quality, worn correctly, when going out. Try any mask under the helmet before the ride, because a mask that slips is a distraction at speed.

### Which suits a Hanoi winter better, a scooter or a semi-automatic?

Neither is ruled out. The Vision manual gives one start routine, hot or cold, with no choke, while the Wave Alpha's has more steps and depends on the air temperature. A scooter's front legshield also blocks some of the wind from the legs, which an underbone offers less of.
