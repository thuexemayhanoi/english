Navigation is the quiet deal-breaker of Vietnamese motorbike trips. Signal is generally good — but the mountains that are worth riding are precisely where coverage drops, and the routing defaults that work for cars can send motorbikes into trouble.

## The expressway trap

The single biggest navigation mistake: car-mode routing that puts you on an expressway (CT-class roads). Motorbikes are
 banned from Vietnamese expressways, and map apps will happily offer them. Learn to refuse CT-road suggestions and follow the old national highways instead — QL (quốc lộ) roads are the two-wheel backbone of the country.

## Google Maps

The default for most riders: good coverage of Vietnam, live traffic, and Street View useful for eyeballing a road before you commit to it. The critical habit is downloading offline areas for the whole route before leaving Hanoi — offline maps keep navigation working through mountain dead zones, and they should be refreshed weekly because road data changes. Download generously around your route, not just the line itself.

## OSM-based offline apps

For mountain riding, OpenStreetMap-based apps (OsmAnd, Organic Maps and similar) have a different strength: they show small roads, tracks, ferries and elevation in fine detail, often including scenic back routes that Google routes you away from. Many long-distance riders keep both — Google for the corridor, OSM for exploring — with the full region downloaded offline. Once the data is on the phone, both families navigate entirely from GPS, so no data plan is needed in a dead zone.

## Habits that matter more than the app

- Mount the phone properly and program the destination before moving (see the phone-mount guide — and remember that touching the phone while riding is a fine).
- Screenshot or write down the route’s key towns as a paper fallback; batteries die.
- Ask locally at fuel stops when a road "looks wrong" — closures and roadwork spread faster by word of mouth than by app.
- In fog or at night, navigate less and stop more.
