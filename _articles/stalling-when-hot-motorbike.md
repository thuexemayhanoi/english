A bike that runs perfectly in the morning and dies after forty minutes of hot traffic is one of motorcycling's most frustrating patterns — the fault hides precisely when the bike is at the mechanic's and appears only in a jam on Giai Phong at noon. But hot-stalling has a short suspect list, and it is learnable.

## Why heat changes everything

Heat expands metal, thins fuel, and raises the electrical resistance of failing components. A marginal part that works cold is defeated hot. So the pattern to record is exact: dies only hot? Restarts after cooling? That pattern is the diagnosis.

## Suspect one: ignition

The classic: an ignition coil or ignition module that fails hot and recovers cold. As components age, their windings' insulation breaks down exactly when hot — the engine dies abruptly, restarts after a cooling rest, and repeats with growing frequency. If the bike dies hot with zero warning (like a switch) and restarts minutes later, ignition leads the list. A plug cap or plug that is marginal can behave the same. This is one to hand to a mechanic with heat-pattern data; electrical parts-swapping by guesswork gets expensive.

## Suspect two: valve clearances

Valves with too little clearance close up as the engine heats: hot metal expansion takes up the remaining gap, the valve stops sealing fully, compression drops, and the engine runs rough and stalls when hot — but starts fine cold. The valve-clearance basics are in valve-clearances-motorbike-basics. This is the most common mechanical hot-stall on small Vietnamese engines, and checking clearances is a routine service item, not open-heart surgery.

## Suspect three: fuel problems of heat

- **Vapour lock / fuel boiling.** Fuel lines routed too near the engine, or a hot day in heavy traffic, can boil fuel in the line or carburettor: bubbles block flow. The engine dies warm, restarts after a rest. Inspect routing; a heat-soaked carburettor also floods easily.
- **A tank not venting.** Vacuum builds as fuel is used and starves the engine — the cap-open whoosh test; see fuel-starvation-power-loss-motorbike.
- **Idle set wrong when warm.** A cold-start idle that is merely high, but is compensating for a rich-running or rough condition, fails when the engine comes down to hot idle — the adjustment logic is in idle-speed-adjustment-motorbike.

## Suspect four: general overheating

An engine genuinely running hot loses power and stalls — the cooling-side diagnosis is in motorbike-overheating-causes, and its rough-idle cousin is in rough-idle-motorbike-troubleshooting.

## What to do in traffic

When the engine starts faltering hot: get out of the lane safely, find shade, and let it cool. Repeated hot stalls are not "a quirk" — each one happens in the worst place, at the worst time. Record the pattern (minutes riding, temperature, restart time) and give the mechanic data: with heat faults, the description is most of the diagnosis.
