Home charging is the default way electric two-wheelers are fed in Vietnam — batteries are designed for household electricity, and the ritual replaces the fuel stop.

## How it works

Most Vietnamese electrics charge the battery on the bike through a supplied charger plugged into a normal household socket; on many models the battery is removable, so you can carry the pack upstairs and charge it where the bike cannot go. VinFast's swappable 1.5 kWh LFP packs are explicitly designed to be charged at home or at public stations, or swapped at cabinets instead.

## How long it takes

Official figures give a sense of scale: the Dat Bike Weaver 200 needs about 1 hour for its first 100 km of claimed range and 3 hours for a full 200 km charge. Smaller moped-class packs with a fraction of that capacity generally finish overnight. Match your charging window to your riding day: an overnight charge covers a commute; a midday top-up covers the evening.

## Habits that matter

- **Charge where it is ventilated** — not inside a sealed room or blocking an exit, and out of direct sun.
- **Follow the manufacturer's charger** — packs and chargers are paired; substitutions risk damage.
- **Do not run to empty every time** — frequent full discharges age lithium packs faster than partial cycling.
- **Unplug when full** rather than leaving packs on the charger for days.

## Charging without a home socket

If you park on the street or in a shared house without sockets, home charging is not available to you — that is the one real deal-breaker. Battery swapping (9,000 VND per swap in VinFast's network) or fast-charging stations then become your fuel model.
