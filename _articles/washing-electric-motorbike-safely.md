Washing an electric motorbike is easy — the design tolerates rain — but the electrics change a few rules from petrol-bike washday.

## What the ratings permit

The IP code on your bike defines what water it is built for. Dat Bike's Weaver 200 is IP65-rated — protected against water jets from any direction, which covers bucket-and-hose washing — and the Weaver++ is rated IP67, higher still. Mopeds and e-bikes without a published rating should be treated conservatively: damp cloth, no direct jets at connectors or controls.

## The safe wash

- **Low-pressure water or a bucket** — never a pressure washer pointed at the battery bay, controller, display or charging port. Pressure jets defeat seals.
- **Avoid the charging port and keyholes** directly; wipe around them.
- **Battery out where possible.** Washing with the bay empty is the cleanest option on removable-pack models.
- **Mild soap, soft cloth** for the display and plastics; brake discs want degreaser-free cleaning so pads keep biting.
- **Dry the connectors** before reinserting any battery or cap.

## The one hard rule

**Never charge a wet bike or a wet battery.** Water bridging charging contacts is the genuine hazard in the whole electric-washing subject. Dry everything, let the charging port air-dry, and only then plug in. This is also why flooded-bike handling matters: a bike that has sat in deep water should be inspected before its next charge, not washed and plugged in.

## Cadence

City grime plus Hanoi humidity argue for a regular gentle wash rather than rare aggressive ones — dirt holds moisture against metal and connectors. Fold washing into the maintenance routine and the bike will stay as corrosion-free as the day it came.
