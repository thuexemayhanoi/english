## Honda Vision - the all-rounder

A familiar sight all over Vietnam ([full Vision 110 review]({{ '/articles/honda-vision-110-review/' | relative_url }})), and the reason is balance: light, low seat, tidy under-seat storage, and a shape that shrinks traffic gaps. Published rental rates: 200,000 VND per day, 800,000-1,000,000 VND per week, 1,800,000-2,000,000 VND per month. The Vision suits most riders, and it is the safe pick if you are choosing blind.

Honda Vietnam's published figures put the Vision in the 110 cc class, with an air-cooled engine, a 97 kg kerb weight and a 16-inch front wheel. The weight is the number a visitor notices first: it is the lighter of the two scooters in the table with a verified weight, so it is the easier one to walk backwards out of a parking row or hold up at a standstill. A larger front wheel also tends to roll over a pothole or a patched road edge with less of a jolt.

## Honda Air Blade - the step up in engine size

The Air Blade is on the published list at 200,000 VND per day, the same daily rate as the Vision, so the choice between them turns on engine size, weight and storage and not on price. For the Air Blade 125, Honda Vietnam publishes a 125 cc liquid-cooled engine, a 110 kg kerb weight, a 23.2 L underseat compartment that takes two half-helmets, and a USB-C charge port. Against the Vision that is a step up from the 110 cc class to 125 cc, with 13 kg more scooter to manage in a tight parking row.

Honda sells the Air Blade as a 125 and as a 160, and the published rate does not name a version. The verified figures here are for the 125, so ask which version a given bike is: the licence class follows engine capacity, as the licence check below explains. ## Honda Click - the sportier sibling

Sharper, sportier bodywork than the Vision. Published rates undercut the Vision: 150,000 VND per day, 600,000-700,000 VND per week, 1,000,000-1,200,000 VND per month. A Click is the value pick when the budget matters and the riding is city-only.

## Yamaha Mio - the light alternative

Yamaha's answer to the Click class: light, easy to park, easy to flick through traffic. Published rates match the Click: 150,000 VND per day, 600,000-700,000 VND per week, 1,000,000-1,200,000 VND per month.

The Click and Mio share a published daily rate, so price does not separate them. If both are on offer, sit on each and let fit decide.

## How to choose between them

- Seat height and fit matter more than spec sheets - sit on all four; the one that lets both feet nearly flat is the one.
- The four are all around the 110-125 cc class: enough for Hanoi, enough for day trips, not tourers.
- Ask what the shop's maintenance covers. It may include an oil change, tyre-pressure checks and a normal inspection, while consumables such as tyres and inner tubes may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.
- Rate differences are small but real on long rentals: a month on a Click/Mio versus a Vision differs by several hundred thousand dong.

### Rider size and weight

Kerb weight is the weight of the scooter itself, ready to ride. It tells you how much machine you are walking, parking and steadying, not how much load it can carry. Honda Vietnam publishes 97 kg for the Vision and 110 kg for the Air Blade 125, and the table holds no verified weight for the Click or Mio.

A smaller or newer rider will find the lighter scooter easier at walking pace. A taller or heavier rider should check the fit on the larger scooter, because knee room and the reach to the bars show up the moment you sit down. This guide holds no maximum-load figure for any of the four, so there is no verified number to quote for rider, passenger and luggage together. A heavier rider, or two adults with bags, is a good reason to ask the shop which scooter it recommends for that load.

### Luggage and passengers

The Air Blade 125 has a published 23.2 L underseat compartment that takes two half-helmets. This guide holds no verified underseat figure for the Vision, Click or Mio, so try the bay with your own helmet and day bag. Honda's wording is two half-helmets, so a full-face helmet or a bulkier bag needs testing rather than assuming.

Passengers are a legal question before they are a mechanical one. A motorbike may carry one passenger besides the rider, and everyone on board must wear a fastened, compliant helmet (Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15, as the [passenger rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-passenger-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out). A couple on one scooter is lawful; three people on one bike is not, outside the narrow exceptions the law lists. For two adults, the Air Blade's larger engine class and heavier build give more margin than the 110 cc Vision, and any scooter you pick should be tried with both people on board before you commit.

### Your route

### Licence class by engine capacity

A new A1 licence covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc (Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15), with a minimum age of 18 (Article 59). An A1 issued before 1 January 2025 keeps a wider scope that runs up to but not including 175 cc (Article 89). The [A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) places the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio among the bikes inside A1. For a foreign visitor, recognition depends on the home licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle category, and riding with no valid licence of any kind is an offence.

Engine capacity decides the class, not the model name, so ask for the capacity of the exact scooter you are offered. That matters most for the Air Blade: a 160 cc version is above the 125 cc line of a new A1, so ask before you assume your licence covers it. The licence routes visitors use, and what the rental side needs, are set out in the [guide for foreign renters]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## What to check when you collect a scooter

The [rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) covers the checks that apply to every bike: lights, brakes, mirrors, tyres, the fuel level and photographing existing damage. These come on top of it and are specific to an automatic scooter.

1. **Start-up and idle.** Start it yourself and listen. It should fire promptly and settle into a steady idle without stalling.
2. **Pull-away.** On a quiet stretch, pull away gently. The take-off should be smooth, with no shudder, slip or whine. Those are the signs of a worn drive described in the [CVT belt and variator guide]({{ '/articles/cvt-belt-variator-scooter-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and any of them is a conversation with the shop before you ride off.
3. **Creep and grab.** At a standstill the scooter should not creep forward or grab as you touch the throttle.
4. **Brake levers.** A scooter has no foot pedal: the two levers work the two wheels. Ask which lever works which wheel and whether the system links them, then try each one at walking pace before you reach traffic.
5. **Seat, key and fuel filler.** Ask to be shown how the seat, the underseat bay and the fuel filler open, and how the key or smart key works if the scooter has one.
6. **Underseat bay.** Open and close it, check that the latch holds and the bay is dry, then fit your own helmet and day bag. The published Air Blade 125 capacity is 23.2 L; for the other three, the test is the only answer.
7. **Stands.** The side stand should lift cleanly and spring back, and a centre stand, where fitted, should hold the scooter firmly.
8. **Passenger fittings.** If you will carry a passenger, check that the rear footrests fold out and lock and that the grab rail is firm.
9. **Charging and dashboard.** If the scooter has a USB-C port, as the published Air Blade 125 specification lists, try it with your own cable. Then photograph the dashboard showing the fuel gauge and the distance reading, so the starting point is on record.

## Common questions

### Which scooter is best for a first-time visitor in Hanoi?

The Honda Vision is a sensible place to start: it is the lighter of the two scooters with a published weight, 97 kg against 110 kg for the Air Blade 125. Check the fit before you agree to it, tell the shop how much riding you have done, and ask which scooter it would put you on.

### How much does Hanoi scooter rental cost per day?

The published daily rate is 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and the Honda Air Blade, and 150,000 VND for the Honda Click and the Yamaha Mio. A deposit is typically taken on top, as the terms section above sets out.

### Do I need a licence to rent a scooter in Hanoi?

All four are above 50 cc, so a licence Vietnam recognises is needed to ride them. A new A1 licence covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc (Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15), and a foreign visitor relies on a home licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle category.

### Can two people ride one rental scooter?

Vietnamese law allows one passenger besides the rider, and each person on board needs a fastened, compliant helmet (Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15). A third person falls outside the law's narrow exceptions.

### What is the difference between the Honda Vision and the Honda Air Blade?

On Honda Vietnam's published figures, the Vision is a 110 cc class air-cooled scooter weighing 97 kg, and the Air Blade 125 is a liquid-cooled 125 cc scooter weighing 110 kg with a 23.2 L underseat bay. Both carry the same published daily rate, so the choice turns on engine size, weight and storage.
