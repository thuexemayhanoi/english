The NMAX and the SH160i are the two status scooters of Vietnamese city streets - one Japanese-premium from Yamaha, one Italian-styled Honda icon. Both come with verified manufacturer data, so this comparison sticks to published figures (Yamaha and Honda Vietnam pages, retrieved 23 September 2026).

## Engine

The SH160i's 156.9 cc eSP+ engine publishes 12.4 kW at 8,500 rpm and 14.8 Nm at 6,500 rpm; the NMAX's 155.1 cc Blue Core VVA publishes 11.3 kW at 8,000 rpm and 14.2 Nm at 6,500 rpm. On paper the SH is slightly ahead on both figures; in the city both are far more than quick enough, and the SH's premium comes as much from badge and resale as from acceleration.

## Safety hardware

NMAX: dual-channel ABS (discs front and rear) plus traction control. SH160i: ABS on both wheels plus Honda's HSTC traction control on the Sport, Special and Premium versions. This is the closest equipment fight in the Vietnamese scooter market - both flagships protect both ends of the braking equation. Our TCS vs ABS guide explains what each layer does.

## Size and comfort

The SH160i is the bigger, heavier machine: 134 kg against 131 kg, seat 799 mm against 770 mm. The SH's 16-inch front wheel (100/80-16) gives it motorway stability that the NMAX's 13-inch wheels cannot match - the classic SH trait covered in our 16-inch wheel guide. The NMAX counters with a roomier two-level seat.

## Storage and range

SH160i: 28 litres underseat, 7-litre tank, published 2.34 L/100 km. NMAX: 24 litres, 7.1-litre tank, 2.26 L/100 km. The SH carries more, the NMAX travels slightly further per litre.

## Price

Honda listed the SH160i Sport at 104,490,000 VND; Yamaha listed the NMAX from 69,000,000 VND. That gap is the SH's badge premium in one number.

## Verdict

The SH160i buys status, 16-inch stability and Honda's strongest resale. The NMAX buys nearly identical safety hardware and 35 million VND of change.
