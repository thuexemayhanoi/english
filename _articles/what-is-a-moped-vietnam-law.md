"Moped" has a precise legal meaning in Vietnam, and it is narrower than the everyday use of the word. Article 3 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines the moped category — xe gắn máy — by three limits at once: an engine of 50 cc or less, or an electric motor of up to 4 kW, and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. Meet all three and the machine is a moped; miss one and it is something else legally.

## What the category buys you

The moped category is licence-free: Article 59 sets the minimum age at 16 and requires no licence class, because there is no licence class to require. That is the whole point of the definition — the category exists to mark the line below which the state does not demand a test.

## What it does not buy you

Everything else still applies. Helmets are compulsory for moped riders and passengers, with the same 400,000–600,000 VND fine as motorbikes. The moped has its own speed ceiling — 40 km/h under the speed regulation wherever it rides — and its own fine bands in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which are lighter than motorbike bands in the lower tiers but converge with them for serious violations. A moped rider who causes an accident sits in the same 10,000,000–14,000,000 VND band as any motorbike rider.

## The boundaries riders confuse

Three confusions recur. First, 50 cc mopeds versus 50 cc-class motorbikes: the definition turns on design speed and category, not on displacement alone. Second, pedal e-bikes: xe đạp máy (motorised pedal bikes) are a separate category outside the motorised classification, with their own rules. Third, tuned machines: a de-restricted "moped" that travels faster than its design speed is no longer treated as the machine its plate describes — riding it out of category is a licence-conditions violation.

## Why the definition matters at rental counters

In Hanoi the practical question is which side of the moped line a rented electric sits on. Ask for the registration category of the machine: moped-category electrics are the licence-free choice for 16-year-olds and licence-less adults; anything faster needs the A1.
