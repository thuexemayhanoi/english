"Moped" has a precise legal meaning in Vietnam, and it is narrower than the everyday use of the word. Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines the moped category — xe gắn máy — by three limits, two of which apply to any one machine: a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, depending on the motor, an engine of 50 cc or less or an electric motor of up to 4 kW.

Meet both and the machine is a moped; miss one and it is something else legally. The clauses below are read in the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH of the Law as amended by Law 118/2025/QH15 (in force from 1 July 2026); none of them is among those the amendment changed.

## The definition, word for word

Article 34(1)(g) reads, in the Vietnamese original:

> Xe gắn máy là xe có hai hoặc ba bánh chạy bằng động cơ, được thiết kế, sản xuất để hoạt động trên đường bộ, có vận tốc thiết kế không lớn hơn 50 km/h; nếu động cơ dẫn động là động cơ nhiệt thì dung tích làm việc hoặc dung tích tương đương không lớn hơn 50 cm3; nếu động cơ dẫn động là động cơ điện thì công suất của động cơ không lớn hơn 04 kW; xe gắn máy không bao gồm xe đạp máy;

A plain English translation, not an official one:

> A moped is a vehicle with two or three wheels, driven by a motor, designed and manufactured to operate on roads, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h; if the driving motor is a heat engine, its working capacity or equivalent capacity is no more than 50 cm3; if the driving motor is an electric motor, the motor's power is no more than 4 kW; a moped does not include a motorised pedal bicycle.

### How the three limits combine

The design-speed limit applies to every machine. The two motor limits are neither both required nor a free choice: each opens with "if" (nếu) and applies only to its own kind of motor, so a petrol machine is measured in cubic centimetres and a battery machine in kilowatts. Two tests therefore decide any one bike, its design speed and the limit for its motor.

Neither test can rescue the other. A 49 cc engine in a machine designed to go faster than 50 km/h fails the speed test, and so does a sub-4 kW electric motor in a machine designed for more. In the other direction, a 110 cc scooter is not a moped however gently it is ridden, because its engine is above the capacity limit.

Two smaller points sit in the same sentence. "Hai hoặc ba bánh" means two or three wheels, so a three-wheeler can be a moped. And "design speed" (vận tốc thiết kế) is not defined separately in the consolidated text; because the sentence ties it to how the vehicle is "designed and manufactured", the natural source for the figure is the manufacturer's data rather than a test ride.

## The motorcycle definition beside it

Article 34(1)(e) defines the motorcycle (xe mô tô) by exclusion: a vehicle with two or three wheels, driven by a motor, designed and manufactured to operate on roads, "except mopeds" (trừ xe gắn máy); for a three-wheeler, the own weight (khối lượng bản thân) must be no more than 400 kg.

The moped sentence carries every limit and the motorcycle sentence adds only the exception and the 400 kg cap for a three-wheeler, so a machine does not have to prove that it is a motorcycle: a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle that fails the moped test lands there. A three-wheeler above 400 kg own weight falls under the car definition in Article 34(1)(a) instead.

## Electric bicycles and pedal-assisted bikes

The moped sentence excludes them by name: "xe gắn máy không bao gồm xe đạp máy" (a moped does not include a motorised pedal bicycle). They are defined in Article 34(2), the list of xe thô sơ (simple vehicles), which the Law keeps apart from the motor vehicles (xe cơ giới) of Article 34(1). Under Article 34(2)(b), a motorised pedal bicycle, the electric bicycle (xe đạp điện) included, is a bicycle with motor assistance, where the motor's power source is cut off when the rider stops pedalling or when the vehicle reaches 25 km/h.

Two tests are built into that definition. It has to be a pedalled bicycle (Article 34(2)(a): at least two wheels, operated by human power through pedals or hand cranks), and its motor has to stop helping when pedalling stops or at 25 km/h. A scooter-style machine that moves on a throttle with no pedalling does not fit, so it is judged under the moped or motorcycle definitions.

Being a bicycle in law removes the licence class, not the rules. Article 57 lists no class for the category and Article 59(1) names no age for it. Article 31(3) still requires the rider and any passenger to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, fastened properly. The [guide to motorised pedal bikes]({{ '/articles/xe-dap-mai-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the category on its own.

## How the classes line up

| Class | Article 34 definition | Licence | Minimum age |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motorised pedal bicycle (xe đạp máy) | Motor cut off when pedalling stops or at 25 km/h (34(2)(b)) | No class in Article 57 | None named in Article 59(1) |
| Moped (xe gắn máy) | Design speed 50 km/h or less; 50 cm3 or less, or 4 kW or less if electric (34(1)(g)) | None; the Article 56(4) conditions | 16 (59(1)(a)) |
| Motorcycle, two wheels, up to 125 cm3 or 11 kW | Motor vehicle that is not a moped (34(1)(e)) | A1 (57(1)(a)) | 18 (59(1)(b)) |
| Motorcycle, two wheels, above 125 cm3 or 11 kW | Same definition | A (57(1)(b)) | 18 |
| Motorcycle, three wheels | Own weight 400 kg or less (34(1)(e)) | B1 (57(1)(c)) | 18 |

## What the category buys you

The moped category is licence-free: Article 59 sets the [minimum age at 16]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and requires no licence class, because there is no licence class to require. That is the whole point of the definition — the category exists to mark the line below which the state does not demand a test.

The exemption itself sits in Article 56(1), which asks every driver for a licence of the right class "except moped drivers referred to in clause 4" (trừ người lái xe gắn máy quy định tại khoản 4 Điều này). Clause 4 says what a moped rider needs instead: to understand the road traffic rules, to have the skill to control the vehicle, and to meet the age and health conditions of Article 59(1)(a) and 59(2). That is a duty on the rider, and Article 56(4) names no test and no certificate. [The guide to riding a moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-50cc-at-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers how it works for 16- and 17-year-olds.

Article 59(1)(a) lets a person who has reached 16 drive a moped, and Article 59(2) requires health suited to the vehicle, leaving the standards to the Minister of Health. Article 9(7) prohibits handing a motor vehicle to someone who does not meet the legal conditions to ride it, so a moped should not be handed to a rider under 16.

## What it does not buy you

Everything else still applies. Helmets are compulsory for moped riders and passengers: Article 33(2) requires the driver and any passenger of a two-wheeled motorcycle, a three-wheeled motorcycle or a moped to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly. The [helmet rules for mopeds]({{ '/articles/helmet-rules-50cc-mopeds/' | relative_url }}) cover the practical side.

Article 33(1) allows a moped one passenger, or two for a patient going to emergency care, a person being escorted for breaking the law, a child under 12, or a frail or disabled person.

The speed ceiling is lower than the design speed. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets the maximum operating speed of mopeds and similar vehicles at 40 km/h (Article 7) on roads other than expressways, which Article 25(3) of the Law closes to mopeds in any case. The 50 km/h in the definition is what the machine is designed to do; the 40 km/h is the maximum operating speed on road sections with no maximum-speed or safe-distance sign (Circular Article 4(2)). [Speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-speed-limits-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets the moped figure beside the motorbike ones.

Papers apply too. Article 56(1) lists the papers a driver carries, and the moped exception is written into its licence requirement; the clause does not say the other papers fall away. At handover, ask how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop; [what a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through the set.

Penalties are set by decree, not by the Law: Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, as changed by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026. This page quotes no amounts, so check any figure against the current decree; [the guide to fines for 50cc moped riders]({{ '/articles/50cc-fines-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the place to start.

## The boundaries riders confuse

Three confusions recur. First, 50 cc mopeds versus 50 cc-class motorbikes: the definition turns on design speed and category, not on displacement alone. Second, pedal e-bikes: xe đạp máy (motorised pedal bikes) are a separate category outside the motorised classification, with their own rules. Third, tuned machines: a moped altered to go faster or fitted with a bigger engine is no longer the machine its registration describes, and Article 9(11) lists unauthorised modification (cải tạo trái phép) among the prohibited acts.

On the third point, Article 2(7) defines modification (cải tạo) as a change to the characteristics of a registered vehicle, or an imported used one, that results in a change of vehicle type as the competent authority prescribes. Whether a particular alteration crosses that line is for the authority, not for this page; a renter or buyer can ask whether the bike is as the manufacturer made it.

## More misreadings, answered from the text

**"Any small bike is a moped."** That is the everyday sense of the word, not the legal one. Honda Vietnam's pages give the Wave Alpha 110 an engine of 109.2 cc and the Vision one of 110 cc, more than twice the 50 cm3 limit, so both are motorcycles: an A1 licence, from age 18. Sixteen is the age for the moped class only (Article 59(1)(a)).

**"A visitor cannot use the moped class."** Neither Article 56(4) nor Article 59(1)(a) mentions nationality; both speak of the person driving a moped. A visitor who meets the age and skill conditions stands where a resident stands, and the rest of the rules stay with them too. [Foreigners on a 50cc bike without a licence]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-ride-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) answers the question in full.

## How to tell what a particular bike is

A rental or a purchase puts the question in practical form: is this machine a moped? The definition suggests four checks, and none of them depends on a badge.

1. **The registration certificate.** It may show the engine capacity (dung tích); ask whether it does, and to see that entry. A capacity above 50 cm3 settles the matter for a petrol bike: it is not a moped.
2. **The manufacturer's stated speed.** The speed half of the test is a design figure, so the maker's data is the natural place to look, not a speedometer reading. Ask what speed the manufacturer states for the model, and whether it is called a design speed or a top speed, because design speed is the law's term.
3. **Both numbers for an electric bike.** Ask for the motor's power in kilowatts and for the stated speed; neither settles the class alone.
4. **Not the sticker.** "50cc" on a fairing, in a seller's description or in a listing title is not evidence of the class.

Official manufacturer pages show why an electric bike needs two numbers. Honda's Vietnamese page for the ICON e: calls it an electric moped (xe gắn máy điện) and gives a maximum motor power of 1.81 kW and a top speed of 48 km/h, inside both limits, taking the stated top speed as the design speed. Yamaha's page for the NEO's gives a rated power of 2 kW and a maximum of 2.3 kW but no top speed, so the page alone does not settle its class. VinFast's Evo has a 2,450 W motor, under the 4 kW line, but a top speed of up to 70 km/h, so on the same reading it fails the speed test. [Electric mopeds versus electric motorcycles]({{ '/articles/electric-moped-vs-electric-motorcycle-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works through that comparison.
