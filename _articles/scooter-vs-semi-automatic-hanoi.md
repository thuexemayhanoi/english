The two machines that carry Hanoi are the automatic scooter and the semi-automatic underbone. Choosing between them is a short exercise in honesty about your riding.

The short answer to scooter vs semi automatic in Hanoi: take the scooter if you have never shifted gears with your foot, if you carry a passenger or shopping, or if you want the least to manage in stop-start traffic. Take the semi-automatic underbone if you already shift with your foot and can live with a rack bag instead of under-seat storage. Price does not settle it, because the published day rate follows the model and not the gearbox. This guide covers only that two-way choice: [the three-way comparison that also covers clutch bikes]({{ '/articles/automatic-vs-manual-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is a separate guide, and [the guide to the best scooters to rent in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-to-rent-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) compares the four scooters one by one.

## Where the work goes: hands, feet and habits

The two types spread the work differently. On a typical automatic scooter both brakes are hand levers, with the left lever working the rear brake (some models link the two brakes, so ask how the bike you are given works), and the feet have nothing to do but rest. On a semi-automatic underbone the right hand keeps the throttle and the front brake, the right foot works the rear brake pedal and the left foot works the gear lever.

A rider who knows one type and steps onto the other carries habits that point the wrong way:

- Underbone to scooter: the left foot reaches for a gear lever that is not there, and the rear brake has moved from the right foot to the left hand.
- Scooter to underbone: the left hand looks for a rear brake lever that is not there, and the right foot has to find a pedal. A startled rider who squeezes only the right-hand lever has braked one wheel.

Both slips are cheap to avoid while the bike is parked: touch each control, say aloud what it does, then ride off.

## The scooter (Honda Vision, Click, Yamaha Mio, Air Blade)

Automatic, step-through, under-seat storage. The pros: zero learning curve on the transmission, best-in-class storage (helmet, jacket, groceries), the roomiest option for carrying a passenger, and the easiest machine for a first-time rider in Vietnamese traffic. The cons: the Vision (686 mm) and Air Blade 125 (687 mm) are wider than the Sirius in the tightest alleys, though not wider than the Wave Alpha, slightly higher day rates on some models (150,000-200,000 VND), and more plastic bodywork to damage in a drop.

Honda Vietnam lists the Vision as an automatic (CVT) of 109.5 cc weighing 97 kg, with an 80/90-16 front tyre and a 90/90-14 rear, and the Air Blade 125 as an automatic (CVT) of 125 cc weighing 110 kg. This guide quotes no manufacturer figures for the Click or the Mio, so it makes no claim about their engines, weight or storage.

## The semi-automatic underbone (Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius)

Foot-shifted gears, no hand clutch. The pros: machines that feel light and narrow (the Sirius, at 665 mm, is the narrowest model quoted here) - the alleys and the crowds of the Old Quarter are their natural habitat; rates at the lowest published level (150,000 VND per day, shared with the Click and Mio); the biggest wheels of the small classes, which handle potholes and rough provincial roads better than scooter wheels; and simple air-cooled single-cylinder engines that a general mechanic is likely to know. The cons: the foot-shift is a real learning curve for total beginners; storage means a rack bag; and two-up comfort is worse.

Three of those claims need a note from the published figures. Lightness is a matter of feel more than kilograms: Honda lists the Wave Alpha 110 at 96 kg and Yamaha lists the Sirius RC 110 at 100 kg wet, against 97 kg for the Vision, a gap too small to separate the types.

Only the Sirius is clearly narrower than the rest, at 665 mm overall width against 686 mm for the Vision, 687 mm for the Air Blade 125 and 689 mm for the Wave Alpha 110, so how narrow an underbone feels depends on which one you get; [the Old Quarter bike guide]({{ '/articles/best-bike-for-hanoi-old-quarter/' | relative_url }}) has the full size table. And the 150,000 VND day rate is the lowest published but not unique, because the Click and Mio scooters are published at the same figure.

The wheel claim holds. Both underbones are listed with 17-inch tyres, 70/90-17 at the front and 80/90-17 at the rear, while the Vision has a 16-inch front and a 14-inch rear. These figures describe the Wave Alpha 110 and the Sirius RC 110; the bike you are handed may be another variant (Yamaha lists more than one Sirius version), so ask which one it is.

For the verified spec side of the semi-automatic question, see our semi-automatic [gearbox explainer]({{ '/articles/semi-automatic-gearbox-explained/' | relative_url }}) and the [Wave Alpha versus Sirius comparison]({{ '/articles/honda-wave-alpha-vs-yamaha-sirius/' | relative_url }}) of the two classic underbones.

## Slopes, ramps and bridges

Hanoi is flat compared with the provinces, but bridge ramps and flyovers are where a stop-start rider meets a hill with traffic close behind. [The hill-start guide]({{ '/articles/hill-starts-motorbike-slopes-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the technique in full. On a scooter, hold a brake, add throttle until the transmission pushes, then release the brake. On a semi-automatic, hold the rear brake, give steady throttle and let the clutch take up as you release, using a little more throttle than feels natural. Practise once on a quiet incline before you need it.

Does the engine separate the two types? On the published peak figures, no. Honda lists 6.59 kW and 9.29 Nm for the Vision and 6.12 kW and 8.44 Nm for the Wave Alpha 110, and Yamaha lists 5.9 kW and 9.0 Nm for the Sirius RC 110. These are small engines of the same class, so a passenger on a slope is where each works hardest, and technique and load decide the climb more than the type does. On either type, an engine that revs up without the bike gaining speed on a ramp is a reason to stop and tell the shop.

## Rain and wet roads

Neither type has a verified edge in the rain. What differs is where your attention goes. On a scooter the hands do everything: a smooth throttle and two levers. On an underbone the left foot is also working the gear lever, so a shift made mid-turn, or while braking over a wet painted line, is an extra input to avoid: change gear while the bike is upright and the road is straight, in closed shoes rather than sandals.

Braking matters more than type. Honda's Wave Alpha page lists no disc version, and Yamaha lists the Sirius RC with a front hydraulic disc and a rear drum while noting that drum and disc versions exist. This guide quotes no brake figure for the Vision, Air Blade, Click or Mio, and no ABS figure for any of the four, so do not assume ABS: ask what braking the exact bike has. [The heavy-rain riding guide]({{ '/articles/riding-motorbike-heavy-rain-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the rest, including the first minutes of rain and when to stop and wait.

## Fuel, storage and carrying a passenger

The table keeps to what Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Motor Vietnam publish for four of the six rental models. A dash means this guide quotes no figure, which is different from the bike lacking the feature.

| Model | Wheels (front / rear) | Tank and claimed use | Under-seat storage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Vision | 16-inch / 14-inch | 4.8 L; 1.82 L per 100 km | bay that fits a helmet; no litre figure |
| Honda Air Blade 125 | - | - | 23.2 L, two half-helmets |
| Honda Wave Alpha 110 | 17-inch / 17-inch | 3.7 L; 1.72 L per 100 km | none published |
| Yamaha Sirius RC 110 | 17-inch / 17-inch | 4.2 L; 2.08 L per 100 km | none published |

On fuel, the claimed figures are manufacturer figures, not real-world ones, and consumption varies with speed, load, temperature and traffic. The three bikes with a figure sit within 0.36 L per 100 km of each other (2.08 minus 1.72 = 0.36 L per 100 km), which is too close to choose a type on. The visible difference is the tank: the Vision's 4.8 L is larger than the Wave Alpha's 3.7 L and the Sirius's 4.2 L, so on the claimed figures the Vision goes further on a full tank than either underbone; the other scooters' tanks are not quoted here.

Under the seat, Honda describes the Vision's bay as fitting a helmet and other personal items, with a separate front compartment and a USB-C port, but gives no litre figure. The Air Blade 125's 23.2 L is the only published figure of the four, worded as two half-helmets. Honda and Yamaha publish no built-in storage for the Wave Alpha 110 or the Sirius RC 110, so an underbone rider plans on a backpack or rack bag, as [the underbone storage guide]({{ '/articles/underbone-storage-options-explained/' | relative_url }}) sets out. A scooter rider should still try the bay with a real helmet and day bag, because a full-face helmet is bulkier than the half-helmets Honda describes.

Under Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 a standard two-wheel motorbike may carry one person besides the rider, with narrow exceptions that [the passenger rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-passenger-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out, and everyone on board needs a compliant, fastened helmet. That holds on both types. No maximum-load figure is quoted here for either, so two adults with bags are a good reason to ask the shop which bike it recommends for that load.

## The honest match

- Never ridden / riding in Vietnam for the first time / carrying a passenger: scooter.
- Already comfortable shifting with your foot, riding the Old Quarter daily, or wanting a 150,000 VND day rate with a gearbox you know (the Click and Mio share that rate): semi-automatic.
- Day trips on mixed roads: semi-automatic (bigger wheels) or the Air Blade-class scooter (comfort).
- Neighbourhood errands at licence-free speeds: neither - a 50 cc or e-bike.

Those bullets leave some in-between riders out:

- Foot-shift experience, first time in Hanoi traffic: either type works. The gearbox is familiar, so the choice comes down to luggage and passengers.
- Daily rides with a bag, a laptop or a second helmet: the scooter, because the bay replaces a rack bag. Try your own helmet in it first.
- Riding through the wet season: neither type has a verified edge, so choose on the brakes and tyres of the exact bike.

## What each costs per day

The published day rates follow the model, not the gearbox. The Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius underbones and the Honda Click and Yamaha Mio scooters are published at 150,000 VND per day; the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade scooters are published at 200,000 VND per day. ## The licence note

Both classes sit in the same licence bracket for the common sizes - the A1-class rules and the IDP routes cover them identically. Transmission is a skill question, not a legal one.

The class follows engine size. [The A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) explains that class A1 under Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc, which places the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Wave and Yamaha Sirius inside it. Honda also lists a larger Air Blade 160, so ask the engine size of the exact bike. For most visitors, recognition depends on a home licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle category, as the A1 guide above sets out.

## Checks at the counter

Sit on both at the shop and try the shift once - the choice often becomes clear once you have sat on each. Contact us to confirm which models are available to try today. These checks go further:

1. Ask which variant of the Air Blade or Sirius it is.
2. Sit on it: both feet should reach the ground and the knees should clear the bars at full lock. Honda lists seat heights of 785 mm for the Vision and 770 mm for the Wave Alpha 110, a 15 mm gap on paper that will not settle fit; sitting on the bike will.
3. Touch every control and ask to be shown what it does: which lever works which brake on a scooter, the shift pattern and how to find neutral on an underbone.
4. Paddle the bike backwards a few metres on level ground to feel its weight, and test your helmet in the bay or plan how a bag will ride on the rack.
5. Check brakes, tyres and lights as [the rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) sets out.

## Common questions

### Is a scooter or a semi-automatic better for Hanoi?

Neither is better for everyone. The scooter suits a first-timer, a passenger, luggage or daily stop-start riding; the underbone suits a rider who already shifts and can live with a rack bag.

### Is a semi-automatic cheaper to rent than a scooter?

Not by type. The Wave, Sirius, Click and Mio are all published at 150,000 VND per day, and the Vision and Air Blade at 200,000 VND, so the rate follows the model.

### Which is safer in the rain?

There is no verified difference between the types. What counts is the brakes and tyres of the exact bike and how smoothly you ride, so ask about braking and ABS before you take either.

### Do I need a different licence for each?

No. The class follows engine size, not the gearbox, and the common rental sizes of both types sit inside A1.
