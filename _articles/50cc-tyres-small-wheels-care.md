On a big-wheeled bike, a tyre is a component. On a 50cc, the tyres are close to the whole safety system: everything the bike does — steering, braking, holding a wet line — passes through two contact patches smaller than a palm each.

## Why small wheels hit harder

- **Less rubber, less life.** Small tyres wear through tread faster for the same kilometres.
- **A pothole is a bigger fraction of the wheel.** What a 17-inch underbone wheel shrugs at can pinch-flat or dent a smaller rim.
- **Less grip in reserve.** When a small tyre reaches its limit — on wet paint, dusty patches, polished asphalt — there is less margin before it slides, not more. The wet-weather consequences are covered in our rain article.

## Choosing replacements

Correct sizes are those in your model's manual or on the current tyres' sidewalls — never a "close enough" size, and never judge by another model's numbers. Beyond size, our replacement-tyres article covers the trade-offs between tread patterns and brands as they apply in Vietnam; for a 50cc, prioritise fresh date codes over famous names — an old stock "premium" tyre is worse than a fresh mid-range one.

## Pressure discipline

Pressures live in your manual, and the only honest check is a gauge: by-thumb is a habit from an era before gauges were free at every station. Under-inflation is the quiet killer of small tyres — flexing sidewalls overheat, and the bike that steers "strangely" at speed is usually just low. Check cold, weekly, and after any puncture repair. Never fill to another bike's numbers.

## The puncture economy

Frequent punctures are a signal, not a run of luck: a tube patched three times is a tube ready to hand you a fourth on a rainy night; a tyre at the canvas picks up everything. Our flat-tyre article covers the roadside options; the honest strategy for a 50cc ridden daily is a fresh tyre plus fresh tube once wear shows, and a patch kit and pump carried.

## The weekly checks

Tread across the crown, sidewalls for cracks and bulges, embedded glass and nail heads, pressures by gauge — five of the ten minutes in our weekly routine. Small wheels reward the attention more than any other component on the bike.
