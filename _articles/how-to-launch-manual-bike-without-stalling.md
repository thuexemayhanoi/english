Every new clutch rider stalls. The engine dies because it was asked to move the bike with too little revs, too fast a lever release, or both. The fix is the same in every case: reach the friction zone slowly, add throttle just before it, and finish the release gradually.

## The basic launch

1. Clutch in, first gear, both feet down.
2. Open the throttle a little — about the amount you would use to walk the bike — and hold it steady.
3. Release the clutch to the friction zone and wait a beat while the bike starts to pull.
4. As speed builds, release the rest of the lever smoothly and let the revs settle.

The order matters: a little throttle before the lever reaches the bite point, never after.

## Uphill launches

The fear is rolling back. Hold the rear brake with your right foot while you bring the clutch to the friction zone — you will feel the rear squat slightly as the engine starts to pull against the brake. Release the brake and finish the launch in one motion. Our hill starts guide covers the full sequence with practice steps.

## Recovering from a stall in traffic

Do not rush. Pull the clutch in fully, brake to a stop if needed, restart the engine, and launch again using the same calm sequence. Stalls feel long to you and look like two seconds to everyone else. Keep the sequence identical every time and the stalls disappear within a few days of practice — the launch drill in our friction-zone guide is the fastest way there.
