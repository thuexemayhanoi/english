Every rider who has done a few Hanoi day trips eventually asks the same question: which mountain trip should be my first? The honest answer depends on three things only — your clutch-and-corner experience, how many days you have, and how you handle things going wrong far from a mechanic. Here is the decision, from easiest upward.

## Step 0: have you done a day trip yet?

If not, start there. Tam Dao and Ba Vi are the two classic first mountain rides (our Tam Dao versus Ba Vi comparison breaks them down), and the day-trip planning basics — fuel, prep checklist, daylight — all live in our how-far-per-day [and prep guide]({{ '/articles/how-far-motorbike-day-trip-hanoi/' | relative_url }})s. A first overnight trip should not also be your first climb.

## Level 1: Mai Chau (2 days, the standard answer)

For most riders, Mai Chau is the right first mountain trip: about 140 km of mostly good sealed tarmac on QL6, one real pass (Thung Khe) that deserves respect but forgives mistakes at sensible speed, and a homestay destination with full phone coverage and easy bail-out options. Published route guides confirm the direct line is good tarmac with fuel and stops all along it. If you can ride a day trip competently, you can ride Mai Chau — see our Mai Chau guide.

## Level 1.5: Pu Luong (2-3 days, the quieter answer)

Pu Luong matches Mai Chau for difficulty but swaps the bus-road opening for valley lanes and terraces, and pairs beautifully with either Mai Chau or Ninh Binh. It suits riders whose goal is calm rather than kilometres (our Pu Luong guide). Difficulty notes are identical: one pass, village-pace roads, easy logistics.

## Level 2: Moc Chau, Ba Be, Lang Son, Cat Ba (2-3 days, real variety)

- **Moc Chau** adds sustained climbing and plateau distances (about 180-200 km each way) — fine for confident riders, a stretch for brand-new ones.
- **Ba Be** is a full transport day (around 200-280 km) but on easier roads; the reward is a lake, not passes.
- **Lang Son** is a border run on the busy QL1A — logistically easy, riding-wise defensive.
- **Cat Ba** is short road distance but adds ferries and salt air; schedule, not skill, is the challenge.

## Level 3: Sapa direct, the Northeast and Northwest loops (3-10 days)

The next tier is where trips stop being weekend-sized. Sapa direct is 300-320 km with a genuinely winding final climb; the northeast loop (Cao Bang, Ban Gioc, Ba Be) and the big northwest loop are 5-10 day undertakings with remoteness, weather and border logistics. Route rankings put the northwest firmly in the experienced-rider category while pointing newer riders toward guided options or easier regions (see our Sapa, northeast and northwest guides).

## Level 4: the Ha Giang loop (3-5 days, earn it)

The loop is Vietnam's most famous ride and its least suitable first mountain trip. Route guides are explicit: sections are muddy or under construction, and the main circuit is recommended for experienced riders; rankings consistently steer newcomers without confident mountain skills toward an Easy Rider (guided) version instead. Our advice: do Mai Chau or Pu Luong first, then the northeast, then Ha Giang. Arrive with earned confidence, not rented courage.

## The decision, compressed

- Never ridden mountains: Tam Dao or Ba Vi day trip.
- Done day trips, want one night away: Mai Chau or Pu Luong.
- Two or three weekends in: Moc Chau, Ba Be, Cat Ba, Lang Son.
- Confident, week available: northeast loop, or Sapa direct.
- Genuinely experienced: Ha Giang, the northwest, then the long rides south.
