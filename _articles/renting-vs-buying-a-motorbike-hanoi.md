Renting vs buying a motorbike in Hanoi comes down to how long you will ride and how much risk you want to carry yourself. For a visit, and for stays of a few months, renting is the simpler and cheaper choice; buying starts to make sense only when you will stay many months, can handle the paperwork and can resell.

Every long-stayer asks this. The arithmetic has a clear break-even, and the paperwork has a clear winner.

Below you get the arithmetic as a formula you can fill in yourself, a side-by-side table, the paperwork questions for each path and the decision questions that settle the in-between cases.

## The money, straight

Purchase prices depend on the model, its age and its condition, so use the price of the bike you are actually offered. Against published rental rates of 150,000-200,000 VND per day, a purchase breaks even only after enough months of rental to cover what it costs you - assuming the bike needs nothing and sells back at what you paid. Each of those assumptions is doing heavy lifting: used bikes need repairs, and resale in a hurry always means a discount.

The formula below lets you test the price of a specific bike.

Below six months, rental is cheaper. Above a year, ownership can win. Between six and twelve months, the tiebreakers below decide. Those three lines are a rule of thumb, and your own numbers can move the line either way.

### A break-even you can fill in

Break-even is the number of months after which owning has cost the same as renting would have. Work it out in three steps:

- Add up what only an owner pays: the purchase price, the registration or transfer costs, the compulsory insurance on the bike, and repairs, parts and tyres.
- Subtract what you realistically expect to get when you sell, after the lower price a hurried sale forces.
- Divide the result by the monthly rental cost of a comparable bike.

Fuel, parking and your own personal cover are paid on either path, so leave them out of both sides. The two numbers you cannot know in advance are repairs and resale value, and they carry most of the uncertainty. Run the formula three times, with a cheerful guess, a middle guess and a bad one. If buying only wins in the cheerful case, treat it as a gamble rather than a saving.

A fixed departure date makes the resale number the one to worry about, because a deadline hands the bargaining power to the buyer.

### The rental side of the sum

Published day rates are 150,000 VND for the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio, and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade. Contact us to confirm current availability of any model. Ask what deposit applies to the model you choose: it is typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions, and it is a separate figure from the rental rate.

Do not multiply a day rate to estimate a week, a month or a longer term. ## The paperwork, straight

Ownership means transferring registration into your name. The requirements for a foreign buyer, and how long the transfer takes, depend on the registration rules in force on the day you buy, so ask the registration office for its current list instead of relying on a seller's or an agent's summary. Riding a bike with no valid registration certificate, or one that does not match the machine, falls into a fine band of its own, and a bike whose lawful origin cannot be shown can be confiscated. The [guide to unregistered vehicle fines]({{ '/articles/unregistered-motorbike-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the legal basis.

Rental sidesteps the transfer, the registration and the resale, but not every question. Ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a police stop: Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15 expects the rider to carry both. Insurance for you as a rider is a separate matter: Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so a renter arranges their own. [The rental insurance guide]({{ '/articles/is-insurance-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the difference between the bike's cover and yours.

### Checks before you pay a seller

- Match the numbers on the registration certificate to the frame and engine of the bike in front of you, and the seller's identity to the name on the paper. A certificate issued for a different frame or engine number is one of the cases the unregistered-vehicle rules cover.
- Ask what happens in the gap between buying and registering. A newly bought bike can be temporarily registered, and that registration only covers what it states.
- Treat a price that looks too good as a question about the papers, not as luck.
- Bring a Vietnamese-speaking friend or a mechanic you trust. The [used motorbike inspection guide]({{ '/articles/used-motorbike-inspection-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists what to look at on the bike itself.

### What the law says about insurance on an owned bike

Article 56(1)(d) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) lists the vehicle owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate among the documents a driver must carry. That insurance is third-party cover: it pays compensation for people injured and property damaged in an accident the insured rider causes, and it does not pay for the rider's own bike or medical costs. The [compulsory insurance guide]({{ '/articles/compulsory-motorbike-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the detail, and that cost belongs on the owner's side of the break-even sum.

## Depreciation, theft and breakdowns

- Rental: theft is a deposit-level exposure; breakdowns are the shop's to fix; you hand the problem back at return.
- Ownership: theft is the full value; breakdowns are yours at full shop price; you sell into a market that knows every trick.

The rental bullet is a general pattern, not a published shop policy. How much a renter owes after a theft or a loss, and what happens after a breakdown, are matters for the rental terms you agree, so ask for both in writing before you ride. The shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, while inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation. Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so do not assume roadside rescue at any hour or a guaranteed response time.

Depreciation is the quiet cost. A bike loses value as it ages and as your leaving date approaches, and a renter never holds that loss.

## Questions that settle the in-between cases

### How long will you stay, and how firm is the date?

A fixed departure date favours renting, because a rental ends when you return the bike while a sale needs a buyer who is ready on your date. An open-ended stay favours buying, because nothing forces you to sell in a hurry. Once you are past six months, [the six-month rental guide]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-six-months-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [the year-long rental guide]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-year-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) describe longer terms. ### Do you know which bike suits you?

Buying before you have ridden for a while risks the wrong type of bike. Scooter or semi-automatic is a real difference in comfort and handling, and [the scooter versus semi-automatic comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-vs-semi-automatic-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets it out. A short rental costs a few days of rate; a wrong purchase costs a resale.

### Where will the bike sleep, and who will fix it?

An owned bike needs a safe place every night and a mechanic you trust. A renter shares the first problem, because a rented bike also has to be parked, but not the second. [The parking guide]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers paid lots and no-parking zones; check what your building offers before you buy anything.

### Does the licence change the answer?

Not by itself. Riding legally is a separate question that depends on your licence, and that is true of a rented bike and an owned one alike. [Can foreigners rent a motorbike in Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) separates renting from riding legally.

## The honest summary

- Under 6 months: rent.
- 6-12 months: rent monthly unless you love a specific bike and have time to resell properly.
- Over 12 months: buying starts to make sense, if the paperwork is done correctly and the resale market is not in a hurry.

[The monthly-rate article]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows the rental side of the arithmetic; contact us to discuss longer-term rental if your stay sits in the middle band.

## A rent-first path if you cannot decide

1. Rent for the first stretch and use it to learn your routes, your parking and which type of bike you like.
2. Write down your real leaving date, or your best guess, and a monthly rental figure for a comparable bike.
3. Fill in the break-even formula for a specific bike you could actually buy, using a cheerful, a middle and a bad estimate.
4. If buying still wins, have a mechanic inspect the bike and confirm the papers with the registration office before you pay.
5. If it does not, ask the shop about a longer term. Early return carries no automatic refund for unused time, so choose a term you expect to use.

## Mistakes that skew the comparison

- **Pricing the purchase at its best case.** The asking price is not the cost: registration, repairs, tyres and the resale discount belong in the sum. Run the formula with a bad estimate too.
- **Working out a month from a day rate.** Weekly and monthly prices are published separately and longer terms are agreed with the shop. Ask for the figure and keep the reply.
- **Treating the deposit as the limit of a renter's risk.** What you owe after theft or damage follows the rental terms. Ask before you sign.
- **Buying a bike with papers nobody can explain because the price is low.** The saving disappears if the bike cannot be registered or is held. Follow the paper trail before the money.
- **Assuming rental includes insurance.** Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so budget for your own cover on either path.

## Questions people ask before they decide

### Is it cheaper to rent or buy for one month?

Renting. A month of rental is a published monthly rate on models that have one, with no purchase, registration or resale to arrange, while a purchase needs all three. Ask the shop for the monthly figure for the model you want.

### Can I rent now and buy later?

Yes, the two decisions are separate. Renting first gives you real information about routes, parking and the type of bike you like. Because early return carries no automatic refund, pick a rental term you expect to use.

### What if I want to rent for longer than a month?

Longer-term rental is agreed with the shop. Contact us to discuss your dates and to confirm current availability of the model you want.
