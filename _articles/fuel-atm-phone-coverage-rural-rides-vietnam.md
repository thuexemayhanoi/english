Out on the rural roads of northern Vietnam, three small logistics questions decide how your day feels: where the next pump is, where the next ATM is, and whether your phone has signal. None of them is difficult — they just need a system.

## Fuel

In the delta and along the national-highway corridors, petrol stations are frequent; the Hanoi stations guide covers the city end. The pattern changes with altitude: in the mountains, stretches can run for hours without a pump, and station hours in remote districts are shorter than you expect. The rules that never fail:

- **The half-tank rule**: below half a tank, the next station you pass is the one you use. Do not bank on the town after this one.
- Fill up at the last district town before a mountain or lake area — Thung Nai, Tam Dao, Perfume Pagoda-style valleys all work this way.
- Petrol is sold by the litre at state-priced pumps — but remote stations are occasionally dry at the end of long weekends; morning fills are the safe bet.
- If your bike’s gauge is unreliable (common — see the fuel-gauge guide), track kilometres, not bars.

## Cash and ATMs

Cities take cards; the countryside runs on cash. Parking lots, ferries, boat trips, homestays, rural restaurants and roadside repairs are all cash-first. ATMs are common in provincial towns but rare in villages, and a withdrawn limit can run out at a festival weekend. Carry a day-and-a-half of trip cash in small notes, plus an emergency note tucked under the seat liner, and refill the wallet at every provincial town.

## Phone coverage

Vietnam’s mobile networks cover the delta and corridor roads well, and coverage in the northern mountains is patchy rather than absent — valleys and passes drop out, hilltop towns come back. Plan accordingly:

- Download offline maps before leaving (see the navigation guide).
- Share your route and expected return time with someone in the city before riding into a low-coverage area.
- Carry a power bank; navigation in a dead zone drains a phone fast.
- In a genuine emergency with no signal, the pattern is to flag help on the road — rural drivers stop readily.
