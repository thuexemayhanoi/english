Can you tour Vietnam on a scooter? Thousands do - but the model you pick changes how the day ends. Using verified manufacturer figures (retrieved 23 September 2026), here is what actually matters beyond city spec sheets.

## Range first

Touring in Vietnam means stretches without dependable fuel stops, especially in the northern mountains. Implied range (tank times published economy): Yamaha NMAX 155 about 314 km, Honda SH160i about 299 km, Yamaha Gear 125 about 300 km, then the 5.5-litre bikes around 250 km. The Lexi 155 and FreeGo 125 (4.2 L and 3.9 L) are city bikes pressed into touring - plan fuel carefully. Method and caveats in our longest-range guide.

## Comfort for hours in the saddle

Bigger wheels smooth broken provincial roads - the SH160i's 16-inch wheels and the Lexi 155's 14s are genuine touring assets, explained in our wheel and wheelbase guides. A broad seat and roomy ergonomics matter more than peak power; fatigue ends more tours than breakdowns do (our riding fatigue guide covers pacing).

## Safety hardware

Dual ABS plus traction control (NMAX 155, SH160i with HSTC) is worth having on mountain passes and wet descents; front ABS (NVX 155, Lexi 155, Vario 160) is the realistic mid-tier. See our ABS guide.

## Practical notes

Service before you go - our pre-trip service guide covers it - and carry luggage within payload limits. And be honest about scope: for full Ho Chi Minh-route touring, many riders still choose a manual motorcycle; our long-distance bike guide compares classes. For regional touring from Hanoi, a well-specified scooter is genuinely viable.
