Short stays run on an IDP; long stays run on a [Vietnamese licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). For expats settling in Hanoi for a year or more, the IDP stops being the right tool — it is built for visitors, while the law's residence-based path is the exchange. Here is how the two routes compare and how to choose.

In practice an expat has four ways to a legal ride: an IDP with the home licence, an exchange, a test, or a bike small enough to need no licence. The table sorts them by situation, and the sections after it give the rule behind each row.

## Which route fits your situation

| Your situation | Route and basis | What it gives you | How long it lasts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home licence and a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP, both in term | [IDP with the national licence]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}); Law, Article 57(6)(b) and 57(8) | The class both documents cover | IDP: three years from issue at most, within the national licence's term |
| Resident with a home licence in term and a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left, or a permanent residence card | Exchange, below; Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Articles 18(7) and 24 | A Vietnamese licence in the corresponding classes | Follows the papers and the foreign licence (Article 24(5)(a)) |
| No motorbike class at home, or a licence that cannot be exchanged | The [Vietnamese A1 route]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) with a test; Circular Article 4 | Class A1: up to 125 cm3 or 11 kW (Law, Article 57(1)(a)) | No time limit (Article 57(5)(a)) |
| A car licence in force, no motorbike class | The same test; theory waived for a car licence in force; foreign ones unstated (Article 4(1)(c)) | A1 or A after the practical test | As the row above |
| Only a bike within the [moped limits]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}), rider 16 or over | Moped class; Law, Articles 34(1)(g), 56(1), 59(1)(a) | A ride with no licence | Nothing to renew |
| Only an IDP, or a home licence past its term | Neither the pair (Law, Article 57(7)(a), 57(8)(b)) nor the exchange (Article 24(1)(c)) | Test route or moped class | As the row you move to |

## Staying on an IDP and your home licence

Article 57(6)(b) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15, consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH) counts an IDP together with a national licence from a 1968 Vienna Convention state as valid in Vietnam, each suited to the vehicle. The IDP runs no more than three years from issue and must fit the national licence's remaining validity (Article 57(8)(a)). The holder carries both, in term, and follows Vietnamese traffic law (Article 57(8)(b)). [How long an IDP lasts]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes the two dates apart.

Two limits matter to a resident. The three-year cap means a longer stay needs a new permit, a question for the authority that issued the first. And a licence with car classes only leaves you at the moped line, below.

## Route one: exchange your national licence

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security governs the exchange in its Article 24, and it replaced Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA on 1 July 2026 (Article 41(6)).

The conditions are a national driving licence that is valid in its issuing country, and a visa or temporary residence card with at least 90 days left (or a valid permanent residence card, or a diplomatic or official identity card) — normally the temporary residence card. The applicant must reside, work or study in Vietnam and need to drive here. Article 24(1)(a) words the outcome as "considered for exchange", and if the office declines, Article 24(3)(c) requires a written answer with the reason.

The licence converts into the Vietnamese classes that match its categories: a motorbike entitlement becomes an A1 or A, a car-only licence becomes car classes. Article 24(5)(b) gives no conversion table, and Appendix XII covers only permits Vietnam issues, so check the engine sizes your home category covers before assuming A1 or A.

Most expats find this the cheaper and faster route. That is a general impression, not a measured comparison. Article 24 lists a dossier and a fee obligation, with no training course and no test, and no fee amount, so ask the office what applies on the day.

Filing is with the Traffic Police Department, a commune-level police office with a reception point, or the National Public Service Portal (Article 24(3)(a)). Article 24(3)(d) gives 1 day from a complete dossier for the Department to pass the data to the Traffic Police Bureau for central management and issue of the licence, electronic version included; a printed licence on request takes up to 2.5 working days. The dossier items and steps are in [the step-by-step exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

## Route two: sit the test

Expats with no motorbike entitlement at home — or with a licence Vietnam cannot exchange — take the [A1 test route]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}): training course, 25-question theory paper, practical circuit, with the question count changing under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA. The 25-question figure comes from a December 2024 Government portal account of the earlier procedure, not from a legal text read for this page.

One fact decides the planning: the test is conducted in Vietnamese only. That is how the paper is set in practice, not a sentence of the Circular: no article of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA names the language of the standard paper, and the Article 14 paper test, run with an interpreter under Appendix I, is written for ethnic-minority A1 candidates who cannot read or write Vietnamese, not for foreigners. Expats who cannot read the language reliably usually arrange the exam through a training centre that guides foreign candidates through the Vietnamese question bank. Article 15(2) makes the training course part of the route, so ask the centre what help it gives foreign candidates.

### Who may sit the test as a foreigner

Article 15 of the circular asks every candidate to meet the age and health conditions of Article 59(1) and (2) of the Law (18 for class A1) and to have a driving training establishment confirm the course is complete. Under Article 16(2) the dossier holds the training document, a valid health certificate and, for a foreigner, a copy of the same kind of residence paper the exchange asks for: a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left, a valid permanent residence card, or a diplomatic or official identity card.

In Articles 4 to 17 only Article 16 names foreign candidates; 16(3)(b) repeats those papers for a licence past its term. Article 14's separate theory format is limited to ethnic minority candidates who cannot read or write Vietnamese.

### What the test covers

For a motorbike the test has three parts in order: a computer theory paper, then two practical exercises on the test ground (Article 4(1)(b), 4(2)(a) and 4(5)(a)). For class A1 and A the theory covers road traffic law, driving technique, the conduct rules for drivers, traffic culture and the harm of alcohol (Article 4(1)(a)).

The content is phased in. Parts of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, among them Article 4(1) to (3) and Appendix II, stay in force until the end of 28 February 2027 (Article 41(6)). Article 4(4) content in Circular 108 applies from 1 March 2027 and its second practical exercise from 1 January 2028 (Article 41(2)).

Appendix I sets 40 questions in 27 minutes for classes A1 and A from 1 March 2027 (Article 41(2)); the Circular 12/2025 parts kept by Article 41(6) run to the end of 28 February 2027. The question count and circuit on [the A1 test page]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) therefore depend on your sitting date, so ask the training centre which format applies.

### A car licence waives the theory paper

Article 4(1)(c) exempts a candidate for class A1 or A who holds a car licence still in force from the theory test. Article 4(1) of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA has the same exemption, in force since 1 March 2025 and kept by Article 41(6) until the end of 28 February 2027, so it applies before and after 1 March 2027.

Three limits apply. The practical test is still sat. The text says "a car driving licence still in force" and does not say whether a foreign car licence counts, so ask the test office first. And Article 16(2)(a) lists the training document for A1 and A candidates without an exception.

## The moped class: no licence at all

The Law draws the moped line in Article 34(1)(g): design speed up to 50 km/h, with a combustion engine up to 50 cm3 or an electric motor up to 4 kW. Article 56(1) puts no licence duty on that class, and Article 59(1)(a) sets its minimum age at 16.

That makes the moped class a legal option while paperwork is in progress, provided the bike meets every limit; [the 50 cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the smallest bikes. ## What the Vietnamese licence gives you

The motorbike classes issued after a test have no time limit (Article 57(5)(a)); a licence issued by exchange runs to the date printed on it (Article 24(5)(a)). Both carry the 12-point balance like any Vietnamese licence (Article 58(1)) and are held in the licence database that the Traffic Police Bureau manages centrally, in electronic form or on a PET card (Articles 18(2) and 24(3)(d)).

After an exchange, the IDP question disappears: you ride on a Vietnamese card like a local rider. The licence is valid in Vietnam under Article 57(6)(a), Article 24(4) leaves the foreign licence with you, and Article 18(4) lets the holder ride the classes written on the card; [what an A1 licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) sets those classes against named models. Article 56(1) still asks for a licence in term, with points left, that suits the bike.

## What changes when a visa is renewed

The term printed on an exchanged licence is fixed when it is issued. Article 24(5)(a) makes it follow the term on one of the Article 24(1)(a) papers and the term of the foreign licence, and it cannot exceed the term set for the Vietnamese class. Article 24 says nothing about extending the date when a visa or card is renewed, and a licence past its term is not valid (Article 57(7)(a)). Treat the printed date as the one that counts.

Article 21(1)(b) separately lets a foreigner holding a licence issued by a Vietnamese authority ask for it to be exchanged before it expires, and Article 21(1)(đ) bars the exchange for someone who has not completed what the authorities require to resolve a road-traffic violation. The text does not say whether a visa renewal is a reason to re-issue under Article 21, so ask when you renew, and ask early.

Write the new visa or card date next to the licence date and diary the earlier. [The long-term rider's licence checklist]({{ '/articles/long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) has the full table of dates, and [when a licence or visa expires mid-rental]({{ '/articles/licence-expiry-during-long-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers a rental that runs past one.

## The residence boundary

The dividing line between "IDP is fine" and "you should exchange" is fuzzy by design, but the practical markers are clear: a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left unlocks the exchange; longer-term visas and cards make the exchange the natural next step; and a multi-year stay on an IDP alone leaves you re-explaining your paperwork when an officer asks for it. Expats also cannot exchange an IDP itself — the exchange [requires the national licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) behind it.

### What Article 18(7) says

The legal frame is firmer than the word "fuzzy" suggests. Article 18(7)(a) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA says a foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam and holds a national licence must go through the exchange procedure for the corresponding Vietnamese licence. Article 18(7)(b) says that where a treaty on driving licences to which Vietnam is a party provides otherwise, the treaty applies.

The IDP route appears to rest on that second line (Article 30(2) applies a treaty-recognised IDP under that treaty): Article 57(6)(b) of the Law recognises an in-term IDP with a national licence from a 1968 Vienna Convention state, although Article 18(7) does not name the Convention. The texts read for this page set no date by which a resident must switch. None of Articles 18(7), 24 and 57(6)(b) names a deadline or a length of stay, and they do not say how the two clauses fit together for a resident.

A resident planning to ride for years should ask the Traffic Police Department which route it accepts for their papers, get the answer in writing and keep it with the licence papers.

## A Hanoi-specific note

The text does not say which counter in Hanoi takes a foreigner's dossier, so ask the Traffic Police Department or a commune-level reception point before you travel with the papers. The exchange does not require the theory test: Article 24 lists a dossier and the fee obligation, and no test.

## Quick answers

### Can I keep riding on my IDP while I live in Vietnam?

Article 57(6)(b) and 57(8) recognise an in-term 1968 Vienna Convention IDP carried with an in-term national licence that suits the bike, and the texts read for this page set no date by which a resident must switch. The other routes are the exchange (Article 18(7)(a)) and the A1 test.

### I hold only a car licence. Can I ride a 125 cc scooter?

Not on that licence. The car classes are separate from the motorbike classes (Article 57(1)), which is why [a car licence does not cover a 125 cc scooter]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). The A1 route is the nearest path; Article 4(1)(c) waives the theory paper for a car licence in force, though the text does not say whether a foreign one counts.
