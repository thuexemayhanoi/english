At the end of a month or more with the same bike, return-day logistics are easy to leave until the last moment — and that is one way long-term renters end up paying late fees. A late return at the end of a monthly term is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than six hours late a full additional rental day may be charged. Here is the rule and how to stay on the right side of it.

## The rule

Late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than six hours late may be charged as a full additional rental day (typically 150,000–200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle). That is the standard rule (see [What Happens If You Return a Rental Motorbike Late in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }})); it applies at the end of a monthly term exactly as it does after a day rental.

The published terms are not split by rental period, so nothing in them treats the end of a long term differently. What they leave open is another matter: how the end date and hour are counted, how a monthly rate and a late hour fit together, and how any charge is settled.

## Why the end date matters more on a long term

On a term of months the end date is the fixed point that most end-of-term questions are counted from: when the bike must be back, when a late charge could begin, when a renewal has to be settled and when the deposit comes up. Three things make it weightier than on a day rental.

- **More has built up.** Months of riding leave a longer trail than a day does: a repaired puncture, a serviced chain, a worn tyre, perhaps a new address, all read against the handover record at the end.
- **More is attached to the rental.** The deposit is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions. [The monthly deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the deposit side of a long term.
- **A decision sits at the end.** Return, renew or change the length. Nguyen Tu publishes longer-term rental as negotiable, so another month is not the only option: contact the shop to discuss it.

### What the lease articles say

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law 91/2015/QH13) sets general rules for a lease of property. What follows is a general guide to two of its provisions, not legal advice: the official text is in Vietnamese, and the paper or messages you agreed with the shop govern your rental.

- **The term (Article 474).** The parties agree the length of the lease, and without an agreement it is fixed by what the hire is for (474(1)). What happens to a lease where neither an agreement nor the purpose fixes a term is set out in the long-term agreement guide, linked below. A written end date shows which term was agreed.
- **Condition and wear (Article 482(1)).** On return the property must be in the state it was received in, natural wear apart, or as agreed, and a fall in value beyond wear lets the lessor claim compensation. The article does not define natural wear, which matters more after months of riding than after a day.

A late return and any agreed penalty fall under Articles 482(4) and (5) and 418, which the Civil Code section of the daily-rental guide sets out. [The clause-by-clause agreement checklist]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets the late-return and penalty clauses beside the other clauses worth reading.

## What the published terms do not settle

| Question to put to the shop | Why it matters at month scale |

| How does the hourly figure sit beside a monthly rate? | The published terms give the hourly figure and the possible extra day, and say nothing about how either sits beside a monthly rate |
| How is a delay of several days counted? | The published figures stop at six hours and one possible extra day, so they are no price list for a longer overrun |
| Is a late charge taken from the deposit or paid separately, and when is the rest settled? | A whole term of rent and a deposit are in play |

## Why long-term renters slip

The irony of monthly rentals: you have been riding for weeks without thinking about return logistics, so the return sneaks up. The common slips: a departure flight hours after the intended return; assuming the shop will be open at 07:00 (opening hours are 09:00–21:00); a final-day trip that runs long; or simply never having confirmed what time the rental day technically ends. The fix for each is the same: book the return time in a message days in advance, and pad it.

### Counting the end date

"A month" is less exact than it sounds. As an illustration of the arithmetic, not a statement of how the shop counts, a term that starts on 14 March ends on 14 April if it is counted by calendar month and on 13 April if it is counted as 30 days after the start date; for a term starting in February the two methods can differ by up to two days.

The hour matters as much as the date, because the charge is counted per hour. "The evening of the 14th" does not say from which hour a delay would be counted, so ask for the end as a date and a time, inside the published hours of 09:00–21:00 daily, and keep the reply in the thread.

### When something changes mid-term

A bike swap, a new address or a renewal can each move the date, the hour or the place of the return. After any change, ask the shop to restate the end date and the return place in the thread. [The long-term agreement guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-long-term-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the terms that drift over months.

## Renewal versus late return

Some "late returns" at month scale are really unplanned extensions. If you know even a day ahead that you need more time, convert the situation into a renewal: [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the process. An extension agreed in advance costs the agreed rate; the same time unarranged costs the hourly fee and, past six hours, may cost a full additional rental day.

### Deciding before the last week

Do not read silence as a renewal: nothing published says a term continues by itself. The extending page linked above describes a renewal arranged after the end date as a late return from the shop's side, so the message belongs before the date. [Paying for a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/paying-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the amount, the dates a renewal pays for and what happens to the deposit.

## An end-of-term timeline

A suggested schedule, counted back from the end date. The intervals are a planning aid, not a rule of the shop; what matters is what each step confirms.

### Two weeks before

Send one message that covers:

- the end date and hour as you understand them, written as a date and a time;
- whether you will return the bike, renew or ask about a different length, and for a renewal the rate and whether your bike is free for it;
- where and how the bike comes back: the shop at 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De, Long Bien, or a pickup that may be available depending on location and arrangement, as set out in [delivery and pickup for monthly rentals]({{ '/articles/delivery-pickup-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }});
- whether any maintenance is due before the term ends.

If you renew, check your own papers too: Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so [your own insurance as a long-term renter]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) should run across the new period, and the dates on your licence and visa should cover it.

### The last week

- Check that the reply sits in the same thread. The shop is open 09:00–21:00 daily and no round-the-clock support is published, so chase an unanswered message during those hours by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674.
- Settle a renewal in writing before the old end date, or settle the return slot, and ask how any charge would be settled, alone or together with the deposit.
- Keep the last days light: a long trip near the end is the final-day slip described above, only slower.

### The last day

Only two things are still open: that the bike reaches the shop inside opening hours at the agreed time, and that you leave with the thread showing the result. Leave earlier than the journey needs and message the shop as soon as a delay looks likely.

## When the end date meets a holiday

The published hours are 09:00–21:00 daily, and nothing published lists holiday exceptions. Around Tet, [the Tet guide for monthly renters]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-through-tet-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) treats the shop's open days as something to confirm and works through where an end date can land against the holiday.

For any public holiday, ask which days and hours the shop will keep, because nothing published says. If the answer is unclear, a return on a day the shop has confirmed is the safer plan, remembering that [ending a term before its date]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) carries no automatic refund entitlement for unused time.

## The return itself

The mechanics are the standard return ([How Returning a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi Works]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }})): condition against the handover record, fuel as agreed, deposit returned per the agreement — [How Deposit Returns Work at Hanoi Motorbike Rentals]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Being late does not change the walk-around; it just adds the fee to it.

### What a long term adds to the last-day record

The return-day record for a short rental is set out in [the return-day record in the daily-rental guide]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and [the inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) covers the walk-around, so start from those. A long term adds four things worth keeping:

- **Odometer, then and now.** Photograph the odometer at the return beside the handover photograph, if the handover set included one; the difference is the distance ridden during the term. The owner's practical guidance, which is not a manufacturer interval, is a periodic check at around 1,500 km or more of use.
- **Maintenance history.** What was done, when and by whom: Nguyen Tu's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, and [a maintenance log]({{ '/articles/maintenance-log-monthly-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) kept through the term turns this into a list instead of a recollection.
- **Parts and consumables.** Inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so note each one replaced, when, and who paid.
- **Wear and damage.** Photograph worn parts from the same angles as the handover set, and ask the shop to note in the thread which marks it treats as natural wear, since Article 482(1) leaves natural wear out without defining it. Keep that thread with the handover photographs, the renewal messages and the payment proofs.

## The one-line version

20,000 VND an hour, a full day that may be charged after six — and avoidable, where the delay is foreseeable, by one message that either books the return properly or turns the extra time into a renewal.
