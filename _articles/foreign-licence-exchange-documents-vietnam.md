The foreign licence exchange documents Vietnam asks for are short to list. Article 24(2) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA names three: a Vietnamese translation of the foreign licence with certified quality, a valid health certificate (with one exception) and a copy of a qualifying visa or residence card. Below, each is taken in turn with what the text asks, what you can check yourself and what it leaves open.

This page covers the paperwork only. Who qualifies, the procedure and the term of the new licence belong to the guide to [exchanging a driving licence for a Vietnamese one]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA took effect on 1 July 2026 and replaced Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA from that date (Article 41(6)), so a checklist written under the older circular should be compared with Article 24 before you rely on it.

## The checklist at a glance

Article 24(2) lists three documents, and Article 24(3)(a) adds what happens when you file. The table sets them side by side, each with its clause.

| Item | What the text asks | Worth knowing | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Translated licence | A Vietnamese translation of the foreign licence, quality certified by a notary office in Vietnam, or made by a Vietnamese representative office abroad where the translator works; stamped across the join with a copy of the licence | The licence must be in term and must not be an IDP | 24(2)(a) |
| Health certificate | In force; the electronic version, or a paper one from a medical facility meeting the legal standards if a technical fault blocks the electronic one | Not required of a foreigner who exchanges for the term shown on the entry visa or temporary residence card | 24(2)(b) |
| Copy of the qualifying paper | A copy or electronic copy of a diplomatic or official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left, or a valid permanent residence card | The original is shown for comparison unless the copy already stands in for it under the certification law | 24(1)(a), 24(2)(c) |
| Originals | Shown for comparison, apart from any already sent | Stated for filing in person | 24(3)(a) |
| Photo | Taken at the counter, taken from identity data, or a white-background portrait of 3 cm by 4 cm | Online, it comes from identity data or is updated by you | 24(3)(a) |
| Fee | The exchange fee, paid when you file | The circular states no amount | 24(3)(a) |
| Application form | None listed | Online you complete the portal's declaration; in person the office enters your details electronically | 24(2), 24(3)(a) and (c) |

## Who the checklist is for

Article 18(7)(a) sends a foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam and holds a national licence to this exchange, unless a driving-licence treaty that Vietnam has joined provides otherwise (point b); [the IDP rules for motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) cover that side.

The licence must be valid — an expired national licence cannot be exchanged and pushes the applicant toward [the test route]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}). Article 24(1)(c) also bars a licence showing a difference in identification, and the circular does not say what that is, which is why checking the set against itself matters.

## The three documents, one by one

The circular says what each item must be and gives no reason for asking, so the checks under each heading are practical suggestions, not conditions in the text.

### 1. The translated foreign licence

Article 24(2)(a) asks for a Vietnamese translation of the foreign licence whose quality is certified by a notary office in Vietnam, or a translation made by a Vietnamese representative office abroad where the translator works. The paragraph ends with a stamp across the join with a copy of the licence ("đóng dấu giáp lai"), and its wording does not say which of the two routes the stamp belongs to, so ask the certifier how it is applied. The second route describes a translation done abroad; a notary office is the route available inside Vietnam.

Uncertified self-translations are not accepted: item (a) names two certifying routes, and a translation you made yourself has neither.

Checks before you pay for it:

- The licence is in term and undamaged, since Article 24(1)(c) excludes a licence past its term or too erased or torn to carry enough information.
- The stamp crosses the join of the translation and the licence copy, as item (a) words it.
- The licence number and every category are carried over exactly as printed. The new licence follows the classes the old one covers (Article 24(5)(b)), and the circular gives no table for reading a foreign category.

### 2. The health certificate

Article 24(2)(b) asks for the driver's health certificate, in force, in its electronic version. Only where a technical fault stops the electronic version being used does the text allow the paper one, and that must come from a medical facility meeting the standards of the law on medical examination and treatment.

The exception sits in brackets at the end of the paragraph: a foreigner who exchanges for the term written in the entry visa or temporary residence card is excepted. Only those two papers are named. The text gives no exception to someone relying on a permanent residence card or on a diplomatic or official identity card, so do not assume one.

The circular does not set medical standards. Article 59(2) of the Law asks people driving on the road for health conditions that suit the type of vehicle and leaves the standards and the examination rules to the Minister of Health, so what the certificate must show is health certification appropriate to the licence class sought, per the health standards of the law.

Two checks: the validity date printed on the certificate has not passed, because the text says only "in force" and gives no number of months; and the facility has told you whether it issues the certificate electronically, since that is the version the circular names first.

### 3. A copy of the visa or residence card

Article 24(2)(c) asks for a copy or electronic copy of one of the papers named in Article 24(1)(a). A copy without certification is matched against the original at the counter, while a copy or electronic copy that the law on certification lets stand in for the original needs no original to be shown.

The original to show is the visa, card or identity card you copied, and if your visa is in your passport, expect to show the passport. Article 21(2)(b), for a licence issued by Vietnam, lists a valid passport for foreigners, but the foreign-licence article does not, so do not carry that item across.

Checks: count the days left on a visa or temporary residence card against the 90 the text asks for, reading the expiry date off the card itself; and make sure the copy shows the number and dates legibly.

## Checking the set against itself

Compare the documents with one another before filing; the text does not say how an office compares them. Lay the licence, its translation, the visa or card and the health certificate side by side and look for these mismatches:

- Family and given names: the same spelling, the same order and the same treatment of accents or transliteration.
- Date of birth: the same day, month and year everywhere, with the translation leaving no doubt about day-month order.
- Dates: the licence's expiry, the days left on the visa or card and the certificate's validity date, each set against the day you plan to file.

If one item differs, ask the reception point before paying for the translation or the medical examination. The text does not say whether a spelling variant counts as a difference in identification, so the answer has to come from the office.

## Where and how it is filed

The dossier goes to the Traffic Police Department or a commune-level police point that accepts licence applications, or via the online public-service portal where available; the applicant has their photo taken at the counter. That is one of three photo options, because Article 24(3)(a) also allows the photo held in identity data or a white-background portrait of 3 cm by 4 cm handed in at the receiving point. One set of the dossier is filed, and the fee obligation is completed at filing, in person or through the portal's payment function.

Online, you complete the declaration as instructed on the National Public Service Portal and answer for its accuracy and legality. Postal service appears in the text only for returning the licence at your request (Article 24(3)(đ)), not as a way to file.

Article 24(3)(d) gives 1 day from a complete dossier for the Department to pass the data to the Traffic Police Bureau for central management and issue of the licence, electronic version included; a printed licence on request takes up to 2.5 working days. [The step-by-step exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the appointment slip and the term printed on the new licence.

Article 24(3)(c) describes two outcomes only: a valid dossier is entered electronically and receives an appointment slip, and a licence that is not exchanged receives a written answer stating the reason. Nothing in the text describes a step for supplementing an incomplete dossier or sets a deadline for one, so arrive with the whole set.

## Which papers the office keeps

Two items in the set stay with the office: the stamped translation joined to the licence copy, and the copy of your visa or card (Article 26(3)). It stores them for two years (Article 26(4) and (5)). The rest of the original dossier, the health certificate and the foreign licence included, stays with you (Article 24(4)). Photograph the stamped translation before handing it over, since Article 24(4) leaves it out of the originals you keep.

## What the exchange does not do

It does not add categories: a car-only licence exchanges into car classes only. It does not accept IDPs — the permit is for riding, not for swapping. And it does not waive the health certification, whatever the home licence says about medical fitness, unless the entry-visa or temporary-card exception in Article 24(2)(b) applies.

## Where an older five-item checklist goes wrong

An earlier version of this page listed five items, including an application form and a passport, and described itself as written under Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA. Four of its sentences are quoted here, each followed by what Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA now says, so that anyone holding the older list can compare. None of the four is current.

- An earlier version said: "The exchange dossier is five items long, and each has a verification catch." Now: Article 24(2) lists three documents, and the application form and the passport are not among them, as the checklist table shows.
- An earlier version said: "Papers evidencing residence in Vietnam of at least three months — typically the temporary residence record issued where you live." Now: Article 24(1)(a) and (2)(c) look instead for one paper from a short list: a diplomatic or official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card with at least 90 days left on it, or a permanent residence card in force. The three-month figure belongs to Article 24(1)(b), which covers Vietnamese citizens abroad, not foreigners.
- An earlier version said: "The foreign licence itself, together with a Vietnamese translation certified by a notary office or by the issuing country's embassy or consulate in Vietnam." Now: Article 24(2)(a) names a notary office in Vietnam or a Vietnamese representative office abroad where the translator works, not the issuing country's embassy or consulate in Vietnam.
- An earlier version said: "The residence record is the second: it must show the continuous three-month period, and a gap between addresses can reset the clock." Now: for a foreigner, Article 24(1)(a) and (2)(c) test the days left on a visa or card, with no continuous-period test and nothing on addresses or a clock that resets.

## Questions to put to the counter

The text leaves two points open. These are questions, not rules:

1. Which day is used to count the 90 days left on a visa or temporary residence card?
2. Which exchange fee applies on the day, since the circular states no amount?
