The exchange dossier is five items long, and each has a verification catch. Under Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, a foreigner exchanging a driving licence for a Vietnamese one files the following.

## The dossier, item by item

1. The application form for licence exchange, completed in the standard format.
2. The foreign licence itself, together with a Vietnamese translation certified by a notary office or by the issuing country's embassy or consulate in Vietnam. Uncertified self-translations are not accepted.
3. A valid passport.
4. Papers evidencing residence in Vietnam of at least three months — typically the temporary residence record issued where you live.
5. Health certification appropriate to the licence class sought, per the health standards of the law.

## The catches that cost repeat trips

The translation is the item applicants get wrong most often: it must be certified by a Vietnamese notary or the issuing country's diplomatic mission, not merely by an employer or a translation shop without certification. The residence record is the second: it must show the continuous three-month period, and a gap between addresses can reset the clock. The licence must be valid — an expired national licence cannot be exchanged and pushes the applicant toward the test route.

## Where and how it is filed

The dossier goes to the Traffic Police Department or a commune-level police point that accepts licence applications, or via the online public-service portal where available; the applicant has their photo taken at the counter. Processing takes days rather than weeks for clean files, and the state issuance fee applies under the Ministry of Finance schedule.

## What the exchange does not do

It does not add categories: a car-only licence exchanges into car classes only. It does not accept IDPs — the permit is for riding, not for swapping. And it does not waive the health certification, whatever the home licence says about medical fitness.
