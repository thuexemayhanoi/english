Locks make theft hard; marking makes theft pointless. A stolen motorbike has to be sold, and it sells best when nothing on it points back to the real owner. Security marking is the layer nobody advertises on the bike but every reseller has to worry about: it turns your bike into evidence.

## What marking kits contain

Three technologies are common. An invisible UV pen writes a code on frame, panels and parts that shows under a police lamp — cheap, invisible, and only as strong as your record-keeping. Engraving or stamping punches a visible code into metal: impossible to wash off, and a bike nobody wants to strip in public. Microdot kits brush or spray thousands of pinhead dots, each carrying a code, across the engine, panels and parts — you cannot sand off what you cannot find, and most thieves cannot be sure they have found them all.

## Why it works

A reseller buying a bike with visible marking on the frame knows two things: the parts are traceable, and an honest buyer with a police check can compare the code against a stolen-bike register. Marked bikes are therefore worth less to thieves and are riskier to chop for parts. Marking also hardens the other layers: a thief who spots microdot stickers on a scooter walks past it more often, the same deterrence logic as the visible hardware in disc-locks-chains-motorbike-security and motorbike-alarms-vietnam.

## The part people forget: registering the code

A code scratched nowhere in a database is a decoration. Whatever kit you use, record the code with the register the kit provides, keep the certificate with your registration documents, and photograph the marked areas. That paperwork is what converts marking into recovery: if the bike turns up in another province, the code on the frame is the statement that survives — trackers tell you where it went (gps-trackers-motorbikes-vietnam), but marking proves whose it is.

## Practical notes for Vietnam

Write the code on non-obvious metal as well as visible spots: inside panels, under seats, on the underside of the rack. Do not engrave painted outer surfaces exposed to weather unless the kit says so — corrosion can start at a raw stamp; use a clear touch-up or the kit's lacquer over the mark. Mark removable accessories too, since panels and mirrors are what actually get resold locally.
