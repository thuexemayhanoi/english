No. Vietnamese licence exams — motorbike and car alike — are run in Vietnamese, and the test rules in Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA name no English-language option. Foreigners sit the same exam, the same question bank, the same circuits as Vietnamese candidates. The one other format in the text, a paper read out with an interpreter, is for ethnic minority candidates who cannot read or write Vietnamese.

What a foreign rider can do instead is set out below: exchange a home licence, which needs no test; ride on a 1968 IDP and national licence together; stay inside the moped class; or prepare for the Vietnamese-language paper.

## What Circular 108 says about the language of the test

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security, in force from 1 July 2026, sets the tests in Article 4 and the A1 and A procedure in Appendix I. It ended Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA, except for provisions that Article 41(6) keeps in force to the end of 28 February 2027.

### Where English appears, and where it does not

A search of the whole Vietnamese text for "tiếng Anh" (English), "ngoại ngữ" (foreign language), "phiên dịch" and "thông dịch" (interpreter) found English in four places only, and an interpreter in one place, Appendix I. The English hits are the IDP definition (Article 3(5)), the IDP booklet layout (Article 27(2)), and the printed IDP and licence-card forms (Appendices XI and IX). None of the English hits is a test provision.

So the test rules read for this page make no provision for an English-language paper, question set or sitting. That describes the Circular, not what a training centre does, and nothing read for this page covers that. Articles 4 to 17 mention foreign candidates only in Article 16 (residence papers, clauses 2(c) and 3(b)), and none sets a different paper or circuit for them.

### The one interpreter format: Article 14

Article 14 gives class A1 candidates who are ethnic minority people unable to read or write Vietnamese a separate theory test. It is a question-and-answer format with multiple-choice answers on paper (Appendix I, Part A, section II). An examiner reads the questions, and an interpreter translates each question and answer and tells the examiner what the candidate has answered. The interpreter may not choose the answer. From 1 March 2027 the paper has 30 questions in 60 minutes, and 27 correct answers pass. The riding test is the ordinary one (Article 14(2)).

The text defines the group by ethnic minority status and by not reading or writing Vietnamese. It does not mention foreign candidates there and names no way for anyone outside the group to qualify. The format reads as assuming that the standard paper is read in Vietnamese, though no article says so in as many words. Article 41(2) applies the theory content that Article 4(4) assigns to Appendix I from 1 March 2027, and this page cannot say how the earlier text treats such a candidate.

### The room rules for the computer paper

In the computer paper of Appendix I, Part A, section I (from 1 March 2027), the examiner keeps people without a duty out of the theory room and bars documents, mobile phones and technical devices. The section names no interpreter, companion or translation tool. Do not plan the test around a phone app or a helper.

## Why this is the decisive fact for planning

The theory test is a timed multiple-choice paper on a computer. Memorisation can carry a candidate through a multiple-choice paper, but a candidate who cannot read Vietnamese reliably is memorising shapes, which is a fragile way to pass and a poor way to learn the road rules the test exists to enforce.

The paper is also the first gate. A motorbike candidate sits the theory paper, then riding test 1, then test 2 (Article 4(5)(a)); failing the paper bars test 1, and failing test 1 bars test 2 (Article 12(1)(b)). A theory pass is kept for one year (Article 12(1)(c)). Settle the language question before enrolling, not after.

## The route that avoids the test

For foreigners holding a valid national licence, the exchange under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA avoids the exam entirely: dossier, translation, residence papers, issuance. A valid motorbike licence exchanges into the corresponding class (A1 or A for a motorbike entitlement) without a Vietnamese theory test. This is why the exchange is the standard recommendation for licence-holders moving to Vietnam, and why the test question mostly matters for foreigners who hold no licence at all.

Article 18(7)(a) goes further than a recommendation. A foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam and holds a national licence must go through the exchange for the corresponding Vietnamese licence, and point (b) applies a driving-licence treaty to which Vietnam is a party where it provides otherwise. The 1968 pair below appears to be the route point (b) leaves open, although Article 18(7) does not name the Convention.

### Where language still enters the exchange

Article 24 asks for no test, but the dossier speaks Vietnamese: Article 24(2)(a) requires a Vietnamese translation of the foreign licence, certified by a notary office in Vietnam or made by a Vietnamese representative office abroad. [The guide to exchange documents]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the other items.

Article 24(1)(c) excludes, among others, a temporary foreign licence, an IDP and a licence past its term. Unless the 1968 pair applies, the holder of one of these comes back to the test for any bike above the moped class.

## Riding without a test: the 1968 pair and the moped class

Article 57(6)(b) of the Law counts an international driving permit together with a national licence, both suited to the vehicle and issued by a state party to the 1968 Vienna Convention, as valid in Vietnam. The rider carries both documents (Article 57(8)(b)). [The guide to the 1968 Convention]({{ '/articles/1968-vienna-convention-idp-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains which permits fit.

No text read for this page sets a date by which a resident riding on the pair must switch to an exchanged licence. A resident planning years of riding will want to ask the Traffic Police Department in writing which route it accepts for the rider's papers.

Article 34(1)(g) draws the moped line at a design speed of no more than 50 km/h with a motor of 50 cm3 or less if combustion, or 4 kW or less if electric. Under Article 56(1) moped riders carry no licence duty, and Article 59(1)(a) sets their minimum age at 16. Before relying on the class, check the bike's design speed and its engine or motor rating, since the definition decides. [The 50 cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the smallest bikes.

## If you must take the test

Candidates without an exchangeable licence prepare like everyone else, plus one layer: a training centre experienced with foreign candidates. Some centres may coach non-Vietnamese speakers through the question bank and the practical circuit; that is a question for each centre, because no text read for this page says what any centre offers.

Budget extra preparation time for the theory paper rather than hoping for a language accommodation that does not exist in the provisions read for this page.

Training comes first and registration for the test follows completion (Article 61(1)). There is no learner-permit stage in between, as [the learner permit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains.

### What a foreign candidate's dossier needs

Article 16(2) lists the training document, a health certificate, for a foreigner a copy of a residence paper such as a visa that needs 90 days or more of validity left, and for a re-sit the earlier results record. None is a language certificate.

### What the paper covers and when the format changes

Article 4(1)(a) lists the question subjects: road traffic law, driving technique, conduct rules for drivers, traffic culture and preventing the harm of alcohol and beer on the road. The Appendix I paper has 40 questions in 27 minutes, and 36 correct answers pass; it applies from 1 March 2027 (Article 41(2)).

Until the end of 28 February 2027 the Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA provisions kept by Article 41(6) govern, so the question count and pass mark depend on the sitting date. [The A1 licence test guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) sets out the counts and the circuit; ask the centre which format applies on yours.

### Questions to put to a training centre

No text read for this page says what centres offer foreign candidates, so ask:

- What language are the study materials in, and does anyone explain the questions in a language I read? Anything on offer is the centre's own service, not part of the exam.
- Which residence paper will you file for me, and will it still have 90 days left on the test date?
- How do you book a re-sit, given that a theory pass is kept for one year?

## A caution about "translation help" claims

Services offering to arrange English testing or special sittings for foreigners are describing something the official system does not provide in the provisions read for this page. A fair test of any such claim is to ask which article provides it: Article 14, through the Appendix I section it points to, is the only place the Circular mentions an interpreter, and it covers a different group of candidates. The exams are standardised nationally. What a legitimate service can do is prepare you for the Vietnamese exam or file your exchange. The exam language itself is fixed, as far as the text goes, and no article lets an applicant pick another language or a separate sitting.

## Quick answers

### Is there an English version of the Vietnamese motorbike theory test?

The texts read for this page make no provision for one. The only interpreter provision is the Article 14 paper for ethnic minority candidates who cannot read or write Vietnamese.

### Can I bring someone to translate, or use an app on my phone?

The text names no interpreter for the computer paper and keeps documents, mobile phones and technical devices out of the theory room. Ask the test office to put any other arrangement in writing.

### Can a visitor sit the test?

The text sets no separate rule for visitors. A foreign candidate's dossier holds the training document, a health certificate and a copy of a residence paper with at least 90 days left (Article 16(2)), so the dossier decides. Ask a training centre whether your visa or card qualifies before paying for a course.
