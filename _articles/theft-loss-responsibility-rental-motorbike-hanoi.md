Nobody wants this article until they need it. The honest version: on a rental, responsibility for a stolen bike depends first on what was agreed at handover and then on the Civil Code, and the renter may have to carry the loss; the deposit alone does not answer it. If it happens, tell the shop, report it to the police and keep what you are given. Prevention is the whole game.

## The responsibility split

The bike is in your custody during the rental. If it is stolen, three things decide who carries the loss: the deposit, the rental paper and any insurance.

- The deposit (typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions) is the first figure to ask about. No theft terms are published for Nguyen Tu rentals, so ask whether it would be kept in whole or in part, and whether anything beyond it can be claimed.
- Beyond the deposit, what the rental paper says comes first; where it is silent, the Civil Code rules below may apply. It may hold the renter responsible for the bike's value, or it may stop at a stated sum. Read the theft clause before you sign, not after the bike is gone. This is a genuine contract-to-contract difference in Hanoi, and it is the one clause worth asking about explicitly.
- On the 2023 text of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, the vehicle owner's compulsory third-party insurance does not cover theft of the bike, as the paragraph below explains.

The 2023 text of Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP insures the owner's civil liability towards third parties and passengers (Article 5). Article 7(1) lists damage outside a contract to the health, life and property of third parties caused by the vehicle in traffic and to the health and life of passengers on the bike; loss of the insured bike itself is not on that list. Decree 220/2026/NĐ-CP amended the decree from 1 July 2026, so ask an insurer for the current wording if the point matters to you.

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, and customers arrange their own. Cover against the theft of a rented bike, if you want it, would have to be arranged by you, for example through your own insurer: ask the insurer in writing whether it covers a rented two-wheeler for theft before you ride.

## How the Civil Code treats a stolen rental

Read this as general information, not legal advice; the official text is in Vietnamese. The Civil Code (Law 91/2015/QH13) treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property. [Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) goes through the lease articles clause by clause, so this section keeps to the few that a theft touches.

### The duty to preserve the bike

Article 479(1) says the lessee must preserve the leased property, carry out its upkeep and minor repairs, and make good the loss if they lose the property or damage it; natural wear from use is not the lessee's responsibility. It is the closest the lease articles come to a theft rule, and it leaves two things open: whether a bike taken by a thief counts as the lessee "losing" it, and whether any exemption applies, such as a theft that happens despite every precaution. The lease articles read for this guide (Articles 472 to 482) do not mention theft, so both questions go to the paper you sign and, if a dispute cannot be resolved, to a lawyer.

### Theft during a late return

Article 482(5) puts the risk to the leased property on the lessee for the time the return is late. It is the only place in those lease articles that speaks of risk, and it covers the overdue period only; it does not define risk or mention theft, so whether it reaches a bike stolen while overdue is for the paper and, failing that, a lawyer. [What Happens If You Return a Rental Motorbike Late in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) gives the published late-return charges. If you can see the return running late, tell the shop before the time passes.

### Damages and the idea of a ceiling

Article 360 says that where a breach of an obligation causes damage, the party under the obligation must make good all of it, unless an agreement or another law says differently; Article 13 states the same default for any infringed civil right. So where the lessee is liable for the loss, a limit such as "up to the deposit" would have to come from the agreement or from another law; whether a stolen bike makes the lessee liable is the open question above.

Articles 584 and 585 cover damage outside a contract: a person whose act infringes another's property and causes damage must compensate, unless the Code or another law provides otherwise (Article 584(1)), and actual damage is compensated in full and promptly, with the parties free to agree the amount and form unless the law provides otherwise (Article 585(1)). Which of these sets of rules would govern a dispute over a stolen rental is a question this guide does not settle.

## What to do in the first hour

1. Rule out a mistake. Check that you are at the right spot and ask nearby staff whether bikes were moved. [Where to Park a Motorbike in Hanoi: Rules and Etiquette]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the checks, including the case where officials took the bike.
2. Report to the police and keep a copy of whatever you are given. Ask the shop what it will want from you for any settlement; your own insurer, if you hold a policy that covers vehicle theft at all, will have its own list.
3. Call the shop with the facts: where, when, what was locked, what the police said. The number is +84 942 467 674, for calls and for WhatsApp or Zalo messages. The shop is open 09:00-21:00 every day and no out-of-hours line is published, so outside those hours send a message, make the police report and call when it opens.
4. Keep every document with your name on it - the contract, the police report, the receipt trail.

Follow the call with a written message saying what you reported and at what time. Agree no figure on the phone: ask for the amount, and the clause it comes from, in writing. If you see the bike, do not confront anyone; give the location to the police.

## What to record

A theft report is only as good as what you can show. Gather these while the details are fresh:

- The time you last saw the bike, the time you found it gone and the exact place.
- The plate number and model, from your handover photographs or the booking messages, and the number of keys you were given.
- What was locked, and where the key was.
- Whether the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate were with the bike or with you.
- The parking ticket, if there was one, and anyone who may have seen the spot, such as an attendant or a neighbouring shop.
- Who took the police report, the date, and what you were handed.

Much of this comes from the handover. [Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) shows what to photograph before you ride off.

## The prevention that actually works

- The lock routine, every single stop: steering lock on, key out, and a disc lock or chain if the bike has one.
- Attended parking (gửi xe) over free sidewalks - the few thousand dong is theft insurance priced correctly. That is a figure of speech: a parking fee is not an insurance policy, and what a ticket or an attendant promises is for the parking operator to say, so ask before relying on it.
- Never leave the key in the bike. Ever. A bike with the key in it has no lock for a thief to defeat, and the shop or an insurer may ask how it was left.
- At night, indoor or guarded parking beats the street, especially in central districts.

## If the papers were on the bike

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 (consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH) says a rider must carry, among other papers, the registration certificate (56(1)(a)) and the vehicle owner's certificate for the compulsory civil-liability insurance (56(1)(d)). For the registration the article names the certificate itself; a certified copy is allowed only for a vehicle mortgaged to a credit institution, together with that institution's original confirmation. Under Article 56(3), a rider whose papers are linked to an electronic identification account may show them through that account. The article is a duty to carry, placed on the rider; it does not say who replaces a lost paper.

After a theft the practical point is this: if the papers were in the bike they have gone with it, so say so in the first call to the shop and when you report it to the police. No replacement procedure is published here, so ask the shop what it will do and what it needs from you. [What Documents Must You Carry While Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the carrying duty.

## Questions to ask before you rent

Put these to the shop before you pay, and keep the answers in a message.

1. If the bike is stolen, what do I owe: the deposit, a stated sum or the bike's value?
2. Is that sum the same whether the bike was locked, in attended parking or left with the key in it?
3. Is there a cap, and is it written in the paper?
4. What do you need from me after a theft, and by when: a police report, the keys, a statement, anything else?
5. Does the paper say anything about a bike stolen while I am overdue?
6. How many keys come with the bike, and who holds the spare?
7. How will the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate be shown at a stop, and where should they stay when I park?

## One more responsibility that riders miss

Handing your rental to a friend is your risk, not theirs: the paper may name only you as the renter, so ask who may ride. ## Quick answers

### What if the bike goes missing from a guarded lot?

Treat it as a theft: tell the shop, report it to the police and show the ticket, your photographs and the attendant's name if you have it. What the lot owes you depends on the operator, so ask for its terms in writing. A ticket does not decide who carries the loss between you and the shop: that is a question for the rental contract and the law behind it.

### What if it happens outside Hanoi?

The steps are the same: report to the police where it happened, then tell the shop. Nguyen Tu's support is primarily for Hanoi and local use and no rescue service is published, so settle the theft questions before a trip, not from the roadside. [Can You Take a Rental Motorbike Outside Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the other points to agree first.

### Does the police report decide who pays?

Not by itself. It records that you reported the theft, and the shop or an insurer may ask for it. Who carries the loss is a question for the rental contract and the law behind it.

### Can the shop replace the bike while a theft is sorted out?

Nothing published says whether a replacement is offered, or on what terms. Ask when you rent whether a replacement counts as part of the same rental or a new one, and what happens to the deposit.
