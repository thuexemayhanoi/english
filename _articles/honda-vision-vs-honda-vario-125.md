The Vision is Vietnam's default scooter; the Vario 125 is an all-new 125 in Honda Vietnam's own words, and it arrives with hardware the Vision's page does not list. Honda Vision vs Honda Vario 125 in short: on Honda Vietnam's figures (retrieved 23 September 2026) the Vision is lighter (97 kg against 111 kg), has more ground clearance (175 mm against 131 mm) and costs less to buy, while the Vario 125 is the stronger, liquid-cooled 125 cc.

## The figures side by side

The Vision page shows the Sport version and the Vario page the Special version; each gives one spec sheet without saying which versions it covers. A cell marked "not published" means the figure was absent from the page, so it stays blank instead of being estimated.

Read the rows as manufacturer data, not road tests. Power and torque are single peak values, the consumption figures are claims whose test conditions Honda's pages do not state, and the prices are Honda's suggested retail prices for buyers, not shop prices and not rental prices.

Two rows carry most of the practical difference: kerb weight (111 kg against 97 kg) and ground clearance (131 mm against 175 mm). Length differs by 6 mm, width by 1 mm and wheelbase by 3 mm, which is no reason to pick either scooter. The Vario stands 51 mm lower overall, but neither page gives a turning-circle figure, so the table cannot say which one turns tighter.

## Engine

The Vision runs a 110 cc air-cooled eSP single - simple, proven and cheap to keep alive, with the whole city's repair ecosystem knowing it. Honda's pages say nothing about repair networks or running costs, so read "cheap to keep alive" as the general case for an air-cooled single with no coolant to service, not as a measured figure. The Vario 125 runs a 125 cc liquid-cooled eSP with PGM-FI, 8.48 kW at 8,500 rpm and 11.9 Nm at 5,000 rpm, plus idling stop for Hanoi's endless red-light queues. The Vario is the stronger engine on paper; the Vision is the simpler one.

The paper gap is 1.89 kW of peak power and 2.61 Nm of peak torque, and the Vario's torque peak comes at a lower engine speed (5,000 rpm against 6,000 rpm). Honda's pages give no top speed or acceleration figure, so the gap cannot be turned into kilometres per hour. Our [power and torque explainer]({{ '/articles/scooter-power-torque-explained/' | relative_url }}) shows what each figure measures.

Cooling is the other split. The Vision is air-cooled and the Vario liquid-cooled, and the Vario's meter carries a coolant warning lamp, so a mechanic has a coolant system to check on the Vario and none on the Vision. Honda's pages make no claim about heat tolerance either way, so the coolant system counts as a servicing item, not as a performance claim.

## Weight and wheels

Honda lists the Vision at 97 kg kerb and the Vario 125 at 111 kg. That 14 kg gap is real at parking speed and when paddling a loaded bike - see our verified [kerb]({{ '/articles/scooter-kerb-weights-vietnam-verified/' | relative_url }}) weight table for how this class lines up. The Vision's 16-inch front wheel is a stability asset on broken streets, explained in our 16-inch [wheel guide]({{ '/articles/16-inch-wheel-scooters-vietnam/' | relative_url }}); the Vario's 14-inch wheels trade a little of that for agility and a lower seat.

The Vario's seat is 16 mm lower (769 mm against 785 mm), which may help a shorter rider, but Honda's tables give neither seat width nor seat shape, so sit on both. The Vision's front tyre is 80/90-16 and the Vario's 90/80-14, with rear tyres of 90/90-14 and 100/80-14. Honda's Vario page calls its tyres wide and tubeless, while the Vision page does not say, and neither page gives handling or wet-grip data.

## Features and price

The Vario 125 page lists a smart key with an alarm function, an LCD meter, a USB-C port, full LED lighting and CBS brakes. The Vision keeps things basic and cheap to run, on the mechanical side at least, with air cooling and the lower claimed fuel use. Honda's list price for the Vario 125 Special is 41,913,818 VND (Street 42,895,637 VND; price list read 27 October 2026).

The Vision's page also lists a Smart Key, an LCD meter, an idling stop, an always-on headlight and a front compartment with a USB-C port, so those no longer separate the two scooters. What the Vario page lists and the Vision page does not are liquid cooling, a front disc with CBS, full LED lighting, wide tubeless tyres, a fuller meter and a front hook for bags. That is a difference between two pages, not proof the Vision lacks them.

The same price list shows four Vision versions, from 31,506,545 VND (Standard) to 36,808,363 VND (Sport), so every Vario version is listed above every Vision version, by at least 5,105,455 VND. The Vario's Street version is the one with the bare handlebar. Treat these as buyers' list prices rather than rental charges: a dealer can quote what a particular version costs in full, and our [new scooter price guide]({{ '/articles/new-scooter-prices-vietnam-verified/' | relative_url }}) lists other models.

## Fuel, tank and range

Honda claims 1.82 L/100 km for the Vision and 2.11 L/100 km for the Vario 125, a gap of 0.29 litres per 100 km, or about 2.9 litres over 1,000 km on our arithmetic. Honda's pages do not state the test conditions behind either figure, and the Vario's range line says only "normal operating conditions".

The tank sizes even out the range. Dividing each tank by its claimed consumption gives about 264 km for the Vision on its 4.8 L tank at a claimed 1.82 L/100 km and about 261 km for the Vario on 5.5 L at a claimed 2.11 L/100 km. Those are our arithmetic, not a Honda range claim for the Vision; for the Vario, Honda's own page says about 260 km on one fill. So the two go about as far between fuel stops, and the Vision's advantage is lower fuel use per kilometre. Further models are compared in our [scooter fuel economy comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-fuel-economy-comparison-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

## Storage, brakes and equipment

### Storage

Honda lists up to 18 litres under the Vario's seat, enough for one half-helmet and personal items. Its Vision page calls the bay large and says it takes a helmet and personal items, but gives no litres and no helmet type, so the two cannot be compared in litres. Both pages list a front compartment with a USB-C port. Take your own helmet to try in each bay; our [underseat storage comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-underseat-storage-comparison/' | relative_url }}) shows what other scooters hold.

### Brakes and lights

Honda's Vario page lists a wave-pattern front disc brake and CBS, its combined braking system, which Honda says helps control when slowing down; it does not mention ABS. Honda's Vision page names no brake type and no ABS, so nothing is said here about the Vision's braking. That is a gap in Honda's page, not evidence of weaker brakes, so squeeze both levers and read the [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) first. On lighting, the Vario page describes full LED front and rear lighting. The Vision page mentions LED decorative lights, a smoked-lens headlight system and an always-on headlight mode that Honda says helps the scooter be seen.

### Smart Key and idling stop

Both pages list Smart Key. The Vision page says it locates the scooter and unlocks it remotely; the Vario page adds an alarm function. The Vision page does not say whether every version has the key, so check the one in front of you; our [smart key guide]({{ '/articles/smart-key-scooters-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains keyless systems and fob batteries. Both pages also list an idling stop. On the Vision, the engine stops once the scooter has been stationary longer than 3 seconds and starts again when you turn the throttle; the Vario page gives no interval. Honda's pages do not say whether either can be switched off, so ask before you rely on it.

## How the differences play out in Hanoi traffic

### Narrow lanes and parking

Hanoi riding alternates between main roads and lanes where you sometimes step a scooter around by hand. The Vision's lower weight helps in a parking row and its 175 mm of clearance helps with raised kerbs and ramps. The Vario's lower seat may suit a shorter rider, but its 131 mm of clearance calls for more care at raised edges.

### Wet roads

Honda gives no wet-grip, braking-distance or wading-depth data for either scooter, and neither page lists ABS, so nothing here ranks them in rain. Riding habits matter more than the model: smooth braking on both levers, a longer gap, and no riding through standing water whose depth you cannot see. For wet-weather choices in general, see the [rainy-season scooter guide]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-rainy-season-commuting-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Carrying a passenger

Neither page lists a maximum load, so ask for the figure in the owner's manual. On paper the Vario has more power and torque in reserve with two aboard, but Honda's Vario page counts one half-helmet in its bay, so check where a passenger's helmet will go.

## Which one

Buy the Vision for simplicity, light weight, low running cost and parts availability everywhere. Buy the Vario 125 if you want a 125 cc with liquid cooling, a front disc with CBS and the fuller meter, and accept the extra weight. Newer riders should also read our 110cc [vs 125cc explainer]({{ '/articles/110cc-vs-125cc-scooters/' | relative_url }}) before deciding that the bigger engine is automatically better.

## Common questions

### Is the Vario 125 faster than the Vision?

Honda's two pages publish neither a top speed nor an acceleration time. The Vision's 6.59 kW and 9.29 Nm sit below the Vario's 8.48 kW and 11.9 Nm, and the Vario is 14 kg heavier.

### Does the Vision have Smart Key and idling stop too?

Yes, on Honda's Vision page, which lists both. It does not say whether every version has the Smart Key, so confirm it on the exact scooter.

### Which scooter uses less petrol?

The Vision uses less, by Honda's claimed 1.82 L/100 km against 2.11 L/100 km, though the Vario's larger tank gives a similar range: about 264 km against about 261 km on our arithmetic.

Nguyen Tu publishes no rental rate for the Vario 125; contact us to confirm current availability for any model. The Vision has a published daily rate of 200,000 VND.
