The school run on an e-bike is a Vietnamese daily ritual — and the smallest passengers need the biggest margins. Here is what the law requires and what practice suggests.

## The legal baseline

Helmets are required for riders and passengers of powered two-wheelers under Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 (pedal-type e-bikes under Article 31(3)) — children riding as passengers are covered. That means a properly fitted child helmet, not an adult helmet perched on a small head: the standard-compliant marking matters at the sizes that exist for kids, and the strap has to be snug enough to survive a head-shake test.

## Choosing the machine and the pace

- The licence-free classes (pedal e-bikes and sub-4 kW mopeds) keep speeds in the 25-50 km/h world — the right territory for carrying children. A1-class machines with a child aboard are legal with the right licence but demand more discipline.
- The quiet motor is a genuine child advantage: no hot exhaust to touch at stops, no fumes at breathing height, no noise the little passenger has to talk over.

## The practical routine

1. **Mount and dismount first, child second.** Both feet down, bike braced, then lift the child on and off — never load a child before you are stable.
2. **Feet on the pegs, hands on the rider or grab rail** — agree the rules before the first ride and repeat them until habitual.
3. **Shorten every journey** — a child passenger halves your safe planning margins: brake earlier, launch gentler, skip the gaps you would take alone. See our two-up [guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-two-people-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) for the technique.
4. **The silent-ride rules double up:** with a child aboard you are invisible and unheard, so position where you are seen.
5. **Weather check:** rain means the child needs the same gear you do — dry layers, clear visor or glasses.

## One habit above all

Ride as if the child will wiggle at the worst moment — because eventually they will — and build the margins to absorb it.
