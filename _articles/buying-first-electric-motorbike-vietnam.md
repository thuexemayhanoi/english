Buying your first electric two-wheeler in Vietnam comes down to four questions — get them in order and the model list shrinks to a handful.

## 1. What may you legally ride?

Above 4 kW requires an A1 licence (up to 11 kW) or an A licence. No licence? Your ceiling is the sub-4 kW, sub-50 km/h moped class and pedal e-bikes. This single question eliminates half the market. See the class guide first.

## 2. Where will you charge?

This is the real deal-breaker. A home or office socket makes any model viable and cheap to run. No socket? You want a swappable-battery model — VinFast's swap network (9,000 VND per battery per swap; 4,500 stations installed as of January 2026) — or a fast-charging brand like Dat Bike. Never buy a fixed-battery bike you cannot charge where you park.

## 3. How far do you actually ride per week?

Honest weekly distance, plus 30 percent margin, is your range requirement — after converting claimed range to real-world (claimed at 35 km/h shrinks roughly a third at 70 km/h). Most Hanoi commuters need far less than they think.

## 4. What is the budget structure?

Sticker prices start low: Yadea's Vietnamese line-up lists models from around 13,990,000 VND, VinFast's Evo opened at 19,990,000 VND excluding battery (with battery rental at 175,000-300,000 VND per month), and premium models climb from there. Compare the total structure — battery rented or owned, charger included, warranty terms — not just the bike price.

## A shortlist to start

- **No licence, student budget:** pedal-assisted or sub-50 km/h models (VinFast Amio, Evo Lite class; Yadea's city models).
- **No licence, more speed:** sub-4 kW scooters reaching 70 km/h (VinFast Evo, Feliz II, Viper).
- **A1 licence, real distance:** Dat Bike Weaver 200 or Weaver++.

Try the format before committing: renting an E-Bike (200,000 VND per day at this shop; contact us to confirm availability) tells you whether electric suits your week at all.
