Vietnam's answer to teenage moped riding is organised through schools, not through licence tests. Here is what the programme is — and, just as importantly, what it is not.

## The legal frame

Decree 151/2024/NĐ-CP arranges road-safety knowledge and safe-moped-riding skills training for students at high schools and vocational institutions. It exists alongside the licence-free regime for 50cc-or-under mopeds: the rider must be at least 16 (Article 59 of Law 36/2024/QH15), and police have publicly clarified that 16-to-18-year-olds may ride mopeds without a licence or training certificate. Training therefore reaches riders through their school rather than acting as a universal permit.

## What the training aims to cover

The programme's purpose, as the decree frames it, is competence before traffic: understanding traffic rules and having basic vehicle-handling skills — the same conditions Article 56(4) imposes on every moped rider regardless of age. In practice the content centres on:

- the road rules that apply to mopeds — helmets, lanes, signals, drink-riding prohibitions
- basic machine control: starting, stopping, slow-speed balance, safe braking
- risk awareness for the situations that actually hurt teenage riders: night riding, passenger carrying, speed differentials, phone use

## What it is not

It is not a licence. Passing a school programme does not permit riding a 50cc-or-over motorbike before 18 — that remains a 400,000–600,000 VND fine under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, whatever the school taught. And it is not optional knowledge: a 16-year-old on a moped is fined at adult levels for adult violations.
