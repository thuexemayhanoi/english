You cannot ride shotgun on a motorbike — but you can teach like you do. A teenager's first month on a 50cc sets habits for years, and a parent who plans that month changes the odds.

## Before the first ride: the law and the kit

The legal frame is short and firm: 16 minimum for a moped, no licence required, helmet for rider and any passenger, adult-level fines. Our age-rules article is the reference version. The gear is part of the lesson, not the shopping: helmet fitted properly, closed shoes, gloves — the teen gear article covers the realistic kit. If gear is negotiable, the lessons are already losing.

## Use the school programme as an ally

Decree 151/2024 organises road-safety and safe-moped training through schools; treat what the school teaches as the syllabus, and your supervision as the practicals. Our training article explains what the programme covers so the two efforts don't talk past each other.

## The four-stage progression

1. **Empty space, engine off.** Walking the bike, both brakes while walking, stand-up balance drills. Boring is the point.
2. **Empty space, engine on.** Straight lines, gentle stops, U-turns both ways, at walking-jogging pace. Where to do this: our practice-spaces article maps Hanoi's options.
3. **Quiet streets, off-peak, parent following on another bike or walking a parallel pavement.** Junctions, indicating, mirror checks, the first real traffic grammar. Ride behind, not beside — you want to see their line and their head, not their profile.
4. **Real routes, short, off-peak first, then peak.** The school route itself, practised at weekend-morning traffic levels before it is ever ridden at 7 a.m.

## The drills that pay

- **Commentary riding.** Have them narrate hazards out loud on quiet streets: "bus indicating, door zone, gravel at the corner". You will hear exactly what they are not seeing.
- **Brake rehearsals.** Straight-line stops from low speed, front brake progressive, practised until boring — the emergency braking article is the drill sheet.
- **The Gap Rule.** They only ride gaps you would ride — until they stop needing the rule.

## The rules that ride along

No passengers in the first month (and never an unhelmeted one, ever); no phone in the hand; lights on always; the bike's weekly checks are the rider's job. Write them down together; signed rules outlast shouted ones. The parents' purchase guide frames the same rules at the buying stage.

## Correcting without crushing

Critique the manoeuvre, never the rider; keep sessions short enough to end on success; and ride yourself the way you tell them to ride — the entire month is also a demonstration, and they are watching you harder than the road.
