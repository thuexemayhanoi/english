On a moving bike in Vietnam's heat, sweat evaporates instantly and lies to you: you are losing water faster than you feel. Dehydration on a long ride does not announce itself as thirst — it announces itself as a headache, a slow brain and late braking.

## Drink before, during, after

Start the ride hydrated: a large glass of water before you dress. On the bike, the rule is a few swallows at every stop, plus a deliberate stop every hour or so — Vietnamese roadside stalls and cafes make this easy. Aim to finish a litre every two to three hours of riding in hot weather, more in the mountains where you climb.

## What to drink

Water is the base. Electrolytes matter on all-day rides: sweat drains salt, and plain water alone cannot replace it — a sachet or a sports drink at lunch keeps the evening cramps away. Skip the heavily caffeinated energy drinks for a long day; they raise heart rate in heat. Cold drinks feel great but sit in the stomach; cool beats iced for riding on.

## Carry it right

Two bottles beat one: one in easy reach under the seat or in a bag, one spare. On a hot ride, freeze one the night before so it thaws as you go. what-to-carry-riding-motorbike-vietnam covers the rest of the day-kit.

## The warning signs

Headache, irritability, dizziness, dark urine, a ride that suddenly feels hard at a speed that felt easy an hour ago — all are dehydration or its teammate heat exhaustion. The response is the same: stop in shade, drink steadily, cool down, and end the ride if you do not improve within half an hour. riding-fatigue-motorbike-vietnam explains how these states compound.
