Yes — foreigners can rent electric two-wheelers in Vietnam, and for many visitors the licence-free classes are the easiest legal way onto two wheels. The catch is the same one that applies to locals: the 4 kW power boundary.

## The licence question first

- **Pedal e-bikes and sub-4 kW mopeds:** no Vietnamese driving licence is required; riders must be at least 16. This is the class most rental e-bikes sit in.
- **Above 4 kW:** a licence is required — A1 for up to 11 kW, A above. An International Driving Permit endorsed for the A1 class, or a Vietnamese A1, is what covers you. Renting a Weaver 200-class machine with only a car licence is riding unlicensed.

Documents worth carrying: passport, IDP with motorcycle endorsement if you plan anything above 4 kW, and the rental agreement.

## Rental practice

Deposit norms in Hanoi are typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle. Accepted payments are cash, bank transfer and QR. The shop's published E-Bike rate is 200,000 VND per day; weekly and monthly e-bike rates are not published, so contact us to confirm current availability and longer-term options. Electric motorbikes above 4 kW are not part of the published rental fleet — availability is always a contact-first question.

## Practical checks before you ride off

1. Confirm the bike's rated power so you know which licence class you are riding in.
2. Ask how and where the battery charges, and what the range realistically is at city speed.
3. Check the helmet fits — it is legally required.
4. Photograph existing scratches; agree how battery damage or a dead pack is handled.
