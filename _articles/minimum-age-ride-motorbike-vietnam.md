The legal minimum age depends on the machine, not on how experienced a teenager looks. Under Article 59 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), which took effect on 1 January 2025, a person must be at least 16 years old to ride a moped (xe gắn máy) and at least 18 years old to hold a class A1 motorbike licence — the licence that covers anything above moped level, up to and including 125 cc.

Two clauses carry that summary. Under Article 59(1)(a), a person of 16 or over may drive a moped; under Article 59(1)(b), a person of 18 or over may be issued a licence of class A1, A, B1, B or C1. The clauses cited on this page are read in the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH of the Law as amended by Law 118/2025/QH15, where none of them carries an amendment note.

## The two age thresholds

Sixteen is the age for mopeds only. A moped means an engine of 50 cc or less, or an electric motor of up to 4 kW, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. Article 34(1)(g) is the source of those limits; it also says that a moped has two or three wheels and that the term does not include a motorised pedal bicycle (xe đạp máy). [What counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) goes through the definition limit by limit.

No licence is needed for this category, but the age floor still applies. Article 56(1) lifts the licence requirement for moped riders, and Article 56(4) replaces it with a duty to know the road traffic rules, to be able to control the vehicle, and to satisfy the age and health conditions that Article 59(1)(a) and 59(2) set. Neither a test nor a certificate appears in clause 4; it states what the rider must know and be able to do. [Riding a 50cc moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-50cc-at-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what a 16-year-old rider takes on with the key.

Eighteen is the age for everything above the moped category. That holds for motorbikes, three-wheelers included: Article 59(1)(b) sets 18 for classes A1, A, B1, B and C1, while Article 59(1)(c) to (đ) set 21, 24 and 27 for the heavier lorry, bus and trailer classes. Read the first sentence of this section as covering motorbikes only: for the heavier classes the age is higher. From 18, a rider can obtain the [A1 licence]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) and legally ride standard scooters and motorbikes such as 110–125 cc automatics.

### Every age in Article 59(1)

Article 59(1) is a ladder, and a motorbike rider meets only its first two steps. In order, with the licence classes each step covers:

| Minimum age | Article 59(1) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Point (a) | Driving a moped (xe gắn máy) |
| 18 | Point (b) | Licence classes A1, A, B1, B and C1, and the road-traffic-law knowledge certificate needed to operate special-purpose machinery |
| 21 | Point (c) | Licence classes C and BE |
| 24 | Point (d) | Licence classes D1, D2, C1E and CE |
| 27 | Point (đ) | Licence classes D, D1E, D2E and DE |

Article 59(3) hands the age of drivers in the army and the police on defence and security duty to the Ministers of Defence and Public Security.

### What an 18-year-old can be licensed for

Class A1 (Article 57(1)(a)) is for riders of a two-wheeled motorbike whose engine is up to 125 cm3 or whose electric motor is up to 11 kW. Class A covers the two-wheelers above those limits as well as the A1 machines (Article 57(1)(b)), and class B1 covers three-wheeled motorbikes and the A1 machines (Article 57(1)(c)). All three open at 18, so the Law has no younger step between the moped and A1 and no older step between A1 and A.

[The licence classes guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) sets the classes side by side, and [the three-wheel B1 licence]({{ '/articles/b1-three-wheel-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has its own page.

## Where the age is measured: the test date and training

For a licence class, the Law ties the age to the test. Article 61(1) lets a person who has finished the training programme and has the age and health required by Article 59 register for it. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, in force from 1 July 2026 (Article 41(1)), repeats the age condition for candidates in Article 15(1) and adds the date it is counted to: the age is counted up to the date of the test.

Illustrative arithmetic, not an official example: a candidate whose 18th birthday falls ten days after the test date has not reached 18 on that date, and Article 15(1) measures the age on that date.

Training comes before the test. Article 60(1) and (2) require training in the programme for the class, at a training facility or in another form of training, but they state no age for starting a course, and Articles 15 and 16 of the Circular state none for enrolment either. Ask the centre what it requires from a trainee under 18. [How to get an A1 licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows the route from enrolment to the card.

## What happens if the age rule is broken

Riding a motorbike before turning 18 has a specific penalty. Under Article 18(4)(a) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as issued, a person aged 16 to under 18 who rides a motorbike of 50 cc or more, or an electric motorbike of 4 kW or more, is fined within a band the decree sets. Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, in force from 15 August 2026, amended Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, so this page quotes no amount; [the guide to underage riding penalties]({{ '/articles/underage-riding-penalties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the schedule as the 2024 text had it, and any figure there needs checking against the current decree.

The Government portal report listed in the sources describes the amendment as clarified wording on penalising 16-to-under-18 motorbike riders. The amended clause is not reproduced on this page, so check its current text before relying on the description of Article 18(4)(a) given here.

One misreading holds that a 16-year-old may not ride any kind of two-wheeler. That is not what the rule does. People of 16 and above may still ride [mopeds]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}); the fine targets only under-18s on licence-required machines. The first half rests on Article 59(1)(a).

For riders under 16, Article 59(1) names no age below 16 for driving any motor vehicle, and Article 56(4) requires a moped rider to meet the age condition of Article 59(1)(a). The penalty for a rider that young is set by decree, not by the Law, and this page quotes none.

## Why it matters when renting

The age thresholds attach to the person riding, so they bind a renter exactly as they bind an owner: Article 56(1) asks every driver to meet the age condition set by law, and Article 59(1) is where the Law sets it. A 16-year-old visitor cannot legally ride a 110 cc scooter even with a parent present, because Article 59(1)(b) puts the A1 licence at 18 and Article 56(1) asks for a licence suited to the vehicle, with an exception only for moped riders. That sentence assumes the visitor holds no licence valid in Vietnam; the questions below take up a foreign licence held under 18.

A moped-style 50 cc — where no licence is required — is the only category open at 16, and shops may still set their own minimum age above the legal line. The moped class is wider than that wording suggests: Article 34(1)(g) also takes electric motors of up to 4 kW, and a motorised pedal bicycle is not a moped at all, because Article 34(2)(b) lists it among the simple vehicles (xe thô sơ), for which Article 59(1) names no age. [Riding an electric moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-electric-moped-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the electric case.

Anyone renting on behalf of a younger rider should understand that the adult who supplies the motorbike carries separate legal exposure, not just the teenager. That exposure has a basis in the Law itself: Article 9(7) prohibits handing a motor vehicle over to someone who lacks the lawful conditions for driving it on the road. The penalty is set by decree and is not quoted here; [lending a motorbike to an unqualified rider]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the 2024 figure, which needs checking against the amended decree.

### If the rider is 16 or 17

Only the moped class is open, so the first question is whether the machine is one. Ask which engine size or motor power the bike has and compare it with the limits in Article 34(1)(g); a model name settles nothing. ### If the rider is 18 or older

An 18-year-old needs a licence valid in Vietnam and suited to the bike. Article 57(6) names the licences that count, among them a Vietnamese licence (point a) and an international permit with the national licence from a 1968 Convention member (point b). ## Three misreadings, answered from the text

**"A parent on the back makes it legal."** It does not. Article 56(1) asks the driver for a licence suited to the vehicle and writes only one exception, for moped riders under clause 4. Article 59(1)(b) puts the A1 licence at 18, and neither article contains an exception for riding with a parent. Article 33(1) limits how many people a rider may carry; it says nothing about who may ride.

**"Electric means no age."** An electric machine with a motor of up to 4 kW and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h is a moped, so 16 applies (Articles 34(1)(g) and 59(1)(a)). Over either limit it is a motorbike: class A1 covers electric motors of up to 11 kW and class A those above, and both open at 18 (Articles 57(1)(a) and (b) and 59(1)(b)).

**"A school course unlocks a bigger bike."** Decree 151/2024/NĐ-CP organises moped-riding training for high-school and vocational students, and that training is not a licence class; [what the programme covers]({{ '/articles/50cc-training-high-school-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has its own guide. Article 59(1)(b) still sets 18 for class A1.

## Questions riders ask

### Is there an upper age limit for riding a motorbike?

Article 59(1) sets a maximum age only in point (e), for drivers of passenger vehicles with more than 29 seats (buses included, the driver's seat not counted) and of sleeper buses: 57 for men and 55 for women. The paragraph names no upper age for riding a motorbike. Article 59(2) asks every driver for health suited to the vehicle.

### Can I apply for the A1 test a few days before my 18th birthday?

Article 15(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA counts the age up to the date of the test, so a candidate who has not reached 18 on that date does not meet the condition. Articles 15 and 16 do not say whether a dossier may be filed earlier. Ask the traffic police department that approves dossiers before paying for a course. Article 61(1) of the Law speaks of a person who already has the age required by Article 59 registering for the test, and no text read here says how that fits a dossier filed before the birthday.

### Can a 17-year-old tourist ride a 125 cc scooter on a licence from home?

Article 59(1)(b) sets 18 for the Vietnamese A1 licence, and Article 56(1) asks every driver to meet the age condition set by law and to hold a licence suited to the vehicle. Article 57(6), which lists the foreign licences valid in Vietnam, mentions no age, and neither Article 56 nor Article 59 says how an official applies the Article 59(1)(b) age to a foreign licence held by someone under 18, so there is no answer here to rely on. Get the traffic police's answer in writing before riding, and read [can tourists ride legally]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [the IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) for the licence side.

### Does a 16-year-old need a medical certificate to ride a moped?

Article 56(4) asks a moped rider to meet the health condition of Article 59(2), which requires health suited to the vehicle and leaves the standards and the medical examination to the Minister of Health. Neither clause names a certificate for moped riders. A test candidate for a licence class does file a health certificate (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 16(2)(b)); that is a dossier paper, not a condition written into the moped rule.
