The inspection you do at handover is the evidence that decides any deposit disagreement later. Walk through this list every time - none of it needs tools or mechanical skill. This rental motorbike inspection checklist runs in one order: whole-bike photographs first (details under Body and photos), then lights, brakes, controls, tyres and chain, fuel, plates and papers, and a clear rule for when to ask for a different bike.

## Before you pick up the keys

Do the walk-round before you pay and before you sign, with whoever hands over the bike still there. The shop's published hours run from 09:00 to 21:00, so a handover late in the day may be after dark: ask for a well-lit spot, and keep your phone torch for the tyres and the underside. The [step-by-step rental guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts this inspection at step five of six.

Have three things ready: a charged phone with room for photographs and a short video; the licence you will ride on, with your permit if you ride on a foreign licence; and your questions about the fuel level to return with and who pays if a tyre is punctured.

Take the whole-bike photographs first, before you touch anything, so they show the bike as it was handed over. Then work round the bike in one direction, starting at the back, and use the same path at the return so that your checks and your photographs line up.

## Lights and electrics

- Headlight on low and high beam - riding unlit between 18:00 and 06:00 is a fine, and a dead headlight is also an equipment violation.
- Brake light - check with both levers.
- Turn signals front and rear.
- Horn - in Hanoi traffic the horn is communication, not aggression.
- Number plate light - on the required-equipment list below, so check it too.

Start the engine before you test any of them. It should start without coaxing and idle without cutting out, and a warning symbol on the dashboard that stays lit is a question for the shop before you leave.

Daylight hides a headlight and a brake light, so turn the bike towards a wall or a shop window and look for the reflection, or ask whoever is with you to stand behind the bike while you press each brake control, the rear pedal included. The same trick works for the indicators.

The law expects a motorbike to have working brakes, a headlight, a brake light, turn signals, a horn, a plate light and a left mirror. The [required equipment guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-required-equipment-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the set, and the [light rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers when lights must be on.

## Brakes

Front and rear, both tested rolling the bike. The front brake should bite without pulling the lever to the bar; the rear should lock under firm pressure. Test them again at low speed before you join traffic.

To test while rolling the bike, push it forward on level ground and squeeze one brake at a time: the wheel should stop turning and the bike should stay put. Feel for a lever or pedal that is spongy, travels almost to the grip or does not spring back, and listen for grinding. For the low-speed test, if the shop agrees, choose a clear stretch away from traffic and brake gently first, then firmer: the bike should stop in a straight line, without pulling to one side.

## Steering and controls

- Turn the bars lock to lock - no notchiness, no cable snagging.
- Throttle snaps closed cleanly.
- Mirrors present and adjustable - riding without the left mirror is an equipment violation in Vietnam.
- Steering lock - turn the bars fully, lock them with the key you were given, then release. A lock that sticks, or a key that has to be forced, is a fault to report now, not at a parking spot.
- Stand - the bike should stand firmly, and the stand should spring clear when you lift the bike upright.
- Levers and footpegs - not bent, not loose.

Check the throttle twice, with the bars straight and with the bars turned: it should snap closed both times. Set the mirrors for your height and arm length before you move, and ask for a loose one to be tightened rather than riding with it.

## Tyres and chain

Tread visible on both tyres, no cracks in the sidewalls, pressures firm. Look for cuts, bulges, and nails or glass lodged in the rubber, and for cords showing through the surface; cords are a reason to refuse the bike. Press each tyre with your thumb, and if one feels soft, ask the shop to check it with a gauge. The right pressure differs by model and load, so ask the shop what it should be.

The shop's maintenance may include tyre-pressure checks, while inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.

Chain on manual and semi-automatic bikes: not rusted stiff, and neither hanging in a loop nor pulled drum-tight. The right amount of slack differs by model, so ask the shop what it should be on this bike. Chain adjustment and tension are among the maintenance items the shop may include. Flag a loose chain at handover, not after.

## Fuel, plates and papers

- Note the fuel level and agree the return level - the [fuel-policy article]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-fuel-policy-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the two conventions.
- Number plate fitted and clean - riding without a number plate is a serious violation. If you are shown the registration papers, compare the characters with them: they should be readable and match. The [number plates guide]({{ '/articles/riding-without-number-plates-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the offence.
- Registration papers: ask at handover how the bike's registration will be shown at a police stop. Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15 expects the rider to carry the registration certificate itself; a certified copy counts only for a bike mortgaged to a bank, together with the bank's original confirmation, and papers already integrated into an electronic identification account can be shown through that account. Keep the shop's number; the guide to [police holding a motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what can happen when papers cannot be produced.

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance: customers arrange their own cover before they ride. That is separate from the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate, which Article 56(1)(d) of the same law lists among the documents a rider must carry, so ask at handover how it will be shown at a stop. The [documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the papers the law expects a rider to carry.

### Keys, helmet and extras

Count the keys you are given and note the number in the chat, along with anything else handed over, such as a lock, if one is supplied; the list at the return should match it.

Vietnamese law requires riders and passengers to wear a helmet, and the [helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the rule. Ask whether a helmet comes with the bike, and whether one is available for a passenger; do not assume either. If one is provided, check the shell for cracks, that the strap fastens and holds, and that it fits snugly without pressing.

## Body and photos

Walk the bike with your phone camera out. Photograph every panel, both sides, the exhaust and the underside, and any scratch that already exists. Send the photos to the shop's chat - a timestamped record that protects both of you.

### What to capture

- The whole bike from the left, right, front and back, standing far enough away that the plate is readable in the rear shot.
- Each existing mark close up, then one step back so its position on the bike is clear.
- Both tyres, the chain if the bike has one, and the levers, mirrors and footpegs.
- The dashboard, showing the fuel gauge and the odometer.
- The plate, and the registration certificate if you are shown it.

### How to make the record count

Shoot in daylight or a lit area, and keep the original files on your phone, unedited. Then film one slow video walk-round along the same path, starting and ending at the plate, with the engine running and the lights, indicators and horn operated. Say the date and the plate number aloud at the start.

Send the video to the shop together with the photographs, on WhatsApp or Zalo at +84 942 467 674; [how to book by WhatsApp, Zalo or walk-in]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the options.

Point out each existing mark to whoever hands over the bike and ask for it to be noted.

## What changes with the bike you are given

The walk-round is the same for every bike, and a few checks shift with the type. The Wave, Sirius, Vision and Air Blade details come from Honda's and Yamaha's own Vietnamese pages, and they apply only if the shop offers that model on your dates.

| Bike | What differs | At handover |
|---|---|---|
| Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius | Semi-automatic underbones: gears are shifted by foot. Honda lists a four-speed gearbox for the Wave Alpha 110 and Wave RSX, and Yamaha lists one for the Sirius RC | Check that each gear engages cleanly and that neutral can be found at a standstill; look at the chain |
| Yamaha Sirius | Drum and disc versions exist, and Yamaha's page for the RC lists a front disc, a rear drum and tyres with inner tubes | Judge the brakes against that bike's own type |
| Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade | Honda lists an automatic (CVT) transmission for both: twist and go, so no gear change | Test the throttle return and both brake levers; the chain check is for semi-automatic and manual bikes |
| Honda Air Blade | Honda's pages for the Air Blade 125 and 160 both list a coolant temperature warning | Watch for it on the dashboard after starting |
| Honda Click, Yamaha Mio | This guide quotes no manufacturer figures for these two models | Ask the shop what type of bike it is; do not assume from the name |

## What to reject outright

Bent frames, brakes that do not stop the bike confidently, tyres with cords showing, missing mirrors, no number plate. A cheap rental with failed brakes is not a bargain.

Add to that list steering that binds or pulls, a throttle that does not close, fuel or oil dripping from underneath, a headlight or brake light that does not work, and an engine that cuts out at idle or needs repeated attempts to start. Each of these is a reason to ask for another bike, or for the fault to be put right before you leave.

### Asking for a different bike

No swap policy is published, so make it a request and keep it specific: "The rear brake feels weak. Can it be adjusted, or is another bike available?" Wait for the answer before you pay or sign. A promise to fix it later is not a fix before you ride.

### Marks you note instead

Scratches, scuffs, a worn seat and a dirty panel are not safety faults. Whether they matter is a question for the deposit conversation, and the photographs you took earlier are how you show they were there from the start.

If the bike passes, sign and ride; your photographs are already taken by then. If anything is marginal, ask for a different bike - availability changes, so contact us to confirm what is in stock today.

## Running it backwards at the return

The return is the handover in reverse. Photograph the bike along the same path and from the same angles, and compare the fuel gauge and every mark with your handover set before you hand over the key. If something differs, say so, show the matching photograph and ask what happens next. [Returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) takes the return step by step.

Keep the handover photographs, the video and the chat until the deposit has been settled. The deposit is typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions, and the [deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the amounts and the return.

## Common questions

### What if I notice a fault after I have left the shop?

Stop somewhere safe if it affects the brakes, the steering or a tyre, photograph the fault and message the shop straight away. Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, and no response time is published, so ask what the shop can do and how soon instead of assuming. Do not try to repair the bike yourself.

### Can I take a short test ride before I decide?

No test-ride policy is published, so ask. A test ride on a public road is riding like any other, so carry the licence that covers the bike. Use it to feel the brakes under load, the steering and the throttle response, and bring the bike back to the shop if anything is wrong.

### Do I repeat the checks when the bike is delivered?

Yes. Delivery in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement; if you arrange it, only the place changes, not the checks. Do the full walk-round wherever you take the bike, in good light, before you sign and while whoever hands it over is still there. [Step 5 of the rental guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) gives the order, and [shop pickup versus delivery]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-pickup-or-delivery-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) compares the two routes.

### Is the checklist different for a rental of several months?

The walk-round is the same, but the photographs matter more because they are the baseline for the whole term. The guide for [three months]({{ '/articles/three-month-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the terms of a long rental, and [the weekly check routine for a monthly rental]({{ '/articles/monthly-rental-inspection-schedule-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers keeping an eye on the bike afterwards.
