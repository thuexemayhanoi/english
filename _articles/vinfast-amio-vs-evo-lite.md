VinFast builds two different licence-free answers for riders who hold no driving licence: the pedal-assisted Amio and the speed-limited Evo Lite. Official data sets them apart clearly.

## Side by side

| | Amio | Evo Lite |
|---|---|---|
| Motor | 800 W in-hub | 2,450 W in-hub |
| Top speed | 30 km/h | below 50 km/h (limited) |
| Battery | 1.024 kWh LFP | two 1.5 kWh LFP packs |
| Range (standard conditions) | up to 65 km | up to 165 km (two batteries) |
| Format | pedal-assist | scooter, swap-ready |
| Licence | none | none (16+) |

## The trade in plain terms

The **Amio** buys simplicity and safety margins: light, slow, gentle, with pedal assistance as a get-home insurance. Its 30 km/h ceiling keeps a new rider out of traffic situations they cannot yet read.

The **Evo Lite** buys reach: nearly triple the standard-conditions range, scooter comfort and weather protection, and the full battery-swap network — the same 9,000 VND per battery per swap economics as the faster VinFast models — at speeds that handle every city road short of arterial flow.

## Who should pick which

- **Under 16, or brand new to two wheels:** the Amio. The pedal format is the training wheels of powered riding.
- **16+, commuting across districts, no licence:** the Evo Lite. The sub-50 km/h limit keeps it in the licence-free class while the swap network removes the charging problem.
- **Planning to get an A1 later?** Neither — the standard Evo or the A1-class machines will matter more.
