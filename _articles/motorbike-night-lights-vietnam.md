Short answer: from 18:00 to 06:00, and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather cuts visibility, a motorbike's lights must be on. Riding unlit in those conditions is a violation under Article 7(1)(g) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP at 200,000–400,000 VND. Tunnels have their own rule — dipped beams inside, at 600,000–800,000 VND — and a broken or missing headlight is an equipment defect at 400,000–600,000 VND before the light-use rule is even reached.

## The window the rule names

The decree defines the duty by clock and by weather: lights from 18:00 the previous evening to 06:00, or in fog, smoke, dust, rain or any weather that limits sight. Two things follow. First, the clock rule is absolute in both directions — a rider at 17:58 in good light has not violated anything, and a rider at 18:05 in bright city glow has. Second, the weather rule works outside the window: a grey Hanoi drizzle at four in the afternoon with visibility down is a lights-on condition under the same clause.

Hanoi's actual dusk lands between the two clock edges for most of the year, and the street-lighting argument does not apply: the rule is the rule, not a brightness test.

## High beam and tunnels

The same low band prices beam misuse: using high beam against oncoming traffic, against pedestrians crossing, or on lit-up stretches through residential areas is a violation in the 200,000–400,000 VND family. Inside road tunnels the requirement is explicit — ride on dipped beam — and failing that sits in the 600,000–800,000 VND band under Article 7(3)(h). A rider who enters a road tunnel on high beam is both dazzling the tunnel and violating the tunnel rule at the heavier price.

## When the light itself is the defect

The light-use rules assume a working light. A bike with no headlight at all, a dead one, or one below the design standard is an equipment violation under Article 14(1)(c) — 400,000–600,000 VND — and the bike should not be ridden at night at all. The bands stack by logic: an unlit rider at night with a dead headlight is one stop producing both findings, the defect and the failure to light.

## Why this is the fine riders should want enforced

An unlit motorbike at night is invisible until it is unavoidable — Vietnamese urban traffic is dense, unlit couriers ride the kerb lanes, and the closing geometry of a dark bike against oncoming headlights kills. For renters the check is one switch-flick at handover and one glance at dusk. For everyone else: lights on at the clock edge, dipped beam in traffic, and no excuses about battery drain that a five-minute charge routine does not solve.
