Riders booking Hanoi accommodation tend to optimise for location and forget the one thing that determines every evening of their stay: where the bike sleeps. Hanoi hotels handle motorbike parking very differently, and asking two questions before you book saves a week of nightly hassle.

## The three hotel parking models

**The garage or courtyard hotel.** Many hotels — especially mid-range ones in the wider districts — have an internal courtyard or ground-floor garage where the night staff watch the bikes. This is the best case: ride in, hand over the keys if asked, retrieve in the morning. Common in the districts, rare in the densest Old Quarter lanes.

**The partner-lot arrangement.** Hotels without space of their own usually have a deal with a nearby attended lot or a kerbside attendant: you park at the partner spot, the hotel tells you where, and the attendant watches the street all night. This is the standard Old Quarter arrangement and works well — the cost is typically modest and paid per night, and the security is better than it sounds, because the attendant is there specifically to watch.

**The informal kerb.** The worst case: "just park out front." On busy streets this means a crowded pavement rack where your bike is moved, nudged and occasionally nudged out of shape by the neighbouring bikes. Accept it only for a night or two, and never without a disc lock on the brake disc.

## Questions to ask before booking

1. "Do you have motorbike parking?" — then get specific: on-site, partner lot, or street?
2. "Is it watched overnight?" — a watched lot is the whole game.
3. "Is there a charge?" — nightly parking fees are normal and modest; surprise fees are annoying, so ask first.

Add the rider's basics regardless of the answer: use the steering lock plus a disc lock every night, and ask whether staff will need to move the bike — if the hotel shuffles bikes in a courtyard, note where yours ends up.

## Location trade-off

The Old Quarter puts you in the walking-distance middle of everything, with partner-lot parking. A hotel one district out — Dong Da, Hai Ba Trung, the Tay Ho lake shore — usually offers on-site parking, wider streets to ride out of, and a shorter daily negotiation. Guests riding daily often prefer the second: the riding is easier and the parking is solved.

## Long stays

For stays of a month or more, apartments with dedicated parking are the better instrument — the apartment-living guide covers that model, and the home-parking guide covers overnight security technique. But for a hotel week in Hanoi, two questions and a disc lock are the difference between a smooth trip and a nightly ritual of hoping.
