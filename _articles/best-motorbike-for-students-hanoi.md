Student budgets change the question from "best" to "cheapest legal". The answer splits on one line: do you have a licence? Without a motorcycle licence you choose between a pedal e-bike and the licence-free moped class. With a motorcycle licence, the Honda Click, Yamaha Mio, Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius carry the lowest published day rate, 150,000 VND, and the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade are published at 200,000 VND.

## Without a motorcycle licence

The legal options are the licence-free classes:

- [E-bikes]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-electric-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) (200,000 VND per day published): no licence required, rider 16 or older. Bicycle-like, slow, cheap to run - no petrol at all. The name covers two different machines, a pedal e-bike and an electric moped, with different rules, set out below.
- [50 cc motorbikes]({{ '/articles/renting-50cc-hanoi/' | relative_url }}): no driving licence required (16+), but there is no published fixed rental price - contact the shop directly for 50 cc rates and availability.

For campus-and-neighbourhood life, these cover everything. They do not keep up with Ring Road traffic, and that is fine. The moped class is designed for no more than 50 km/h, so the ceiling belongs to the class and not to one machine.

### What "licence-free" means in law

Under Article 59(1)(a) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), a person of 16 or older may ride a moped, and [the 50 cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the licence position and the rules for 16-to-18-year-olds.

### Telling a pedal e-bike from a moped

A pedal e-bike's assistance stops when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h (Article 34(2)(b)); a moped is limited to a 50 km/h design speed and, if electric, 4 kW (Article 34(1)(g)). [The electric bike licence guide]({{ '/articles/electric-bike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the licence classes for electric machines above the moped limit.

A label on a rental sheet does not settle which category a machine is in, so ask two questions. Can it be ridden on the throttle alone, with no pedalling? If so, it does not fit the pedal e-bike definition. What are its rated power and design speed? A scooter above 4 kW, or designed for more than 50 km/h, is not a moped and falls under those licence classes.

The helmet law applies on both sides of the line: it covers riders and passengers of mopeds and of motorised pedal bicycles, pedal e-bikes included ([the helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }})).

## With a licence

The Honda Click and Yamaha Mio class at [150,000 VND per day]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is the student value pick - the lowest day rate among the full motorbikes on the published list, level with the Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius, with the Vision at 200,000 VND as the next step up. On weekly and monthly rates the Click/Mio class drops to 600,000-700,000 VND per week and 1,000,000-1,200,000 VND per month - the monthly rate is what a student staying a semester should be looking at. Longer terms are negotiable; contact us to discuss longer-term rental.

### Which licence the published bikes need

An A1 licence goes to riders of 18 or older (Article 59(1)(b)) and covers a two-wheeled motorbike with a petrol engine up to and including 125 cc or an electric motor up to and including 11 kW; class A starts above those limits (Article 57(1)).

Honda and Yamaha publish engine sizes inside that limit for the bikes on the list: 110 cc for the Vision, 125 cc for the Air Blade 125, 109.2 cc for the Wave Alpha 110 and 110.3 cc for the Sirius RC 110. Each figure belongs to the version named. Honda also sells the Air Blade as a 160 with a 160 cc engine, which is above the 125 cc limit of a new A1, so ask which version you would be given. No engine figure is quoted here for the Click or the Mio, so ask for theirs as well. [The A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) explains what the class covers, including the rule that keeps A1 licences issued before 1 January 2025 valid for larger bikes.

This page chooses the bike. If you are a foreign student whose licence is not yet valid for it, [the foreign student licence guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-student-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the routes.

## Matching the bike to student life

Once the licence class is settled, four things separate the bikes on the list: weight, storage, gearbox and whether a second person rides. The figures below are what Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Vietnam publish on their model pages (retrieved 23 September 2026). Where no figure is quoted here, that is a gap in this guide, not a statement that the bike lacks the feature.

### Weight, for the parking row

At a campus gate or in a dormitory shed you may have to walk the bike backwards out of a tight row, and weight is what you feel then. Honda lists kerb weights of 96 kg for the Wave Alpha 110, 97 kg for the Vision and 110 kg for the Air Blade 125, and Yamaha lists 100 kg wet for the Sirius RC 110. A wet weight and a kerb weight are measured differently, so compare the Sirius loosely. Of the two scooters published at 200,000 VND, the Vision is 13 kg lighter than the Air Blade 125.

Seat height is quoted here only for the Wave Alpha 110, at 770 mm. For the others, sit on the bike and put both feet down before you commit.

### The under-seat bay and a laptop bag

Honda lists the Air Blade 125's under-seat bay at 23.2 L, worded as two half-helmets. No bay figure is quoted here for the Vision, Click, Mio, Wave Alpha 110 or Sirius RC 110, and a litre figure would not say whether a particular laptop bag fits anyway. Test the bag you really carry, with the helmet if that has to go under the seat too, and check that the seat closes without you pressing on it. [The commuting guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-commuting-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) works through storage and the other checks for a daily route.

### Automatic or foot-shift

The Wave Alpha 110 and Sirius RC 110 figures list a four-speed rotary gearbox, so those two are semi-automatics shifted with a foot; the Vision, Air Blade, Click and Mio are automatics. The Wave, Sirius, Click and Mio share one published day rate, so between those four the choice is about the gearbox and not the budget. A student who has never changed gear with a foot may find an automatic simpler in the first weeks of Hanoi traffic. [The scooter and semi-automatic comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-vs-semi-automatic-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets the trade-offs side by side.

### A second person on the bike

The law's default is the rider plus one passenger, with a short list of named exceptions ([the passenger rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-passenger-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }})), and everyone carried needs a helmet. Outside the exceptions, three friends on one rented bike is not legal, so it is no way to split the cost. No payload figure for the bikes on the list is quoted here: if you expect to ride two-up often, ask the shop which bike it would give you for that.

## The budget beyond the rate

- Fuel: it follows the distance you ride. Honda lists 1.72 L/100 km for the Wave Alpha 110 and Yamaha lists 2.08 L/100 km for the Sirius RC 110, manufacturer test figures that speed, load and traffic will move. No consumption figure is quoted here for the other bikes, and pump prices change, so work out your own cost.
- Parking: campus lots and attended street parking (gửi xe) can charge each time you park - ask what your campus or dormitory charges and budget for it.
- [Deposit]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}): typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND held for the period - real money for a student, and the reason handover photos matter.
- Helmets: ask whether a helmet comes with the bike you choose and check its fit at handover; [what to check on a rental helmet]({{ '/articles/do-hanoi-rentals-include-helmets/' | relative_url }}) lists the checks.
- Insurance: Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so renters arrange their own cover and the cost belongs in the budget.
- Papers at a stop: ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop.

The other lines of a term budget, such as consumables and late-return charges, are totalled in [the international students rental guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-international-students-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and [the semester rental guide]({{ '/articles/students-semester-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers matching a rental to the term.

### Weighing the 50,000 VND step

The step from 150,000 VND to 200,000 VND per day is 50,000 VND. The published figures show little for it: the Wave Alpha 110 and the Vision list 96 kg and 97 kg, which is no real difference, and the Air Blade 125's 23.2 L bay is the only storage figure quoted here. Decide what the extra 50,000 VND would buy you, such as a bay that fits your bag, and test that on the bike instead of assuming it.

A student staying a semester should compare monthly figures, so ask what each bike you are weighing costs by the month before you settle on the day rate alone. [The rental price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists what is published.

## What students should not do

Rent above the licence they hold, buy a cheap used bike for one semester (registration transfer plus resale loss eats the saving - [renting vs buying]({{ '/articles/renting-vs-buying-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) runs the numbers), or leave the bike unlocked around campus. An unlocked bike is the easiest one to take, so lock it every time and ask what the rental agreement says about theft before you ride away.

## Questions students ask

### Can a 16-year-old student rent a motorbike in Hanoi?

At 16 the law opens only the moped class: Article 59(1)(a) lets a person of 16 or older ride a moped, while the A1 licence needs 18 (Article 59(1)(b)). Whether a shop applies an age limit of its own is a separate question, so ask when you contact the shop. The 16-to-18 position is set out in [the licence rules for 50 cc riders under 18]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Does a Wave or Sirius need a different licence from a Vision?

No. The class follows engine size or electric power, not the gearbox. The Wave Alpha 110 and Sirius RC 110 figures (109.2 cc and 110.3 cc) sit inside the A1 limit just as the Vision's 110 cc does, so a licence that covers one covers the others, provided the version you are given stays at or below 125 cc.

### Can a flatmate and I share one rented bike?

Ask whether the rental agreement limits who may ride, and keep to it. A friend may ride only a class that their licence covers, whoever pays. Sharing a rented bike has its own section in [the guide to renting as an international student]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-international-students-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### What if I leave Hanoi before the rental ends?

There is no automatic refund entitlement for unused time, so ask at the start how an early return is handled. [The guide to renting for a student semester]({{ '/articles/students-semester-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers fitting a rental to your term dates.
