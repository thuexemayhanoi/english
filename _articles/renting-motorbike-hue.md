Hue rewards a motorbike more than almost any city in Vietnam. The imperial capital is spread out — citadel, royal tombs scattered along the Perfume River, pagodas, and the DMZ an hour north — and its calm, tree-lined streets are the gentlest introduction to Vietnamese riding anywhere in the country. Renting here is easy, cheap, and the riding is genuinely lovely.

## The rental scene

Hue's rental shops cluster in the Pham Ngu Lao/Le Loi tourist streets south of the Perfume River, and most guesthouses arrange bikes. Expect automatic scooters in the 100,000-150,000 VND per day band, with semi-automatics slightly cheaper; Hue's prices sit a touch under Hoi An and Da Nang because it sees fewer pure-beach tourists. Shops rent by the day, week or month, an
d the same national licence rules apply as everywhere in Vietnam: over 50cc needs a valid Vietnamese licence or a 1968-convention IDP. Our tourist-licence overview covers the detail.

## Rides worth renting for

**The tomb circuit.** Hue's imperial tombs — Khai Dinh, Tu Duc, Minh Mang — dot the hills and riverbanks south and west of the city along quiet, shaded roads. A half-day loop of three tombs on an automatic scooter is the classic Hue ride: light traffic, gentle bends, and food stalls in the villages between.

**Thanh Toan bridge and the countryside.** A short, flat run east through rice paddies to a famous 18th-century tiled bridge. Twenty minutes from the centre and you are entirely rural — the best easy "first ride in Vietnam" loop we know of.

**The Hai Van Pass (north).** The southern ascent of the pass starts essentially at Hue's doorstep: ride the QL1A coastal stretch 60-80 km south, cross the pass, and descend into Da Nang. The full guide is here; many travellers do it one-way and hand the bike back by arrangement, or loop back the same day.

**The DMZ (north).** A longer, more serious day ride along the old QL1 to Quang Tri: war sites, the Hien Luong bridge over the Ben Hai river, and Highway 1 traffic that demands real attention.

## Hue-specific practicalities

- Hue is the wettest big city in Vietnam in the autumn months; drizzle arrives on schedule. Ride with the low-visibility mindset when the mist comes off the Perfume River.
- Parking at the citadel and tombs uses the standard Vietnamese guarded lots (a few thousand dong per entry); our national parking guide explains how tickets work.
- The city itself is compact and calm; a scooter is also just the best way to move between the guesthouse streets, the citadel and Dong Ba market.
