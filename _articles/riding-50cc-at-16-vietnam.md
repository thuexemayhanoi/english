Sixteenth birthday, in Vietnam, unlocks exactly one motorised option: a moped of 50cc or less. Here is what the law says, precisely. The count is of the moped and motorbike classes; the motorised pedal bicycle, a simple vehicle in the Law's terms, has its own rules. The moped itself needs no licence but comes with an age condition, a duty of knowledge and skill, and the ordinary road duties.

## The age rule

Under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), a moped — a two- or three-wheel vehicle designed for no more than 50 km/h with an engine of 50cc or less, or an electric motor of 4 kW or less — requires no driving licence. Article 59 sets the minimum rider age at 16. Article 56(4) adds three conditions for the person riding a moped: knowledge of the traffic rules, skill in operating the vehicle, and the age and health limits that Article 59 sets.

Below 16, riding a moped or any other motorbike on public roads is not legal: Article 59(1) names no age under 16 for driving a motor vehicle, and Article 56(4) makes the age in Article 59(1)(a) a condition for the moped rider.

### What Article 56(4) says

The clause is short enough to read in the original. It asks three things of whoever drives a moped on the road: that they "hiểu biết" (grasp) the "quy tắc giao thông đường bộ" (road traffic rules), that they have "kỹ năng điều khiển phương tiện" (skill in operating the vehicle), and that they are "đủ điều kiện" (meet the conditions) "về độ tuổi, sức khỏe" (on age and health), which the clause traces to Article 59, clause 1 point (a) and clause 2.

In one English sentence, as this page's own rendering and not an official translation: a moped rider has to be familiar with the rules of the road, be able to handle the machine, and qualify on age and health. The clause puts no adjective such as "basic" in front of the skill.

The clause names no test, course, certificate or card as proof of the knowledge or the skill. Article 56(1) expressly excepts moped riders from the licence requirement, while the age and health condition still binds every driver, and Article 59(2) requires health appropriate to each vehicle, leaving it to the Minister of Health to set the health standards and the medical examination; neither clause mentions a medical certificate.

### The sixteenth birthday and what sits below it

Under Article 59(1)(a) the age for driving a moped (xe gắn máy) is "đủ 16 tuổi trở lên", which means 16 full years or more. The other minimum ages in Article 59(1) are 18, 21, 24 and 27, for licence classes and other qualifications. What follows for a rider below 16 is a matter for the penalty decree, not for the Law, and [the guide to underage riding penalties]({{ '/articles/underage-riding-penalties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) deals with it.

The motorised pedal bicycle (xe đạp máy), the electric bicycle included, is a different matter. Article 34(1)(g) leaves it out of the moped definition and Article 34(2)(b) counts it with the simple vehicles (xe thô sơ). Article 59(1) sets no minimum age for it, so the 16 on this page is not its rule, while Article 31(3) still requires a helmet. [The guide to motorised pedal bicycles]({{ '/articles/xe-dap-mai-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes that category on its own terms.

## What 16 does not unlock

A 110cc Wave and a 125cc scooter are motorcycles in law, because each sits above the moped limits (Article 34(1)(e) and (g)), so each needs a licence; the lowest class, A1, opens at 18 (Articles 57(1)(a) and 59(1)(b)). Class A, for two-wheelers above 125 cm3 or an electric motor above 11 kW, also opens at 18 (Articles 57(1)(b) and 59(1)(b)).

The boundary is enforced: Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as issued set a fine band in Article 18(4) for 16- and 17-year-olds who ride a motorbike above the moped class. Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP has changed Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP since 15 August 2026; no amount is quoted here, and any figure on a linked page, the one on underage penalties included, follows the 2024 text and needs checking against the current decree.

### Which machines are open at 16 or 17

For a 16- or 17-year-old, machine by machine:

| Machine | How the Law classes it | Open at 16 or 17? |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol: engine up to 50 cm3, design speed up to 50 km/h | Moped (Article 34(1)(g)) | Yes, with no licence (Articles 56(1) and 59(1)(a)) |
| Electric: motor up to 4 kW, design speed up to 50 km/h | Moped (same clause) | Yes, on the same terms |
| Two wheels, engine over 50 and up to 125 cm3, or electric motor over 4 kW and up to 11 kW | Class A1 (Article 57(1)(a)) | No: A1 opens at 18 |
| Two-wheeler over 125 cm3 or 11 kW; any three-wheeled motorcycle | Class A or B1 (Article 57(1)(b) and (c)) | No: both open at 18 |
| Motorised pedal bicycle | Simple vehicle (Article 34(2)(b)) | Article 59(1) sets no age |

### The label does not decide it

A listing that says "50cc" settles nothing. Article 34(1)(g) applies two tests together, one on design speed and one on engine capacity or motor power, and says nothing about gearbox, clutch or body shape. A two-wheeler that fails either test falls into the motorcycle class of Article 34(1)(e), and a 16- or 17-year-old cannot ride it. Ask for the engine capacity or motor power and the design speed as the maker states them; [what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) works through each limit of the definition.

## The school training question

Decree 151/2024/NĐ-CP organises road-safety knowledge and safe-moped-riding skills training for high-school and vocational students. What it does not do is create a universal certificate requirement: traffic police have publicly clarified that 16-to-18-year-olds may ride mopeds, including electric mopeds up to 4 kW, without a licence or certificate, with the training operating through schools. [Our training article]({{ '/articles/50cc-training-high-school-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains what the programme actually teaches.

### What the Law says about school guidance

Article 6(2) of the Law covers the same ground. It makes the traffic police responsible for leading, together with the agencies running upper-secondary schools and vocational institutions, the arranging of guidance on safe moped riding for students at those institutions.

That provision describes what public bodies arrange, and it is not a condition of riding: the conditions are in Article 56(4), which names no course. Article 60 covers the training of people who want a driving licence, and no licence class opens at 16, so it is not the route to riding a moped either. This page relies on the Government portal's report of the decree, listed in the sources, and not on the decree text, so ask the school or the traffic police what the current programme covers.

## The duties that come with the key

At 16 the rider carries the same obligations as any adult: a fastened, standard-compliant helmet; no alcohol; lights, lanes, and signals obeyed; the moped kept roadworthy. Insurance is compulsory for the vehicle. Parents handing over a bike are handing over legal responsibilities, not just keys — [our parents' guide]({{ '/articles/buying-50cc-for-your-teenager-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the decision honestly.

### Helmet, passengers and the rider's conduct

Article 33 is the Law's clause for riders of two- and three-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds, and it has no separate wording for young riders.

- **Helmet.** Article 33(2) obliges both rider and passenger to wear a helmet built to the national technical regulation, chin strap properly done up. [The helmet rules for 50cc mopeds]({{ '/articles/helmet-rules-50cc-mopeds/' | relative_url }}) go through the duty.
- **Passengers.** A rider carries one passenger; two are allowed only in four listed cases, one of which is a child under 12 (Article 33(1)). [Carrying a passenger on a 50cc]({{ '/articles/carrying-pillion-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the practice.
- **Conduct.** Article 33(3) bars riding abreast, using an umbrella or an audio device other than a hearing aid, riding with both hands off the handlebars or on one wheel, towing or pushing another vehicle, sitting sideways, standing on the bike, changing riders while moving, turning round to steer and riding blindfolded.

Article 9 lists acts barred for every driver: driving with any alcohol concentration in the blood or breath (the text states no permitted level), racing, weaving through traffic, revving continuously, and holding and using a phone or another electronic device on the move (clauses 2, 5 and 6).

### Lights and expressways

Article 20(1) requires the front lights on from 18:00 until 06:00 the next day, and in rain, fog, smoke, dust or other bad weather that restricts visibility. Article 25(3) lists motorbikes and mopeds among the vehicles barred from expressways.

### Papers, inspection and insurance

Article 56(1) names four papers that must be on the driver: the vehicle registration certificate, a licence matching the vehicle, the inspection certificate on technical safety and environmental protection for motor vehicles, and the owner's certificate of compulsory civil-liability insurance (points (a) to (d)). The exemption for moped riders is attached to the licence requirement in the opening sentence of that clause; the clause does not separately release a moped rider from the other papers it lists.

For mopeds, Article 42(2) limits inspection to exhaust emissions, and Article 9(8) bars putting a motor vehicle on the road when it does not meet the legal rules on technical safety and environmental protection. Motorbike insurance is outside what Nguyen Tu offers; customers arrange cover themselves. [Papers to carry on a motorbike]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists each one and how it may be shown.

### Handing over the keys

Article 9(7) prohibits giving a motor vehicle to anyone who does not meet the legal conditions for driving it on the road. For a 16- or 17-year-old those conditions can be met on a moped (Articles 56(4) and 59(1)(a)) but not on a 110cc scooter. Imagine a 17-year-old who asks to borrow a parent's scooter of that size for a short trip: since the first licence class opens at 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), a parent who agrees would be handing the keys to someone who lacks the conditions Article 9(7) speaks of.

The penalty for a wrongful handover is set by decree; [lending a motorbike to a rider who does not qualify]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers it, with a figure that follows the 2024 text and needs checking against the current decree.

## Practical advice (not legal advice)

Start with supervised rides on quiet streets before joining rush hour; wear gear, not just a helmet; and pick a bike the rider can put both feet down on. [The licence article]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the canonical statement of the rules on this hub.

### Learning the rules the clause asks for

Under Article 56(4) a moped rider has to know the rules, and the Law sets out its road traffic rules in Chapter II, Articles 10 to 33. Two are worth reading with a new rider. Article 11(2) sets the order in which road signals prevail: the traffic controller's instructions first, then traffic lights, road signs and road markings. Article 22(1) makes a vehicle entering from a non-priority or branch road give way to vehicles on the priority or main road from any direction. Reading them with a parent beforehand beats learning them in traffic.

## Questions riders and parents ask

### Can a 15-year-old ride a 50cc moped?

No. Article 59(1)(a) sets 16 for driving a moped, and Article 59(1) names no younger age for any motor vehicle.

### Does a 16-year-old need a licence, a test or a school certificate?

Not for a moped. Article 56(1) carves moped riders out of the licence rule, and Article 56(4) names no test or certificate. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, which governs licence tests, the issue and exchange of licences and the issue of international driving permits (Article 1(1)), does not mention mopeds at all, and the school guidance of Article 6(2) is offered where students study, not required before riding.

### Does anything change on the 18th birthday?

For a moped, no: the exception from the licence requirement does not depend on age, and the conditions of Article 56(4) carry on. What the birthday opens is the licence classes: Article 59(1)(b) opens classes A1, A, B1, B and C1 to people of 18 or over, and [the route to an A1 licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has its own guide.

### Did the 2026 amendment change any of this?

Law 118/2025/QH15 amended Law 36/2024/QH15 with provisions taking effect on 1 July 2026, and the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH flags each amended clause with a note. None of the clauses relied on here carries one, Articles 33, 34(1)(g), 56 and 59 among them; Article 60 has notes at clauses 3(e) and 7 to 9, but this page uses only its clauses 1 and 2, which carry none.
