Den Soc, the temple complex dedicated to the boy god Thanh Giong, sits at the foot of the Soc mountains in Soc Son district — roughly 30–40 kilometres north of central Hanoi, about an hour’s ride. It is one of the most atmospheric of the short northern rides: a pilgrimage site wrapped in a legend every Vietnamese schoolchild knows.

## The route

Ride north out of the city on the Soc Son corridor past the airport-side districts; the temple complex is well signed as you approach the mountains. The road is flat, wide and fast for most of the way — an easy ride for any bike, and often combined with Co Loa citadel or a Dai Lai lake loop for a full northern day.

## The temple and the legend

The Soc complex — upper, middle and lower temples among old trees — marks where, in the legend, Thanh Giong halted after defeating the invaders, removed his armour and flew to heaven from the mountainside. A large bronze statue of the hero, erected for Hanoi’s millennial celebrations in 2010, stands near the summit above the temple. It is a steep walk up, or a ride up a side road, and the platform gives wide views over the northern delta. The festival in the days after lunar new year transforms the site; outside festival season it is a calm half-day’s visit, with modest dress expected at the temples.

## Practical notes

- Weekend mornings are busy with pilgrims; weekday visits are quiet.
- The temple car parks are attended — a good base for walking between the three temples.
- Add the surrounding Soc Son lakes if you have the afternoon free.
