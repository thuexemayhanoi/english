Nha Trang is Vietnam's original beach resort: six kilometres of sand, an island-studded bay, and a long, straight, palm-lined coastal boulevard that is one of the most pleasant city rides in the country. Renting a motorbike here is cheap and useful — but Nha Trang is also where a lot of tourists ride badly (sand, rain, no licence), so this guide focuses on doing it right.

## Rental basics

Shops line the tourist streets behind the beach — Tran Phu's side streets and the backpacker blocks north of the centre — and nearly every hotel can arrange a bike. Published guides put average daily rental around 150,000 VND for the standard automatic scooter class, with discounts for multi-day and weekly hire. Deposit norms are cash or, less wisely, a passport; pay cash.

The national licence rule applies as everywhere: anything over 50cc requires a valid Vietnamese licence or a 1968-convention IDP endorsed for motorbikes. Tourist police presence along the beach strip is real, and riding without papers on the main boulevard is how visitors lose an afternoon and a fine. See our legal overview.

## Riding the city

The Tran Phu beach boulevard is the pleasure ride: flat, wide, slow. The city behind it is a normal dense Vietnamese downtown — our nationwide riding-culture primer applies. Two local specifics: sand drifts across beach-side corners after wind and storms (treat every shaded corner as if it were wet), and the evening boulevard cruise is dense with families who will not be looking for you.

## The rides worth the rental

**North along the coast.** The run north past Hon Chong and out toward Doc Let's white sand is the region's classic half-day: fishing villages, salt fields, and long straight coastal road with the sea in view. Under 100 km return from the centre.

**South over the hills.** The coast road south past Cam Ranh requires care — fast highway sections with heavy traffic — but delivers some of the country's loveliest bays. This is a real ride: take water, fuel up, and avoid it in rain.

**Inland waterfalls.** Ba Ho (three waterfalls) is a well-known day ride north of the city on a mix of highway and narrow lanes. The last stretch is rough — expect dirt, not tarmac — and lock the bike at the trailhead lot.

## Season warning

Central-coast rain and storms peak in the autumn months, and Nha Trang's beach riding goes from easy to miserable quickly. Our nationwide monsoon guide shows the window; the coast south of Da Nang through Nha Trang is best in the dry half-year, roughly spring to late summer, when the coastal road is at its clearest.

## Fitting Nha Trang into a bigger trip

Nha Trang sits mid-way on the classic Hanoi-to-Saigon ride and is a natural overnight stop for north-south riders. Travellers doing the whole country by segments usually arrive by train (the station is central) and rent locally for two or three days.
