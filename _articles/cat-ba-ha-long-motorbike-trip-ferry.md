Cat Ba is the only sea-island motorbike trip you can do in a weekend from Hanoi: ride about 135 to 150 kilometres east to the coast, put the bike on a ferry, and spend a day or two on forest roads and coastal bends in Lan Ha Bay country. The trip's one non-negotiable piece of planning is the ferry — motorbikes reach Cat Ba only by boat, schedules vary by season, and the crossing times set the shape of each day.

## The ride to the coast

Two main road options exist, and both matter for riders. The first is the classic National Highway 5 (QL5) through Hai Phong territory to the Tan Vu-Lach Huyen sea-crossing bridge, which lands you on Cat Hai island next to the Dong Bai ferry port — about 135 km and three and a half hours from central Hanoi by most published estimates. The second continues on QL5 and then QL18 around to Ha Long city and the Tuan Chau ferry terminal, about 150 km, which pairs the island with a look at Ha Long Bay from the mainland.

One rule is absolute: the Hanoi-Hai Phong and Ha Long expressways are closed to motorbikes, so QL5 and QL18 are the legal routes. Our motorbikes-on-expressways explainer covers the rule and the fines; the ferry-and-train transport guide covers everything else about moving a bike around Vietnam by boat.

## The ferry crossing

From Dong Bai (near Cat Hai), ferries run to Cai Vieng on Cat Ba island in roughly 30 minutes, followed by about half an hour of riding to Cat Ba town. From Tuan Chau near Ha Long, boats run to Gia Luan on the island's north side. Published rider accounts put a motorbike ticket at roughly 80,000 VND including the driver, with a pillion paying about 60,000 VND more — treat these as indicative and check current prices and timetables at the terminal, because sailings change with season and weather. Photograph the return schedule board when you arrive; missing the last ferry back means an unplanned island night.

Salt air is hard on a bike: after the trip, rinse the machine (especially the chain and any exposed metal) and re-lube the chain. If you rent, tell the shop where you are going so they can prep and check the bike over on return.

## On the island

Cat Ba is bigger than most visitors expect — a national park covering most of the interior, with a crossing road over the hills between the two ferry ports. The island ride is short but genuinely fun: forested climbs, a viewpoint over Lan Ha Bay, and the descent to the town's waterfront. Park in paid lots near the town beach; our Hanoi parking habits guide applies anywhere — helmet with you, documents with you, never leave them with the bike.

## A workable plan

- Day 1: leave by 8 a.m., QL5 to the bridge, ferry mid-day, ride the island crossing road, sleep in Cat Ba town. About four hours of riding plus the crossing.
- Day 2: morning loop of the island or a boat trip into Lan Ha Bay (park the bike securely), then an afternoon ferry and an evening ride home — or break the return with a night in Ha Long city and make it three days.
- Budget with our trip-budget guide: fuel for roughly 300 km round trip, two ferry crossings, island parking and lodging.
