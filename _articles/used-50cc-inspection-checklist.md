A used 50cc is cheap to buy and cheap to fix — but only if you know which faults are cheap and which are the price of the bike all over again. Here is the order that finds the expensive ones first. Bring a friend, ride to the seller, and allow twenty minutes.

## One: papers before passion

If the paperwork is wrong, nothing else matters. Check the registration matches the frame and engine numbers, that the seller is the named owner (or can explain the chain sensibly), and that the bike is not sold as "papers coming later". Our used-sport-underbone guide covers the paperwork patterns that recur in Vietnam's informal market.

## Two: engine health

- **Cold start.** Arrive early and ask the seller not to start it. A cold start tells you more than any warm demonstration.
- **Smoke on start-up or revving**: blue smoke means oil is burning — a worn bore or seals on a small engine can approach the bike's value to fix.
- **Knocks and rattles** at idle that change with revs — walk away from bottom-end noises. Our diagnosing-noises article covers what each sound tends to mean.
- **Oil condition**: milky oil means water intrusion; glittery oil means metal is moving where it should not be.

## Three: electrics

Lights, horn, brake light, indicators, and a charging check: engine running, headlight on, rev gently — the light should brighten slightly, not dim. Battery and charging faults on cheap 50s are common and our battery guide explains the system.

## Four: brakes, wheels, tyres

- Both brakes stop the bike straight and without pulsing or grabbing
- Wheel bearings: with the bike on its stand, grab each wheel and rock it side to side — any clunk is a bearing or a worse problem
- Tyre age and sidewall cracking — small wheels suffer more from aged rubber; our tyre guide covers what to look at

## Five: frame, forks, straightness

Park it, stand in front, sight down the forks: bent fork legs or a twisted frame from an old crash reappear as handling ghosts forever. Turn bar lock to lock and feel for notchy steering-head bearings.

## Six: the ride

Your money is still in your pocket — spend thirty minutes of it on the ride: starts cleanly from hot, pulls through the rev range without stutter, brakes straight, and holds a line with light hands on the bars.

## Red lines

Smoke + paperwork problems + frame damage: any two of the three, walk. The market has another bike tomorrow.
