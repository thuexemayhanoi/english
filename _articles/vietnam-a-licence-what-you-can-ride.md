Class A is the ceiling of the Vietnamese motorbike licence system. Under Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), A covers two-wheeled motorbikes above 125 cc or above 11 kW — every size, every power, one class. Where [A1]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) stops at the 125 cc / 11 kW line, A covers everything past it, from a 150 cc commuter to a litre-class tourer.

## What Article 57(1)(b) gives class A

The Law is read here in its consolidated text, 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes the amendments of Law 118/2025/QH15, and the English wording is our translation of the Vietnamese. Under Article 57(1)(b), class A goes to whoever drives a two-wheeled motorbike whose cylinder capacity is above 125 cm³ or whose electric motor power is above 11 kW, and the clause adds every kind of vehicle that class A1 covers, so a class A holder is not confined to the big machines. The lower side of the same line is Article 57(1)(a): A1 stops at a capacity of 125 cm³ or a motor power of 11 kW.

### Where the line sits

Both figures are inclusive on the A1 side, so a machine rated at exactly 125 cm³ belongs to A1 and one rated above it belongs to A. The line is drawn on the machine, so the rating the manufacturer publishes for the exact variant is the figure to use, and a number inside a model name proves nothing. The texts read for this page do not say which document fixes the capacity, so ask at handover which capacity the bike's own papers state. The table sets five two-wheelers, taken from the manufacturers' own Vietnamese model pages, against the line.

An A1 issued before 2025 and not yet exchanged also reaches these five (Article 89(2)(a)), and [the old licence classes guide]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) owns the transitional table.

### Electric machines

For an electric two-wheeler the line is motor power rather than top speed: above 11 kW needs class A, and up to 11 kW sits within A1. Speed matters at the bottom of the scale: Article 34(1)(g) sets the moped limits at a design speed of 50 km/h and, for an electric motor, 4 kW, each as a maximum, and a moped needs no licence class (Article 56(1) and (4)). [The guide to licences for fast electric motorbikes]({{ '/articles/fast-electric-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers fast electric machines and the class each one needs.

## Who can hold it and how

The minimum age is 18, the same as A1. Training follows the same structure — licensed centre, fixed minimum hours (class A: 32 hours including 20 theory under Circular 14/2025), then the test. Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD amended Circular 14/2025/TT-BXD from 1 July 2026 and keeps class A at 20 theory and 12 practical hours, and Law Article 60(2) also allows other forms of training, so a licensed course is not the only way to the test. Article 4(4)(a) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA puts classes A1 and A under the same Appendix I, which gives one set of figures for the computer paper, and [the A1 licence test guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) sets out the paper, the yard tests and which version applies on your sitting date. Article 41(2) applies that content from 1 March 2027, and until the end of 28 February 2027 parts of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA stay in force (Article 41(6)). The licence is issued with no expiry date, and it carries the same 12 points as every other class.

Article 57(5)(a) is the source of the no-expiry rule, and it has limits. A Vietnamese licence issued to a foreigner against a foreign one takes its term from one of the qualifying documents of Article 24(1)(a) and from the foreign licence (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a)). A card with no expiry date can still be revoked: Article 57(7)(b) treats a licence revoked under Article 62(5) as one without force, and Article 62(5)(a) names a holder who does not meet the health conditions for the class on the conclusion of a medical facility. The 12 points come from Article 58(1) and move with the card when it is exchanged, reissued or upgraded (Article 58(4)). [The licence validity guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers how long each card lasts and when it is revoked.

### Health, training and the age check

Article 59(1)(b) is the source of the age of 18, and Article 15(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA counts it up to the day of the sitting, so the test date is the date that decides the age condition. Article 59(2) asks for a health condition suited to the type of vehicle, with the Minister of Health setting the standards, and the test dossier carries a valid driver's health certificate (Article 16(2)(b)).

On training, Article 60(1) of the Law requires theory and practical instruction under the programme for the class. Article 60(2) names training establishments and also other forms of driver training, and Article 60(8) leaves the programme to the Minister of Construction, so the course hours are set in a Ministry circular, not in the Law. Article 15(2) of the Circular asks the candidate for confirmation that the course is complete. [The route guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the steps in order and gives the course hours now in force for A1 and A.

### Four ways to hold a class A card

Each of these rests on its own clause. The car-licence entry is a shortcut inside the first route, not a separate way to hold the card.

- **A passed test.** Article 62(1) of the Law issues a licence to a candidate who passes the test. Article 16(2) of the Circular lists the dossier, and Article 16(1) has the training establishment submit it.

- **A car licence in force.** A candidate for A1 or A who holds one is excused the theory paper (Article 4(1)(c)), but Article 4(2)(a) still has the candidate ride the practical tests, and the car classes are a separate branch of Article 57(1), so a car licence does not cover a motorbike. The Circular does not say whether a car licence issued abroad counts, and [the guide to riding a 125 cc on a car licence]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the rest.

- **An old licence, exchanged.** Article 89(3)(b) turns an old A2 into class A, while an old A1 comes back as class A carrying a restriction (Article 89(3)(a)): machines below 175 cc or 14 kW only. The restricted card is not the unrestricted class A that this page describes.

- **A foreign licence, exchanged.** Article 18(7) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA requires a foreign national licence holder who needs to drive here to exchange it for the matching Vietnamese licence, unless a treaty Vietnam has joined provides otherwise.

## What A does not give you

Three boundaries matter. It does not cover three-wheelers — that is class B1. It does not put you on an expressway — motorbikes of every class are barred from expressways regardless of engine size. And it does not override the alcohol rules — the zero-tolerance bands apply to every rider of every class.

### Three-wheelers

Article 57(1)(c) issues class B1 for three-wheeled motorbikes together with the vehicles that A1 covers, while Article 57(1)(b) words class A for two-wheeled motorbikes. Article 57(2) has a separate rule on the A1 card for a rider with a disability whose three-wheeled motorbike is built for people with disabilities.

### The expressway ban

Article 25(3) of the Law lists motorbikes, mopeds and similar vehicles among those that may not use an expressway, and the clause names no licence class and no engine size. Its carve-out is for the people, vehicles and equipment that manage and maintain the expressway. [The expressway guide]({{ '/articles/motorbikes-on-expressway-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the rule from the rider's side.

### Alcohol

Article 9(2) of the Law prohibits driving a vehicle in road traffic with alcohol in the blood or breath, and it draws no line between licence classes. [The drink-driving guide]({{ '/articles/drink-driving-limits-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the penalty bands; its figures follow Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as issued, and Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended the decree from 15 August 2026, so check the current amounts.

### The papers that go with the card

Article 56(1) asks a driver for a licence with points remaining, in force and suited to the type of vehicle being driven, and lists that licence among the papers to carry (Article 56(1)(b)). Beside it sit the registration certificate (56(1)(a)), the technical-safety and environmental inspection certificate, as the law prescribes (56(1)(c)), and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate (56(1)(d)). A paper integrated into the electronic identification account can be shown through that account (Article 56(3)). A rental adds a question the Law does not answer: at handover, ask how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be produced if you are stopped.

## The fine line for riding without it

[An unlicensed rider]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) on a machine above 125 cc is fined under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP as issued at a higher band than on a smaller machine, because the decree prices the violation by the machine being ridden. Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended that decree from 15 August 2026, and the figures in the linked guide follow the 2024 text, so check the current band before relying on any amount.

An A1 holder who borrows a friend's 300 cc tourer is riding a machine that A1 does not cover: Article 57(1)(a) stops at 125 cm³ or 11 kW, and a 300 cm³ engine is class A territory under Article 57(1)(b). An A1 issued before 2025 and not exchanged reaches only machines below 175 cc (Article 89(2)(a)), so it does not cover that tourer either.

### The friend who lends the keys

Article 9(7) lists among the strictly prohibited acts the handing of a motor vehicle to a person who does not meet the legal conditions to drive it in road traffic. The lender therefore has a prohibition of their own, separate from the rider's. [The lending guide]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the owner's side.

## Foreign licences and class A

The Law reads a foreign licence by the type of vehicle it permits, not by Vietnamese class letters. Article 57(6)(b) makes valid in Vietnam an international permit alongside the national licence issued by a state party to the 1968 Convention, each suited to the type of vehicle the holder may drive, and Article 57(8)(b) has the holder carry both, in term. Article 57(6)(c) and (d) add the treaty routes. None of these clauses turns a foreign category into class A; they ask whether the document is suited to the vehicle.

Exchange is the other route. Article 18(7) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sends a foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam on a national licence to the exchange for the matching Vietnamese licence, unless a driving-licence treaty that Vietnam has joined says otherwise, and the 1968 Convention pair of Article 57(6)(b) is that exception. Article 24(5)(b) exchanges a licence that covers several classes into the corresponding classes. Neither the Law nor the Circular lists which foreign categories correspond to class A. An office that will not exchange a licence must say so in writing and give the reason (Article 24(3)(c)), and for a question the texts leave open, such as how a foreign big-bike category is read, the cautious course is to ask the traffic police for an answer in writing.

## Practical Hanoi context

Settle the licence before the model. A Vietnamese licence for a bike above the A1 line has to be class A, or an older card whose written scope reaches it, and a foreign licence goes through the clauses of Article 57(6) or the exchange.

A model name can leave the class open: Honda Vietnam lists the Air Blade as separate 125 and 160 models, and only the 160 is above the line. A published daily rate for the Honda Air Blade does not say which one it means, so ask which Air Blade is on offer and what engine capacity it is rated at. Customers arrange their own motorbike insurance, because Nguyen Tu does not provide it, and a policy's licence wording is worth reading before a big bike, not after.

## Quick answers

### Does class A need an A1 first?

Article 15 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sets the conditions for a test candidate: age on the day of the sitting, health and a completed course, and Article 16(2) lists the dossier. Holding an A1 first is in neither, and Article 60(3) of the Law, which lists the class upgrades trained as upgrades, names car and trailer classes only. Neither text says how a training centre treats an A1 holder who enrols for class A, so ask the centre before paying.

### Is there still a 175 cc tier?

Not for a licence issued under the current Law: Article 57(1) draws one line between A1 and A, at 125 cm³ or 11 kW. The 175 cc figure survives in Article 89, which keeps the scope of licences issued before 1 January 2025 (Article 88(1)) until they are exchanged, and it appears again on the exchanged card of an old A1, which reads class A with a limit below 175 cc or 14 kW (Article 89(3)(a)).

### Does holding class A mean any bike is on offer?

No. Class A answers the licence question only, and whether a bike is on offer is a separate one: contact us to confirm current availability of any model. The deposit is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions, and [the rental requirements guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the papers and the deposit at handover.
