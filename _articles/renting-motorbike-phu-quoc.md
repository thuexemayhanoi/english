Phu Quoc is Vietnam's big island playground: a 570 km² slice of granite and jungle in the Gulf of Thailand, ringed by beaches and increasingly stitched together by sealed roads. Nearly every visitor rents a scooter here — the island is too spread out for walking, too small for cars to feel necessary, and the rental culture is the most casual in the country. Casual is fine; careless is not, because Phu Quoc's back roads remain genuinely rough.

## Rental basics

Rental is arranged through guesthouses, beach resorts, and shops around Duong Dong town and the main tourist beaches; semi-automatics and automatics both circulate. Prices are island prices — typically at or above mainland-city rates for the scooter class, with the usual weekly discounts. Helmets are legally required and should be included; the island's sandy, sometimes gravelly surfaces make a decent helmet more than box-ticking.

Licence rules are national and unchanged: over 50cc requires a valid Vietnamese licence or a 1968-convention IDP for motorbikes. Island police checkpoints are not unknown, and hospital options on the island are limited — the national overview of what tourists can legally ride matters here more than anywhere, because an accident on a rough island road without valid papers is the bad version of this trip.

## What the island demands

Island guides are blunt about Phu Quoc's roads: the main arteries are sealed and decent, but significant secondary roads — some still famously rough — mix potholes, sand, laterite and mud after rain. The southern hills around Ho Quoc pagoda include proper slopes that a laden 110cc takes slowly, and the northern forests hide single-lane concrete ribbons where two bikes barely pass.

Rules of thumb:

- Day one: stick to the sealed main roads (Duong Dong, Long Beach, Ong Lang, Sao beach).
- Dry season only for the deep north and forest tracks.
- Never ride the beach itself on a rental — salt and sand kill the drive chain, and the rental shop keeps your deposit for exactly this reason.

## The rides worth doing

- **South island loop:** Duong Dong to An Thoi along the coastal road — pepper farms, the pagoda slopes, and the Sunset Town area, with the cable-car station as a turnaround.
- **North island:** the sealed run up through the national-park fringe to Rach Vem and the "kissing" fish restaurants on the northern coast — the island's best swimming stops.
- **Cape Ganh Dau / Vinpearl area** in the north-west for sunset, with roadside squid-drying racks for photos instead of doorways.

## Getting there with a bike

You cannot ride to Phu Quoc — travellers fly in (the island has a large airport) or take the ferry from Ha Tien or Rach Gia; cars and motorbikes can travel on the ferries, but almost nobody hauls a rental bike over.
