The A1 licence is the workhorse motorcycle licence in Vietnam, and the class almost every renter needs to think about. Its scope is set by Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15).

Two things decide whether a bike is covered: the engine capacity or motor power of the bike itself, and whether the licence was issued before or after 1 January 2025. An A1 first issued from that date stops at 125cc and 11 kW, while an older A1 keeps a wider scope, up to but not including 175cc and 14 kW.

## What a new A1 licence covers

- Petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125cc
- Electric two-wheelers up to and including 11 kW
- Minimum age: 18 (Article 59)

That covers the 110cc-class scooters and underbones with manufacturer figures in the table further down: the Honda Vision, Honda Wave Alpha 110 and Yamaha Sirius RC 110 all sit inside A1, and so does the Honda Air Blade 125, because the law's wording is "up to 125cc", not "below 125cc". The Honda Click and Yamaha Mio, also named on Nguyen Tu's price list, have no manufacturer figure published here, so their class is a question for the papers of the bike you are offered.

### How the law words the A1 line

Article 57(1)(a) issues class A1 to a rider of a two-wheeled motorbike with a cylinder capacity up to 125 cm³, or an electric motor power up to 11 kW. This and the later quotations are translations; the Vietnamese text calls the class hạng A1. Three details matter at a rental counter.

- **Power is inclusive too.** Exactly 11 kW is also inside A1.
- **The law measures the machine, not its name.** Honda lists its SH Mode 125 at 124.8cc and Yamaha lists its Gear 125 Hybrid at 124.9cc, so a "125" in a name is a label rather than a measurement.
- **Petrol and electric are measured differently.** The law gives a combustion engine a capacity limit and an electric motor a power limit.

### Age and term

Article 59(1)(b) sets the minimum age for an A1 licence at 18, the same age it sets for A, B1, B and C1. Article 59(1)(a) separately lets a person aged 16 or over ride a moped, the category below A1, and the law lists no licence class for mopeds.

A Vietnamese A1, A or B1 licence has no time limit (Article 57(5)(a)); the exception is a licence issued to a foreigner in exchange for a foreign one, covered below. [The step-by-step guide to getting an A1]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the course and the tests behind the card.

## The pre-2025 transitional rule

Article 89 of the law preserves the scope of A1 licences issued before 1 January 2025: petrol bikes from 50cc up to (but not including) 175cc, or electric machines from 4 kW up to (but not including) 14 kW. No retest is required. This is why some Vietnamese riders with old A1 licences legally ride 150cc bikes.

The date is the law's own start date, 1 January 2025 (Article 88(1)). The wider scope belongs to a licence that has not yet been exchanged or re-issued under the new law (Article 89(2)), and Article 89(1) keeps that licence usable for the term written on it. An A1 first issued on or after 1 January 2025 follows Article 57 from the start, with the 125cc and 11 kW ceilings; a card that replaces an older A1 follows Article 89(3)(a), below.

If the holder wants the card exchanged or re-issued, Article 89(3)(a) turns the old A1 into class A with a restriction: two-wheeled motorbikes below 175cc, or electric motors below 14 kW, and nothing larger. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA fixes the printed wording (Appendix IX, item 4(g)), and its Article 21 lists the exchange cases, among them a damaged card and a holder who simply wishes to exchange. The dossier in Article 21(2) names a health certificate and, for foreigners, a valid passport; it names no test.

A card that reads A with this restriction is not an unrestricted class A, so the limit in its wording is the limit that counts, not the letter. [The old licence classes guide]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows the other pre-2025 classes through the same rule.

### What each licence covers

| Licence in hand | Petrol two-wheeler | Electric two-wheeler | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 issued from 1 January 2025 | up to and including 125cc | up to and including 11 kW | Article 57(1)(a) |
| A1 issued earlier, not exchanged | from 50cc to below 175cc | from 4 kW to below 14 kW | Article 89(2)(a) |
| That A1 exchanged to class A with the restriction | below 175cc | below 14 kW | Article 89(3)(a) |
| Class A without a restriction | above 125cc, plus everything A1 covers | above 11 kW, plus everything A1 covers | Article 57(1)(b) |

Imagine two riders offered the same 160cc scooter, one holding an A1 issued in 2023 and never exchanged, the other an A1 issued in 2026. The first is inside the old scope, because 160cc is below 175cc. The second is not, because 160cc is above the 125cc ceiling of Article 57(1)(a), and the model name on the bike changes neither answer.

## What A1 does not cover

- Petrol bikes above 125cc or electric machines above 11 kW on a new licence (that needs class A, which covers everything above the A1 thresholds)
- Three-wheel motorbikes (class B1)

The full class structure (A1, A and B1) is in [Vietnam Motorbike Licence Classes Explained (A1, A, B1)]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}).

### Class A: above the line

Article 57(1)(b) gives class A to two-wheeled motorbikes above 125cc or above 11 kW, together with every machine A1 covers. A new A1 therefore stops at the line even when the step to a 150cc bike looks small. [What the class A licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a-licence-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) covers what the higher class allows.

### Three-wheelers

Article 57(1)(c) gives three-wheeled motorbikes to class B1, which also covers the A1 machines, and [the B1 three-wheel guide]({{ '/articles/b1-three-wheel-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers that class. One exception sits in Article 57(2): a person with a disability who rides a three-wheeled motorbike made for people with disabilities is issued an A1. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA (Appendix IX, item 4(b)) prints a restriction on that card limiting it to that vehicle.

### Car licences

The car and truck classes, from B in Article 57(1)(d) onward, are a separate family and include no motorbike class, so a car licence does not stand in for an A1. [Riding a 125cc with a car licence]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains what that means for a rental scooter.

## Electric two-wheelers: kilowatts, and a speed test

For an electric machine the A1 ceiling is motor power, 11 kW, and Article 34(1)(g) adds a floor of its own. A moped is a two- or three-wheeled engine-driven vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if it has a combustion engine, a capacity of no more than 50 cm³ or, if electric, a motor of no more than 4 kW. A machine that misses any one of those limits is not a moped; under Article 34(1)(e) it is a motorbike, and the licence classes apply to it.

Electric two-wheelers therefore fall into three groups:

- **A moped** (4 kW or less and a design speed of 50 km/h or less): no licence class, minimum age 16. [What counts as a moped under Vietnamese law]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) gives the definition in full.
- **A motorbike up to 11 kW:** class A1, minimum age 18. This includes a machine under 4 kW that is designed to go faster than 50 km/h.
- **A motorbike above 11 kW:** class A.

The table shows the effect. The Honda ICON e: sits inside the moped limits, while the Honda CUV e: is a motorbike on both counts, above 4 kW and above 50 km/h, so it needs an A1. The Yamaha NEO's page gives a power figure under 4 kW but no top speed, which leaves the design-speed limit unanswered until you have the figure.

Pedal-assisted electric bicycles, whose assistance cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or at 25 km/h, are a separate non-motorised category (Article 34(2)(b)). Which category a rented e-bike belongs to depends on the machine, so ask for its power and design speed before assuming either. [Electric motorbike power and licence class]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-power-kw-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) puts named models on the kilowatt ladder, and [the licence guide for fast electrics]({{ '/articles/fast-electric-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers machines above the speed line.

## For foreign visitors

Most visitors do not hold a Vietnamese A1. Instead, recognition depends on your home licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle category — see our IDP [guide]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}).

The routes sit in Article 57(6) of the law, which names four kinds of licence valid in Vietnam: one issued by a Vietnamese authority; a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP together with a national licence from a state party, each fitting the vehicle (Article 57(6)(b)); a licence from a country or territory with which Vietnam has signed a treaty or arrangement on mutual recognition of licences (Article 57(6)(c)); and a foreign licence accepted under an international treaty to which Vietnam is a party (Article 57(6)(d)), each again fitting the vehicle.

The IDP route means carrying both documents (Article 57(8)(b)). None of these turns a foreign licence into an A1; they make it valid for the vehicle type it covers. [The tourist riding guide]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) asks which route a short-stay rider can use.

A foreigner who lives, works or studies in Vietnam can instead ask for the Vietnamese licence itself by exchanging a national licence that has not expired. The exchange is into the corresponding Vietnamese class (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(1)(a) and 24(5)(b)). An IDP cannot be exchanged (Article 24(1)(c)), and the Vietnamese licence issued lasts only as long as the qualifying document and the foreign licence allow: a visa or temporary residence card valid for 90 days or more, or a permanent residence card (Article 24(5)(a)). [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the dossier.

Riding a 110cc scooter with no valid licence of any kind is an offence (Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 18), and Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, in force from 15 August 2026, amends that decree. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how the offence is framed, and any fine figure should be confirmed in the current text of the decree before you rely on it.

It also complicates insurance and liability after an accident. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance; customers arrange their own cover, so check what your policy says about licence conditions before you ride.

## What to check before you take a rental

A shop names a model, but the law compares your licence with the machine you are handed. These checks put that comparison on the table before you ride.

**About your licence**

- **Read your own card.** A licence holder may ride the classes written on the licence (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(4)), and a restriction in its wording narrows them.
- **If your licence is foreign,** check that it and any IDP cover motorcycles and are still in term (Article 57(6)(b) and 57(8)).

**About the bike**

- **Ask how the bike's registration certificate will be shown at a stop** (a paper a rider carries under Article 56(1)(a)), and ask what capacity or power the bike is rated at. If the figure disagrees with the model name, ask the shop to explain before you ride.
- **For an electric machine,** ask for the rated motor power and the design speed, and which of the three groups above the shop places it in.
- **Keep the class question apart from the handover questions.** Papers, deposit and helmets are in [the motorbike rental requirements guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

To ask whether a particular bike is available and which class it falls into, [contact the shop]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) before you travel.
