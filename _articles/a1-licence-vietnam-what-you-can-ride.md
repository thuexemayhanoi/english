The A1 is Vietnam's standard motorbike licence, and almost every bike you will see on a Hanoi street falls inside it. But the limits moved in 2025, and the difference matters if you ride anything unusual.

## The limits

An A1 licence issued from 1 January 2025 under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) covers:

- two-wheel motorbikes with engines up to **125cc**
- electric two-wheelers with a maximum power up to **11 kW**

Licences issued before that date keep the old 175cc limit — no retest is required. Anything above the limit requires the full A class.

## Typical bikes inside A1

Nearly all everyday Vietnamese bikes qualify: Honda Vision, Air Blade, Lead, Click, SH, SH Mode, Wave, Future; Yamaha Janus, Grande, FreeGo, Sirius, Exciter; Suzuki Address and Raider; Vespa and Piaggio scooters. The popular "sport underbones" such as the Exciter 150, Raider R150 and Winner X are 149–155cc machines — within the old 175cc A1 range, but above the 125cc limit of a new post-2025 A1.

If you are renting, the safe question is "what engine size is this bike?" — not "is this a small bike?" A tall SH or a sporty-looking Exciter can be a different licence class from what its seat height suggests.

## Why it matters even if police rarely ask

Riding above your licence class is an administrative offence with fines set by decree, but the bigger risk is indirect. After an accident, an invalid licence typically voids insurance payouts, gives the other party's lawyer an easy argument, and can turn a simple collision into a personal-liability problem running into thousands of dollars.

## If your licence is from abroad

A foreign A1-equivalent with a valid 1968-convention IDP that shows a motorcycle category puts you in roughly the same position as a local A1 holder. A car-only licence or a 1949-convention IDP does not cover you for any motorbike, of any size.
