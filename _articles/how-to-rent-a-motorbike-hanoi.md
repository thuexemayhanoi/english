[Renting a motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/topics/rental/' | relative_url }}) is a five-minute transaction at most shops once you know the sequence. This is the process from search to riding away.

## Step 1: Decide what kind of bike you need

Three categories cover almost every visitor: automatic scooters (twist-and-go, best for stop-start traffic), semi-automatic underbones like the Honda Wave (no clutch lever, gears shifted by foot), and full manual motorcycles. If you have never ridden before, an automatic is the safest starting point; if your route stays inside the city, anything 110-125 cc is plenty. Choose by transmission first, engine size second; [automatic vs semi-automatic vs manual]({{ '/articles/automatic-vs-manual-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) walks through the choice.

## Step 2: Check your paperwork before you go

To ride legally you need a [licence that is valid in Vietnam]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}): a Vietnamese A1 licence, a licence from your home country together with a 1968-convention IDP, or a licence exchanged for a Vietnamese one. Bikes of 50 cc or less do not require a driving licence, but the rider must still be 16 or older. Shops will hand you a bike without asking - the legal responsibility is yours, and riding without a valid licence is a [2-4 million VND fine up to 125 cc and 6-8 million VND above it]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

## Step 3: Contact the shop and confirm availability

[Message ahead]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) - WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674, or walk in. Ask two things: is the model you want available on your dates, and what are the [current rates]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Availability of any specific model changes week to week, so confirm before you plan around it. Nguyen Tu is at 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De, Long Bien, open 09:00-21:00 daily.

## Step 4: Agree the terms before money moves

Confirm the rental period and price, the deposit, and how the deposit is held and returned. [Deposits at Hanoi rentals]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) typically run 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on the bike. Ask what the shop checks at return - photos of the bike at handover protect both sides. Get the payment method settled too: cash, bank transfer and QR payment are the standard options in Hanoi.

## Step 5: Inspect before you ride

Walk the bike: lights, horn, brakes, mirrors, tyres, fuel level, existing scratches. Photograph anything you see. Thirty seconds here prevents a deposit argument later - the [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) lists exactly what to look at.

## Step 6: Ride, return, recover the deposit

Return the bike in the agreed condition, inside opening hours, on time - [late returns are charged by the hour]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Once the shop confirms the bike is fine, the deposit comes back to you.

That is the whole process. The guides linked above and in the related guides below expand each step - [documents]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), deposits, booking, prices and inspection.
