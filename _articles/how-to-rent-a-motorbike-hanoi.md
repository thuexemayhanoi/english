[Renting a motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/topics/rental/' | relative_url }}) is a five-minute transaction at most shops once you know the sequence. This is the process from search to riding away. In short: check that your licence covers the bike, confirm a model with the shop, agree the deposit and price, inspect and photograph the bike, then ride it and bring it back on time.

## The whole rental at a glance

To rent a motorbike in Hanoi you work through six steps, and the order matters: the licence check comes before you fall for a model, and the terms come before any money moves. The table is the timeline. Each step below sets out what to decide, what to check or ask, and a mistake to avoid.

| Step | When | You decide | You check or ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Kind of bike | Before you travel | Automatic, semi-automatic or manual; how long you need it | Which licence you will ride on |
| 2. Paperwork | Before you travel | Whether your licence and permit cover the bike | Licence, permit and passport are packed |
| 3. The shop | When you book | Model, dates, collection or delivery | Availability on your exact dates |
| 4. Terms | Before paying | Whether the deposit and conditions suit you | Deposit, insurance, fuel, who pays for what |
| 5. Inspection | At handover | Whether the bike is fit to ride | Brakes, lights, tyres, photos of every mark |
| 6. Ride and return | During and at the end | Where you ride; when you hand it back | Local rules; condition and deposit at return |

## Step 1: Decide what kind of bike you need

Three categories cover almost every visitor: automatic scooters (twist-and-go, best for stop-start traffic), semi-automatic underbones like the Honda Wave (no clutch lever, gears shifted by foot), and full manual motorcycles. If you have never ridden before, an automatic is the safest starting point; if your route stays inside the city, anything 110-125 cc is plenty. Choose by transmission first, engine size second; [automatic vs semi-automatic vs manual]({{ '/articles/automatic-vs-manual-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) walks through the choice.

Three questions narrow the choice before you look at a single model:

- **Who is riding?** Every rider needs a licence that covers the bike, so Step 2 can rule options in or out. A passenger or luggage changes how much space and power you want.
- **For how long?** Nguyen Tu rents by the day, the week or the month, and longer terms can be discussed.
- **Where will you ride?** Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so mention any trip beyond the city to the shop before you agree the rental.

The trap is to pick a model first and check the licence second. A bike that looks right in photos is no use if your licence does not cover it, so hold the choice of model loosely until Step 2 is done.

## Step 2: Check your paperwork before you go

To ride legally you need a [licence that is valid in Vietnam]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}): a Vietnamese A1 licence, a licence from your home country together with a 1968-convention IDP, or a licence exchanged for a Vietnamese one. Bikes of 50 cc or less do not require a driving licence, but the rider must still be 16 or older. Whether or not a shop asks to see your licence, the legal responsibility is yours, and riding without a valid licence is a [2-4 million VND fine up to 125 cc and 6-8 million VND above it]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). The stakes go beyond the fine: after a crash, an invalid licence can complicate an insurance claim and liability, as the [guide to the 1968 Vienna Convention]({{ '/articles/1968-vienna-convention-idp-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains.

Three checks are easy at home and hard at the counter:

- Your national licence includes a motorcycle category.
- If you ride on a foreign licence, your permit is an IDP issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention with a motorcycle category shown on it, carried together with the national licence.
- Your passport is packed alongside the licence and permit.

The common mistake is relying on a document that does not count: an IDP carried without the national licence it translates, a permit issued under the 1949 convention, or a licence whose category does not cover the bike. None of these makes riding legal here. If none of the licence routes applies to you, a 50 cc bike is the licence-free class to ask about, and its availability and price are by enquiry with the shop. The [eligibility guide for foreigners]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the routes in detail.

## Step 3: Contact the shop and confirm availability

[Message ahead]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) - WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674, or walk in. Ask two things: is the model you want available on your dates, and what are the [current rates]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Availability of any specific model changes week to week, so confirm before you plan around it. Nguyen Tu is at 112 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De, Long Bien, open 09:00-21:00 daily. Landing at the airport first? [Renting a Motorbike When You Arrive at Noi Bai Airport]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-noi-bai-arrival/' | relative_url }}) covers the first day.

Your first message can be short: the dates and the time you will return the bike, the model and the number of bikes, and whether you will collect from the shop or want to arrange delivery. Ask for the total for your dates and the deposit in the same message. The usual way plans unravel is treating a model as booked because it was available when you first asked. Ask for the reply to confirm your exact dates, and do not build flights or tours around one particular bike until the shop has confirmed it.

## Step 4: Agree the terms before money moves

Confirm the rental period and price, the deposit, and how the deposit is held and returned. [Deposits at Hanoi rentals]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) typically run 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on the bike. Ask what the shop checks at return - photos of the bike at handover protect both sides. Get the payment method settled too: cash, bank transfer and QR payment are the standard options in Hanoi.

The agreement can be brief, so raise these points yourself before you pay:

- **Days and times.** When a rental day starts and ends, and by what time the bike is due back.
- **The deposit.** What can be deducted from it, and for what.
- **Costs that may fall on you.** The shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tyre-pressure checks, while inner tubes and tyres may be your responsibility depending on the situation.
- **Use.** The fuel level at handover and at return, who else may ride, and whether the bike may leave Hanoi.
- **Problems.** Whom to call if the bike breaks down, and what the shop will and will not cover.

Two terms are worth stating plainly. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so arrange your own cover before you ride. Returning early carries no automatic refund entitlement, so choose the rental period you will actually use.

Finish by sending the key terms in a message to the shop's number: a line of text gives both sides a written record. [Understanding a motorbike rental agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the terms to confirm. A common mistake is handing over money or a document before these answers are clear, and a passport is the wrong thing to leave as security: a deposit does that job.

## Step 5: Inspect before you ride

Walk the bike: lights, horn, brakes, mirrors, tyres, fuel level, existing scratches. Photograph anything you see. Thirty seconds here prevents a deposit argument later - the [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) lists exactly what to look at.

Collecting the bike and having it delivered change where this happens, not what you check. If delivery has been arranged, the same walk-round happens at your door, and [motorbike delivery to the Old Quarter]({{ '/articles/motorbike-delivery-old-quarter/' | relative_url }}) covers a hotel handover there. Work through the bike in this order:

1. Photograph the whole bike before you touch it: both sides, front, back and every existing mark. Sending the photos to the shop in a message gives them a timestamp.
2. Start the engine and check the lights, horn and indicators.
3. Test both brakes, first with the bike standing and then at walking pace somewhere clear.
4. Look at the tyres, mirrors, stands and steering.
5. Compare the fuel level with what you agreed in Step 4.
6. Check that the bike's registration papers are with it.

If a check fails, say so before you ride and ask for a different bike. The mistake to avoid is the reverse: riding off with a doubt because the handover feels rushed, then trying to prove later that a scratch or a weak brake was there from the start.

## Step 6: Ride, return, recover the deposit

### While you have the bike

Start with a quiet stretch near the shop before you join heavier traffic. The [first-time renter's walkthrough]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first hour and the first day. A few habits are worth fixing from the start:

- **Fasten the helmet.** Vietnam's [helmet law]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) requires riders and passengers to wear a helmet that meets the national standard, correctly fastened.
- **Carry your licence,** and your permit with it if you ride on a foreign licence.
- **Message early.** If something feels wrong with the bike, tell the shop before it becomes a problem on the road.

**Check where you may ride.** Hanoi has run a pilot low-emission zone since 1 July 2026. The Government Portal reports that all road vehicles, including motorcycles, mopeds and cars, are prohibited from entering Zone 1 from 7:00 p.m. to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Zone 1 is a list of named streets, among them Old Quarter streets such as Hang Dao, Hang Bac and Ma May. The pilot is phased, so check the current notice before planning an evening ride there; [renting in Hoan Kiem]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-hoan-kiem/' | relative_url }}) covers the district around the lake.

### Returning the bike and the deposit

Return the bike in the agreed condition, inside opening hours, on time - [late returns are charged by the hour]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Once the shop confirms the bike is fine, the deposit comes back to you.

Treat the return as the handover in reverse. Agree a return time that leaves a margin before any flight or train you have to catch. Photograph the bike again from the same angles, compare the fuel level with what was agreed, and keep the agreement and your handover photos until the deposit is back in your hands. If your plans change, message the shop before the agreed return time, not after it.

The common mistake at this stage is arriving without the handover photos: they are your record of how the bike looked when you took it, and a mark you did not make is hard to argue about without them. [Returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) walks through the return in full.
