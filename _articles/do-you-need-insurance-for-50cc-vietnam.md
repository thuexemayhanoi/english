Yes. The licence exemption for 50cc bikes has nothing to do with insurance — compulsory cover attaches to the vehicle, not to the rider's licence.

## The rule

Article 56 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) requires every vehicle owner participating in traffic to hold compulsory civil-liability insurance, with the mechanics set out in Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP. A petrol moped is a motor vehicle in that framework, and its certificate should be carried when riding.

## The fine

Under Article 18(2) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, a rider who cannot produce a valid compulsory insurance certificate is fined 200,000–300,000 VND — the same band as other document offences, and the same for mopeds as for motorbikes.

## What the cover actually is

Compulsory insurance is third-party cover: it compensates people you injure and property you damage, within the decree's limits. It does not repair your own bike and does not pay your medical costs. Its real value is legal compliance plus a backstop against the worst outcome — hurting someone else with no cover means paying that compensation personally.

## The practical notes

- Buying a used 50cc: check the insurance is current and transfers sensibly, or buy fresh cover immediately.
- Renting: the rental side keeps fleet cover; confirm the certificate copy is with the bike before leaving.
- Parents of teenage riders: the insurance duty follows the vehicle owner. Handing a moped to a 16-year-old without valid cover exposes both of you.
