Vietnam's 150-class manual bikes (xe con tay) are its most romantic purchases - and its most questionable ones. All three current models have fully verified manufacturer data (retrieved 23 September 2026), so the question of who needs one can be answered with numbers.

## What the class costs to run

| Spec | Winner R | Exciter 155 VVA | CBR150R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claimed economy | 2.59 L/100 km (UDC) | 2.07 L/100 km | 2.91 L/100 km |
| List price | 46,360,000 VND | 55,200,000 VND (limited ABS) | 72,290,000 VND |
| Kerb weight | 124 kg | 123 kg wet | 139 kg |

Against the semi-automatic class - claims as low as 1.47 L/100 km and prices from 18.8 million VND - the manual class burns roughly 40-100 percent more fuel per kilometre and costs two to four times as much.

## Who the data says needs one

1. Riders who travel long distances regularly. The CBR150R's 12-litre tank and fairing are the only verified combination built for sustained highway work.
2. Riders whose terrain or load demands torque - the Exciter's 14.4 Nm carries a passenger uphill far better than any 110.
3. Riders who ride for pleasure. This is a legitimate need - the clutch, the six-speed gearbox and the 9,000-rpm power band are the point.

## Who the data says does not

City-only commuters. In stop-start traffic, a manual clutch adds work with no benefit a 1.56 L/100 km semi-automatic does not deliver better. Carrying a passenger purely in the city: a Future 125 FI does it cheaper and cooler in summer.
