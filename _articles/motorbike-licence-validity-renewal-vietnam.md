Motorbike licences in Vietnam do not expire, with one exception: a licence that a foreigner receives by exchanging a foreign licence carries a term. Under Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), the A1 and A classes — the two-wheeled motorbike classes — are issued with no time limit, and so is the three-wheeled class B1. You do not renew them the way a car licence is renewed every few years.

## What "no time limit" covers

An [A1 or A licence]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) issued after a test has no end date and needs no periodic renewal. What takes it out of use is revocation under Article 62(5) or, until points are restored, the loss of every point (Article 58(3)). Licences Vietnam issues follow these rules; an IDP or a foreign licence has its own dates.

That last one matters — a licence with all 12 points deducted stops being usable even though it has no expiry date to lapse.

Translated plainly, Article 57(5)(a) says that licences of classes A1, A and B1 have "no time limit". [What the A1 class lets you ride]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) sets those classes against real bikes.

### When a licence has no effect

Article 57(7) names two situations in which a licence stops counting: its term has run out, or it was revoked under Article 62(5). A motorbike licence issued after a test has no term to run out, so revocation is the one that applies to it.

Article 62(5) gives three grounds for revocation:

- the holder fails the health requirements for the class, as a medical facility concludes;
- the licence was issued against the rules, or the holder used fraud to be issued, exchanged or reissued a licence (point (b), in the wording amended by Law 118/2025/QH15 from 1 July 2026);
- a licence held in a penalty case has passed its holding period, or the time limit for carrying out the penalty decision has run out, and the offender does not collect it without a legitimate reason.

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 25, sets the steps: the revocation decision follows within 3 working days of the case being found. After a revocation for health or improper issue, Article 25(2)(a) points to the Article 21(2) to (4) procedure for reissue. After a revocation for an uncollected licence, Article 25(2)(b) bars a new licence for 5 years from the day the violation was found, and the person then trains and tests again as a first-time applicant.

## What still has a time limit

Car and commercial classes (B, C, D and the rest) remain time-limited licences under the same law.

Article 57(5)(b) and (c) give the terms: 10 years from the date of issue for classes B and C1, and 5 years from the date of issue for C, D1, D2, D, BE, C1E, CE, D1E, D2E and DE.

For a car licence past its term, Article 19(2) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sets a theory test when it expired from 30 days up to less than 1 year before the complete dossier was filed, and theory, driving-course and road tests from 1 year. The paragraph is worded for car licences and does not say how an exchanged motorbike licence past its term is handled.

### One number for two kinds of licence

Article 18(2) of the Circular gives each person a single licence number, shared by licences without a term and licences with one, in electronic form or on PET material. A holder who wants the two kinds integrated asks for an exchange (Articles 18(3) and 21(1)(d)). The motorbike classes on such a licence carry no term, and the car classes do. Articles 18 and 21 do not say how a car end date affects the motorbike entitlement, so ask the Traffic Police Department before that date arrives. And pre-2025 licences continue under Article 89's transitional rule: they remain usable as issued, per the validity printed on them.

### The exception: a licence exchanged by a foreigner

"No expiry" is the wrong answer for a Vietnamese licence that a foreigner receives by exchanging a foreign one. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a), says its term is to be in line with the date on one of the papers that made the exchange possible, in line with the term of the foreign licence, and not beyond the term set for the Vietnamese licence. Article 24(1)(a) lists those papers. They are a diplomatic ID, an official ID, a permanent residence card in force, or a visa or temporary residence card with 90 or more days left.

The motorbike classes have no term of their own, so the last limit adds nothing and the printed date follows the papers. Under Article 57(7)(a) a licence past its term has no effect, so the printed date is where its effect ends. Article 24 does not say whether a later visa or card renewal changes a date already printed, or how an exchanged motorbike licence is replaced once it has lapsed.

Article 21(1)(b) lets a holder of a Vietnamese licence, foreigners included, ask for an exchange before it expires. How that clause fits with Article 24 is not stated, so put the question to the Traffic Police Department in writing before the printed date. [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the dossier and filing routes, and [the expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets exchange against sitting the test.

## Term by licence type

| Licence | Term | What ends it | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vietnamese A1, A or B1 issued after a test | None (Article 57(5)(a)) | Revocation (Article 57(7)(b)); every point deducted bars riding on it (Article 58(3)) | Nothing to renew; ask for a reissue or exchange only in the cases of Circular Articles 20 and 21 |
| Vietnamese A1, A or B1 issued to a foreigner by exchange | In line with the visa, card or ID paper behind it and with the foreign licence (Circular, Article 24(5)(a)) | The printed date passing (Article 57(7)(a)); revocation | Read the printed date, diary it, and ask the Traffic Police Department about the route before it arrives |
| Any licence issued before 1 January 2025 | As written on the licence (Article 89(1)) | The written date if there is one; revocation | Keep riding on it as written; exchange is a choice, not a deadline |
| Car classes B and C1; C, D1, D2, D and the trailer classes | 10 years and 5 years from issue (Article 57(5)(b) and (c)) | The term passing (Article 57(7)(a)) | Exchange before the term (Article 62(2)(c)); past it, Circular Article 19(2) sets the tests |
| Foreign national licence or IDP | Not a Vietnamese licence; an IDP lasts at most 3 years from the day it was issued, and no longer than the national licence still has to run (Article 57(8)(a)) | The earlier of the two dates | Read both dates; [how long an IDP lasts]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes them apart |

## Paper licences

Older motorbike licences printed on paper remain valid under the transitional provisions, but a reissue — triggered by loss, damage, or a detail change — produces the current PET card.

Article 18(2) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA adds that a licence is now issued either in electronic form or on PET material, so a printed card is one choice and the electronic licence the other. [Old licence classes after 2025]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what each old class still allows and how it maps onto the new ones.

For a no-term licence issued before 1 July 2012, Article 62(3) goes further and encourages an exchange for a licence under the Law. An encouragement is not a duty, and Article 62(3) attaches no date to it. Article 18(8) of the Circular treats a licence as valid when a competent authority issued it and its printed form number, issue date and expiry date match the licence information system, or its data is linked to the electronic identity account or the police applications.

## Renewal-style situations

The procedures that feel like renewal are reissues: a [licence lost or damaged]({{ '/articles/lost-motorbike-licence-replacement-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is reissued with the same classes, except that a pre-2025 motorbike class is mapped to a current class (Article 89(3)); a change of registered details is updated onto a new card; a licence whose points have been fully deducted comes back only after the recovery process.

For motorbikes, "renewal" is really "reissue when something happens to the card".

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA is more exact than that phrase, because it separates a reissue from an exchange. A lost licence that is still in term is reissued (Article 20(1)); the other cases are exchanges, listed in Article 21(1). Article 62(2) of the Law names the same occasions in general terms and adds foreign licences still in effect and licences issued by the defence or police ministries.

| Situation | Route | Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA |
|---|---|---|
| Licence lost, still in term | Reissue, using the Article 21(2) to (4) dossier and procedure | Article 20(1) |
| Licence damaged and no longer usable | Exchange | Article 21(1)(a) |
| Holder wants a new licence before the old one expires | Exchange | Article 21(1)(b) |
| Date of birth, name or nationality differs from the ID card, citizen ID or passport | Exchange to match the ID | Article 21(1)(c) |
| Holder wants a no-term licence and a term licence integrated | Exchange | Articles 18(3) and 21(1)(d) |

### Dossier, channel and timing

The dossier in Article 21(2) is a valid health certificate, a passport still in term for a foreigner or a Vietnamese settled abroad, and papers showing the correct details when the exchange fixes a discrepancy. Article 21(3)(a) names the online route first: the National Public Service Portal, the national identity application or the police applications. The in-person route, at the Traffic Police Department or at a commune-level police office that has a reception point, is for someone with no level-2 electronic identification, no licence data in the system, or a portal fault.

The Department has 1 day from a complete, valid dossier to send the data to the Traffic Police Bureau, which issues the licence; a printed card on request takes up to 2.5 working days (Article 21(3)(c)). The Circular states a duty to pay a fee but no amount, so ask at the counter or on the portal.

### When a reissue or exchange is refused

Article 20(2) refuses a reissue when the licence's details are missing from the licence information system or from the register of issued licences, or when the holder has not finished what the authorities require after a traffic offence. Article 21(1)(đ) applies the offence rule to exchanges too, and Article 62(4) of the Law sets it. Keep the card safe and, for an old licence, ask the Traffic Police Department whether its details are in the system.

## Points and the 12-point balance

Article 58 gives a licence a balance of 12 points (clause 1). A licence that has not lost all its points and has had no deduction for 12 months from the latest one goes back to 12 (clause 2). Once every point is gone, the licence cannot be used to ride; after at least 6 months from that day the holder may sit a traffic-law knowledge test run by the traffic police, and passing restores 12 points (clause 3). An exchange, a reissue or an upgrade keeps the balance as it was, so a new card does not wipe points (clause 4).

Article 56(1) of the Law asks a driver for a licence that is in effect, has points left and suits the vehicle, with the moped class as the exception (clause 4). [The points system guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-points-system-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains deductions, and [what happens when all points are lost]({{ '/articles/lose-all-licence-points-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) follows the recovery path. Penalties for licence offences sit in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, not in the licence rules. Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended that decree with effect from 15 August 2026, so read any amount in [the expired licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/expired-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) or [the fine for riding without a licence]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) against the amended decree.

## Checking your licence before a Hanoi rental

Whatever the rental period, the licence duty falls on the rider. Before you book, run through four checks:

- Read the class on the licence against the bike you want.
- Look for an end date on the licence; a licence a foreigner received by exchange carries one, and it should fall after the last day of the rental.
- If you hold a Vietnamese licence, confirm the point balance is not zero.
- Carry the licence, on the card or, where it has been linked, through the electronic identification account (Article 56(1) and (3)).

Renters arrange their own motorbike insurance, because Nguyen Tu does not provide it. To ask which documents will be wanted at handover, call, WhatsApp or Zalo +84 942 467 674 rather than assuming. [The long-term rider's licence checklist]({{ '/articles/long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts every date on one list, and [when a licence expires mid-rental]({{ '/articles/licence-expiry-during-long-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers a date that falls inside a booking.

## Questions about validity and renewal

### Does a Vietnamese A1 licence expire?

Not if it was issued after a test (Article 57(5)(a)). A licence issued to a foreigner by exchange is the exception (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a)).

### Is a lost licence still valid?

Losing the card is not one of the two cases in Article 57(7), so the licence itself has not lost effect. Article 20(1) of the Circular lets the holder of a licence still in term ask for a reissue. Article 56(1) of the Law still asks the driver to carry the licence, and Article 56(3) allows it to be shown through the electronic identification account only where it has been linked to that account. Without either, wait for the reissue before riding.

### Do I have to swap an old licence for a new one?

Article 89 sets no deadline. Article 89(1) keeps a licence issued before 1 January 2025 in use for the term written on it.

### Does an IDP renew like a licence?

An IDP is not a Vietnamese licence and runs its own clock: Article 57(8)(a) gives it at most 3 years from the issue date, and it cannot outlast the national licence it travels with.
