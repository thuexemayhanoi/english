Underbones carry remarkable loads in Vietnam — but the riders who do it safely are the ones who respect how a load changes the bike. Weight placement, securing, and adjusted riding habits matter more than raw cargo size.

## The two rules of placement

**Low and forward, never high and rear.** A heavy box on the floor between your feet is close to ideal: low centre of gravity, near the steering axis. The same box strapped on the pillion seat, behind you, lightens the front wheel and makes the bike wander at speed. The rear rack is for moderate loads only — and the higher you stack it, the slower you should ride.

Keep the heaviest items against the frame, and pack total weight as evenly as practical from side to side. An underbone is narrow and light; a one-sided load makes itself felt at every corner.

## Securing the load

- **Cargo net** for the floor-and-seat load: cheap, fast, and the correct tool for soft bags.
- **Bungee cords with hooks** for boxes: stretch the net or cords over the load to fixed frame points, never to moving parts or the chain side.
- **Nothing on the bars**: handlebar bags steer with the front wheel, adding mass that fights you in slow turns.
- **Check after the first kilometre**, then periodically — cords relax, nets shift.

The general luggage guide covers tie-down patterns in more detail; the principle is always "if it can move, it will move at the worst moment".

## What a load changes

- **Launches**: more revs, slower engagement feel. A loaded centrifugal clutch bites later; feed the throttle gradually.
- **Braking**: distances grow significantly. Brake earlier, use both brakes, finish slower.
- **Cornering**: the bike resists lean changes and holds its lean longer. Plan turns earlier and make them wider.
- **Slow speed**: a loaded bike is calmer in a straight line but more top-heavy if the load is high. Keep high loads off the seat.

## Legal and practical limits

Vietnamese road rules cap motorbike loads by dimension and weight relative to the bike's design, and a police check applies to overloading as it does to any vehicle — keep loads within sane bounds and obviously secured. Also consider the people around you: a load wider than your shoulders in dense traffic endangers others, not just you.
