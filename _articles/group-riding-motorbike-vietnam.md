Riding with friends — to a day trip, a waterfall, or just across town — is one of the best parts of having a bike in Vietnam. It is also how experienced riders end up riding above their level: the group quietly sets a pace that matches its fastest, least careful member. Decide the pace in advance and the group becomes safer than solo riding.

## Agree on three things before setting off

Route, pace and regroup points. The rule that matters most: ride your own ride. Nobody improves by riding at a speed that feels fast for them while watching a friend's back wheel.

## Stagger, do not pair up

On open road, ride offset — one rider slightly left of the lane centre, the next slightly right — with a comfortable two-second cushion to the bike ahead. Staggering gives you an escape lane instead of a rear-end if someone brakes. In dense traffic, drop the formation entirely: filtering is a solo skill, and riding-motorbike-blind-spots-vietnam applies double when there are more of you.

## Signals only work if everyone knows them

Point at hazards, hold a hand up to signal slowing, use a clear arm for turns. Agree on the basics at the start; hand-signals-motorbike-vietnam has the core set. In a group, one rider signals and the next passes it back — that is how a signal reaches the tail.

## The slowest rider leads

It sounds backwards, but putting the least experienced rider in front keeps the group together without them being chased. The experienced rider sweeps at the back, where they can see and manage problems.
