Vietnam's rainy season drops a month of rain in an afternoon, and the rain gear you carry decides whether that is a nuisance or a soaked, dangerous ride. Wet clothes distract, chill and — worst of all — make you rush.

## The poncho: Vietnam's default, honestly assessed

The one-piece riding poncho covers you and half the bike in one motion, packs small and costs little. Its weaknesses: it balloons at speed, can catch on mirrors and bars, and offers nothing to your legs. For city riding at moderate speed it is genuinely fine. On faster roads, anything that flaps is a hazard — a two-piece suit stays put.

## The two-piece rain suit

A proper jacket-and-pants set keeps its shape at speed, keeps your legs dry and lasts years. Look for taped seams, a high collar, and cuffs that close over your gloves rather than inside them — water runs downhill. If you ride daily through the season, this is the upgrade worth making.

## Feet, hands, eyes

Wet shoes are miserable and slippery: keep a plastic bag over socks trick for emergencies, but real waterproof shoes are better. Wet gloves lose grip — a thin waterproof glove over your riding gloves works. And a clear visor or cap visor keeps rain out of your eyes — visibility-riding-motorbike-vietnam applies double when everything is grey and smeared.

## Visibility is part of rain gear

Rain dulls every colour and every driver's attention. Bright rain gear or a reflective strip is the one upgrade every cheap poncho needs; glue-on reflective tape costs almost nothing.
