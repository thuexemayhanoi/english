Numb, tingling hands after an hour on a Vietnamese highway are usually blamed on the road. The road is a contributor, but the mechanism is resonance: every engine pulse and every bump sets the handlebar singing at a frequency your nerves read as pain. Bar-end weights exist to spoil that song.

## How they work

A weight at the very end of the bar changes the system's resonant frequency and dissipates vibration energy as tiny movements instead of one big oscillation. That is why manufacturers fit them from the factory — removing bar-end weights to fit bar-end mirrors or grips is a real, measurable comfort downgrade at speed. The physics is the same as putting a finger on a ringing ruler: mass at the tip kills the swing.

## What heavier weights can and cannot fix

Heavier aftermarket weights, or weights combined into mirrors, genuinely reduce buzz for many bikes. What they cannot fix is vibration that comes from a fault: worn engine mounts, loose fasteners, a tyre gone out of round, or a drivetrain problem — the diagnostic list lives in handlebar-vibration-motorbike. If the buzz appeared suddenly or grows with a specific speed, find the fault before shopping for weights. Weights are the last step of a fix, not a substitute for one.

## The rest of the system

Vibration control is a stack: weights kill resonance, grips absorb what is left (the trade-offs are in handlebar-grips-choosing-motorbike), lever angle decides how hard your wrists clamp, and your hand position decides how long the load is sustained — setting levers for a neutral wrist is covered in adjustable-levers-motorbikes. Bar-end mounted mirrors also add or subtract mass at exactly the right point; if you swap mirrors, feel the difference at highway speed before assuming the new setup is neutral (aftermarket-mirrors-motorbike-upgrade).

## Practical notes

Bike-specific threaded weights are the easy fit; universal weights rely on expansion plugs that loosen under constant vibration — check them. Avoid oversize weights on scooters with light bars: they can make the steering feel vague at parking speeds. And after any bar-end change, test at your real cruise speed for at least half an hour — vibration comfort is only visible where you actually ride.
