Across Vietnam, small dogs ride pillion. They stand in front baskets, sit in footwell carriers, or peer out of backpack-style slings, and it looks so normal that nobody questions the setup — until a doorframe, a horn blast or a pothole turns a casual carry into a lost animal in moving traffic. Carrying a pet safely is about restraint, position and heat, in that order.

## The core rule: restrained, never free

An unrestrained animal on a moving bike has no idea what a swerve means. A dog that jumps at a cat on a pavement does so in front of a truck. Whatever the carrier, the animal must not be able to exit it — a zip or clip the pet cannot open — and the carrier itself must be anchored so a bump cannot throw it off.

## Carrier types and where they belong

A rigid plastic pet carrier lined with something grippy is the most secure option: it protects the animal in a tip-over, and it can be strapped into the scooter footwell between the rider's feet, where the weight sits low and the rider can feel it. Backpack-style carriers with proper ventilation work for the smallest animals, worn on the rider's chest rather than the back — a bag behind you hides the animal from your attention and changes your balance. Front baskets — the options are covered in front-baskets-scooters-vietnam — are the most common and least safe position: only acceptable for a basket with a clip-in leash shortener and a lid, and never in dense traffic at speed. ## Heat and breathing

Dogs do not sweat; a sealed carrier in Vietnamese sun becomes an oven in minutes, and a panting animal in a dark bag in traffic is in trouble long before the rider notices. Ventilation holes on multiple sides, light colours, shade stops on longer rides, and water. Never leave the animal in the carrier on a parked bike.

## In traffic

Short trips, quiet roads at first, and a slow build-up of what the animal tolerates. Watch the animal, not just the road ahead — the first ride tells you whether the setup works or needs rethinking. And as with any load, a cargo net over a soft carrier keeps it from shifting: cargo-nets-bungee-cords-motorbike. Plan for the failure case: if the carrier fell at 30 km/h, would the animal stay inside it? That is the standard.
