A month is a commitment. The cheapest way to avoid a month on the wrong bike is to test the machine on your real routes first. In Hanoi, a test ride before a monthly motorbike rental means a day or two on a daily rental: it settles fit, controls and load, but not fuel use, wear or the seat in week three.

## The practical approach: a short rental first

The straightforward pattern is to rent the bike daily for a day or two, ride your actual commute and errands, and then convert to a monthly rental on the same or a similar machine, although no trial or conversion policy is published, so each step is something to ask the shop. Contact us to confirm current availability for both the trial and the month. If the trial goes well, [Starting a Monthly Motorbike Rental: The First Week]({{ '/articles/starting-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the set-up conversation.

### What the trial days cost and commit you to

The published daily rates are 150,000 VND for the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio, and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade. A daily rate prices a day of trial and says nothing about a month, so ask for the monthly figure for the specific bike; [the monthly price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) gives the wider picture.

The deposit is typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions. [The monthly deposit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the deposit on a monthly rental in more detail.

### Spend the two days differently

Use the first day for the bike and the second for your life. On day one, work through the kerb, throttle, brake and U-turn stages in daylight at a quiet hour; on day two, ride your commute at the hour you would really travel it, with your usual load, including the after-dark stretch if you ride home in the evening. If you are new to Hanoi traffic, keep day one off the busiest roads.

## What to evaluate on a trial

Not just "does it start" — does the seat fit you after an hour, does the bike handle the speeds and slopes of your route, does the storage fit your daily bag, can you manage the weight at walking pace and when parking? Run the full [Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) as well, since a bike you may take for a month deserves a stricter look than a day-tripper.

The stages below follow the order a ride naturally takes.

### At the kerb: fit and reach

Wear the shoes you will ride in and put both feet down: if only your toes reach, junctions will feel unsteady. Check that you reach the bars and the brake levers with your elbows slightly bent, and set the mirrors to show the lane behind you rather than your own arms. Then wheel the bike backwards a few metres and lift it onto its stand and off again, because parking will often ask for it.

### The first minutes: throttle, gears and brakes

Pull away on a quiet street and notice how the bike answers small movements of the throttle. Honda's pages list an automatic (CVT) transmission for the Vision and the Air Blade, so there is no gear to change. Honda lists the Wave Alpha 110 with a four-speed mechanical rotary gearbox, and Yamaha's page for the Sirius RC 110 gives a four-speed rotary gearbox and a wet multi-plate centrifugal clutch: on those, shift through the gears where you can think about your foot, and find neutral at a standstill. No manufacturer figures are quoted here for the Honda Click or Yamaha Mio, so ask the shop what type each is.

Test both brakes at low speed in a clear place, and note how far each brake lever or pedal travels before the bike slows and whether it stops straight. Yamaha's Sirius RC 110 page shows a hydraulic disc at the front and a drum at the rear, and it also mentions drum and disc versions of the bike, so judge brakes against the bike's own type.

### Stop-start traffic and a slow U-turn

A bike that feels fine at steady speed can be tiring in a queue, so spend part of the ride in slow, stop-start traffic and notice whether you can hold it steady at walking pace and whether it restarts without fuss. Then, in a quiet open space such as a wide side street or a car park, ride a slow U-turn and a figure of eight both ways: how tightly the bike turns decides how it copes with narrow lanes and parking rows.

### With your load and your passenger

Ride a short stretch again with the weight you will really carry: the bag, the shopping, the second person if there is one. Pulling away, braking and parking all feel different when the bike is loaded. The passenger rules are in the legal section below.

### Lights and horn, ideally after dark

If you will ride home in the evening, ride a short stretch after dark and see how far the headlight reaches on your own road. Article 20(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the front light to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather that limits visibility, and [the headlight rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers when it must be on. Press the horn once to confirm that it works, somewhere it will not disturb anyone: on the road, Article 21(1) allows it only to warn of a situation that could become unsafe or to signal an overtake.

### Reading the result

| What you noticed | What it points to | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Only your toes reach the ground, or the bars feel a stretch away | A size problem that repeats at every stop | Ask for a different bike; do not count on getting used to it |
| Brakes feel soft, uneven or noisy | A fault, or a brake type you have not met | Ask for a check before you decide |
| Fine alone, awkward when loaded | The month will include loads | Ride the same loaded route on another bike |
| Gear changes or the queue wear you out | A daily cost, not a quirk | Compare [an automatic with a semi-automatic]({{ '/articles/automatic-or-semi-automatic-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) |
| The bike suits you but the terms are unclear | The ride settled the bike, not the deal | Settle the terms before you convert |

## Talk to the shop about the plan

Tell the shop upfront that you are trialling before a month. They can tell you whether the same bike can stay with you for the month, what the monthly rate is if published, and whether the trial period counts toward anything, though none of these is a published term, so treat each as a question to ask. For models without a published monthly rate, this is also when you confirm the current monthly price.

Put these to the shop before the first ride, and keep the replies in writing:

- Is a short test ride possible before I pay for a day, and where may I take it? No test-ride policy is published.
- Which variant is this bike, and what is its engine size? The licence class follows the engine.
- Does a helmet come with the bike, and is one available for a passenger?
- How will the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate be shown at a stop?
- Who pays for a puncture or other consumables if one happens during the trial?
- Who do I contact if the bike develops a fault, and could another bike be possible? Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use and no round-the-clock rescue is published, so say so if you plan to ride beyond the city.

## What a short ride cannot tell you

A trial can only start to answer two things, fuel and comfort:

- **Fuel use.** Two days give a rough figure at best: note the odometer and the fuel level at handover and what you add, then divide litres by distance. Real consumption varies with speed, load, temperature and terrain, so use the result to compare bikes on your route, not as a monthly budget.
- **Comfort over weeks.** An hour on the seat says little about your back, wrists and knees after weeks of daily riding, so treat a borderline seat or reach as a likely daily irritation. [How a month changes the choice of bike]({{ '/articles/choosing-motorbike-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers how route, body, storage and mileage interact.

Two more things are better planned than tested, distance and parking:

- **Distance and wear.** A trial cannot show how a bike ages. The owner's practical guidance, which is not a manufacturer interval, is periodic checks at around 1,500 km or more of use, so ask when a mid-term check could fit; [the high-mileage guide]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the rhythm.
- **Where the bike sleeps.** Wheel the bike into and out of the real place once; [daily parking for a monthly renter]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the options.

## The test ride and the law

The usual road rules apply to a trial ride; these are the points that matter most here. They come from Law 36/2024/QH15 as amended by Law 118/2025/QH15 and consolidated in text 55/VBHN-VPQH (March 2026); they are general information, not legal advice.

### Licence

Article 56(1) requires a licence that is valid for the type of vehicle, except for moped riders (Article 56(4)). A moped is limited by design to 50 km/h and has a combustion engine up to 50 cm3 or an electric motor up to 4 kW (Article 34(1)(g)), and a person aged 16 or over may ride one (Article 59(1)(a)). Class A1 covers a two-wheeled motorbike up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor up to 11 kW, and class A covers more (Article 57(1)(a) and (b)).

On the manufacturers' pages the Honda Vision is 110 cc, the Honda Wave Alpha 110 is 109.2 cc, the Yamaha Sirius RC 110 is 110.3 cc and the Honda Air Blade 125 is 125 cc, so a trial on any of them is above the moped class and needs a licence valid for it. Honda also lists an Air Blade 160, so ask which Air Blade variant you would receive; the 160 is above the A1 limit, and [the A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) covers what the class allows. A licence issued abroad or an international driving permit counts only on the conditions the law sets, and [the guide on whether tourists can ride legally]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers them.

### Helmet and passengers

Article 33(2) requires the rider and any passenger on a two-wheeled motorbike or moped to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the strap fastened correctly; [the helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the rule. Article 33(1) allows one passenger, and two only in the cases it lists, which include a child under 12, so a trial with a partner means one passenger.

### Papers and cover

Article 56(1) lists the documents a rider carries, among them the licence, the registration certificate and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate, and Article 56(3) lets papers integrated into an electronic identification account be shown through that account. For a rental, ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so cover for yourself is yours to arrange before the trial; [the guide to documents riders must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the full set.

## What to agree before you switch to a monthly term

A good trial ends with a conversation. These are the points worth settling, in writing, before a daily rental becomes a monthly one.

First, the bike, the rate, the deposit and the changeover:

- **The bike and the rate.** Confirm that the monthly bike is the one you rode, ask whether the days already paid count toward the month, and ask for its monthly rate in writing.
- **The deposit.** Ask what deposit applies to the monthly term, whether any deposit already paid carries over, and how and when it is dealt with at the end.
- **The changeover.** Ask when the daily rental ends and the monthly term starts, so that no hours fall between them. Separately, the published late-return charge is 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged, typically 150,000-200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle.

Then payment, upkeep and the end of the term:

- **Payment and upkeep.** Cash, bank transfer and QR payment are the accepted forms; ask how and when the monthly amount is due, and which consumables, such as tubes and tyres, fall to you.
- **Ending early.** There is no automatic refund entitlement for early return, so ask what would happen if the month had to end sooner; [ending a monthly rental early]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the question.

When the monthly term begins, take a fresh set of photographs if the bike is a different one. [The monthly rental overview]({{ '/articles/monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains how the month works.

## When a trial is not needed

If you already ride the model regularly, or you have rented the same bike from the shop before, a long trial adds little — [Choosing the Right Motorbike for a Month in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/choosing-motorbike-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is enough. Trials matter most for new riders and first-time visitors to Hanoi traffic.

## Quick answers about trying a bike

### Can a daily rental be converted into a monthly one?

Daily and monthly are both rental periods on offer, and longer-term rental is negotiable, but no conversion rule is published; ask the shop the questions in the section on what to agree before switching, or contact us to discuss it.

### What if the trial bike turns out to be wrong for me?

Say so while it is still a daily rental, and be specific: "the rear brake feels weak" is easier to act on than "it feels odd". No swap policy is published, so ask what is possible, including another bike.

### Does a good trial replace the handover inspection?

No. The trial shows whether the bike suits you, while the handover inspection shows whether this particular bike is in order and records its condition.

## The one-line version

A day or two of daily rental on your real routes is the honest test — then convert to monthly with the same shop, the same bike and no surprises, which depends on the answers you get to the questions above.
