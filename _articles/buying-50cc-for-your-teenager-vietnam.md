At sixteen, Vietnamese law opens exactly one motorised door: the moped. How a family walks through it decides whether the bike is a genuine step toward independence or a rolling gamble.

## What the law allows — and what it demands

A 50cc-or-under moped requires no licence, and the minimum rider age is 16. That is the entire licence story. What comes attached: helmet for rider and any passenger, all traffic rules at adult fine levels, and an age boundary that stays hard — a 16-year-old on anything of 50cc or more is fined 400,000–600,000 VND, and no school programme waives it. Our age-rules article states the law precisely.

## Choose the bike for the rider, not the rider for the bike

- **Both feet flat on the ground, seated.** Confidence starts with footing at stops.
- **Big wheels over small** where the routes are rough — an underbone handles Hanoi's broken patches better than a scooter-style moped.
- **Sound condition over style.** A dull, well-maintained machine that brakes straight beats a pretty one that doesn't. Our used-bike checklist is the tool — run it with your teenager watching, so they learn what "good" looks like.
- **Lights, brakes, horn working** on day one; a 50's ageing electrics are a safety system, not a convenience.

## Gear is part of the purchase

The helmet is legally required and the single most important item — certified, properly fitted, and worn every ride. Our teen gear article covers the rest: closed shoes, gloves, eye protection. Budget the gear with the bike or the gear will be skipped.

## Train before traffic

The school training programme covers rules and basic skills; extend it at home. Quiet-lot practice, then quiet streets, then supervised traffic — our teaching guide lays out the progression, and the first-week plan matters more than the bike chosen.

## Rules agreed before the keys

Write the family rules down before the handover: no passengers until competence is proven (and never an unhelmeted one — the fine and the physics both land on the household), no phone in the hand, no riding after dark at first, and the bike's checks — tyres, brakes, lights, oil — are the rider's weekly job, not the parents'. Our pre-ride checklist is written for exactly that habit.

## The honest framing

A 50cc at 16 is legal, common and manageable. What separates good outcomes from bad is not the engine — it is the gear, the training, and the rules practised while the rider is still listening.
