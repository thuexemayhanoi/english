A month or a semester in Hanoi is exactly the window where renting monthly beats daily rates, and a manual or semi-automatic underbone is the class most long-term visitors end up on. Here is the practical picture.

## Which class, honestly

- **Semi-automatic underbone (xe so)**: the default for daily Hanoi life. No clutch hand, carries a passenger, total parts availability, and it is the class our price-published fleet covers (Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius at 150,000 VND per day). For monthly pricing on any specific model, contact us — we confirm current availability and rates individually rather than publishing extrapolated figures.
- **Manual-clutch (xe con tay)**: worth it if you already ride confidently and want a sport underbone or something like a CT125-class machine. As a first-ever bike it makes a hard month harder.

The choosing guide covers the class decision in more depth, including the automatic-scooter alternative.

## What monthly rental changes

- **Deposits**: expect a deposit — typically in the 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 VND range depending on the vehicle and conditions. On longer rentals, confirm exactly what the deposit covers and what counts as chargeable damage.
- **Maintenance**: on a long rental, agree who services the bike. At Nguyen Tu, maintenance may include oil changes, chain adjustment, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection — and for high-mileage months the owner's practical guidance is periodic checks around every 1,500 km or so of use. That is owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval.
- **Consumables**: inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation — settle that understanding at pickup, not after the first puncture.
- **Insurance**: we do not provide motorbike insurance; arrange your own cover. The compulsory-insurance guide explains what the mandatory policy covers for vehicles in Vietnam.
- **Returns**: late returns are charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than six hours late may mean a full additional day. Plan the return handover like you planned the pickup.

## The learner case

If you cannot yet ride, rent monthly after learning, not during — or start the month on a semi-auto while you practise, then switch. Learning on a daily-rate bike and converting to monthly once confident is the sequence that works.

Monthly rental is the local-experience sweet spot: your own bike-equivalent for the length of a real stay. Contact us to confirm current availability of semi-automatic and manual models for your dates.
