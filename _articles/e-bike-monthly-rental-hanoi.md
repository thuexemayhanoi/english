Electric bikes suit a particular Hanoi life: short commutes, quiet streets, no fuel stops. Whether they work as a monthly rental depends on what the shop publishes and what it can confirm for you.

Short answer: no monthly rate is published for electric bikes, so price and availability for a month must be confirmed with Nguyen Tu before you plan around it. A month changes the questions. A day on an electric bike is a trial, while a month is a routine, so settle these points first: which legal class the machine falls into, the licence and age that follow from it, helmets, where and when the bike charges, how far it has to go on a charge, and what to ask the shop before you commit.

## What is published

The E-Bike has a published daily rate of 200,000 VND. There is no published weekly or monthly rate for the E-Bike, and no monthly rate can be assumed or extrapolated from the daily price. If you want an electric bike for a month, contact Nguyen Tu to confirm the current monthly price and availability.

Several other published terms apply to a long rental of any bike:

- Rental periods are daily, weekly and monthly, and longer terms are discussed directly with the shop.
- The deposit is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions.
- Payment is by cash, bank transfer or QR payment.
- Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so you arrange your own.
- A late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour.
- Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use.

## Where e-bikes fit

E-bikes make sense for riders whose daily loop is short and predictable — a few kilometres between home, work and shops — and for riders who prefer not to handle a petrol bike at all. For longer cross-city commutes, the charging time and range become real constraints; the general trade-offs are covered in [Best Motorbike for Commuting in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-commuting-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

Not every routine suits an electric bike for a month. The table runs from the easiest routine to the hardest, and the sections that follow take each condition in turn.

| Your month looks like | An electric rental is | Because |
|---|---|---|
| One fixed loop, with a socket you can use while you are at home | Straightforward | the bike can charge while you are at home and awake, once it has cooled |
| One fixed loop, but no socket at home or work | Possible with a plan | charging needs a shared or workplace socket, or a swap point if the model supports one |
| A passenger or a child on most days | Check the class and the load first | load shortens range, and the legal class sets the licence and age |
| Several unplanned cross-city trips a day | A weak match | a flat battery needs a socket, while a petrol bike needs only a pump |

## Legal note

Depending on classification, some electric two-wheelers sit in a lighter legal category than petrol motorbikes, but the exact rules depend on the machine's rated power. Do not assume an e-bike needs no licence without checking; the law and licences hub has the verified classifications.

Here is the short version from Law 36/2024/QH15; the full classification sits in [the electric bike licence guide]({{ '/articles/electric-bike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and the [law and licences hub]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}). The class depends on the machine's design speed as well as its rated power, and it separates three kinds of electric two-wheeler.

### Three classes, three sets of rules

| Class | What the law says | Licence and age |
|---|---|---|
| Pedal-assist electric bicycle (xe dap may, including xe dap dien) | Motor assistance that is cut when the rider stops pedalling or the bike reaches 25 km/h; listed among non-motorised vehicles (Article 34(2)(b)) | No driving licence |
| Electric moped (xe gan may chay dien) | Designed for no more than 50 km/h, with an electric motor of no more than 4 kW; excludes the pedal-assist bicycle (Article 34(1)(g)) | No driving licence; rider at least 16 (Article 59(1)(a)) |
| Electric motorcycle | Above those limits: more than 4 kW, or designed for more than 50 km/h | Class A1 up to and including 11 kW, class A above (Article 57(1)); rider at least 18 for either (Article 59(1)(b)) |

Both conditions in the moped definition must hold, so a machine of 4 kW or less that is designed to go faster than 50 km/h is not a moped. [The kW ladder guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-power-kw-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists models on each side of that line.

The label on a rental counter does not settle the class. Ask for the bike's rated motor power in kW, its design top speed, and whether it is a pedal-assist bicycle whose motor cuts out at 25 km/h or when you stop pedalling.

## Helmets for a daily routine

Every class above needs a helmet. On a pedal-assist bicycle, Article 31(3) requires the rider and any passenger to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly. On a moped or motorcycle, Article 33(2) sets the same duty for the rider and anyone carried.

Over a month the practical questions are fit and ownership, so ask whether a helmet is provided and whether it fits your head. [The e-bike helmet guide]({{ '/articles/e-bike-helmet-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains what to look for in one.

## Charging at home for a month

A month turns charging from an errand into a routine, and the routine needs a place, a time and a charger you can trust.

### What the fire police advise

The fire and rescue police of the Ministry of Public Security published advice for people who charge electric vehicles on 25 July 2023, after a number of electric-vehicle fires. It is general advice for electric vehicles, not guidance for any particular rental bike. Its main points are these.

Before and while charging:

- Follow the manufacturer's instructions and use a charger that matches the battery. Do not charge when the charger or the vehicle shows a fault, and replace a battery that swells or cracks.
- Wait about 20 minutes after riding so the battery has cooled, and do not charge for more than 8 hours continuously.
- Switch off the vehicle's power key while charging.

Where and when:

- Charge somewhere dry and airy. Indoors, avoid an enclosed spot: the advice gives 0°C to 35°C as the condition for charging in a room, unless insulation or cooling measures are taken.
- Keep the bike, battery and charger away from flammable goods and from fire or heat sources.
- Check on the charge regularly. Stop charging before you leave the house or go to sleep; the advice says never to charge overnight or when no adult is at home.

Read together, these points rule out plugging in at bedtime and unplugging in the morning. A routine built on the ministry's advice charges the bike while you are at home and awake, once it has cooled after the last ride.

That has a planning cost over a month: a window when you are home and awake has to exist in your week, and the 8-hour limit caps a single charge. Whether the battery can reach a useful level inside those limits is a question for the shop and for the manufacturer's manual for that model.

### What a rental adds

The same advice lists causes that matter more on a borrowed bike: batteries of unknown origin or ones that have been repaired or modified, chargers that do not match the battery, extras such as alarms, horns or brighter lights fitted beyond the manufacturer's design, and overloading. In practice that means using only a charger that the manufacturer or the shop confirms matches the battery, fitting nothing to the bike's wiring, and telling the shop about any swelling or fault instead of trying to repair it. Ask what the rental agreement says if the charger fails or goes missing, and who bears the cost of a damaged battery.

### Apartments and shared buildings

If you live in an apartment block, the building has a say. The Ministry of Construction's consolidated text of QCVN 04:2021/BXD, the national technical regulation on apartment buildings (version of 30 September 2026), adds fire-safety requirements for electric-vehicle charging areas through an amendment that takes effect on 15 December 2026. It has separate provisions for newly built apartment buildings and for existing ones; for an existing block they take its actual space and infrastructure into account, so what applies to yours is a question for its management.

Inside a building, a charging area is to be a separate fire compartment with an automatic fire alarm, camera monitoring connected to a control room staffed round the clock, and an automatic extinguishing system. Outside, it is to keep the fire-separation distance from neighbouring buildings, never less than 3 m, which can drop to no less than 1 m where a solid non-combustible wall or partition at least 2 m high stands between them.

Those are technical requirements for how a building lays out its charging areas, not rules written for a tenant's own socket. For a renter the practical step is to ask the management whether charging is allowed and where, and to note the answer; [the guide to charging from a rented apartment]({{ '/articles/charging-electric-motorbike-apartment-rental/' | relative_url }}) covers that conversation. If there is no socket you can use, [the guide to where to charge in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/where-to-charge-electric-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the other options, such as swap cabinets for models that support them.

## Planning range around a daily routine

Range is a figure from a test, not a promise about your route. [The real-world range guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-range-real-world-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains why claims shrink; this is the worksheet version for a month.

1. Write the loop down: home to work and back plus regular errands, as one daily distance, noting any climb, bridge or fast road.
2. Ask the shop for the manufacturer's claimed range for the exact bike and the condition it was measured in. A claim without its condition cannot be compared with your route.
3. Compare the claim with your daily distance and leave a margin. The stated condition is the best case; speed, temperature, terrain and load all change the result.
4. In the first week, note the battery display when you leave and when you arrive on your usual loop, and plan the month from that figure.
5. Treat the charge as a fixed appointment inside the limits above.

The same kind of figure comes with very different conditions, which is why step 2 matters. These are the manufacturers' own statements, quoted to show the difference; they say nothing about which models any shop has.

| Honda ICON e: | About 71 km | No speed or load stated; ECO mode adds about 15% over standard mode |

## Rain, heat and battery care

The ministry's advice names a damp charging socket among the causes of short circuits and fires, so after a wet ride dry the charging port and the plug before connecting. Do not charge a bike that has stood in flood water until the shop has checked it. In hot weather, let the bike cool before it goes on charge and park it out of direct sun where you can. [The guide to riding an electric motorbike in Hanoi rain]({{ '/articles/riding-electric-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) covers waterproof ratings and wet-road technique.

## Set-up questions to ask

Before committing to a month on an e-bike: where will you charge it, does your building allow charging, what does the range cover on your route, and what happens if the battery or charger fails mid-term? Charging arrangements at home are the question renters most often overlook.

Put these to the shop by message or call before the month starts, and keep the answers in writing.

**The machine**

- Which class is this exact bike (pedal-assist bicycle, moped or electric motorcycle), and what are its rated power in kW and its design top speed?
- What range does the manufacturer claim for it, and under what condition?
- Is the battery removable, so it can be charged indoors, or must the bike be plugged in where it parks?

**Battery and charger**

- Which charger comes with the bike, and what happens if it fails or goes missing?
- If the battery fails mid-term, is there a replacement bike, or does the rental pause?
- Which parts count as consumables that may be the renter's responsibility, and how is damage to the battery or charger handled?

**Papers and money**

- What is the deposit for this bike, and what conditions does it depend on?
- If the bike is a registered motor vehicle (a moped or motorcycle), how will its registration certificate and compulsory insurance certificate be shown at a stop?
- What should the cover you arrange yourself include for an electric bike? [The electric insurance guide]({{ '/articles/do-electric-motorbikes-need-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the place to start.

**Logistics**

- Can the bike be delivered and collected? Delivery and pickup in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement.
- Which areas is it meant for, given that support is primarily for Hanoi and local use?
- How does the shop handle ending early or extending? Early return carries no automatic refund for unused time.

## Short answers to common questions

### Do I need a licence for a monthly electric rental?

It depends on the class, not on the word e-bike. A pedal-assist bicycle needs no driving licence. A moped, meaning 4 kW or less and designed for no more than 50 km/h, needs none either, with a minimum age of 16. Anything above those limits needs class A1 or A, and the minimum age is 18 for both.

### Can I charge the bike in my apartment?

Only if the building allows it, and the fire-police advice above is worth following. Ask the management in writing.

### What does a monthly electric rental cost?

Ask the shop for a quote for the exact machine and period, together with the deposit for it. The [contact page]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) has the phone, WhatsApp and Zalo number, and the shop is open 09:00–21:00 daily.

### What if the battery or charger fails mid-month?

The terms are not published, so ask before you sign who you contact, whether a replacement bike is possible and who bears the cost. Stop charging at once if the charger or the bike shows a fault, as the fire police advise.
