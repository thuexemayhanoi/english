You do not need to be a mechanic to know something is wrong - you need to notice that something changed, and act on it. Here is the renter's triage. Soft brakes, steering that fights you, a rear wheel that locks and a fuel leak mean stop and call; a new noise, smell or hesitation is a same-day report, sent before you ride on.

## Stop-now symptoms

These mean park the bike and call, not ride it to the shop gently:

- Brakes that pull to the bar or floor without biting: brakes are the one system with no "keep going" option.
- A rear wheel that locks or surges on its own: chain derailment risk - [the chain article]({{ '/articles/loose-chain-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers it.
- Steering that suddenly fights you, or a wobble at speed that was not there yesterday: it could be a wheel, bearing or frame problem, or a tyre that has gone soft, and only a mechanic can say which.
- Fuel smell plus visible leak: fire risk beats every plan for the day.

Stopping means pulling over away from the traffic line, switching the engine off, keeping the key with you and calling the shop, not riding on gently to save time.

### Other signs that belong on this list

Each of these can also fail while you ride, so treat them the same way:

- Grinding that you feel at the brake lever or pedal, or a deep metallic knock when you accelerate; [the guide to diagnosing motorbike noises]({{ '/articles/diagnosing-motorbike-noises-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) separates harmless ticks from sounds that mean stop.
- A sharp, bitter smell like hot plastic or wiring, or smoke from anywhere on the bike; [the guide to burning smells]({{ '/articles/burning-smell-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) lists the common ones.
- An engine that keeps getting hotter or losing power on an open road, not only in a standstill jam.
- A chain that has come off, hangs loose or drags.
- A new pull, wobble or noise after a fall, a kerb strike or a hard pothole hit: treat it as damage until a mechanic has looked, and say what happened when you report it.

Once the bike will not run or will not move, it is a breakdown, and [what happens if a rental motorbike breaks down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the call.

## Report-soon symptoms

Ride gently to the shop, same day:

- Any new noise - a clicking, grinding, whining that arrived.
- A misfire, hesitation or a flat spot that developed.
- A smell of burning, oil spots under the parked bike, an engine that suddenly runs hot.
- Lights, horn or indicators intermittently dead - electrical faults that matter more than they look, because each one is a signal the law sets rules for.

Message the shop before you set off and let it decide whether a gentle ride in is acceptable. A symptom here moves up to the stop-now list if it gets worse while you ride or arrives with grinding at the brake lever, smoke, a sharp electrical smell or steady loss of power.

### Why a flickering light or horn is not cosmetic

Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the front light to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather that limits visibility (Article 20(1)). It requires a turn signal before a turn, used continuously through the turn (Article 15(2)), and allows the horn only to warn of an unsafe situation or to signal an overtake (Article 21(1)).

The practical point is separate. A headlight that cuts out on a wet evening, or an indicator that works on one side only, leaves you unable to do what those articles describe. Report it the same day and ask the shop before anyone replaces a part. ## Stop or report: the symptoms side by side

The right-hand column lists what a mechanic may check, not diagnoses: from the saddle, several causes feel the same.

| What you notice | Which list | What a mechanic may check |
|---|---|---|
| Brake lever or pedal travels to the grip or floor | Stop now | Worn pads or shoes, a cable that needs adjusting, air in a hydraulic line, a fluid leak |
| Rear wheel locks or surges | Stop now | A slack or jumping chain, a rear brake that drags |
| Wobble, pull or heavy steering | Stop now | A soft tyre, a bent rim after a pothole, loose wheel or steering bearings |
| Petrol smell with a wet patch | Stop now | A fuel line connection, tap or tank seal, or a carburettor overflow on bikes that have one |
| New noise | Report soon, stop if grinding at the brakes or a deep knock | A chain, brake pads, a wheel bearing, a loose panel or an engine part |
| Hesitation or misfire | Report soon | The fuel supply or the ignition parts, which a mechanic separates by testing |
| Oil spots, burning smell, hot engine | Report soon, stop if the smell is sharp and electrical or there is smoke | A weeping gasket or a leak dripping on the hot exhaust, low oil, a dragging brake |
| Lights, horn or indicators cutting out | Report soon | A bulb, a loose connector, a switch or the battery |

## The renter's actual skill: describing

Mechanics diagnose from change. Useful: "a ticking noise started this morning, only when moving, faster with speed." Not useful: "it feels weird." Note when it happens - moving or stopped, cold or hot, accelerating or cruising - and you have done your half.

### What else helps a mechanic

Add the rest of the picture when you can:

- Where it seems to come from: front or rear, left or right, under the seat.
- What changes it: speed, braking, bumps, turning, a passenger or luggage.
- Since when: this morning, after the last fuel stop, after rain, after a pothole.
- The odometer reading and any warning light that is on.
- What happened to the bike: a fall, a kerb strike, a deep puddle. Say it plainly, because a mechanic reads a symptom better with the whole story.

The handover walk-round is where you learn what normal looks like for this bike. [The rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) covers what to check and photograph before you ride away.

### An illustrative message

The details in brackets are yours to fill in.

> Hello, this is [name]. I have the [model], plate [number]. Since this morning the rear brake pedal goes further down than yesterday and the bike takes longer to stop. I have stopped at [place] and I am sending a map pin, a photo of the dashboard and a photo of the rear wheel. Should I leave the bike here or bring it in?

## What to photograph and film

Take pictures once the bike is stopped and safe, never while riding. They turn a feeling into something the shop can check.

1. The dashboard: any warning light that is lit, the odometer and the fuel gauge.
2. The part the symptom seems to come from, from a short distance. Look, do not touch: the exhaust can stay hot after riding.
3. The ground under the bike, with a coin beside any drip or wet patch for scale.
4. Both tyres from the side, showing tread and sidewall.
5. A short clip of the noise, filmed with the bike stationary and the engine at idle. If it only happens on the move, write down when it happens instead.
6. Your location, as a map pin.

Do not rev the engine or test the brakes at speed to demonstrate a fault, and keep smokers and open flames away from a bike that smells of petrol. ## How to report it

Stop somewhere safe before you take out the phone, then work through three steps.

1. Message the shop: +84 942 467 674, by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo. Its published hours are 09:00-21:00 daily.
2. Send the description, the photographs and the map pin, and ask what the shop wants done.
3. Follow the answer and keep the thread: a dated message that records what you were told is your record.

### Questions worth putting to the shop

- Should I keep riding, or leave the bike where it is?
- Where should I bring it, and when?
- Is anything charged for the check or the work?

### What not to do

- Do not ride on to see whether it clears.
- Do not top up oil or fluids, tension the chain or adjust the brakes yourself. Ask the shop what the bike needs.
- Do not hand the bike to a roadside workshop before you have called; [can you use outside repair shops on a rental motorbike]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains which jobs can be reasonable.

## Is it the bike or the conditions?

Some changes of feel come from the situation, not from a fault. Put the situation back and see whether the feel goes back with it; if it does not, report it.

- After rain or standing water: brakes feel weaker until they dry. [Riding a rental motorbike in Hanoi rain]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) covers drying them with a few gentle applications.
- With a passenger or luggage: the bike steers and stops differently, and [renting a motorbike as a couple in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-couple-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers what two-up changes. A change at the same load as yesterday is not the load.
- On a bike you do not know: each model has its own normal, so ask the shop what a healthy bike of that model sounds and feels like.
- After a fill-up: a hesitation soon after fuelling is worth reporting with the station and the time. Which petrol suits a small rental bike is in [the rental motorbike fuel guide]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-fuel-what-petrol/' | relative_url }}).

## Why renters under-report

Because they fear being charged. Know the split: the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, and a symptom reported early gives that maintenance the chance to catch it.

What the renter may owe is different: inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, and damage from a crash or neglect (the flat battery from leaving the ignition on) is something to ask the shop about before you ride.

A noise reported on day two gives the shop the chance to treat it as maintenance; the same noise ignored until the gearbox lets go is a conversation nobody enjoys. A report does not settle who pays for a particular repair, so ask before work starts. On longer or heavier rentals the owner's practical guidance is a periodic check at around 1,500 km or more of use; that is owner guidance, not a manufacturer interval, and [high-mileage rental riding]({{ '/articles/high-mileage-rental-motorbike-inspection/' | relative_url }}) explains it. A symptom is a reason to bring the bike in sooner, whatever the odometer says.

## Questions renters ask

### Can I keep riding if the problem only appears sometimes?

An intermittent fault is still a fault. If the symptom is on the stop-now list, an occasional appearance is not a reason to carry on. If it is on the report-soon list, note when it appears and let the shop decide whether a gentle ride in is acceptable.

### Is every new noise serious?

No. A loose panel or heat shield can rattle like something worse, and a renter cannot always tell them apart by ear. That is why the job is to describe the change and not to diagnose it, the point [the loose chain guide]({{ '/articles/loose-chain-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) makes about clunks.

### What if it happens after the shop has closed, or far from Hanoi?

The shop's support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, and no rescue or response time is promised. Make the bike safe, send the message so it is waiting, and ask what the shop wants you to do when it can answer. If you plan to leave the city, settle that first: [can you take a rental motorbike outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers it.

### What if the shop cannot fix it quickly?

Whether another bike is possible depends on current availability, so contact the shop to confirm it. Do not assume a replacement will be on hand.

## The habit

When something feels different, it is different. Anything on the stop-now list means park and call; for anything on the report-soon list, once the shop agrees: ride it back, describe the change. Report early: a message costs nothing, and a bike that fails on the road costs everyone more.
