You do not need to be a mechanic to know something is wrong - you need to notice that something changed, and act on it. Here is the renter's triage.

## Stop-now symptoms

These mean park the bike and call, not ride it to the shop gently:

- Brakes that pull to the bar or floor without biting: brakes are the one system with no "keep going" option.
- A rear wheel that locks or surges on its own: chain derailment risk - the chain article covers it.
- Steering that suddenly fights you, or a wobble at speed that was not there yesterday: wheel, bearing or frame problem.
- Fuel smell plus visible leak: fire risk beats every plan for the day.

## Report-soon symptoms

Ride gently to the shop, same day:

- Any new noise - a clicking, grinding, whining that arrived.
- A misfire, hesitation or a flat spot that developed.
- A smell of burning, oil spots under the parked bike, an engine that suddenly runs hot.
- Lights, horn or indicators intermittently dead - electrical greys that police can also fine you for (equipment violations).

## The renter's actual skill: describing

Mechanics diagnose from change. Useful: "a ticking noise started this morning, only when moving, faster with speed." Not useful: "it feels weird." Note when it happens - moving or stopped, cold or hot, accelerating or cruising - and you have done your half.

## Why renters under-report

Because they fear being charged. Know the split: maintenance is the owner's side of a Nguyen Tu rental - oil, chain, tyre pressure, inspection are the shop's job, and reporting a symptom early is how that system works. What the renter owns is crashes, neglect (the flat battery from leaving the ignition on) and consumables the agreement assigns. A noise reported on day two is maintenance; the same noise ignored until the gearbox lets go is a conversation nobody enjoys.

## The habit

When something feels different, it is different. Ride it back, describe the change. The shop would rather see a healthy bike for nothing than a destroyed one for a deposit.
