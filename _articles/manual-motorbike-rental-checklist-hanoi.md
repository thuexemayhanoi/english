Ten minutes of checks at pickup saves a day of trouble later. Work down this list before the bike leaves the street.

## Paperwork first

Confirm you have a licence that covers the bike and that the rental arrangement is clear: the deposit at Nguyen Tu is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle, late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than six hours late may mean a full additional rental day. Insurance is not provided — riders arrange their own. Full requirements are in the rental requirements guide.

## The machine

- **Clutch:** pull the lever — smooth, no binding, and no free play that lets the bike creep in gear with the lever in. The lever adjustment guide covers what correct feels like.
- **Gears:** click through the full pattern with the engine off, then find first and neutral again with it running.
- **Brakes:** squeeze the front and press the rear while pushing the bike; both should bite before half-travel.
- **Chain:** look for obvious rust, a chain sagging like a necklace or one drum-tight, and listen for slap on the test ride.
- **Tyres:** tread present, sidewalls without cracks, pressures firm (press the seat and bars hard — a soft bike squats).
- **Lights and horn:** headlight high/low, brake light front and rear, indicators if fitted, and a horn that works — in Hanoi traffic the horn is a safety tool.
- **Fuel:** agree the fuel level at pickup in words — full, half, empty — not a shrug. The rental fuel policy guide covers the common arrangements.
- **Test ride:** two slow laps of the street, testing both brakes and every gear, before you commit.

## Photos

Photograph all four sides, the odometer, and any existing scratch. Timestamped photos settle every disagreement about damage instantly.

## What to skip

Do not adjust or repair a rental bike yourself — report the issue to the provider instead, and for anything mechanical, the basic maintenance knowledge guide helps you describe the symptom correctly rather than attempt the fix.
