Semi-automatic underbones like the Honda Wave, Blade, RSX, Future and Yamaha Sirius have no clutch lever, yet they do have a real gearbox. Learning to shift one smoothly takes about ten minutes of practice.

## The pattern

The gear lever sits by your left foot, and the pattern is rotary: neutral at the bottom, then first, second, third, fourth as you click up. Click down at any time and you drop one gear. The classic trick for riders who lose count: click down repeatedly — the pattern cycles, so a few clicks always finds a low gear quickly.

## Shifting up

1. Close the throttle briefly.
2. Click the lever up one firm stroke.
3. Re-open the throttle.

The moment of closed throttle unloads the gears, which is what lets them engage without a clutch. A shift made with the throttle still open clunks; a shift made with a longer pause settles in almost silently.

## Shifting down

Same idea, other direction: brief throttle close, click down, throttle back. Downshift as you slow so the engine is never asked to pull from too high a gear — a lugging, knocking engine means you should have clicked down earlier.

## Stopping

Click down through the gears while braking, arriving in first (or neutral) as you stop. Many riders downshift to neutral during the last metres of a slow roll — semi-automatics allow this comfortably.
