The Super Cub and the Sirius are the two most famous names in Vietnamese motorcycling - one for being the world's best-selling design, the other for being everywhere in Vietnam at the lowest possible price. Honda and Yamaha publish full specifications for the current versions (retrieved 23 September 2026).

## The core difference

The Sirius RC 110 is a 110.3 cc air-cooled semi-automatic with a four-speed rotary gearbox, at 21,993,000 VND for the alloy-wheel RC version. The Super Cub C125 is a 123.9 cc air-cooled heritage machine with a four-speed gearbox, at 88,353,813 VND. The price gap is the story: the Cub costs four times as much and does not go four times as fast.

## The verified numbers

| Spec | Sirius RC 110 | Super Cub C125 |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 110.3 cc, SOHC, air-cooled | 123.9 cc, air-cooled |
| Max power | 5.9 kW @ 8,000 rpm | 6.87 kW @ 7,500 rpm |
| Max torque | 9.0 Nm @ 5,000 rpm | 10.15 Nm @ 5,000 rpm |
| Fuel tank | 4.2 L | 3.7 L |
| Claimed economy | 2.08 L/100 km | 1.55 L/100 km |
| Kerb weight | 100 kg wet | 109 kg |
| List price | 21,993,000 VND (RC) | 88,353,813 VND |

Real-world fuel economy varies with speed, load and conditions. Neither manufacturer publishes the seat height for these models on the retrieved pages.

## What it means on the road

As transport the Sirius is unbeatable: cheapest to buy, simple to fix, 4.2-litre tank, and the RC version adds a front hydraulic disc over the drum version. The Super Cub counters with genuinely better engineering numbers - more power, more torque at the same 5,000 rpm, and a claimed 1.55 L/100 km that is one of the best figures in the verified range.

But nobody buys a Super Cub for the spec sheet. They buy the silhouette, the finish and the history. That is a legitimate reason - just be clear it is styling, not speed, that the extra 66 million dong buys.

## Which one for you?

If a motorbike is a tool, buy the Sirius and bank the difference. If the bike is partly a statement and you keep it for years, the Super Cub is the only underbone in Vietnam that looks like its own poster.
