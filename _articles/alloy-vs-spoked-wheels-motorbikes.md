Look at any Vietnamese underbone model range and the version ladder tells a story: the cheapest bikes wear spoked wheels, the sport versions wear cast alloy. Both wheel types are manufacturer-published options on the same bikes - Honda's Blade page lists the Sport version with alloy wheels and the others with spoked wheels, and Yamaha's Sirius range spans drum, disc and alloy-wheel RC versions (all retrieved 23 September 2026). Here is what the difference means.

## Construction

Spoked wheels use a steel rim laced to the hub with dozens of individual spokes, each under tension, typically sealed with a rubber band so a tube-type tyre can hold air. Cast alloy wheels are single-piece metal castings, usually paired with tubeless tyres where the design allows.

## Tube tyres versus tubeless

This is the practical difference on Vietnamese roads. The Sirius page is explicit: its wheels run 70/90-17 and 80/90-17 tyres with inner tubes. Tube-type tyres can deflate fast when punctured and need a repair shop with the right tube; tubeless tyres usually lose pressure slowly and can often be plugged roadside. Our flat tyre repair guide covers the roadside reality, and the tyre replacement guide covers what to fit when the time comes.

## Strength and repair culture

Spoked wheels flex and can absorb sharp impacts - and when a rim is bent or a spoke breaks, any Vietnamese roadside shop can true or relace it cheaply. Alloy wheels are more rigid, resist going out of true at all, but a hard pothole strike can damage a casting that cannot be trued back - replacement is the usual fix.

## Weight and looks

Alloy castings on small bikes are typically a little heavier than a well-built spoked set, but the versions that wear them (Blade Sport, Sirius RC) are sold on appearance and on pairing the wheels with a front disc brake - the alloy version is usually the braking upgrade as well.

## What to choose

Daily commuting on bad roads far from cities: spoked wheels and tubes are the pragmatic choice. City riding where you value the disc brake, styling and slightly lower puncture drama: the alloy-wheel versions.
