Commuting in Hanoi means stop-start traffic, tight filtering, kerb-height parking and a lot of time below 40 km/h. For that job the semi-automatic underbone - foot-shifted, no clutch lever - is the local default, and the manufacturers' own data (retrieved 23 September 2026) makes the ranking unusually objective.

## The verified shortlist

- Honda Wave Alpha 110 - 96 kg, 1.72 L/100 km, 770 mm seat, 18,841,091 VND list. The lightest and cheapest; the definition of a Hanoi workhorse.
- Yamaha Sirius RC 110 - 100 kg wet, 2.08 L/100 km, front disc on the RC version, 21,993,000 VND list.
- Honda Blade - 98-99 kg, 1.85 L/100 km, disc-braked Sport version available, 21,943,637 VND list.
- Honda Wave RSX - 98 kg, 1.56 L/100 km with PGM-FI, U-box that fits a half helmet, 25,664,727 VND list.
- Honda Future 125 FI - 104 kg, 1.47 L/100 km, 756 mm seat, from 30,622,909 VND list. The strongest and thriftiest commuter.

## What matters in Hanoi traffic

Weight first: every kilogram counts when pushing the bike into alley parking spots. Width second: the Sirius (665 mm wide) and the Wave Alpha (689 mm) filter through gaps that a scooter's bodywork cannot. Economy third: at Hanoi's distances, the gap between 1.47 and 2.08 L/100 km is small per week but real per year.

## What does not matter

Top speed and outright power. In gridlock, the Wave Alpha's 6.12 kW is indistinguishable from a Future's 6.83 kW. Do not pay for acceleration you cannot use.
