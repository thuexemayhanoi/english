Roundabouts and big junctions are where Vietnamese traffic rules meet Vietnamese traffic behaviour — and they are where most new foreign riders have their first close call. The law sets out signals and priority — traffic-light-rules-vietnam covers it — but junction craft is practical knowledge.

## Roundabouts: yield, then flow

Approach ready to stop. In practice, traffic already on the roundabout usually has priority, but entry speed and eye contact matter more than theory. Pick your lane early: outer lane for the first exit, inner lanes for later exits — and signal your exit. Locals treat roundabouts as merges rather than strict priority zones, so move with the flow rather than asserting rights.

## The invisible lane

At big junctions, expect motorbikes to gather in front of cars at the stop line, filling the shaded box area — then flow away from the line in all directions when the light changes. That first ten seconds after green is the risk window: bikes fan out across the junction, and turning cars cross through them. Wait a beat, watch the front wheels around you, and exit through a chosen gap, not the middle of the swarm.

## Turning right

Right turns are their own game — including the unusual signal-governed right-on-red case covered in right-turn-on-red-vietnam. Always check left before a right turn: bikes overtake on both sides here, and the one coming up your inside is the one you cannot see.

## Junctions without lights

The working rule is big road first, slow everything else: make eye contact, move predictably, and never accelerate through a gap a bus could also take. Defensive-riding-motorbike-vietnam generalises the mindset.

## The one-line version

Yield on entry, pick your lane before the roundabout, give the first ten seconds of green to the swarm, and always check left when turning right.
