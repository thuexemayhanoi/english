Spec sheets say 124.9 cc, 155.1 cc, 156.9 cc - never the round number on the badge. The reasons are regulatory thresholds, engineering reality and marketing convention, and understanding them makes you harder to mislead.

## What displacement is

Engine displacement is the swept volume of the cylinders - bore area times stroke times cylinder count. It is fixed geometry, measured to a decimal in engineering documents, and it never lands exactly on a round number because bore and stroke are chosen for packaging and performance, not for the badge.

## Why the decimals sit just under thresholds

Manufacturers deliberately size engines just under regulatory class limits - a licence class, tax bracket or homologation category - so the bike qualifies for the friendlier rule. The verified Vietnamese examples (retrieved 23 September 2026): 124.8 cc for the Honda SH Mode 125 and 124.9 cc for the Yamaha Janus, FreeGo, Gear and Latte 125 - all just under the 125 cc boundary; 155.1 cc for the Yamaha NVX, NMAX and Lexi 155; and 156.9 cc for the Honda SH160i, Vario 160 and Air Blade 160.

## Why this matters for licences

Vietnamese licence classes are set by engine capacity, and the boundary cases are exactly where those decimals matter - a 124.9 cc bike and a 125.0 cc bike can fall on different sides of a rule. Our licence classes guide covers what each licence lets you ride, and our A1 guide covers the class most 125s sit within. Never rely on the badge - the registration papers carry the true figure, and our documents guide covers what to carry.

## And for buyers

Displacement correlates with, but does not determine, performance - a two-valve 124.9 and a four-valve 124.9 publish very different outputs (see our valve count and power explainers). Compare verified output figures, not capacity, when ranking models - our spec-sheet guide shows where each number lives.
