Yes. Foreigners [rent motorbikes in Hanoi]({{ '/topics/rental/' | relative_url }}) every day, and no local licence, residence status or Vietnamese bank account is needed to do it. The catch is one sentence long: renting is easy, riding legally depends on your licence.

Hanoi motorbike rental for foreigners comes down to two separate questions: what the rental needs, and what the law needs before you ride. This guide covers foreigners of every kind, from a visitor staying a week to a resident of several years. If your trip is short, [the guide for short-stay tourists]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) answers the same question from the visitor's side, and [the step-by-step rental overview]({{ '/articles/how-to-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts the whole sequence in order.

## The rental side

A passport for the contract, a [cash deposit]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) - typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND - and the [rental price]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). That is the whole transaction. Cash, bank transfer and QR payment all work. Shops in Hanoi are used to foreign customers and the process takes minutes.

### What a foreign renter brings

The [documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and the [rental requirements guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) set out the paperwork in full. Your passport is the identity document for the rental agreement, and a phone number lets the shop reach you about the bike. A residence card, work permit or student visa is not on the list, because renting is not an immigration matter.

The deposit depends on the vehicle and the conditions, so ask for the figure that applies to the bike you want instead of assuming the range above. Rental periods run by the day, the week or the month, and longer-term rental is negotiable with the shop.

### What the rental does not cover

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own. Read your travel or medical policy for motorbike exclusions and licence conditions before you ride. As [the guide to legal riding for tourists]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains, an invalid licence can invalidate an insurance claim after a crash and shift liability onto the rider, whoever caused it, and a rental agreement may also hold the renter responsible.

## The legal side

Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) lists the licences valid on Vietnamese territory. For a foreign rider there are three practical routes:

- A Vietnamese licence (under the 2025 law the [A1 class covers bikes up to 125 cc]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}), which includes most rental scooters; A1 licences issued before 2025 keep their old scope, up to but not including 175 cc).
- A home-country motorcycle licence plus an IDP issued under the 1968 Vienna convention. Your country must be a 1968-convention state - [which countries' IDPs work in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists them.
- A foreign licence formally exchanged for a Vietnamese one, which longer-term residents can do.

Article 57 words its own list a little differently, as [the tourist-riding guide]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets it out: a Vietnamese licence (by test or by exchange), an IDP or national licence issued by a member of the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic and appropriate to the vehicle, and a licence from a country or territory with which Vietnam has a mutual-recognition treaty or agreement. The three routes above fall under the first two items. The third item depends on a treaty or agreement between Vietnam and the issuing country, so confirm it from an official source before you rely on it.

If you hold none of these, riding a rental bike is riding unlicensed: a 2-4 million VND fine up to 125 cc, 6-8 million VND above, and the bike can be held by police. Both bands come from Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which puts the lower band on a motorbike of up to 125 cc or an electric machine of up to 11 kW and the higher band on anything bigger. The same article covers a licence that does not fit the bike you are riding, so matching the bike to the licence matters as much as holding one. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the detail.

### Which situation are you in?

The table covers a standard rental scooter of up to 125 cc.

| What you hold | Legal on a scooter up to 125 cc? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A Vietnamese A1 or A licence | Yes, within its class | Carry it; A1 reaches 125 cc |
| A national motorcycle licence plus a 1968-convention IDP with a motorcycle category | Yes, if the licence covers that size of bike and both are carried | Carry both originals |
| An IDP, but the national licence left at home | No | Carry the national licence too |
| A national licence from outside the 1968 convention, or a 1949-convention IDP | Not on those documents alone | Exchange the licence once you hold a qualifying residence card, or use the licence-free class |
| No motorcycle licence anywhere (a car licence alone does not count) | No | Use the licence-free class or, as a resident, take the Vietnamese A1 test |

Before you fly, check four things: the IDP refers to the 1968 Vienna Convention, it shows a motorcycle category, your national licence itself covers motorcycles, and you will carry the originals of both, because photocopies and photos are not substitutes. Get the permit before you travel. [The IDP rules guide]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) explains why the permit is a translation of your licence and not a licence of its own.

## What about your home car licence?

A car licence does not cover motorcycles in Vietnam, and neither does a 1949-convention or non-convention permit. Article 57 sets car classes and motorbike classes apart, so [a car licence does not cover even a 125 cc scooter]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), whether it is Vietnamese or foreign. An IDP does not change that: it translates the categories your national licence holds, so a car-only licence behind an IDP gives car categories in Vietnam and no motorbike category. Check the [licence guides]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}) before you fly if riding is central to your trip.

## Short stay or settled in

Which document does the work depends on how long you stay. [The long-term expat guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) puts it in one line: short stays run on an IDP, long stays run on a Vietnamese licence.

### Riding on an IDP

Both documents carry their own expiry dates, so compare them with the day you plan to return the bike. Article 18 of the Decree puts a licence that is no longer valid in the same fine band as having none, which makes an expiry date in the middle of a long rental a real problem rather than a formality.

### Exchanging a licence once you live here

Under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA a foreigner can exchange a valid foreign national licence for a Vietnamese one. The dossier includes a certified Vietnamese translation of the licence and a certified copy of a diplomatic or service identity card, or of a temporary or permanent residence card valid for three months or more. It goes to the Traffic Police Department or to a commune-level police point that accepts applications. An IDP cannot be exchanged, and neither can a foreign licence that is out of date, so the national licence has to be in order first.

Residents with no motorcycle entitlement at home take the Vietnamese A1 test instead, which [the expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) describes. Moving here rather than visiting? See the guides for [expats moving to Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-expats-moving-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), [English teachers]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-english-teachers-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [digital nomads]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-digital-nomads-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## No motorcycle licence: the 50cc and e-bike options

A licence-free 50 cc or e-bike rental is a legal, low-speed way to see the city if your paperwork does not reach further. At 50 cc or less no licence is needed at all, which is why [licence-free 50 cc bikes]({{ '/articles/renting-50cc-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [e-bikes]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-electric-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) are options for visitors who have no motorcycle licence at home.

The law calls this class mopeds: two- or three-wheel vehicles designed for no more than 50 km/h, with an engine of 50 cc or less or an electric motor of 4 kW or less. [Foreign nationality changes nothing here]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-ride-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}): the rider must be at least 16, understand the traffic rules, have basic handling skills, wear a standard-compliant helmet and follow every traffic rule. A crash on a moped brings the same liability, insurance and paperwork questions as any other crash, and fines follow the same schedule as for motorbikes.

For electric two-wheelers the line is power. At 4 kW or less, within the moped definition above, no driving licence is required; above 4 kW a licence is required, class A1 up to 11 kW and class A above that. Pedal-assist electric bicycles are a separate, non-motorised category. Ask for the rated power of any electric machine before you book, because it decides which licence class you are riding in.

## Misunderstandings that catch foreign renters

- **"The shop rented to me, so I am covered."** No rental document makes you legal to ride: only a licence Vietnam recognises does, as [the documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts it. Renting and riding are separate matters.
- **"An IDP is my licence."** It is a translation of the national licence behind it. Article 18 of the Decree puts a 1968-convention IDP carried without the matching national licence in the same bands as riding with no licence.
- **"A photo of my licence will do."** Photocopies and photos of documents are not substitutes for the originals.
- **"Everyone rides without a licence."** That is not a legal argument: the fine bands and the insurance consequences above apply to the rider, whatever other people do.

## Common questions

### What should I do if the police stop me?

Stay calm and hand over the documents the officer asks for: the originals of your licence and, if you ride on one, your IDP. If your documents are not in order, a fine is the normal outcome, and the serious consequences arrive after an accident, as the [international driving permit rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) point out.

### Can I take a rental bike outside Hanoi?

Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so say where you plan to ride when you book and ask before you set off. Raise any trip beyond the city before you rent, not after you have left.

### Is there a minimum age for foreign riders?

The law sets 16 for the licence-free moped class and 18 for a licence such as A1, which is the class a standard rental scooter needs, so a rider under 18 cannot legally ride one. A shop's own conditions for young renters are something to ask about, not assume.

### Can I rent if I have never ridden a scooter?

A shop can decline a rental it judges risky, so say honestly how much you have ridden when you enquire. A licence says you may ride; it does not say you are ready for Hanoi traffic, and [the first-time rental walkthrough]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first hour and the first day.
