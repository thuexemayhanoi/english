Yes. Foreigners [rent motorbikes in Hanoi]({{ '/topics/rental/' | relative_url }}) every day, and no local licence, residence status or Vietnamese bank account is needed to do it. The catch is one sentence long: renting is easy, riding legally depends on your licence.

Hanoi motorbike rental for foreigners comes down to two separate questions: what the rental needs, and what the law needs before you ride. This guide covers foreigners of every kind, from a visitor staying a week to a resident of several years. If your trip is short, [the guide for short-stay tourists]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) answers the same question from the visitor's side, and [the step-by-step rental overview]({{ '/articles/how-to-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts the whole sequence in order.

## The rental side

The transaction is three items: a passport for the contract, a [deposit]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) - typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND - and the [rental price]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Cash, bank transfer and QR payment all work. Ask the shop how long handover takes.

### What a foreign renter brings

The [documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the paperwork in full. Your passport is the identity document for the rental agreement.

The deposit depends on the vehicle and the conditions, so ask for the figure that applies to the bike you want instead of assuming the range above. Rental periods run by the day, the week or the month, and longer-term rental is negotiable with the shop.

### What the rental does not cover

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so customers arrange their own. Read your travel or medical policy for motorbike exclusions and licence conditions before you ride. As [the guide to legal riding for tourists]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains, an invalid licence can invalidate an insurance claim after a crash and shift liability onto the rider, whoever caused it, and a rental agreement may also hold the renter responsible.

## The legal side

Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) lists four kinds of licence valid on Vietnamese territory. The last two both rest on a treaty, so the list below treats them as one:

- A Vietnamese licence, gained by test or as a foreign licence formally exchanged for a Vietnamese one, which longer-term residents can do. The [A1 class covers bikes up to 125 cc]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}), which includes most rental scooters; A1 licences issued before 2025 keep their old scope, up to but not including 175 cc.
- A home-country motorcycle licence plus an IDP issued under the 1968 Vienna convention, both appropriate to the vehicle. Your country must be a 1968-convention state - [which countries' IDPs work in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists them.
- A treaty licence: from a country or territory with which Vietnam has a mutual-recognition treaty or agreement, or recognised under an international treaty Vietnam has joined, each appropriate to the vehicle. Confirm it from an official source before you rely on it.

If you hold none of these, riding a rental bike is riding unlicensed. That is a fine under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and police can hold the bike while the case is processed (Article 48 of the same decree; [when police can impound a motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the cases). Article 18 also covers a licence that does not fit the bike you are riding, so match the bike to the licence as carefully as you check that you hold one. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the detail.

### Which situation are you in?

The table covers a standard rental scooter of up to 125 cc. The licence-free class it mentions is the 50 cc moped class, explained further down.

| What you hold | Legal on a scooter up to 125 cc? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A Vietnamese A1 or A licence | Yes, within its class | Carry it; A1 reaches 125 cc |
| A national motorcycle licence plus a 1968-convention IDP with a motorcycle category | Yes, if the licence covers that size of bike | Carry both originals |
| An IDP, but the national licence left at home | No | Carry the national licence |
| A national licence from outside the 1968 convention, or a 1949-convention IDP | Only under a treaty or agreement | Confirm any treaty route officially; otherwise exchange the licence once you hold a qualifying residence card, or use the licence-free class |
| No motorcycle licence (a car licence alone does not count) | No | Use the licence-free class or, as a resident, take the A1 test |

Before you fly, check your IDP and national licence against [the checklist in the 1968 Vienna Convention guide]({{ '/articles/1968-vienna-convention-idp-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and carry the originals of both. Get the permit before you travel.

## What about your home car licence?

A car licence does not cover motorcycles in Vietnam, and neither does a 1949-convention or non-convention permit. Article 57 sets car classes and motorbike classes apart, so [a car licence does not cover even a 125 cc scooter]({{ '/articles/ride-125cc-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), whether it is Vietnamese or foreign. An IDP does not change that: it translates the categories your national licence holds, so a car-only licence behind an IDP gives car categories in Vietnam and no motorbike category. Check the [licence guides]({{ '/topics/law-licences/' | relative_url }}) before you fly if riding is central to your trip.

## Short stay or settled in

Which document does the work depends on how long you stay: short stays run on an IDP, long stays run on a Vietnamese licence.

### Riding on an IDP

The IDP and the national licence behind it each carry their own expiry date, so compare both with the day you plan to return the bike. Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP punishes riding on a licence that is no longer valid as riding with no licence, as [the expired-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/expired-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains, so an expiry date in the middle of a long rental is a real problem and not a formality.

### Exchanging a licence once you live here

Under Article 24 of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA a foreigner who can show residence of three months or more can exchange a valid foreign national licence for a Vietnamese one. An IDP cannot be exchanged, and neither can a foreign licence that is out of date. [The licence exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the dossier and where to apply.

Residents with no motorcycle entitlement at home take the Vietnamese A1 test instead, which [the expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) describes. Moving here rather than visiting? See the guides for [expats moving to Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-expats-moving-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), [English teachers]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-english-teachers-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [digital nomads]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-digital-nomads-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## No motorcycle licence: the 50cc and e-bike options

A licence-free 50 cc or e-bike rental is a legal, low-speed way to see the city if your paperwork does not reach further. A moped (50 cc or less, designed for no more than 50 km/h) needs no licence, which is why [licence-free 50 cc bikes]({{ '/articles/renting-50cc-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [e-bikes]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-electric-bike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) are options for visitors who have no motorcycle licence at home.

Foreign nationality changes nothing here: [the licence-free moped guide]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-ride-50cc-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the rider's duties, what happens after a crash on a moped and how fines apply.

For electric two-wheelers the line is power. An electric moped of 4 kW or less needs no driving licence; above 4 kW a licence is required, class A1 up to 11 kW and class A above that. The rated power of an electric machine decides which licence class you are riding in. Ask for it before you book.

Availability is a separate question. Nguyen Tu has historically offered 50 cc and electric options, but the current stock of any model needs checking first. Contact us to confirm current availability before you plan around one.

## Misunderstandings that catch foreign renters

- **"The shop rented to me, so I am covered."** No rental document makes you legal to ride: only a licence Vietnam recognises does, as [the documents guide]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) puts it.
- **"An IDP is my licence."** It is a translation of the national licence behind it, so you carry both originals; [the IDP rules guide]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) adds that photocopies and photos of documents are not substitutes.
- **"Everyone rides without a licence."** That is not a legal argument: the fine and the insurance consequences above apply to the rider, whatever other people do.

## Common questions

### What should I do if the police stop me?

Stay calm and hand over the documents the officer asks for: the originals of your licence, your IDP if you ride on one, and the bike's registration certificate. If your documents are not in order, a fine is the normal outcome, and if you cannot produce the licence or registration papers police can hold the bike until the papers are produced and any fine is settled (Article 48(3) of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP). On a rental, release normally runs through the owner or an authorised person, so keep the shop's phone number with you; [the impound guide]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the detail. The serious consequences arrive after an accident.

### Can I take a rental bike outside Hanoi?

Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use. Tell the shop where you plan to ride when you book, and raise any trip beyond the city before you rent rather than after you have left.

### Is there a minimum age for foreign riders?

The law sets 16 as the minimum age for the licence-free moped class and 18 for a Vietnamese licence such as A1, which is the class a standard rental scooter needs. Above the moped class the practical minimum is 18; [the minimum-age guide]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the detail. A shop's own conditions for young renters are something to ask about, not assume.

### Can I rent if I have never ridden a scooter?

Ask whether the shop has conditions for first-time riders, and say honestly how much you have ridden when you enquire. A licence says you may ride; it does not say you are ready for Hanoi traffic, and [the first-time rental walkthrough]({{ '/articles/first-time-renting-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first hour and the first day.
