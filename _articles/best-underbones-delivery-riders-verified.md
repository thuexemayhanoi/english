Delivery work is the hardest test of a Vietnamese motorbike: eight-plus hours a day, heavy stop-start cycles, cargo, and every dong of fuel coming out of earnings. The manufacturers' published data (retrieved 23 September 2026) points clearly to the machines that suit it.

## Economy first

A delivery rider covering 100 km a day saves roughly a litre of fuel per week choosing a 1.47 L/100 km Future 125 over a 2.08 L/100 km Sirius - significant against thin margins. The verified economy leaders: Future 125 (1.47), Wave RSX (1.56), Super Cub (1.55), CT125 (1.61), Wave Alpha (1.72).

## Cargo and durability second

The Wave RSX's U-box takes a half helmet or a delivery box mount; the CT125's rack styling and 165 mm clearance suit food runs over bad kerbs and alley tracks; the Wave Alpha's simplicity - no published storage, minimal equipment - is exactly why it survives abuse cheaply. The 110-class bikes also have the smallest published engine-oil capacities (0.8 L drain for the Wave/Blade/RSX class), making oil changes cheap.

## What to avoid

Manual-clutch sport bikes. A 2.59 L/100 km Winner R or 2.91 L/100 km CBR150R burns your profit, and a heavy clutch is misery in ten-hour traffic - the semi-automatic format wins for commercial use, as our scooter-versus-semi-automatic explainer details.

High-mileage work lives or dies on maintenance discipline - our high-usage maintenance guide covers the schedule mindset, and the owner of this site historically recommends periodic checks around every ~1,500 km for rental bikes used hard.
