Holding a phone while riding is one of the defined distraction violations of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP: 800,000–1,000,000 VND for motorbike riders under Article 7(4)(đ), with licence points deducted besides. The band sits between the small document offences and the signal violations — the law treats the phone as mid-grade dangerous, and the crash statistics agree.

## What exactly is banned

The clause targets using a phone while driving — the rider who texts, scrolls, or takes a call in hand. Handheld navigation falls inside it: a rider holding a mounted-phone's worth of directions on a phone in one hand is riding one-handed and distracted, which is the conduct the clause polices. The checkpoints read it that way too.

## What is not banned

A phone in a proper mount, used as navigation without the rider's hands leaving the controls, is a different situation — the violation targets handheld use. Bluetooth calls through a helmet set leave the hands free. The difference the law draws is attention and control: eyes up, hands on bars.

## The delivery-rider problem

Vietnam's app-delivery economy runs on handlebar phones, and enforcement has followed the risk accordingly — the 2025 schedule's phone fine is multiple times the earlier band precisely because the behaviour multiplied. Riders renting motorbikes should not copy the delivery rider's phone-in-hand navigation habit; the fine band applies to tourists identically.

## The practical setup for visitors

Mount the phone before moving, set the destination before kicking off, and let voice guidance do the work. In Hanoi traffic the phone-in-hand moment is also the moment the flow changes — the rider looking down is the rider the flow moves around, and the fine is the cheaper of the two outcomes.
