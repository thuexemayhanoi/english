The step-through frame is Vietnam's default motorbike shape, and it is not an accident of style. It is a design argument - one the country has voted for with tens of millions of purchases.

## The step-through logic

A conventional motorcycle hangs its fuel tank at the top tube between your knees. An underbone drops the tank low and forward, leaving an open spine down the middle. That single change produces everything the format is loved for: you mount in skirt, work clothes or carrying a delivery bag; both feet plant flat at stops; the low tank keeps mass low, which shows up in the friendly kerb weights (96-104 kg across the commuter class); and there is no hot tank between your knees in 38-degree traffic.

## What the design costs

Structural simplicity favours modest power - which is fine, since the class makes 5.9-6.9 kW and does not need more. There is no tank-top real estate for a full console, and the frame layout puts storage in compromises: Honda publishes a U-box for the Wave RSX and rack styling for the CT125, but no litres for the commuter class.

## Why it pairs with the semi-automatic

The open frame and the rotary gearbox are a matched set: the frame makes mounting trivial and the centrifugal clutch makes launching trivial - together they explain why the format is the easiest thing on two wheels in Vietnamese traffic.
