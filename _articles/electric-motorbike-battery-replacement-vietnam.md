Every electric motorbike eventually needs its battery renewed — by cycle fatigue, fault or age. Vietnam gives you more routes to that than most markets.

## Route 1: Warranty first

A failing pack inside its warranty window is the manufacturer's problem. Dat Bike ships a 3-year battery warranty; hold the paperwork, report capacity issues early, and let the warranty do the paying. Check the percentage-fade thresholds in your own model's terms.

## Route 2: Buy a new pack

On owned-battery bikes, a new pack from the manufacturer is the clean option — matched to the charger and controller, warranty attached. Fixed-battery machines like the Weaver 200 (72 V 68 Ah lithium-ion) are serviced through the brand network; the pack is replaced as a unit rather than opened. We do not publish replacement prices because they vary by model and year: ask the brand's dealer for a written quote, and treat any third-party quote with suspicion — cells, firmware and chargers are engineered as a set.

## Route 3: Escape to rental

VinFast's swap-model owners can move to rented batteries (175,000 VND per month for one pack, 300,000 for two) instead of buying a replacement pack — the rental pool absorbs ageing permanently. If your bike is swap-ready, replacement anxiety is simply a subscription decision.

## Route 4: Part out honestly

When a bike plus a new pack exceeds the market value of a healthy used example, some riders sell the bike as-is with the battery condition disclosed and let the buyer choose the route. Disclose properly — a hidden tired pack is the electric equivalent of a wound-back odometer.

## The prevention ledger

Shaded parking, partial-charge storage, the right charger and no deep-flood charging all delay this day; our battery lifespan guide has the habits. Replacement is a planned cost, not a surprise, when the pack's health is monitored from year one.
