Riding 15,000-30,000 km a year changes what "best" means. Purchase price amortises away; economy, parts availability and mechanical simplicity dominate. Manufacturer data (retrieved 23 September 2026) sorts the field quickly.

## The high-mileage shortlist

- Honda Future 125 FI - 1.47 L/100 km, 4.6-litre tank, air-cooled simplicity, 0.7 L oil drain. The default long-distance commuter.
- Honda Wave RSX - 1.56 L/100 km, PGM-FI, 4.0-litre tank, U-box, 0.8 L oil drain.
- Honda Wave Alpha - 1.72 L/100 km, 96 kg, the simplest and cheapest to keep alive; 0.8 L oil drain.
- Honda CT125 - 1.61 L/100 km, 5.4-litre tank, 165 mm clearance, 0.8 L oil drain, rack. The high-mileage explorer.

## Why air-cooled semi-automatics win

No radiator, no coolant, no clutch plates consumed in traffic - the air-cooled four-speed underbone is the cheapest architecture in Vietnam to run at scale, and its parts are everywhere (our spare-parts [availability guide]({{ '/articles/spare-parts-availability-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the supply chain). Honda publishes full engine-oil capacities for this class (drain figures above), which keeps servicing cheap and fast.

## What loses money at high mileage

Liquid-cooled sport bikes: the Winner R (2.59 L/100 km, 1.1 L oil) and CBR150R (2.91 L/100 km, 12-litre fills) cost more at every fuel stop and every service.
