"Can I ride to X and back in a day?" is the most common trip-planning question, and the honest answer starts with a number most guidebooks skip: a loaded motorbike averaging around 30 km/h including stops is doing well in northern Vietnam. Town crossings, roadworks, animals and lunch all eat the clock. From that number, the whole day-trip map falls into place.

## The 50 km radius (half-day trips)

One to two hours out: Co Loa, Bat Trang, the pagodas at Thay and Tay Phuong, most of the Red River islands. These trips fit comfortably in a morning or afternoon and still leave energy for the city. This is also the right first trip for a new rider — close enough that tiredness or weather never turns into a crisis.

## The 85–100 km band (full-day trips)

Two to three hours each way: Tam Dao (about 85 km, roughly two hours), Ba Vi, Dai Lai, the Perfume Pagoda, Quan Son, Ninh Binh (roughly 100 km, about 3.5 hours). This is the classic day-trip band, and it has a rule: leave by 8 a.m. or the return leg finishes in the dark, which you do not want on rural roads. One full stop and one fuel stop each way is the comfortable rhythm.

## The 100–150 km edge

Thung Nai (about 100 km up the Highway 6 corridor), the Hung Kings temples in Phu Tho (about 90 km) — trips at this distance work as day trips only with an early start, a clear plan and a willingness to ride home tired. Past this band, the return ride stops being fun; overnight instead.

## What shrinks your range

- **Rain**: an hour of heavy rain costs you an hour plus energy.
- **Mountains**: hairpin sections drop average speeds dramatically.
- **Two-up riding**: add 20–30% to your time estimates.
- **50cc or electric bikes**: range is a hard limit, not a preference — see the 50cc day-trip range guide.

## A simple rule of thumb

Round-trip distance should not exceed 200 km for a comfortable day, and anything past 150 km each way should include an overnight. When in doubt, plan the turnaround time first — "we turn around at 1 p.m. wherever we are" — and let the distance fall where it may.
