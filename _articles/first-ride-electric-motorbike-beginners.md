Your first ride on an electric motorbike will be easier than your first ride on anything with an engine — and that is exactly the danger. Nothing about the machine needs learning; everything about the street still does. This briefing covers the five things that are different, and the habits that keep them safe.

## 1. The launch: torque with no warning

Twist the throttle and the machine moves — immediately, smoothly, and in silence. There is no clutch to find, no gear to catch, no engine note to tell you (or anyone else) that you are moving. For the first hour, treat every throttle opening as deliberate: cover the rear brake at standstill, and open the throttle like you mean it, not like you are testing it.

## 2. The silence: you are invisible twice over

Day one on an electric is the day to build the compensation habits: assume pedestrians have not heard you (they have not), assume the car merging has not heard you (it has not), and position yourself where eyes will find you — the visibility guide covers light, position and gear. The silent-riding safety article is the single most important read for a new electric rider.

## 3. The brakes: two systems, one stop

On machines with throttle-controlled regenerative braking (verified on the Dat Bike Weaver 200), easing off the throttle slows the machine through the motor — learn to use it as your first-stage brake, with the friction levers for the final stop and emergencies. Practice one firm straight-line stop in a quiet street before you need one in traffic.

## 4. The display: your fuel gauge is honest, but slow to update

Range displays settle as the system learns your riding — do not judge the machine's range on the first five minutes. Start your first ride with a full charge (or fresh packs) purely so the first ride is about riding, not about the display.

## 5. The licence line: know your machine's class

Licence-free means 4 kW and under (the VinFast scooter class, pedal-assist machines); above that an A1 licence is required (the Dat Bike class). The kW ladder guide maps the verified machines. Renting first is the sensible way to learn the format — Nguyen Tu rents an E-Bike at 200,000 VND per day (contact us to confirm current availability).

## The first-ride checklist

- Helmet on and strapped (the law, not advice).
- Mirrors adjusted, lights checked, tyre condition glanced at.
- Quiet street first: throttle, regen, brake, U-turn.
- Then traffic — riding at the speed of predictability, not the speed of the machine.

Electric riders skip the clutch-technique learning curve entirely — the whole safety budget goes to streetcraft. Spend it there.
