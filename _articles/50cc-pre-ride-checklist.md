Two minutes before the first ride of the day catches almost everything a 50cc will otherwise surprise you with. The routine is short because it is meant to happen every time — a checklist that takes ten minutes gets skipped by day three.

## The walk-around (30 seconds)

Circle the bike once, looking: anything leaking under it, anything hanging off, tyre profiles visibly round, no luggage straps dangling near wheels. Nudge the bike off its stand and roll it a metre — frees the brakes, tests the steering, and you feel the machine before you mount it.

## Brakes (20 seconds)

Squeeze the front lever: firm within a third of the travel. Press the rear pedal: same. Roll forward a bike-length and stop hard with each alone — the bike must pull up straight. A lever that changes feel day to day is a 50cc writing you notes; read them.

## Tyres (15 seconds)

One glance at each tyre's crown for embedded glass or nails, one thumb-squeeze only as a low-pressure suspicion check — the real pressure test is weekly with a gauge, per our maintenance routine. Anything visibly flat-spotted or sagged: gauge or pump before riding.

## Lights and horn (15 seconds)

Engine on. Headlight, taillight, brake light under both controls, horn. On older 50s the electrics are the least reliable system on the bike; a dead brake light is both an offence risk and a genuine safety gap in traffic.

## Oil and fuel glance (10 seconds)

Sight-glass or dipstick if the model has easy access — the level check takes two minutes properly and is worth doing weekly; the pre-ride version is a glance for fresh leaks under the engine. Fuel: enough for the trip, plus reserve margin. Small tanks punish optimism.

## Kit and papers (20 seconds)

Helmet on and strapped — properly fastened, not ceremonially. Gloves, closed shoes. In the bike or on you: registration copy, insurance certificate, phone charged. The documents article lists what should physically ride with you. Tell someone your route if it leaves the ordinary.

## The habit

Do it in the same order every time, so the order runs itself. After two weeks it stops feeling like a checklist and starts feeling like putting the bike on — which is the point: safety that costs no attention gets spent every ride, and the two minutes you "do not have" are cheaper than any single minute of the alternative.
