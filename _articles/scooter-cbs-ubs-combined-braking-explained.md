Most 125-class scooters in Vietnam sell with a braking aid that buyers routinely confuse with ABS: combined braking, badged CBS (Combined Braking System) by Honda and UBS (Unified Braking) by Yamaha. Understanding what it does - and does not do - prevents the most dangerous false assumption in scooter buying.

## What CBS/UBS actually does

Squeezing one lever on a CBS scooter applies measured braking to both wheels, front and rear together, in a fixed proportion. The reason is simple: most riders under-use the front brake, where most stopping power lives (our disc vs drum guide covers the physics). CBS makes the front brake work even when the rider only pulls the rear lever, shortening stopping distances for typical users.

## What CBS does not do

It is not ABS. If you grab the brake hard enough to lock a wheel on wet paint, CBS will not prevent the lock - ABS modulates pressure per wheel to keep it rotating, as our ABS guide and TCS vs ABS explainer cover. A CBS scooter stops better than an unequipped one, and worse than an ABS one, in the wet.

## Verified models with CBS/UBS (retrieved 23 September 2026)

- Honda Vario 125 - front disc with CBS
- Yamaha Gear 125 Hybrid - front disc, rear drum with UBS

Several other models in our verified database publish ABS instead; many budget models publish no combined system at all, and we do not assume one. When a shop tells you a bike "has ABS", verify - CBS is often what is meant.

## Why it matters in Vietnam

Wet-season junctions and red-light panic stops are exactly where fixed-proportion braking helps most. But technique still outranks hardware: our emergency braking guide covers the practice, and our rainy-season guide ranks verified ABS models if you want the stronger system.
