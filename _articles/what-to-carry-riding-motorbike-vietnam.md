What you carry on a motorbike in Vietnam falls into two lists: things on your body, and things in the bike. The split matters more than the lists themselves, because the first list survives crashes and theft, and the second does not.

## On you, always

- Your licence (Vietnamese, or your home licence with an IDP — see documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam for exactly what police checks require)
- Phone with the rental shop's number saved, plus a little cash separate from your wallet
- Nothing attached to your hands or around your neck — no bags slung crossbody while riding; use the compartment

The legal carry set for riders is covered in the documents article; on a rental, keep the shop's paperwork with you as well, since the bike's registration stays with the rental agreement arrangements your shop specified at handover.

## In the bike's storage

Small automatics have under-seat room for a rain jacket, a lock, and a small pouch. Semi-automatics and manuals give you the floor space under the seat spine or a rear rack instead — a bungee cord and a small bag solve it. Useful contents:

- A compact rain suit during the wet months; Hanoi rain is sudden
- A phone charging cable if your rental has a USB socket; many do not, so carry a power bank instead
- A basic setup for a puncture situation: the shop's number and willingness to call them — most rentals carry a toolkit with the bike; confirm what is under the seat at handover (rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist)
- A spare tube or tyre levers only if the shop provides them and you know how they work — see punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike before attempting roadside repair yourself

## Never leave in a parked bike

Documents, cash, laptops, and anything you would be upset to lose. Under-seat compartments are not safes, and street parking is public. This is also why you should never leave the bike's registration papers in the bike — losing them turns a traffic stop into a complicated afternoon.

## Clothing choices

Covered shoes beat flip-flops every time: hot exhausts and kickstands leave marks. A light long-sleeve layer saves you from sun and exhaust-fume grime on long rides. Helmet is legally required and rentals normally include one — see do-hanoi-rentals-include-helmets.

## The one-sentence version

Carry on your body what the law and your safety require; keep in the bike only what rain and small repairs need; leave nothing in a parked bike that money or paperwork could replace.
