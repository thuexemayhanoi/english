What you carry on a motorbike in Vietnam falls into two lists: things on your body, and things in the bike. The split matters more than the lists themselves, because the first list survives crashes and theft, and the second does not. The papers the law asks a rider to carry start with the licence, the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate; the rest of this page covers everything else.

## On you, always

- Your licence (Vietnamese, or your home licence with an IDP — see [What Documents Must You Carry While Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for exactly what police checks require)
- Phone with the rental shop's number saved, plus a little cash separate from your wallet
- Nothing attached to your hands or around your neck — no bags slung crossbody while riding; use the compartment

The legal carry set for riders is covered in the [documents article]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}); on a rental, keep the shop's paperwork with you as well, and ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop.

### The legal set in short

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the papers a driver carries on the road. For a motorbike rider they are a driving licence valid for the class of vehicle, the registration certificate and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate; the documents article above covers the detail.

The compulsory certificate is third-party cover, so it does not replace cover for your own injuries; [Compulsory Motorbike Insurance in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/compulsory-motorbike-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what it pays.

### Phone, power and the shop's number

Save the shop before you ride: +84 942 467 674 works for calls, WhatsApp and Zalo, and the shop is open 09:00-21:00 daily. No after-hours service is published; plan so that you do not depend on a night-time reply, and read [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) before you need it.

Navigation keeps the screen lit, so carry a power bank in a pocket or the compartment while riding. It needs no wiring into a rented bike, where fitting anything may not be allowed. Holding a phone while riding is a defined violation, so mount the phone and set the route before you move; [Using a Phone While Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/phone-use-while-riding-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what the rule covers and what it does not.

### Cash and a backup of your papers

Keep a few small notes where you can reach them without opening a bag, because attended parking and small roadside purchases are simplest with small notes. Keep the rest apart from the wallet, so that one loss does not take everything.

Photos of your licence, the photo page of your passport and any rental paperwork you are given, saved on the phone, help you describe what is missing if a paper is lost. They are a backup, not a substitute: Article 56(3) lets papers that have been integrated into an electronic identification account be shown through that account, and nothing in it turns a picture in a phone gallery into the paper itself, so carry the paper.

## In the bike's storage

Whether a small automatic has under-seat room for a rain jacket, a lock and a small pouch depends on the model, and a litre figure only goes so far, so put your own things in and close the seat at handover. Honda Vietnam's pages give figures for some models. The current Air Blade page lists a 23.2-litre under-seat compartment that takes up to two half helmets, with a divider that splits it into two sections and a USB-C port inside under a waterproof cover, on the 125cc and 160cc versions. The Vision page lists an under-seat compartment for a helmet and personal items, with no litre figure, and a covered front compartment with its own USB-C port.

A rental can be an earlier model year than the one on a page, and a page does not say that a model is on hand, so treat these as things to look for. [Contact us]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}) to confirm current availability.

It is easy to boil storage down to one line: "semi-automatics and manuals give you the floor space under the seat spine or a rear rack instead — a bungee cord and a small bag solve it." Neither half holds for every bike, and a strap is safer than a bungee cord. Some semi-automatics have a small compartment and some have none: Honda's Wave RSX page lists a U-box that holds one half helmet and other personal items, while its Wave Alpha 110 page names no storage compartment among its features.

Where there is none, a rear rack may be all there is, and a small bag held on with a strap, not a bungee cord, solves it once you have looked at the bike and asked whether you may use the rack. Whichever you use, read how to [load and secure a bag]({{ '/articles/carrying-luggage-motorbike-safely-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) so that nothing can swing or fall into traffic.

Useful contents:

- A compact rain suit during the wet months; Hanoi rain is sudden
- A phone charging cable if your rental has a USB socket; check the bike at handover, and carry a power bank if it has none
- A basic setup for a puncture situation: the shop's number and willingness to call them — whether a toolkit comes with the bike is a handover question, so confirm what is under the seat ([Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}))
- A spare tube or tyre levers only if the shop provides them and you know how they work — see [Who Pays for a Punctured Tyre on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/punctured-tyre-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) before attempting roadside repair yourself

Worth adding if there is room:

- A dry bag or a sealed pouch for the phone, papers and cash in a shower
- A small cloth to wipe the seat and the mirrors once the rain stops
- Any lock or chain that comes with the bike: ask whether one does, and whether you may add your own

## Never leave in a parked bike

Documents, cash, laptops, and anything you would be upset to lose. Under-seat compartments are not safes, and street parking is public. This is also why you should never leave the bike's registration papers in the bike — losing them turns a traffic stop into a complicated afternoon. That applies whenever you walk away from the bike. If the shop tells you a paper is meant to stay with the bike, ask how you are expected to look after it while you park.

### What goes with you when you walk away

- The key. Take it out of the ignition and keep it on you, even for a minute. Ask at handover how many keys come with the bike and whether the shop holds a spare.
- The helmet. A helmet hung from a mirror or a handlebar is easy for someone to lift, so carry it in, or lock it to the bike if you must leave it; [Helmet Theft in Vietnam: Locks, Carrying and Common Sense]({{ '/articles/helmet-locks-theft-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers both.
- The phone, the power bank and the wallet: when you park, take them with you rather than leaving them in the compartment.
- Your passport, any rental paperwork you are given and any other paper with your name on it.
- A laptop, a camera or a bag of shopping.

Where you park matters as much as what you leave: attended parking (gửi xe) is a safer place for a bike than a dark corner of the street.

## Clothing choices

Covered shoes beat flip-flops every time: hot exhausts and kickstands leave marks. A light long-sleeve layer saves you from sun and exhaust-fume grime on long rides. A helmet is legally required, and whether your rental includes one is a question for handover — see [Do Hanoi Rentals Include Helmets?]({{ '/articles/do-hanoi-rentals-include-helmets/' | relative_url }}).

Article 33(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the rider and any passenger to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly.

## What the law keeps off the bike

Some things people pack out of habit are restricted while riding, and the rules sit in Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15. Paragraph 3(c) bars the rider from using an umbrella or audio equipment, hearing aids excepted, and paragraph 4(b) bars a passenger from using an umbrella. Pack a rain suit instead of an umbrella, and keep the earbuds in a pocket or the compartment while riding.

Paragraph 3(đ) bars using the motorbike to carry, shoulder or transport bulky objects, and loading goods beyond the prescribed limit. Paragraph 4(a) bars a passenger from carrying or shouldering bulky objects. The paragraph gives no size for "bulky", so use judgement: a bag that pulls on your arms, hides a mirror or hangs from the handlebars is already the wrong bag.

## Questions riders ask

### Does Nguyen Tu provide motorbike insurance?

No. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, and customers arrange their own. [Do You Need Your Own Insurance to Ride in Vietnam?]({{ '/articles/do-you-need-insurance-to-ride-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) looks at that question from the rider's side.

### How far may a load stick out?

Under Article 33(5), a load may not extend more than 0.3 metres beyond the width of the rack on each side, or more than 0.5 metres behind the rack as the manufacturer designed it, and its height measured from the road may not exceed 2 metres.

## The one-sentence version

Carry on your body what the law and your safety require; keep in the bike only what rain and small repairs need; leave nothing in a parked bike that money or paperwork could replace.
