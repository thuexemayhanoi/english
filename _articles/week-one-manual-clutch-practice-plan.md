One focused week is enough to move from "never touched a clutch" to riding calmly in light Hanoi traffic. The plan below assumes about forty-five minutes to an hour a day, on quiet streets before they fill up. Adjust freely — the order of skills matters more than the day numbers.

## Day 1: controls and the friction zone

Engine off, seated: work every control until your hands find them without looking. Then, engine on, stationary: find the friction zone ten times — clutch out to the creep point, clutch back in, brake. This drill alone teaches your left hand the single most important skill on the bike.

## Day 2: straight lines and stops

Launch, ride twenty metres at walking pace, stop with both brakes, put a foot down, repeat. Add finding neutral each time you stop. Thirty reps minimum. If the day feels repetitive, that is the point — you are building the launch-stop loop that traffic will demand of you a thousand times a week.

## Day 3: slow-speed control

Long friction-zone crawls, rear brake against clutch, and the first gentle ninety-degree turns at walking pace. Work on feet-on-pegs balance and eyes up. The slow-speed clutch guide has the drill list.

## Day 4: gears and flow

Upshift through second and third on a quiet stretch, then downshift back to first, matching revs as you release the clutch. Then combine: launch, shift up, slow, downshift, stop. Begin riding short loops around the block, dealing with corners one at a time. If a shift surprises you, pull the clutch in, brake gently, sort yourself out, continue.

## Day 5: real conditions, small doses

Pick a calm hour and ride a simple route you already know as a passenger or pedestrian: one junction with traffic lights, one right turn onto a bigger road, one U-turn, back to base. Total riding time maybe twenty minutes. The goal is not speed — it is doing every drill correctly with other vehicles present.

## Day 6: consolidation

Repeat the Day 5 route at a busier time. Add one bridge approach if you feel ready: wide lane, no lane changes, exit early and loop home. Watch your own habits: stalling at lights means go back to Day 2 drills; wobbly launches mean the friction zone needs another session.

## Day 7: review and the next step

Ride the full route, then honestly grade launch, stop, slow-speed, shifting, junctions. Anything below "calm" gets another session next week. Riders who follow this structure typically manage light traffic by week's end; the realistic timeline article explains what traffic-ready really means.

A few rules for the whole week: no passenger, no phone navigation on the bars while learning, and no rain-day heroics — wet roads are not the day to learn clutch finesse.
