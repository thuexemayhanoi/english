The stand question sounds trivial until the day a bike rolls off one. In Vietnam — where streets slope, surfaces are polished tiles, and parking is contact-sport-close — using the right stand, right way, is a daily safety habit.

## Side stand: the everyday default

Fast, one-motion parking: the bike leans at a designed angle with the stand's foot as the pivot. Its two failure modes are Vietnamese classics: soft and sloped ground (hot asphalt, sand, tiles — the stand sinks or slides and the bike lies down; use a flat puck or a small board under the foot when in doubt) and pointed-downhill parking (roll-off). The habit: point the bike's nose uphill or flat, leave it in gear, and rock-test the lean before walking away. The wider parking-terrain realities are in where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi.

## Centre stand: the stable answer

The centre stand holds the bike vertical on two feet — the correct answer for anything longer than a shop-stop: more stable on slopes, narrower footprint in tight parking, and it lifts the rear wheel for maintenance (chain lubing and adjustment is covered in chain-maintenance-motorbike-vietnam — the centre stand is the chain-care stand). The technique is a learned, full-body motion: stand on the lever, grip a solid frame point (not plastic or mirrors), and lift with the legs. Its weaknesses: heavy bikes with soft suspension can still settle, and on steep side-slopes the vertical bike can still be tipped by contact — the long-term storage checklist builds on the centre stand for exactly this reason (storing-motorbike-long-term-vietnam).

## Paddock stands: the workshop tool

Paddock stands (front and rear, with pins or cups matched to the bike) lift wheels fully for wheel work, bearing checks and proper cleaning — the home mechanic's tool rather than a street answer. Under an unmodified scooter or underbone, the centre stand already does most of what paddocks offer; paddocks earn their place on bikes without centre stands.

## The daily rules

Side stand for minutes, centre stand for hours, ground-check every time, and a cover over the top when it sleeps (motorbike-covers-parking-outdoors-vietnam). The humble stand is the least glamorous safety system on the bike — and the one that fails in front of an audience.
