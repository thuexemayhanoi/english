One sentence in the traffic law puts petrol 50s and electric mopeds in the same box: both are xe gắn máy — no licence, minimum age 16, helmet compulsory. From the second sentence onward, they are different machines, and the choice between them is about how you ride, not how the law sees you.

## The shared rule

Article 3 of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines the moped category as vehicles designed for no more than 50 km/h with engines of 50cc or less, **or electric motors of 4 kW or less**. Everything legal flows identically from there: same age floor, same helmet duty at the same fine levels, same traffic rules, same compulsory-insurance framework. The licence article carries the details; the electric licence article covers where the moped line sits in the electric spectrum (pedal e-bikes below, licence-class electrics above).

## Refuel versus recharge

A petrol 50 refuels in a minute at any of the city's stations, and a jerry can at home extends that indefinitely. An electric moped recharges over hours from a household socket — fine overnight, restrictive mid-journey. For predictable daily loops with a home plug, the electric never feels the constraint; for improvised long days, the petrol 50 wins without contest.

## The way they deliver power

Electric motors deliver their push from zero rpm — brisk, silent launches that surprise riders and pedestrians alike. Petrol 50s build revs into their urge and announce themselves doing it. Neither is "faster" in a way that matters at moped speeds; they differ in character — and in the electric's case, in the obligation to ride the silence responsibly: pedestrians do not hear you coming, so lights and defensive habits carry more of the load.

## Upkeep

- Petrol 50: the classic small-engine routine — oil, chain, brakes, carburettor or injection, filters — covered across our maintenance articles.
- Electric moped: batteries, controllers and connectors instead — our electric maintenance article covers the care pattern; the big cost event is eventual battery replacement.

## Costs and range honesty

We publish no fixed figures for either class: petrol consumption depends on condition and right hand; electric range depends on battery age, load, speed and terrain, and any claimed range should be treated as a manufacturer's test-condition number, with real-world range lower. Compare real bikes, ask owners, and judge each machine on its condition — our running-costs article covers the petrol side of the ledger.

## Choosing

Predictable flat commutes with home charging and a taste for silence: the electric moped. Longer loops, spontaneous trips, and a fuel network instead of a socket: the petrol 50. The law will not help you decide — for once, it genuinely does not mind.
