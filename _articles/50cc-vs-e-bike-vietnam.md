If you cannot or do not want to hold a Vietnamese motorbike licence, Vietnamese law gives you two genuine two-wheel options: a petrol moped of 50cc or less, or a pedal-type electric bicycle. They sit in different legal boxes and serve different riders.

## The legal boxes

A 50cc petrol bike (or an electric scooter-style moped up to 4 kW) is a xe gắn máy: no licence, rider at least 16, helmet compulsory. A pedal electric bicycle (xe đạp điện) is legally a bicycle with assistance that cuts out when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h: no licence, no minimum driving age in the motorbike sense, and it rides under bicycle rules. Our electric-bike licence guide unpacks that category carefully — the dividing lines matter because scooter-style "e-bikes" above the assistance limits are actually mopeds in law.

## Speed and range

A pedal e-bike assists up to 25 km/h; beyond that you are pedalling unaided. A 50cc petrol bike holds 35–45 km/h on the flat all day and refuels in a minute at any of the petrol stations described in our fuel guide. The e-bike's range depends on its battery, load and terrain, and when it runs out you are pushing a heavy bicycle home. The petrol bike is the only one of the two that genuinely keeps up with city flow.

## Effort and arrival

The e-bike arrives without sweat only if your route is flat and the battery is fresh; Hanoi's bridge ramps and long rises drain both battery and the fiction of effortless travel. The 50cc makes no demands on your legs at all.

## Costs and upkeep

E-bikes: cheap to recharge, but batteries age and replacement is the big cost event; the rest of the machine is low-maintenance. 50cc: cheap per-kilometre fuel, but it needs the classic small-engine care — oil, chain, brakes, tyres — all covered in our maintenance articles. Purchase price comparisons are unreliable to publish here because both categories trade in informal and fast-moving markets; judge each bike on its condition.

## Weather and theft

Both are open to the rain. E-bike electronics and connectors dislike flooding more than a carburetted 50cc does. Theft risk follows the bike, not the power source — a lockable parking spot and a disc lock matter either way.

## Who should choose what

- Short flat commutes, no licence concerns, family use with younger teenagers: the pedal e-bike.
- Ages 16+, longer or hillier routes, keeping up with traffic: the 50cc.
- Visitors staying months without a motorcycle licence: often a monthly e-bike rental is the practical, low-commitment choice.
