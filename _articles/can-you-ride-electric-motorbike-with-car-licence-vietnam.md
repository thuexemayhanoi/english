Short answer: a car licence — Vietnamese or foreign — covers no electric two-wheeler above the licence-free line. But electrics have a wrinkle petrol bikes do not: many of them need no licence at all, so the car-licence question only actually arises for machines above 4 kW.

## How the classes split

Under Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), car classes (B, C, D and the trailer classes) and motorbike classes (A1, A, B1) are separate categories covering separate vehicle types — nothing in a car class reaches a two-wheeler, petrol or electric. Independently of any licence:

- Pedal-type electric bicycles are non-motorised vehicles — no licence of any kind.
- Electric mopeds at 4 kW and under, designed for 50 km/h or less — no licence, rider 16+.
- Above 4 kW — an A1 licence (up to 11 kW) or an A licence is required.

So a car-only licence holder has two legal electric options: ride a licence-free class machine, or get an A1 licence for anything faster.

## What riding above your class costs

Riding a machine above 4 kW on a car licence is treated as failing to meet licence conditions — the same band as no licence: 2,000,000–4,000,000 VND for machines in the up-to-125 cc / up-to-11 kW bracket under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. The verified example is the Dat Bike Weaver 200: a 6,000 W machine, firmly in A1 territory — the checkpoint checks the licence against the machine, not against your driving history.

## Where the confusion comes from

Some countries fold light two-wheelers into car licences, and visitors assume the carry-over. Vietnam does not, and licence exchange works the same way: a foreign car-only licence exchanges into Vietnamese car classes only. The petrol version of this question — the 125 cc case — has the same answer for the same reason.

## The practical routes

1. **Licence-free class**: swap-capable scooters (the VinFast class) and pedal-assist machines are legal for car-licence holders because they are legal for everyone 16+.
2. **Get the A1**: a car licence neither helps nor blocks an A1 test in Vietnam — it is a separate qualification.
