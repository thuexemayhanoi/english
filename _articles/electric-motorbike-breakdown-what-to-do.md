Most electric "breakdowns" are not breakdowns — they are flat batteries. Triage the two, and the roadside problem shrinks.

## First question: is it the charge?

Check the gauge, then the obvious: is the charger connected at your last stop? Did you actually unplug it this morning? A swap-model rider's flat pack is a one-cabinet problem (9,000 VND per battery per swap); a fixed-battery bike with an empty gauge needs a socket within reach — a friendly shop, a hotel or a Dat Charge station (the Weaver++ adds 100 km in 20 minutes there).

## Second question: is it a fault?

Work through the cheap checks in order:

1. **Kill switch and key state** — the silent equivalent of "is the fuel tap on".
2. **Charge port and connectors** — wet or corroded contacts will refuse to charge or run; dry and inspect.
3. **Brake cut-off switches** — many electrics refuse to start if a brake lever switch is stuck; free it.
4. **App diagnostics** — connected brands surface fault codes remotely; Dat Bike's app, for instance, also files service requests from the roadside.

## Third: call in the brand terms you already own

Warranty support on modern electrics is surprisingly mobile: Dat Bike publishes a 48-hour nationwide on-site warranty, meaning the mechanic comes to the bike rather than the reverse. Swap-model riders additionally carry a battery that is not theirs — a suspect rented pack is the network's problem, exchanged on the spot. Know your own model's terms before the day you need them; our warranty [guide]({{ '/articles/aftermarket-parts-warranty/' | relative_url }}) collects the verified ones.

## What not to do

Do not open battery packs or controllers roadside — high-voltage diagnosis belongs to brand service. Do not charge a bike that has been submerged; it goes to inspection first. And do not push a dead machine miles in the heat: the app, the warranty and the network exist precisely so that you do not.
