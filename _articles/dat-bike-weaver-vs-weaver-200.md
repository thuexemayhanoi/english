The Weaver and the Weaver 200 are the same idea at two scales: an A1-licence underbone electric for Vietnamese roads. The comparison between them is almost entirely about range — and the official numbers make the gap unusually clear.

## Head to head

| | Weaver | Weaver 200 |
|---|---|---|
| Motor | 5,000 W hub | 6,000 W hub |
| Top speed | 80 km/h | 90 km/h |
| Claimed range | 100 km | 200 km at 35 km/h; 130 km at 70 km/h |
| Charging | ~3 h | 1 h for the first 100 km; 3 h full |
| Weight | (not published) | 120 kg, 165 kg max load |
| Sealing | (not published) | IP65 |
| Brakes | (not published) | hydraulic discs front and rear, throttle-controlled regen |
| Battery claims | (not published) | 150,000 km / 15 years; 3-year battery warranty |
| Licence | A1 | A1 |

Fields not published on the Weaver's official page stay empty here rather than being filled from the 200.

## What the upgrade buys

The Weaver 200 doubles the claimed range and, just as importantly, changes the charging curve: an hour restores the first 100 km of claimed range, so a lunch stop meaningfully refills the machine. Add the published durability story (the 150,000 km battery-life claim, 3-year battery warranty and 48-hour nationwide on-site service) and the 200 is not just longer-legged — it is the more completely specified product on paper.

The Weaver's remaining argument is the classic one: fewer of everything means less to buy and less to maintain for a rider whose day never exceeds its 100 km claim. A commute of 20 km a day never meets the 200's advantages.

## Who should pick which

- **Weaver**: A1 holders with city-scale days, buying the simplest version of the format. Review.
- **Weaver 200**: riders whose week includes long commutes, intercity sections or delivery-style duty — anyone who has ever watched a range display with concern. Review.
