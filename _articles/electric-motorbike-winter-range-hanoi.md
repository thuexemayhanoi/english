Hanoi's winter is not harsh by world standards, but it is exactly the kind of mild cold — 10–17 °C, damp, weeks long — that quietly trims electric range. Riders who budget for it never notice; riders who trust the summer display learn about it on a drizzling Tuesday in January.

## Why the display falls faster

Battery chemistry works by electrochemical reactions, and reactions slow in the cold. A pack that delivered a comfortable margin in November gives noticeably less per bar in January. The manufacturer figures — the Evo's up-to-165 km, the Weaver 200's 200 km at 35 km/h — are standard-condition claims; cold, damp, headlight use and thicker cold-weather gear all pull the real figure down. Our real-world range guide gives the full list of shrinkage factors; winter is simply several of them at once.

## The winter habits

- **Charge after the ride, in the evening** — a pack charged indoors overnight starts the day warm and full, the best winter state.
- **Give the machine a minute** — riding gently for the first stretch lets the pack and motor reach working temperature; flooring it from cold is the least efficient thing a cold battery can be asked to do.
- **Keep the spare pack indoors** — a removable pack left on a cold bike overnight loses its edge; swapping in a warm one genuinely helps on the coldest mornings.
- **Do not chase 100% in the cold** — an hour of extra charging for the last bars buys less range in winter than simply riding the first kilometre gently.
- **Expect the dip, do not fight it** — if the commute used 30% of the battery in October, budget 40% for the same ride in January.

## The Tet-week trap

Winter also contains Tet: weeks of long family trips, passenger loads and riding to schedules. A machine that does the commute with room to spare can arrive at an all-day Tet ride already marginal. The day-trip range-planning guide covers how to plan those routes on electric, and the winter-storage article covers the opposite case — the bikes that sit the season out.

The dip is temporary and honest: the pack returns to its warmer-weather behaviour when the season turns. The rider's only real job is to not be surprised by it.
