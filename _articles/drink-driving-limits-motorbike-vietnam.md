Vietnam has no "one beer is fine" allowance. Article 8 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) prohibits operating any vehicle with alcohol in your blood or breath: any detectable alcohol while riding a motorbike or moped is an offence.

## The fine bands for motorbikes and mopeds

Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP (Article 7) sets the punishment bands for motorbike and moped riders — the same levels for both:

| Blood alcohol | Breath | Fine (VND) | Extra penalties |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 50 mg/100 ml | Up to 0.25 mg/L | 2,000,000 – 3,000,000 | 4 licence points deducted |
| Over 50–80 mg/100 ml | 0.25–0.4 mg/L | 6,000,000 – 8,000,000 | 10 licence points deducted |
| Over 80 mg/100 ml | Over 0.4 mg/L | 8,000,000 – 10,000,000 | Licence suspension 22–24 months |

Refusing an officer's alcohol test is itself punishable at the top fine band, and drug use while riding carries the same 8–10 million VND band.

Pedal-type electric bicycles are treated separately, with lower fines under the decree's non-motorised vehicle schedule — but the zero-tolerance prohibition still applies to their riders too.

## What this means in practice

- One beer can put you in the lowest band; more puts you higher
- The police conduct breath tests at checkpoints, especially at night and around holidays
- Alcohol involvement after a crash can invalidate insurance claims and worsens your liability position

## If you plan to drink

Leave the bike. Take a taxi or ride-hailing car, walk, or arrange your evening so the motorbike is not part of the return trip. This is also the advice we give our own rental customers: a drink-driving fine, or worse, costs far more than any taxi.
