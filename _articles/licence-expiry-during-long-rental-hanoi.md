Long rentals outlast paperwork. Visas get extended, licences expire, IDs renew — and a bike that was legal to ride in week one can be illegal to ride in week five without anything about the bike changing.

## The rule that decides everything

Your right to ride comes from your documents, not from the rental agreement. If your licence or visa lapses mid-term, riding stops being legal until it is renewed, even though the rental continues. The document requirements are in long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi and idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules.

## Renew before the expiry, not after

Start renewals early: visa extensions take processing time, IDP or licence questions depend on your nationality, and Vietnamese licence procedures have their own queues. A month of buffer turns a crisis into paperwork. Track every expiry date from day one of the rental.

## What to do with the bike meanwhile

Keep the bike but stop riding it — guarded parking, as in keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi, and let the shop know. Riding on expired documents risks fines and, in bad cases, the situations described in police-impound-motorbike-vietnam. A bike parked safely for a week costs nothing compared to that.

## Tell the shop

Inform Nguyen Tu if your documents expire mid-term, especially if the expiry affects the rental end date or your plans to extend. It is a normal conversation for a long-term renter, and the shop would rather know before the return date than at it.

## The one-line version

Documents expire, rentals continue — renew early, park the bike rather than ride on expired papers, and keep the shop informed.
