In July 2025, Hanoi redrew its own map. The city's 30 former districts were reorganised into 126 wards and communes — a national administrative reform intended to shrink local government. For residents it meant new paperwork; for visitors riding around the city, the change matters in quieter ways: addresses, maps and the names people actually use.

## What actually changed

The old district layer of government — Dong Da, Ha
i Ba Trung, Hoang Mai, Thanh Xuan, Bac and Nam Tu Liem, and the rest — disappeared as administrative units, replaced by 126 new wards and communes across the city. Streets, buildings and landmarks did not move. What changed is the official second line of every address: where a card once said "Dong Da District, Hanoi," it now names the new ward.

## Why this matters to a rider

**Navigation.** Digital maps in Vietnam are updated unevenly. Some points still list the old district names; some now show new wards; a search for an address using the old district structure sometimes returns the right pin with the wrong label, and occasionally vice versa. The practical fix: search by street name and number, or by a landmark's common name, rather than trusting the district line. The navigation guide covers how addresses work generally — the restructuring adds one more reason to pin your destination before you ride.

**Addresses in messages.** Hotels, landlords and shops may give you an address in either system. If a delivery or rental pickup point matters, confirm the street number and street name specifically, and share a map pin — the ward name alone has stopped being a safe identifier.

**Names in daily use.** This is the part that will not change: Hanoians still say "Dong Da," "Tay Ho," "My Dinh" and mean places, exactly as Londoners say "Soho" long after any boundary moved. Every area guide on this site therefore keeps the familiar names while noting the new structure. As a visitor you will never be wrong using the old neighbourhood names conversationally — only on forms.

**Paperwork.** If you rent long-term, register a bike, deal with a fine or exchange a licence, the office landscape has shifted: some administrative service centres moved or merged as part of the reform. Check the current location of the ward-level office you need rather than trusting an address published before July 2025, and carry the documents the law requires when you ride.

## What did not change


Traffic rules, licence classes, petrol stations, parking conventions, bridges, one-way systems — none of it moved. The restructuring is a government-shape change, not a road change. Ride exactly as you would have, and enjoy the fact that Hanoi's geography is still the geography you read about here.
