New riders in Vietnam make a predictable set of mistakes — the same ones, in the same order, whether they learned in Hanoi or arrived with a fresh licence. Knowing them in advance is worth a month of experience.

## Mistake 1: Riding above their scanning speed

New riders look at the wheel in front instead of scanning several seconds ahead, then ride faster than they can react to what they have not yet seen. The fix is not "slower" but "matched": speed matched to how far ahead you actually look. Defensive-riding-motorbike-vietnam covers the scanning habit.

## Mistake 2: The right-hand curb lane

It feels safest, away from cars. It is where door-handles, pedestrians stepping out, curb-side potholes and left-turning buses all live. Ride where the traffic flows, slightly left of the curb lane's edge.

## Mistake 3: Front-brake fear

Told once that the front brake is dangerous, new riders rely on the rear — and roughly double their stopping distance. The front brake, squeezed progressively, is the primary brake on any two-wheeler. Emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam covers the technique; practice it in an empty lot before traffic needs it.

## Mistake 4: Skipping the helmet fit and gear

A loose helmet or sandals is the combination that turns a five km/h fall into a hospital visit. Helmet-fit-guide-vietnam covers the fit; the floor of gear is closed shoes and long trousers.

## Mistake 5: Riding without the licence they need

The licence requirements for foreigners are strict, and riding without the right licence carries fines and impound risk — renting-a-motorbike-hanoi-without-licence covers the reality. Sort it before week one, not after a checkpoint. Learning-to-ride-monthly-rental-hanoi covers the learning path legally.

## The one-line version

Look far ahead, brake with the front, skip the curb lane, wear real gear, hold the right licence — the five corrections that turn a risky first month into a normal one.
