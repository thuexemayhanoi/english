Vietnam's electric two-wheelers now split into two fuel models: swap a full battery at a cabinet in under a minute, or plug in at home overnight. Both work — but they fit different lives.

## How swapping works

VinFast's Evo, Feliz II and Viper carry two 1.5 kWh LFP batteries under the seat, removable by hand. Instead of charging, you exchange a flat pack for a full one at a V-Green battery-swap cabinet. VinFast installed the first 4,500 stations by January 2026 and plans 45,000 cabinets across 34 provinces and cities by Q1 2026 — a network it says would be roughly 1.5 times the number of fuel stations. A swap costs 9,000 VND per battery. Crucially, you do not have to own the batteries at all: VinFast rents them at 175,000 VND per month for one or 300,000 VND for two, maintenance and degradation on them being the ope
rator's problem.

## How home charging works

Dat Bike takes the opposite approach: a large fixed battery (72 V 68 Ah on the Weaver 200) charged through a cable, with a Dat Charge fast-charging network as the away-from-home option — the Weaver++ claims 100 km of range from 20 minutes at a Dat Charge station. Home charging costs only your household electricity, but the battery is yours, and a full charge of the big pack takes hours (1 hour for the first 100 km, 3 hours for a full 200 km on the Weaver 200).

## Which fits you

| | Swapping | Home charging |
|---|---|---|
| Time cost | under a minute | hours (or 20 min at fast chargers) |
| Needs home socket? | no | yes (or station access) |
| Battery ownership | optional (rental) | yours |
| Model choice | VinFast swap models | Dat Bike, most others |

If you cannot park near power, swapping is transformative. If you have a socket where you park, charging while you sleep is the cheaper and simpler ritual — and you never queue.
