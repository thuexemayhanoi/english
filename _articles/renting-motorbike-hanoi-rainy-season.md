Hanoi's rainy season runs roughly May to October, with the heaviest, most predictable bursts from June to August: those months have the highest totals in the table below, though it says nothing about which days will be wet. Renting through it is completely normal — the whole city rides through it daily — but it changes how you plan. The same table shows rain on 10 to 18 days a month from May to October, so "daily" describes the city's routine, not rain every day.

The changes are practical ones: gear that stays dry, a bike whose storage and brakes suit wet roads, a handover that looks at lights and seals, and a plan for a bike that comes back rained on.

## What the season actually looks like

The table gives Hanoi's average monthly rainfall and number of rain days for 1991 to 2020, taken from the data file that Viet Nam's national meteorological and hydrological service supplies to the World Meteorological Organization.

| Month | Average rainfall | Average rain days |
|---|---|---|
| April | 79 mm | 16 |
| May | 188 mm | 17 |
| June | 231 mm | 16 |
| July | 300 mm | 18 |
| August | 335 mm | 18 |
| September | 195 mm | 14 |
| October | 126 mm | 10 |
| November | 48 mm | 7 |

Rain builds in May, peaks from June to August and tails off through October. That matches the World Bank Group and Asian Development Bank climate profile for Vietnam, which puts heavy monsoon rainfall in the north from May to October.

A rain day is a day with at least 0.1 mm of rain, so 18 rain days in July is not 18 days of steady downpour. The figures say how much rain falls, not at what hour or for how long, so read each day's forecast instead of assuming a pattern.

The same profile adds that El Niño Southern Oscillation influences monsoonal circulation and drives complex shifts in rainfall that vary by region, so treat the averages as a guide to the season, not a promise for the days you rent.

## Gear decisions at rental time

Nothing in the terms Nguyen Tu publishes mentions rain gear, so do not assume the rental includes any; ask at handover and plan to bring your own.

- A rain suit or poncho worn over normal clothes keeps the rider dry; buy or pack it before the first storm instead of shopping for one in the middle of it
- Keep it within reach: under the seat if it fits, otherwise in a bag. What to carry and where is covered in [What to Carry While Riding a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/what-to-carry-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }})
- If the bike has a USB socket, keep its cover shut: Honda's Vision manual says not to use the socket in wet conditions
- A clear-visored helmet beats sunglasses in a downpour. Ask at handover which helmet comes with the rental and whether its visor is clear; [Do Hanoi Rentals Include Helmets?]({{ '/articles/do-hanoi-rentals-include-helmets/' | relative_url }}) has the wider helmet picture
- Closed shoes keep spray off the feet, and a second pair for the next morning helps

## Bike choice in the season

Wet roads put two questions to the bike: where dry things go, and how it stops.

On storage, Honda lists 23.2 L under the seat of the Air Blade 125 but publishes no litre figure for the Wave Alpha 110's storage box (its manual also describes a document pouch in a compartment under the seat, and says not to leave valuable or fragile items in the box), and none is published for the Sirius RC 110, so an underbone rider should plan on a bag. Honda's Vision manual, in its washing notes, warns that water in the under-seat storage can damage documents or other items, so keep phone and papers in a sealed pouch even under the seat.

On stopping, the Wave Alpha and Vision manuals say the same thing: wet roads are very slippery and damp brakes work less effectively, so take special care when braking, and if the brakes are wet, apply them at low speed while riding to dry them. Neither singles out one brake type. The Wave Alpha page lists no disc version, while Yamaha lists the Sirius RC with a front hydraulic disc and a rear drum. Do not assume ABS on any rental: ask what braking the exact bike has, and leave extra stopping distance whichever it is.

This guide found no source showing either type coping better in rain. ## At handover in the wet season

The usual handover routine applies in any month. These checks matter more when rain is likely, and if it is raining, ask to do the walk-round under cover.

| Check | At handover | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Lights and horn | Switch on headlight, brake light, indicators and horn | Rain cuts visibility at any hour |
| Headlight lens | Look for water inside the lens | Some misting is temporary; a lot of water needs checking |
| Brakes | Squeeze both levers and ask what braking this bike has | Wet brakes bite later |
| Tyres | Look at the tread across the whole tyre | Worn tyres grip wet roads less well |

Honda's two manuals say the mist clears a few minutes after the engine starts and the headlight is on, and that a large amount of water inside the lens should be checked by an authorised dealer, which for a renter means pointing it out to the shop.

Ask two more questions before you leave: which number to call if the bike stops, and what the shop expects if it comes back wet and muddy.

## After a soaking, and the return

Wipe the seat and the bike with a cloth when you stop for the night. Mud and wet paint hide scratches, so a bike wiped before it is photographed gives a clearer record at the return. [What to check after your motorbike gets soaked]({{ '/articles/check-motorbike-after-heavy-rain/' | relative_url }}) lays out the checks after heavy rain: lights, horn, starter, brakes and air filter.

Report a light that flickers, a starter that clicks without turning or a brake lever that stays soft after drying the same day, with a photo or short video. Whether such a fault is the renter's cost or the shop's depends on the agreement, so ask before you ride and keep the answer in a message.

Ask whether the shop expects the bike washed. If you wash it, follow Honda's manuals: no high-pressure hose, which can damage moving or electrical parts, and no direct spray at the exhaust, where water can prevent starting and cause rust, or at the air filter.

## Flooded streets and storm warnings

Flooding is a monsoon fact at the scale of the Red River Delta. The climate profile describes the Red River and Mekong deltas as receiving floods annually with the monsoon season and names Hanoi among the urban areas in their vicinity; it also rates Vietnam's exposure as extremely high for flooding, including flash flooding, and very high for tropical cyclones. It says nothing about which Hanoi streets hold water or when, so this guide makes no claim about particular streets.

The practical rule is general: do not ride into standing water when you cannot see how deep it is. Go round it, wait or turn back. The Wave Alpha and Vision manuals give no safe water depth and no instructions for riding through floodwater, so there is no manufacturer figure to lean on; ask the shop for the manual of the bike you rent.

If the engine stops in water, move the bike to dry ground, do not keep cranking the starter, and phone the shop. [The breakdown guide]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out what to say on that call. Ask in advance what the shop can do if the bike stops, especially on a trip to Ba Vi or the Perfume Pagoda: support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, and [the guide to taking a rental outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the terms.

When a storm warning is in force, the sensible plan is not to ride. Ask the shop how your rental days are treated.

## A rental that spans the season

For weeks or months of wet riding, the question is who looks after the bike. Honda's maintenance schedules in both manuals say the air filter should be serviced more often in wet or dusty riding, and the crankcase breather more often when riding in the rain.

Nguyen Tu's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment or tension, tyre-pressure checks and a normal inspection, and its own practical guidance, not a manufacturer interval, is a periodic check around 1,500 km or more of use. Ask what your rental includes and when to bring the bike in; inner tubes and tyres are consumables that may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.

## The honest trade-off

The rainy season is also when Hanoi is greenest and its day trips are at their best — the Perfume Pagoda road and Ba Vi in particular. That is a matter of taste rather than measurement, so choose each trip by the day's forecast and the flood and storm-warning advice above. Renting in this season is not a worse experience, just a scheduled one: mornings out, afternoons flexible, rain gear always under the seat or in a bag, with the forecast deciding how far that plan holds.
