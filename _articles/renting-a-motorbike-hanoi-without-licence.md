Every rental shop in Hanoi knows that some of its customers ride without a valid licence. None of them will say it is fine. Here is what actually happens to unlicensed riders.

## What the law does

Riding with no valid licence is a fine of 2,000,000-4,000,000 VND for bikes up to 125 cc and 6,000,000-8,000,000 VND for bigger bikes. Alongside the fine, traffic police can hold the vehicle until the situation is resolved - and a rider who cannot produce any papers at all can have the bike held until papers appear. A fine is the good outcome.

## What an accident does

The fine is the smallest risk. If an unlicensed rider causes an accident, they face the accident-violation bands (up to 10-14 million VND for serious cases), potential criminal liability for injury or death, and - most importantly - civil liability for the damage. The other party's insurers or lawyers will ask for your licence. The shop renting to you is not a defence; the law puts the duty on the rider, and the shop faces its own penalty for handing a bike to an unqualified rider.

## What your travel insurance does

Most travel policies that cover motorcycling cover licensed riding. An unlicensed rider is typically excluded from medical coverage in a country where a licence is required - meaning the hospital bill, the repatriation, the whole accident, lands on you personally. Read the exclusion wording before you ride, not after.

## The legal alternatives if you have no motorcycle licence

- Rent 50 cc or less: no driving licence required (rider 16+). Low speed, but fully legal.
- Rent an e-bike in the licence-free class: same effect at bicycle-like speeds.
- Get legal before you ride: a 1968-convention IDP behind your existing motorcycle licence, or a Vietnamese A1 for longer stays.

## The bottom line

Nobody will stop you renting. The licence question only ever surfaces at a police stop or a hospital - the two moments when it is too late to fix. If your paperwork does not cover a motorcycle, ride a 50 cc bike, ride an e-bike, or ride nothing. The licence-fine article has the exact bands; the impound article explains bike-holding.
