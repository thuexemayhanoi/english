Fuel is the one part of a rental that is always the renter's, and the only argument is about how much is in the tank at each end.

## The standard convention

Most Hanoi rentals hand over the bike with a small, noted amount of fuel and expect it back at a similar level. Note the level at handover - it goes on the contract or into your handover photos. Return it where you found it and nothing is owed. Some shops hand over a full tank and expect a full tank back; agree which convention applies before you ride.

## What petrol costs and where to get it

Petrol in Vietnam is inexpensive by global standards, and filling a 100-125 cc underbone or scooter from near-empty costs tens of thousands of dong - a rounding error against the rental itself. Petrol stations are dense in Hanoi; on a rental you will pass several a day. In the far provinces stations thin out on small roads, but any route of consequence has fuel.

## The fuel type

Common rental bikes take regular unleaded (RON 92 is the standard grade at Vietnamese pumps; RON 95 is also sold). A rental underbone or scooter is not fussy - the standard grade is what shops and owners use. Do not experiment with anything labelled for two-strokes or racing fuel.

## Practical habits

- Fill when the gauge hits the last bar rather than pushing your luck; walking a dead bike to a station in 35-degree heat is a self-inflicted problem.
- Note the odometer at handover; on long rentals the fuel spend tells you if the bike is running right - sudden thirst is an early sign something needs the shop's inspection.
- Keep fuel receipts on delivery-day rentals so the return-level conversation is short.

## Who pays for what

Fuel is always the renter's: it is consumption, not damage, and no shop refunds the petrol left in the tank at return. The reverse applies too - nobody itemises the half-litre you left in there. The convention exists to keep both sides honest with the minimum of arithmetic.
