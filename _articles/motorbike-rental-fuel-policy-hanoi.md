There is no single motorbike rental fuel policy in Hanoi: shops differ, and none is published for Hanoi Motorbike Rental Nguyen Tu, so the policy is whatever you agree before you ride. Settle which convention applies (full-to-full, same level or as-is), photograph the gauge at handover and ask what happens if the level is different when you bring the bike back.

Fuel is the one part of a rental where the only argument is how much is in the tank at each end. This guide sets out the conventions you may be offered, the questions to put in the booking chat, how to record the gauge, how far a tank goes on two common rental models, and what to ask at the return.

## The standard convention

A common convention is to hand over the bike with a small, noted amount of fuel and expect it back at a similar level. Note the level at handover - it goes on the contract or into your handover photos. Return it where you found it and nothing is owed. That describes the convention, not a published rule for any shop, so ask what applies if the level differs. Some shops hand over a full tank and expect a full tank back; agree which convention applies before you ride.

The three arrangements below are conventions, not rules. Read the table as a list of things to ask for, not as a description of what any shop does.

| Convention | What it means | Ask at handover | Ask at return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-to-full | Handed over with a full tank, returned with a full tank | What counts as full: to the neck of the filler, or until the nozzle stops? | Whether you refuel yourself or a shortfall is charged, and at what rate per litre |
| Same level | Handed over at a noted level, returned at that level | What the level is, in words and in a photograph of the gauge | What a lower level costs, and whether a higher one earns any credit |
| As-is | Handed over with whatever is in the tank, returned with whatever is left | What the level is, and whether it is enough for your first ride | Whether any adjustment is made either way |

### Which convention suits which rental

- **Full-to-full** leaves the least to interpret, as long as both sides mean the same thing by "full". It needs a pump stop on the way back, so plan that stop if a flight, a train or a hotel check-out follows the return; [the guide to returning a rental motorbike]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lays out the day backwards from your deadline.
- **Same level** saves filling up before you set off, but it depends entirely on a record of the starting level. Without a photograph and a line in the chat, it comes down to memory.
- **As-is** is the quickest to state, and the starting level carries the risk: a bike handed over nearly empty can mean a pump stop before you reach anywhere. Photograph the gauge and ask what the shop will do if the level is low.

Whichever you choose, ask for it in writing. [How to book a motorbike rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows how to turn a message thread into a record, and [what a rental agreement should cover]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists fuel among the terms to read before you sign.

1. Which convention applies: full-to-full, same level or as-is?

3. What does "full" mean: to the neck of the filler, or until the nozzle stops?
4. If you return it lower, do you refuel first, or is the shortfall charged? At what rate per litre, and is it paid separately or taken from the deposit?
5. If you return it higher, is there any credit?
6. Which petrol does the bike take, and where is the filler cap?
7. If the gauge looks wrong, or you are unsure of the level, who do you tell and when?

## Recording the fuel gauge at handover

A photograph of the gauge is a simple fuel record, and it needs no tools. Take it before you pay and before you ride, with whoever hands over the bike still there.

1. Turn the ignition on first. A gauge may show nothing with the key off.
2. Take one photograph that shows the gauge and the odometer together, then a second from a slightly different angle in case glare hides the needle.
3. Photograph the filler cap and note how it opens, so that you are not working it out at the pump.
4. Send the photographs in the booking chat with one line in words, such as "gauge at about a quarter, odometer as in the photo", so that the shop holds the same record.
5. Keep the photographs and the chat until the rental and the deposit are settled.

### What a gauge photograph can and cannot show

A needle or a row of bars shows a coarse level, not litres, and the same needle can look different from another angle. That is why the photograph and the line in words belong together: the photograph shows what the gauge said, and the words say what you both took it to mean. Gauges on small bikes can also mislead, as [why a motorbike fuel gauge can mislead]({{ '/articles/fuel-gauge-inaccurate-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains, so the odometer in the same frame is your second record.

If the bike has no gauge, or the needle looks stuck, say so in the chat, photograph the odometer and agree in words how the level will be judged, for example "full to the neck". Some bikes use a fuel tap with a reserve position instead of a gauge, which [fuel taps and reserve]({{ '/articles/fuel-tap-reserve-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains; ask how the tap on your bike works.

The [checklist article]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) folds the fuel level into the handover walk-around; read it together with this one before you collect the bike.

## How far a tank goes: a stated calculation

Range is arithmetic on two published numbers, the size of the tank and the fuel consumption the manufacturer claims: range in kilometres = tank in litres ÷ consumption in litres per 100 km × 100. Both figures are published for two semi-automatic models, the Honda Wave Alpha 110 and the Yamaha Sirius RC 110. Whether either variant is in the rental fleet today is a question for the shop: contact us to confirm current availability.

| Bike | Tank | Claimed consumption | Tank ÷ consumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Wave Alpha 110 | 3.7 L | 1.72 L/100 km | 3.7 ÷ 1.72 × 100 = about 215 km |
| Yamaha Sirius RC 110 | 4.2 L | 2.08 L/100 km | 4.2 ÷ 2.08 × 100 = about 202 km |

These are the manufacturers' claimed figures, not a promise about your ride. Speed, load, temperature, terrain and stop-start traffic all change real consumption, so read each result as the distance to an empty tank at the claimed figure, not as a plan. The guide to the [best motorbike for day trips from Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-day-trips-from-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) applies the same sum to day-trip routes and plans its refill at half a tank.

### What the sum says about a fuel dispute

The tank is small. The whole tank of a Wave Alpha 110 is 3.7 L, so even a disagreement about "empty to full" is a disagreement about 3.7 L, and any gauge difference converts to litres by multiplying the fraction by the tank size.

Imagine a renter on a Wave Alpha 110 who is handed the bike at half a tank and returns it at a quarter. If the gauge were exact, the gap would be a quarter of 3.7 L, which is 0.925 L (3.7 ÷ 4). Real gauges are not exact, so the example shows the method, not a figure to expect.

### Checking your own consumption

Fill until the pump stops and note the odometer. At the next fill, divide the litres added by the kilometres ridden since then and multiply by 100: the result is your litres per 100 km. It will not match the claimed figure, and what matters is a sudden jump between two fills, the early warning described under the habits below. [What changes a motorbike's fuel consumption]({{ '/articles/improving-fuel-economy-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers the causes.

## What petrol costs and where to get it

On the two models above, a fill from near-empty is 3.7 L or 4.2 L at most. The bill is the price per litre shown at the pump times that number, so read the price at the pump instead of trusting a figure quoted in a guide.

Petrol stations are dense in Hanoi; on a rental you will pass several a day. In the far provinces stations thin out on small roads, but any route of consequence has fuel. Even so, start a longer ride with a full tank instead of counting on the next pump; [using petrol stations in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/hanoi-petrol-stations-guide-riders/' | relative_url }}) covers how a fill works at the station.

## The fuel type

Check which petrol the bike you are given takes before the first fill, because the answer depends on the model. Honda's owner's manual for the Wave Alpha (September 2025 file) says to use unleaded petrol only. It recommends RON 91 or higher for one model type and RON 95 or higher for another, allows petrol containing ethanol up to 10% by volume, and warns against petrol with a high alcohol content, old or dirty petrol and petrol mixed with oil. It also says that petrol below the recommended octane lowers engine performance.

That is one manufacturer's manual for one model, and other models may differ. This guide does not quote their manuals, so ask the shop which grade your bike takes. [What petrol to use in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-fuel-what-petrol/' | relative_url }}) covers choosing a grade at the pump. Do not experiment with anything labelled for two-strokes or racing fuel.

## Practical habits

- Fill when the gauge hits the last bar rather than pushing your luck; walking a dead bike to a station in 35-degree heat is a self-inflicted problem.
- Note the odometer at handover; on long rentals the fuel spend tells you if the bike is running right - sudden thirst is an early sign something needs the shop's inspection.
- Keep fuel receipts on delivery-day rentals so the return-level conversation is short.
- Fill the same way each time: a bike leaning on its side stand may take less petrol than the same bike upright, so stop at the same point and use the centre stand if the bike has one.
- If the agreed level has to be matched at return, refuel on the last stretch rather than the day before, so that riding to the shop does not change it.

## When you bring the bike back: what to ask

Compare the gauge with your handover photograph before you hand over the key. [Returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the whole return; this table is the fuel part.

| At return | What to ask | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| The gauge matches your handover photograph | Ask for the end of the rental, fuel included, to be confirmed in the chat | The handover and return photographs |
| The gauge is below the agreed level | Whether you can refuel before handing over, or how a shortfall is charged: litres, rate per litre, and whether it is taken from the deposit or paid separately | A receipt from your last fill |
| The gauge is above the agreed level | Whether any credit applies; none is published, so do not count on one | The handover photograph |
| The level was never agreed or photographed | What level the shop expects, before you ride back and not at the counter | A photograph of the gauge and the odometer now |
| The gauge looks wrong | Ask the shop to look at the bike with you and to note it in the chat | Gauge and odometer photographs from both ends |

If a fuel charge is proposed, ask for the litres and the rate in writing before you agree to it, and compare it with your handover record.

## Who pays for what

Fuel is consumption, not damage: under each convention in the table, the petrol you burn is replaced or paid for by you, and what is left to agree is the starting level, the finishing level and what a shortfall costs. The reverse applies too - nobody itemises the half-litre you left in there. A larger surplus is a different matter, so ask whether petrol left above the agreed level earns any credit; none is published. The convention exists to keep both sides honest with the minimum of arithmetic.

## Short answers

### Do I have to return a rental motorbike with a full tank in Hanoi?

Only if full-to-full is what was agreed. No fuel rule is published for Nguyen Tu, so ask which convention applies when you book and keep the answer in the chat.

### Is fuel included in the rental price?

It is not confirmed as part of the rental, so treat it as a question. The [price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the published rates; ask what, if anything, they cover beyond the bike.

### What if the fuel gauge does not work?

Say so in the chat before you ride, photograph the odometer and agree in words how the level will be judged.

### How far will a tank take me?

On the claimed figures, about 215 km on the Wave Alpha 110 and about 202 km on the Sirius RC 110, as the calculation above shows. Real distance varies with speed, load, temperature and terrain, so refuel well before the tank is empty.

### What if the bike runs out of petrol on the road?

No roadside rescue or response time is published, so keep a margin and refuel when the gauge reaches the last bar. If it happens, move the bike somewhere safe and message the shop; [what to do if a rental breaks down]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first minutes at the roadside.
