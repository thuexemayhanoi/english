There is no single motorbike rental fuel policy in Hanoi: shops differ, and none is published for Hanoi Motorbike Rental Nguyen Tu, so treat it as a question to settle before you ride. Agree which convention applies (full-to-full, same level or as-is), the starting level, and what happens if the level is different when you bring the bike back.

The fuel question in a rental comes down to how much is in the tank at each end. This guide sets out the conventions you may be offered, the questions to put in the booking chat, how to record the gauge, how far a tank goes on two models, what Honda's manual says about petrol and filling, and what to ask at the return.

## Conventions you may be offered

One convention you may be offered is to hand over the bike with a small, noted amount of fuel and expect it back at a similar level. Note the level at handover - it goes on the contract or into your handover photos.

The idea behind it is that you return it where you found it and nothing is owed, and that the reverse applies too - nobody itemises the half-litre you left in there. The convention exists to keep both sides honest with the minimum of arithmetic, but whether a particular shop ignores or charges a small difference is a question to ask, not something to assume. Some shops hand over a full tank and expect a full tank back; agree which convention applies before you ride.

The three arrangements below are conventions you may be offered, so read the table as a list of things to ask for.

| Convention | What it means |
|---|---|
| Full-to-full | Handed over with a full tank, returned with a full tank |
| Same level | Handed over at a noted level, returned at that level |
| As-is | Handed over with whatever is in the tank, returned with whatever is left |

Full-to-full leaves the least to interpret, as long as both sides mean the same thing by "full". It needs a pump stop on the way back, so plan that stop if a flight, a train or a hotel check-out follows the return; [the guide to returning a rental motorbike]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lays out the day backwards from your deadline.

Whichever you choose, ask for it in writing. [How to book a motorbike rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows how to turn a message thread into a record, and [what a rental agreement should cover]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists fuel among the terms to read before you sign.

## What to agree before you ride

Put these questions in the booking chat before you collect the bike. The shop is on +84 942 467 674 by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo, open 09:00-21:00 daily, and a written answer is something you can point to when you return.

1. Which convention applies: full-to-full, same level or as-is?
2. What level will the bike be at when you take it?
3. What does "full" mean: the fill limit the manufacturer gives, or until the nozzle stops?
4. If you return it lower, do you refuel first, or is the shortfall charged? At what rate per litre, and is it paid separately or taken from the deposit?
5. If you return it higher, is there any credit?
6. Which petrol does the bike take, and where is the filler cap?
7. If the gauge looks wrong, or you are unsure of the level, who do you tell and when?

Ask, too, whether fuel is part of the rental price: [what a Hanoi motorbike rental includes]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists it among the items to settle before you pay, and the [price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the published rates.

## Recording the fuel gauge at handover

A photograph of the gauge is a simple fuel record, and it needs no tools. Take it before you pay and before you ride, with whoever hands over the bike still there.

1. Turn the ignition on first. A gauge may show nothing with the key off.
2. Take one photograph that shows the gauge and the odometer together, then a second from a slightly different angle in case glare hides the needle.
3. Photograph the filler cap and note how it opens, so that you are not working it out at the pump.
4. Send the photographs in the booking chat with one line in words, such as "gauge at about a quarter, odometer as in the photo", so that the shop holds the same record.
5. Keep the photographs and the chat until the rental and the deposit are settled.

### What a gauge photograph can and cannot show

A needle or a row of bars shows a coarse level, not litres, and the same needle can look different from another angle. That is why the photograph and the line in words belong together: the photograph shows what the gauge said, and the words say what you both took it to mean. Gauges on small bikes can also mislead, as [why a motorbike fuel gauge can mislead]({{ '/articles/fuel-gauge-inaccurate-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains, so the odometer in the same frame is your second record.

Honda's manual for the Wave Alpha says that a needle at the red mark means about 1.02 L is left in the tank, a little over a quarter of its 3.7 L; another model's marks may mean something else, so ask what the last mark on your bike stands for.

If the bike has no gauge, or the needle looks stuck, say so in the chat, photograph the odometer and agree in words how the level will be judged, for example "filled to the manufacturer's fill line". Some bikes use a fuel tap with a reserve position instead of a gauge, which [fuel taps and reserve]({{ '/articles/fuel-tap-reserve-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) explains.

The [checklist article]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) folds the fuel level into the handover walk-around; read it together with this one before you collect the bike.

## The fuel type

Check which petrol the bike you are given takes before the first fill, because the answer depends on the model. Honda's owner's manual for the Wave Alpha (September 2025 file) says to use unleaded petrol only. It lists the Vietnam type at RON 91 or higher, with the RON 95 or higher line belonging to the Myanmar type, and says that petrol below the recommended octane lowers engine performance.

On alcohol, the manual warns against petrol with a high alcohol content, old or dirty petrol and petrol mixed with oil. It allows ethanol-blended petrol, which it says is sold as Gasohol, up to 10% ethanol, and says that more than that can damage the tank's paint and the rubber fuel lines, rust the tank and make the engine run poorly; if the bike runs badly, it advises trying a different petrol.

That is one manufacturer's manual for one model, and other models may differ. This guide does not quote their manuals, so ask the shop which grade your bike takes. [What petrol to use in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-fuel-what-petrol/' | relative_url }}) covers choosing a grade at the pump. Do not experiment with anything labelled for two-strokes or racing fuel.

## Filling the tank and the fuel valve

The Wave Alpha manual's filling steps start with opening the seat, so on that model the filler cap sits under it. You turn the cap anticlockwise until it stops, lift it off and fill no higher than the lower edge of the filler neck; to close, turn the cap clockwise until the arrow marks on the cap and the tank line up, then close the seat. Its warning is to switch the engine off, keep away from heat, sparks and flame, handle petrol outdoors only and wipe up spills at once.

That limit is a manufacturer's fill line for that model, so when you agree what "full" means, ask where the line is on the bike you are given. The manual also describes a two-way fuel valve, ON for normal riding and OFF for parking, storage or transport, and its starting steps begin with making sure the valve is ON; if your bike has one, ask the shop to show you the valve and its positions at handover.

## Practical habits

- Fill when the gauge hits the last bar rather than pushing your luck; walking a dead bike to a station in 35-degree heat is a self-inflicted problem.
- Note the odometer at handover; on long rentals the fuel spend tells you if the bike is running right - sudden thirst is an early sign something needs the shop's inspection.
- Keep fuel receipts on delivery-day rentals so the return-level conversation is short.
- Fill the same way each time: a bike leaning on its side stand may take less petrol than the same bike upright, so stop at the same point and use the centre stand if the bike has one.
- If the agreed level has to be matched at return, refuel on the last stretch rather than the day before, so that riding to the shop does not change it.

## When you bring the bike back: what to ask

Compare the gauge with your handover record before you hand over the key. [Returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the whole return; this table is the fuel part.

| At return | What to ask |
|---|---|
| The gauge matches your handover record | Ask for the end of the rental, fuel included, to be confirmed in the chat |
| The gauge is below the agreed level | Whether you can refuel before handing over; if a charge is proposed, ask for the litres and the rate in writing and compare them with your handover record |
| The gauge is above the agreed level | Whatever you were told about credit; none is published, so do not count on one |
| The level was never agreed | What level the shop expects, before you ride back and not at the counter |
| The gauge looks wrong | Ask the shop to look at the bike with you and to note it in the chat |

## Short answers

### Do I have to return a rental motorbike with a full tank in Hanoi?

Only if full-to-full is what was agreed, so ask which convention applies when you book and keep the answer in the chat.

### What if the bike runs out of petrol on the road?

Keep a margin and refuel when the gauge reaches the last bar, and ask the shop before you ride what help it can give if you run dry. If it happens, move the bike somewhere safe and message the shop; [what to do if a rental breaks down]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first minutes at the roadside.
