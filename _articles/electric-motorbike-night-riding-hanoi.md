Hanoi after dark is a different traffic system: fewer cars, more street-food traffic, unlit trucks reversing, and roads that look the same but hide their potholes better. An electric motorbike adds two night-specific factors to manage — the lights drawing on the same battery as the motor, and a silence that night traffic is not expecting.

## Lights and range

Everything on the bike eats from one pack. The headlight, taillight and display are small loads next to the motor, but they are loads, and night range lands a little under day range — one more reason not to start a night ride on a half-empty machine. The verified line-up shows how lighting quality varies: the VinFast Viper carries LED projector headlights in its published feature list, while other models' lighting should be checked on the actual machine, not the brochure. On any of them, keep the lens clean — Hanoi dust on a headlight is an unnoticed range cut and a visibility cut at once.

## The silence problem, at night

In daylight the silence issue is being overtaken; at night it is being invisible in both senses. Drivers scanning for headlights register a motorbike's single small beam poorly among shop-front glare. The defences are the same ones the visibility guide covers, weighted for night: light-coloured or reflective gear, a lit, high position in the lane at junctions, and patience at unlit junctions where nobody has seen you. Do not rely on the horn at night more than in the day — it is a signal, not a shield.

## Surfaces after dark

Unlit streets hide three things electric riders feel first: sunken manholes (small wheels, direct impact), the polished-slick paint of road markings when the evening damp rises, and parked vehicles that merge into the kerb line. Ride at a speed where the headlight cone gives you stopping distance, not just viewing distance.

## Charging foresight

A night out should end at a charged machine, not start from one. The habit is simple: plug in or swap on arrival home, so the late return is a 100%-battery late return. Night riding rewards the boring disciplines — full pack, clean lens, predictable lane position — and punishes nothing else.
