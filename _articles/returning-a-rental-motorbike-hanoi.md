The return is the mirror of the handover, and it takes ten minutes if the handover was done right. Here is exactly what happens.

## Before you ride back

- Fill or match the agreed fuel level - the fuel convention was set at handover, and matching it avoids any arithmetic at the counter.
- Give the bike a wipe if it is filthy; nobody charges for dust, but a clean bike speeds up the condition check.
- Arrive inside opening hours (09:00-21:00) and on time - late returns are charged by the hour.
- Bring the contract, the key, and your handover photos.

## The walk-around

The shop checks the bike against the state it left in, using the same eyes your photos used: panels and scratches, mirrors, lights, brakes, tyres. If the handover photo set exists, this is a thirty-second comparison, not a negotiation. Fair wear - dust, a few extra kilometres, a scuffed lever - is part of renting; damage beyond that is what the deposit conversation is about.

## The paperwork

Contract closed, bike and keys and registration certificate back together, and any balance settled - extra hours, a fuel shortfall, a consumable that was yours under the agreement. Payments and settlements run in cash, bank transfer or QR as usual.

## The deposit

If the bike matches, the deposit comes back on the spot, normally in cash. Count it before you leave. If there is a dispute, the record is the handover photos and the contract - which is why both articles above are worth their two minutes before you rent.

## Early and late

- Handing back early does not automatically create a refund - unused days are the shop's discretion, not your entitlement. The early-return article covers it.
- Late is charged by the hour (20,000 VND per hour at Nguyen Tu, with a full additional rental day - typically 150,000-200,000 VND - possibly charged beyond six hours). If you know you will be late, message ahead; shops are reasonable about planned extensions they hear about in advance.

Done right, the return is the anticlimax it should be: photos, fuel, handshake, cash.
