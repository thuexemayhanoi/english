The Lexi 155 is Yamaha's comfort-oriented 155: the same Blue Core VVA engine family as the sporty NVX and premium NMAX, but wrapped in a roomier body with 14-inch wheels and an upright, relaxed riding position. All figures below come from the official Yamaha Motor Vietnam Lexi 155 VVA-ABS product page (retrieved 23 September 2026); anything not published there is left out rather than estimated.

## Engine and performance

A 155.1 cc single-cylinder four-stroke, liquid-cooled Blue Core engine with VVA variable valve timing. Official maximum power is 11.3 kW at 8,000 rpm and maximum torque 14.2 Nm at 6,500 rpm - identical headline figures to the NMAX, which is why our Lexi vs NMAX comparison focuses on everything around the engine instead. Published consumption is 2.19 litres/100 km, from the smallest tank in the 155 class at 4.2 litres - worth knowing when planning longer rides; real-world figures vary.

## Brakes and wheels

A front hydraulic disc with ABS and a rear drum - front-ABS only, unlike the NMAX's dual-channel setup (see which scooters have ABS in Vietnam). The wheels are the Lexi's defining feature: 90/90-14 front and 100/90-14 rear, giving it more stability at speed than the 12- and 13-inch wheels common in the class, a trade-off our wheelbase and stability explainer covers.

## Size, weight and comfort

Yamaha lists 118 kg weight, a low 768 mm seat, dimensions 1968 x 719 x 1138 mm, a long 1,353 mm wheelbase and 135 mm ground clearance. That combination - light weight, low seat, long wheelbase, big wheels - is aimed at riders who spend long days in the saddle or carry a pillion regularly.

## Equipment and warranty

VVA engine tuning, front ABS, and Yamaha's published warranty of 3 years or 30,000 km, whichever comes first. Yamaha's list price at retrieval time was 48,900,000 VND for the Lexi 155 VVA-ABS premium version, from 48,500,000 VND across versions. Against Honda's premium-lite offering, our Lexi vs SH Mode 125 comparison weighs the value trade.

## Who it suits

Commuters and weekend riders who want 155-class performance without the aggressive riding position - taller riders in particular get more room than the compact sporty scooters offer. It sits between the Janus/FreeGo budget tier and the NMAX in Yamaha's own range (mapped in our Yamaha lineup guide).
