The battery is the part of an electric motorbike that ages — slowly, predictably, and in ways you can influence. Here is what determines how long a Vietnamese electric pack lasts.

## What the manufacturers claim

Dat Bike publishes the boldest verified figure: the Weaver 200's lithium-ion pack carries a battery life claim of 150,000 km (described as 15 years) and a 3-year warranty. VinFast chose LFP chemistry for its swappable 1.5 kWh packs specifically for durability and safety, and its rental model moves ageing risk off the rider entirely. Treat long claims as best-case: real packs fade by cycle count, storage conditions and heat.

## What ages a pack

- **Cycles.** Every charge-discharge cycle shaves a small amount of capacity. Deeper cycles age faster than shallow ones.
- **Heat.** Vietnam's climate is a battery's hardest test. Packs parked in direct sun age measurably faster; shaded parking is battery maintenance.
- **Sitting empty.** A pack stored flat for months degrades even unused. Store at partial charge.
- **Wrong chargers.** Pack-and-charger pairs are engineered together; substitutions are the classic avoidable killer.

## Habits that extend life

1. Charge to full for the day's riding but avoid leaving packs at 100 percent for days on the charger.
2. Do not habitually run to zero — partial use suits lithium chemistries.
3. Park in shade; charge in ventilated, cool spots.
4. Use the manufacturer's charger, always.
5. On swap models, let the network carry the ageing: rented packs are refreshed by the operator.

Reduced range per charge is the symptom; app diagnostics give numbers. A pack near end-of-life is a replacement decision, not a bike funeral — see our battery [replacement guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-battery-replacement-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for costs and choices.
