Riding gear costs money, and most riders in Vietnam have a gear budget that does not match the catalogue. The good news: protection is not linear with spend — a small amount of money, spent in the right order, buys the large majority of the risk reduction.

## The priority order, argued honestly

First: the helmet — a certified one, fitted properly, fastened always. Nothing else on the list touches this; the style question (full-face vs open-face — full-face-vs-open-face-helmet-vietnam) is worth settling at purchase time. Second: footwear that covers the ankle — flip-flops remain the national injury generator; rider-footwear-motorbike-vietnam covers the logic. Third: gloves — hands are the reflex-down body part in every fall; riding-gloves-vietnam-climate covers the climate-appropriate options. Fourth: a jacket with armour, or armour under clothes when a jacket will not be worn — riding-jacket-vietnam-climate and body-armour-motorbike-riders cover the two routes. Beyond that: the accessories that improve daily riding (covers, locks, storage) — important, but never before the body is covered.

## Why this order

It is built on what actually happens in Vietnamese street falls: head and hands hit almost always; feet and ankles frequently; the covered-by-clothing torso takes road rash and joint impacts. The full dressing philosophy is in what-to-wear-riding-motorbike-vietnam — this article is its budget-ranked companion.

## The fake-gear trap

Budget gear fails at its weakest certification: marketplace replicas of known brands, "armoured" jackets with foam shapes, decorative helmets. The budget answer is not fakes but honest lesser gear: a plain certified helmet from a real shop beats a replica premium one; basic certified armour beats "racing-style" costumes. When money is tight, spend it on the least glamorous real thing.

## The sequencing habit

Buy one level at a time and buy it once: helmet first week, boots second month, gloves before the next rainy season. Riders who buy in priority order are fully covered within a season; riders who buy randomly own three branded T-shirts and no gloves.

Limited money is not a reason to ride unprotected — it is a reason to buy in the order that protects the body parts that fall first.
