A motorbike ridden once a month seems like the easiest life a machine could have. It is not. Low usage is its own wear pattern: everything that needs movement to stay healthy — tyres, seals, batteries, fuel systems — degrades parked, while the parts that need mileage to wear out barely age. The rare-rider's maintenance is about the calendar, not the odometer.

## The four things that rot parked

- **The battery** discharges steadily and, left flat long enough, dies permanently sulfated. The care and charging routine is in battery-care-motorbike-vietnam; for the rarely ridden, a monthly charge (or a maintenance charger) is the single highest-value habit — the slow-drain story is in battery-draining-overnight-motorbike.
- **The tyres** age by heat, UV and time, not by tread wear — a low-usage bike eats tyres by *date*, and the date logic is its own topic in tyre-age-replace-by-date. Flat spots from parking months in one position are real: move the bike, or at least roll it, and keep pressures above normal when parked long.
- **The fuel** goes stale and, worse, the half-empty tank condenses water every humid night: gum in the carburettor or injector and rust in the tank. Keep the tank full, add fresh fuel periodically, and if the bike sits for months, treat the fuel before storage rather than after — the tank-rust story is in fuel-tank-rust-prevention-treatment and the full storage routine is the one this article walks through below.
- **The seals and cables** dry and stiffen: fork seals, brake caliper dust boots, cable internals. Rubber does not know the bike is resting.

## The calendar, not the odometer

For a rare rider, schedule by time: a monthly start-and-roll session (engine to full temperature, brakes exercised, brakes cycled, everything switched on) does more good than any odometer-based interval. Oil, on a low-usage bike, still needs changing by time — combustion byproducts and humidity age oil even at near-zero kilometres; follow the manual's time-based interval rather than distance-based. The general scheduling framework is in motorbike-service-when-vietnam.

## The return-to-service check

After any months-long rest, before riding into traffic:

1. Tyre pressures and sidewalls (cracks from storage are common).
2. Brakes: lever feel, pad discs clean of surface rust — the first applications wipe it; see brake-squeal-grinding-motorbike for what sound is normal.
3. Lights, horn, switches; throttle snap-back.
4. Fluid levels and any weeping under the bike after the first warm-up.
5. A first ride in a quiet area: brakes, steering, any vibration — the after-rest bike has opinions and it shares them early.

## The honest alternative

If a bike will genuinely not be ridden for a season or more, do a real storage routine — full tank, battery tender or removal, treated fuel, covered — rather than a pretend one. A bike parked properly and ridden monthly stays honest; a bike parked carelessly and ridden "when I feel like it" is the bike that greets you with a flat battery, a gummed carburettor and cracked tyres in the same week.
