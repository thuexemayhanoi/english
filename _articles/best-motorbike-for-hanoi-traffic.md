Hanoi traffic is dense, slow and negotiated centimetre by centimetre. The bike that wins it is not the powerful one - it is the narrow, light, calm one.

## What the traffic actually demands

- Narrow width: gaps between buses and taxis are bike-width. Slim underbones and mid-size scooters fit; big bikes do not.
- Low weight: you will put a foot down every few hundred metres. A 100-110 kg scooter is manageable at every light; a heavy cruiser is a gym subscription.
- Automatic transmission: no left hand and left foot doing work while your attention is on six vehicles moving around you.
- Good brakes and horn: stopping hard once a day and being heard constantly are both survival features.
- Storage: under-seat space swallows a helmet, a rain jacket and shopping - one less thing to juggle at every stop.

## What does not work in Hanoi traffic

Big engine sizes add nothing - traffic rarely clears 40 km/h. Manual clutch bikes are work at rush hour. Wide bodywork scrapes things. Anything you would hate to drop at a standstill is the wrong tool.

## The honest ranking for a visitor

1. Mid-size automatic scooter (Vision/Click/Mio class) - the best all-round city tool.
2. Semi-automatic underbone (Wave/Sirius class) - narrower and cheaper, one skill to learn.
3. Licence-free 50 cc or e-bike - the legal answer for riders without a motorcycle licence, at lower speeds.
4. Manual - only for riders who already ride manuals confidently.
