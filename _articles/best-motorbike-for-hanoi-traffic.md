Hanoi traffic is dense, slow and negotiated centimetre by centimetre. The bike that wins it is not the powerful one - it is the narrow, light, calm one.

The best motorbike for Hanoi traffic is therefore a light automatic scooter of the Honda Vision, Honda Click or Yamaha Mio class, or a small semi-automatic underbone if you already shift with your foot, with brakes that bite, mirrors that stay where you put them and a horn that works. The sections below turn that into a handover routine: what each characteristic does in the traffic, what the manufacturers publish, and how to judge the bike you are actually given.

## What the traffic actually demands

- Narrow width: gaps between buses and taxis are bike-width. Slim underbones and mid-size scooters fit; big bikes do not.
- Low weight: you will put a foot down every few hundred metres. A 100-110 kg scooter is manageable at every light; a heavy cruiser is a gym subscription.
- Automatic transmission: no left hand and left foot doing work while your attention is on six vehicles moving around you.
- Good brakes and horn: stopping hard once a day and being heard constantly are both survival features.
- Storage: under-seat space swallows a helmet, a rain jacket and shopping - one less thing to juggle at every stop.

The table pairs the characteristics a handover can test with a check; transmission and storage follow from the class you choose.

| Characteristic | Why it matters | Handover test |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Sets which gaps you can use | Sit on it, hold the bars as in a queue, and judge the room your elbows and mirrors take |
| Weight | Makes each stop and restart easier or harder | Lift it off the stand, roll it back a few metres and hold it upright, both feet down |
| Low-speed control | Holds a steady crawl without surging or stalling | Ride a short stretch at walking pace, straight and through a slow turn |
| Brakes | Stops you when a gap closes | Roll the bike, squeeze each brake in turn and ask which type it has |
| Mirrors | Shows what is beside and behind you | Adjust both in your riding position and check they hold |
| Visibility | Lets others see and hear you | Check headlight, brake light, indicators and horn with the engine running |

## The classes that work

Automatic scooters - the Honda Vision, Honda Click, Yamaha Mio class - are the default answer: light, twist-and-go, under-seat storage, made for exactly this. Semi-automatic underbones - the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius class - are the Vietnamese workhorse: narrower, cheaper, simpler to fix anywhere, with the foot-shift as the single trade-off. Both classes appear on rental lists at 150,000-200,000 VND per day; contact us to confirm current availability.

The gearbox choice has its own guides, so it is not repeated here: [the scooter versus semi-automatic comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-vs-semi-automatic-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets the two types side by side, and [the automatic, semi-automatic or manual guide]({{ '/articles/automatic-vs-manual-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) adds the clutch bikes.

## What does not work in Hanoi traffic

Big engine sizes add nothing when the traffic is this slow. Manual clutch bikes are work at rush hour. Wide bodywork scrapes things. Anything you would hate to drop at a standstill is the wrong tool.

For scale on what "wide" means, the manufacturers publish 740 mm for the Yamaha NMAX 155 and 805 mm for the Honda CT125. That is 51 mm and 116 mm more than the widest of the four rental-class bikes in the next section. Wider bodywork leaves fewer gaps to use, but a width figure alone does not say which gaps are open.

## What the published sizes show

| Bike | Overall width | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Yamaha Sirius (Sirius RC 110 figures) | 665 mm | 100 kg (wet) |
| Honda Vision | 686 mm | 97 kg |
| Honda Air Blade (125 version) | 687 mm | 110 kg |
| Honda Wave (Wave Alpha 110 figures) | 689 mm | 96 kg |

Each row describes the version named in it. The Air Blade is also sold as a 160, and the Wave and Sirius ranges include several versions, so the bike you are handed may differ from the row. Honda states a kerb weight and Yamaha a wet weight, which are not the same basis, so treat a few kilograms as a tie.

### Width

Width sets what fits, and the published widths barely separate the bikes a rider is likely to be handed. The Vision, Air Blade and Wave Alpha sit within 3 mm of each other, which is nothing you will feel on the road. The Sirius is the only one measurably slimmer, by 21 to 24 mm, and even that is small beside a bag on the hook, an elbow held out or a passenger's knees. Use width to rule out wide bikes, not to choose between small ones. No width is quoted here for the Click or Mio, so compare them by sitting on them.

Width answers what fits, not what is allowed. No Vietnamese legal text names lane splitting or filtering, so it is neither expressly permitted nor expressly banned, while Article 13 of Law 36/2024/QH15 still requires a vehicle to travel within a single lane. [The lane splitting guide]({{ '/articles/lane-splitting-legal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out how those rules apply, so a narrow bike is not by itself a reason to use every gap you see.

### Weight

You hold the bike up at every light. On the published figures the Wave Alpha 110 (96 kg), the Vision (97 kg) and the Sirius RC 110 (100 kg wet) are a tie. The Air Blade 125 at 110 kg is 13 kg heavier than the Vision, which is a reason to weigh the bigger scooter against what you really carry. [The commuting guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-commuting-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers storage and the other checks for a daily route.

If you cannot hold it upright comfortably at a standstill, ask for another bike.

## Low-speed control, brakes and visibility

Width and weight can be read off a spec sheet. The three characteristics that follow cannot, so they are worth testing with your own hands.

### Low-speed control

In a crawl, what counts is how smoothly the bike pulls away and how steadily it holds a slow speed. That depends on the throttle, the idle and the steering. Ask whether you can ride a short stretch at walking pace in a straight line and then through a slow turn. The throttle should snap closed when you release it, the engine should idle without cutting out, the bars should turn from lock to lock without snagging, and the bike should not surge when you open the throttle gently.

Honda's Vision page describes an idling-stop system that cuts the engine at a stop and restarts it when the throttle is twisted. If the bike you are given has a system like that, watch it restart before you rely on it at a junction.

### Brakes

In traffic you brake for gaps that close, so what counts is that both brakes bite smoothly and that you know which lever or pedal does what. The published picture is thin, so treat the braking of the exact bike as a question, not an assumption.

Three questions cover it. Which brakes does the bike have at the front and the rear? Does one lever work both wheels, as it does on some scooters with a combined system? Does it have ABS? [The guide to scooters with ABS]({{ '/articles/which-scooters-have-abs-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) shows that ABS can be a version option rather than a feature of a whole model, so do not read it into a model name. Drum or disc matters less than condition, and a worn brake of either kind is a reason to ask for another bike.

Test by rolling the bike on level ground and squeezing one brake at a time: the wheel should stop turning, and the lever or pedal should not travel to the grip or feel spongy. Then, if the shop agrees, brake gently on a clear stretch before you join traffic, and the bike should stop in a straight line. [The scooter versus semi-automatic guide]({{ '/articles/scooter-vs-semi-automatic-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows where each brake sits on each type, which is worth knowing before you reach for one in a hurry.

### Mirrors, lights and horn

Set the mirrors sitting on the bike, in your normal riding position, before you move. Convex mirrors show a wide view but make traffic look smaller and farther away than it is. Treat the mirror as a warning that something is there and turn your head to see where. [The mirror setup guide]({{ '/articles/mirror-setup-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the adjustment.

Check that both mirrors are present, adjustable, free of cracks and firm enough to hold their place when you tap them. If a mirror is loose or missing, ask for it to be fixed before you ride.

Visibility works in both directions. Honda's pages for the Vision and the Wave Alpha 110 both describe a headlight that stays lit whenever the bike is running, and both say it makes the bike easier to notice on the street. The Wave Alpha page adds that this holds in daylight. This guide quotes no such feature for the Sirius, Air Blade, Click or Mio, so look at the headlight when the engine starts.

Then press each brake control to check the brake light, test the indicators, and sound the horn. In Hanoi a short tap on the horn tells people near you that you are there, so a weak horn is worth reporting at handover. [The rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) carries the full walk-round, from photographs to tyres. Two things are worth asking at the counter: which exact version of the bike you are being given, and how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop.

## The honest ranking for a visitor

1. Mid-size automatic scooter (Vision/Click/Mio class) - the best all-round city tool.
2. Semi-automatic underbone (Wave/Sirius class) - narrower and cheaper, one skill to learn.
3. Licence-free moped - the legal answer for riders without a motorcycle licence, at lower speeds. A moped has a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a combustion engine of 50 cc or less or an electric motor of 4 kW or less (Article 34(1)(g), Law 36/2024/QH15); the minimum age is 16 (Article 59(1)(a)) and no licence is needed. Contact us to confirm current availability.
4. Manual - only for riders who already ride manuals confidently.

The list is ordered by rider type, not by a measured score, so read it as what suits whom rather than a league table. "Licence-free" also describes a legal class and not a size: [the 50cc myths guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-motorbike-myths-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains that the licence exemption is the only exemption, so the other traffic rules still apply, and that a pedal-type electric bicycle and a 50cc petrol bike are different legal classes.

## Published costs and paperwork

The published day rates follow the model, not the width or the weight. The Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio are published at 150,000 VND per day, and the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade at 200,000 VND per day, so the two lightest bikes in the size table sit at different rates. For other rental periods and for 50cc bikes and E-Bikes, use [the rental price guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and do not infer one model's price from another's.

A deposit is typically 2,000,000-5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so arrange your own before you ride.

## Common questions

### Do the rental bikes have ABS?

This guide quotes no ABS fitting for any of the six bikes with a published day rate, so do not assume it. Ask which brakes the exact bike has, and check the badge or the listing for the version you are given.

### Which bike suits a first-timer?

An automatic scooter of the Vision, Click or Mio class, because there is no gear to find while you watch the traffic. The beginner guide above covers the first week in more detail.
