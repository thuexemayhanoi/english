Yes, and the student situation is usually the easier one. Foreign students registered at Vietnamese universities are residents by definition — the question is only which route to the licence fits what they already hold. "Easier" is an impression, not something measured, and "residents by definition" is shorthand: Article 24(1)(a) lists people who reside, work or study as separate cases and still asks for a visa or card in term, so enrolment alone is not what the rules check.

A student can reach a legal ride in four ways: exchange a home licence, ride on a 1968 international permit with the home licence for a short stay or an exchange semester, sit the A1 test, or ride a moped from 16 with no licence. The table sets them against the situations a student may be in.

## The routes at a glance

| Student situation | Route and legal basis | What it needs | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home licence with a motorbike class that has not expired, and a visa or temporary residence card whose remaining term is 90 days or more, or a permanent card | Exchange under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA (Articles 18(7)(a), 24) | Translated licence, health certificate unless the visa-or-card exception applies, copy of the visa or card (Article 24(2)) | [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| Home licence plus a 1968 Convention permit, both in term | The pair: Law 36/2024/QH15, Articles 57(6)(b) and 57(8) | Both documents with you, each fitting the bike | [The motorbike IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) |
| No motorbike licence, aged 18 or over | A1 test: Law, Article 59(1)(b); Circular, Article 16(2) | Course certificate, health certificate, copy of the visa or card | [The A1 step-by-step guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) |
| Aged 16 or 17, or not yet licensed | Moped class: Law, Articles 34(1)(g) and 59(1)(a) | A bike within every moped limit | [The legal definition of a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) |
| Only a permit, or a home licence whose term has ended | Neither route: the pair needs both documents in term (Article 57(7)(a), 57(8)(b)) and the exchange excludes a permit and an expired licence (Article 24(1)(c)) | The test route or the moped class | Rows three and four |

A foreigner who exchanges for the term on the entry visa or temporary residence card is excepted from the health certificate (Article 24(2)(b)); [the exchange document checklist]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains it.

## If you hold a motorbike licence from home

Students with a valid national licence including a motorbike category exchange it under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24, which superseded Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA from 1 July 2026 (Article 41(6)). The residence condition — a visa or temporary residence card whose remaining term is 90 days or more, or a permanent residence card — can be met by the paper a student already holds; a student in a university dormitory can ask the international office which paper that is and when it ends, because the Circular's list names the visa or card and no dormitory record. The translation and certification requirements for the licence are the same as for any other applicant.

### What Article 24(1)(a) says about people who study

The clause is written for any foreigner who "resides, works or studies in Vietnam" ("cư trú, làm việc, học tập tại Việt Nam"). Studying stands beside living and working, so a student has no separate list of papers and no special rule.

The same sentence asks for a national licence still in term and a need to drive here. The Circular's verb is "considered" (được xét đổi): the office decides, and a refusal needs a written answer with the reason (Article 24(3)(c)). Article 24(2) lists three items for the dossier, and none is an enrolment letter or a dormitory record.

### How the visa or card term sets the licence term

Three things shape the term of an exchanged licence under Article 24(5)(a): the term printed on the visa, card or other Article 24(1)(a) paper behind the application, the term of the foreign licence, and the maximum the Law sets for the Vietnamese class. For a motorbike class that maximum is no limit at all (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 57(5)(a)), so the printed date comes from the papers behind the exchange.

The clause gives no formula. The table applies one reading, the earlier of the two printed dates, to invented dates; it is arithmetic, not a promise about what an office will print, and the date on the issued licence is the one that counts.

| Home licence ends | Visa or card ends | Earlier date |
|---|---|---|
| 14 March 2031 | 30 June 2027 | 30 June 2027, so the card sets the end |
| 15 January 2027 | 30 June 2028 | 15 January 2027, so the home licence sets the end |

The 90-day condition is arithmetic too. Imagine a student who files on 10 February 2027: ninety days later is 11 May 2027, so a card ending on 30 April 2027 has 79 days left on the filing date and one ending on 20 May 2027 has 99. Nothing in the text says which day the count runs to, so a student near the line should ask the reception point which day it counts to.

### When the card is renewed or the studies end

Article 24 sets the term when the licence is issued and describes no extension. A licence past its printed date lapses (Article 57(7)(a)), and the text does not say whether renewing the visa or card moves that date, so ask the licensing office before renewing, not after the licence has lapsed.

## A short stay or an exchange semester: the permit pair

Under Article 57(6)(b) of the Law, a permit issued by a Vienna Convention state is valid in Vietnam together with that state's national licence, when both suit the vehicle; Article 57(8)(b) asks the holder to carry both, in term. For a student here for one semester or a short programme, the pair is one lawful way to ride; [the guide to motorbike permits]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) explains which permits and classes count.

### The two dates that limit the pair

Article 57(8)(a) sets the permit's life at three years from issue at most and requires it to fit within the national licence's remaining validity. The three years run from issue, not arrival, so a one-semester student should check that both documents are in term on the last day of riding, not only on arrival.

A longer programme changes the sum. Imagine a student who arrives on 1 September 2026 with a permit issued on 8 January 2025 and a home licence ending in 2031, and who plans to ride until 30 June 2028. Three years from issue is 8 January 2028, a little under six months before the last riding day, so the pair stops first and the student needs another route, such as the exchange if the papers qualify. The dates are invented; [how long an IDP stays valid]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works through the clause with more dates.

### Why the pair is the exception, not the default

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(7)(a), tells a foreigner who holds a national licence and wants to drive in Vietnam to go through the exchange; point (b) lets a driving-licence treaty that Vietnam has joined provide otherwise. The 1968 Convention pair looks like that treaty route, though Article 18(7) names no convention.

Neither clause sets a date by which a student who stays for years must move from the pair to the exchange. A student on a multi-year programme can ask the traffic police which route they accept, get the reply on paper and keep a copy alongside the licence documents. The permit cannot be traded in: Article 24(1)(c) excludes a permit from the exchange. [The long-term expat route guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) weighs the routes for a resident.

## If you hold no motorbike licence

The A1 test route is open from age 18: training course, theory paper, practical circuit. The exam is in Vietnamese only, so students with limited Vietnamese usually enrol through a centre experienced with foreign candidates — some universities' international offices can point to one. That is a general impression rather than a rule, and whether a particular international office can recommend a centre is a question to put to it. "Vietnamese only" is a working assumption, not a quoted rule, and [whether the test can be taken in English]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}) sets out what the Circular says: no provision for an English paper, though no article states that the standard paper is in Vietnamese.

This page quotes no fee, so ask the centre or the test office for the current figure. A licence issued after the test has no time limit (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 57(5)(a)), unlike an exchanged licence, whose term follows its papers.

### What a foreign candidate's file holds

The training centre submits the test application with each candidate's file (Article 16(1)). Under Article 16(2) the personal file holds the training certificate or confirmation of completing the course, a health certificate in force and, for a foreign candidate only, a copy of a residence paper of the kind the exchange also needs, so the 90-day visa or card question returns at the test.

Holding a car licence in force waives the theory paper for class A1 or A (Article 4(1)(c)); the clause is silent on a car licence issued abroad, so a student with one should ask the test office before relying on it. The format also depends on the sitting date: Article 41(2) applies new test content from 1 March 2027, while Article 41(6) keeps some provisions of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA alive until 28 February 2027. [The A1 licence steps]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cover the rest of the procedure.

## The licence-free option students overlook

Students under 18, and riders of any age who prefer the lightest legal option, can ride the [licence-free categories]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}): mopeds and licence-free electrics from 16, pedal e-bikes under the pedal-bike rules. For campus-and-market radius riding, this is often the pragmatic answer for the first semester while the licence paperwork catches up.

### What the moped class covers

Under Article 34(1)(g) a moped runs on two or three wheels and is limited three ways: a design speed of at most 50 km/h, at most 50 cm3 if the engine is a combustion engine, and at most 4 kW if the motor is electric. Article 59(1)(a) sets 16 as the minimum age. Article 56(1) leaves the moped rider outside the licence duty, and Article 56(4) instead requires knowing the traffic rules and being able to control the vehicle.

The speed limit and the limit for the bike's own motor both apply. Scooters of 110 cc and 125 cc are above the moped line and need a licence valid in Vietnam that suits their class, which can be issued from 18 (Article 59(1)(b)); [what counts as a moped under Vietnamese law]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) shows how to check a bike against the limits.

## Practical notes

The student visa itself is not the licence document — it is one of the papers the exchange and the test file ask for. Students planning multi-year stays should complete the exchange early, because Article 18(7)(a) sends a foreigner with a national licence who wants to drive here to the exchange unless a treaty provides otherwise. And the points system applies once the Vietnamese licence exists: student riders accumulate deductions like everyone else. Article 58(1) of the Law sets the balance at 12 points.

Riding above the moped class without a licence valid in Vietnam is an offence. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives figures from the 2024 decree text, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP revised from 15 August 2026; check any figure there against the amended decree. This page quotes none.

## Questions to take to the international office and the counter

1. Which residence paper do I hold, and what end date is printed on it?
2. How many days will remain on it on the day I file? The condition is 90 days or more.
3. Does the international office know a training centre that has worked with foreign candidates?
4. Does my health, travel or student insurance cover riding a motorbike, and does it set a condition on the licence?
5. Does the university set a rule of its own on student motorbikes or campus parking?
6. Where do I file the exchange dossier in Hanoi, in person or online? Article 24(3)(a) lists the Traffic Police Department, the National Public Service Portal, and commune-level police offices where a reception point has been set up.

## Quick answers

### Is a student visa enough to ride a motorbike?

No. Article 56(1) asks a rider above the moped class for a licence that is in term, has points left and suits the bike, and a visa is not a licence. A visa with 90 days or more to run is one of the papers the exchange and the test file ask for: it opens a route and is not the route itself.

### Do I need a permit if I plan to exchange?

The exchange needs the national licence, not the permit (Article 24(1)(c)), and the slip given at filing is described in Article 24(3)(c) only as a slip for collecting the result. Until the Vietnamese licence is issued, a rider with a permit and a national licence, both in term, is on the pair (Article 57(6)(b)).

### Can I exchange if my visa has under 90 days left?

Not on that visa: the 90-day condition sits in Article 24(1)(a). Which other paper you can hold is a question for the immigration authority, since the Circular only sets the condition. The pair names no visa term (Article 57(8)).
