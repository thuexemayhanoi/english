Vietnamese upholsterers are among the best value trades in the country: a scooter seat rebuilt with better foam and a new cover costs a fraction of its Western equivalent and can transform a long ride. If a gel pad is a bandage, a re-foamed seat is the cure.

## What a rebuild involves

The old cover comes off, the dead foam comes off, and new foam is shaped — usually denser foam layered with a softer top, and sometimes reshaped: slightly wider for sit bones, subtly dished to stop you sliding forward, or lowered for shorter riders. The new cover is stitched and stapled on; good shops offer waterproof-grade covers with better seams than stock, and can add a grippy centre panel so you stop sliding in the rain.

## Who benefits

Riders whose foam has collapsed (you can feel the seat pan through the seat), taller riders who want legroom via a flatter profile, riders who slide forward under braking, and anyone whose seat pan has cracked — the cause of many mysterious saddle pains. ## The process and the pitfalls

Go to a real upholsterer with motorbike experience, sit on the bike with them watching, and describe where it hurts. Ask for firmer rather than softer — soft foam feels great in the shop and dies in an hour. A seat raised even a couple of centimetres changes your reach at stops; test. And if the bike is a rental, seat rebuilds are a modification: ask the owner first.

## Value

A rebuilt seat costs little relative to what riders spend chasing comfort with accessories, and it survives the accessories: at resale, a fresh, well-made seat reads as a cared-for bike (preparing-motorbike-for-sale-vietnam).
