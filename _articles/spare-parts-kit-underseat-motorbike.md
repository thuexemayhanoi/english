Vietnamese roadside repair culture is a marvel: within a short ride of nearly anywhere, a roadside stand can fix most things. "Nearly anywhere" is the gap an under-seat spare kit exists to close — and the gap is exactly where problems always happen.

## The spares that earn their space

Bulbs: a headlight or brake-light bulb dying at night is both dangerous and a five-minute fix — with a bulb (the replacement logic is in headlight-bulb-replacement-motorbike). Fuses: cheap, tiny, and the difference between riding home and pushing — matched to the bike's fuse values, stated in the manual. Spark plug and the plug spanner that fits it: for older carburetted bikes especially. Inner tube (for tube-type wheels) or a puncture-repair consideration: the flat-tyre reality and the roadside repair economics are covered in flat-tyre-repair-motorbike-vietnam — carrying a tube can matter when the nearest repair stand is far or unsure of your odd size. Small hose/clamp for fuel and vacuum lines. Cable ties and a patch of tape — not parts, but the closest thing to them.

## What stays home

Heavy tools, spare oil (bulky and messy), anything whose replacement needs a workshop's press or stands anyway — carrying them is cargo, not capability. The dividing line: only spares that convert a strand into a ride, installed with hand tools. The tools themselves are a separate list — the dedicated rider toolkit is covered in motorbike-rider-toolkit-essentials; the spares kit assumes those tools are already there.

## Where and how to carry

Under the seat (where the model provides the space) in a sealed bag — humidity and rain rust spares as efficiently as it rusts everything else; a rusted spare is cargo too. Check the kit occasionally: a bulb that has rattled around loose in a bag of tools for a year may already be broken. And know the bike's reality: many modern scooters trade under-seat storage for a fuel tank or battery, so the kit may live in a top box instead — the storage options are covered in top-boxes-racks-motorbike-vietnam thinking.

## The habit that finishes it

Knowing where the fuse box and bulb holders are, before the night it matters, is half the spare's value. Practice the bulb swap once. The fuel-side contingency belongs to the reserve tap — fuel-tap-reserve-motorbike — and between the tap, the tube and the bulbs, the classic Vietnamese strandings are all covered by a bag that fits under a seat.
