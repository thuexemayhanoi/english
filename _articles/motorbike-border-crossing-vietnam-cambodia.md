The flat Mekong provinces south of Saigon end at the Cambodia border, and the crossing most travellers know is Moc Bai — the big gate on the Saigon-Phnom Penh road. Crossing it with a motorbike is possible, occasionally smooth, and consistently more complicated than crossing without one. As with the Laos border, rules change and enforcement varies: treat this as a map of the problem, not a guarantee.

## The border gates

The main Vietnam-Cambodia international crossings include Moc Bai (Tay Ninh → Bavet), Xa Mat, Tinh Bien (An Giang) and Ha Tien (→ Prek Chak). All process tourist visas for Cambodia for most nationalities (e-visa holders must use gates that accept them). Moc Bai is the busiest and the one all Saigon-Phnom Penh buses use; Ha Tien is the most convenient for travellers coming from Phu Quoc via ferry.

## What the paperwork looks like

Forum reports and travel write-ups agree on the shape of it:

- The bike's registration must be in order and, critically, linked to the person riding it — rider accounts of taking Vietnamese bikes into Cambodia describe checks on the bluecard/ownership document and purchase contracts.
- The rider needs a passport, a Cambodian visa, and — per rider reports — vehicle paperwork processed at the border, with insurance certificates often arranged in the border shops on the spot.
- Smaller, non-international gates may refuse vehicles entirely even when locals walk through; vehicle crossing is realistically an international-gate affair.
- Reports vary by year and gate: some riders describe smooth processing, others describe refusals and turnarounds. Anecdotal success is not policy, and neither is anecdotal failure.

## The rental-bike question

The pattern will be familiar from [Laos guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-border-crossing-vietnam-laos/' | relative_url }}): a rental bike is not in your name, and a bike not in your name does not cross. Rental agreements from HCMC shops do not include cross-border clauses, and no deposit you paid covers a bike that leaves the country. If you rented in Saigon, ride the delta and come back — see our Saigon rental guide for what that region offers.

## What travellers actually do

- **Bus it.** Saigon-Phnom Penh is a 6-hour bus run through Moc Bai, cheap and frequent. Rent in Phnom Penh or Siem Reap instead — Cambodia has its own rental scene.
- **Fly-and-rent.** For Angkor-bound trips this is what nearly everyone does.
- **Ride to the border, walk through, tour the Cambodian side independently**, then return for the bike. The Vietnamese side (Chau Doc, Ha Tien, the delta) is excellent riding in itself.
- **Organised cross-border motorcycle tours** with permits, support vehicles and guides do exist for overlanders with their own registered bikes.

## Before you plan around a crossing

- Verify current gate policy with official Vietnamese and Cambodian sources — rules for vehicles have changed repeatedly, and blog reports age fast.
- Check licence requirements on both sides: a Vietnamese licence does not authorise you in Cambodia, and an IDP may or may not be accepted — see our licence [overview]({{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}) for the Vietnamese side.
- Read our travel-insurance guide: crossing a border on a motorbike is a common policy exclusion, and hospitals in rural Cambodia are a long way from anywhere.

The short version: the border is easy to cross; your motorbike is the hard part. Plan a ride that treats the border as a destination, not an obstacle.
