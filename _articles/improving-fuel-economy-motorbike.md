Motorbikes are already cheap to run, but in Hanoi traffic a badly ridden bike can use half again as much fuel as a well-ridden identical one. The difference is not the bike — it is the right wrist, plus a few maintenance basics.

## The riding habits that matter most

Smooth throttle changes beat aggressive ones: every hard launch and full-throttle squirt out of a junction burns extra fuel for seconds you do not gain. Ride at steady speeds where the road allows, and look far ahead so you roll off early for lights instead of braking hard and re-accelerating. Idling a cold engine for many minutes warms nothing faster than riding gently does.

## The maintenance that pays

Tyre pressure is the quiet one: soft tyres increase rolling resistance and wear — tyre-care-motorbike-vietnam explains how to check. A clogged air filter richens the mixture and drinks fuel — air-filter-care-motorbike-vietnam. Engine oil past its best increases internal friction — checking-engine-oil-motorbike. A bike that is serviced on time motorbike-service-when-vietnam simply uses less than one that is not.

## The weight and drag list

Carrying a passenger, a full top box or a week of shopping costs fuel on every trip. A box left on for the one day a month it is needed is a permanent penalty; so is a windshield big enough to catch city air. Remove what you do not need for the riding you actually do.

## Fuel quality and myths

Use the petrol your owner's manual specifies; a higher octane than the engine is designed for does not add power or economy. No additive in a bottle recovers what bad habits waste — and rental riders should note the fuel policy basics in rental-motorbike-fuel-policy-hanoi before assuming who fills what.

## What to expect

A healthy underbone or scooter ridden smoothly in the city is very economical; the same bike ridden flat-out everywhere is not. If your fuel use suddenly jumps with no change in riding, suspect a clogged filter, dragging brakes or a rich carburettor — that is the signal to see a mechanic rather than buy fuel-saving gadgets.
