Half the riders in Hanoi navigate by phone, and the phone lives either in a pocket (requiring stops) or on a mount (requiring judgment). A mount that holds the phone is the easy part; a mount that holds the phone through Vietnamese potholes, rain and vibration is the actual question.

## The vibration problem nobody mentions

Motorbike handlebars vibrate, and sustained vibration is bad for phone camera stabilisation hardware — the classic failure is a rattling camera after months on a cheap mount. Quality mounts dampen vibration; some riders add a small damper or mount on the mirror stalk instead. This is the quiet reason a "solid" cheap mount costs more than it seems.

## Types, honestly compared

Claw-and-side-arm mounts grip the phone's corners: best all-round hold for rough roads. Mag-style magnetic mounts are quickest one-handed but ask whether the magnet plus Hanoi potholes plus your case is a trustworthy combination — good ones are, weak ones launch phones. Silicone-band cradles are cheap and fine for smooth streets and marginal for the rest. Whatever the type, the test before trusting it: fit the phone, grab it, try to shake it off over a rough patch at walking pace, then check again after a rainy ride.

## Weather and placement

Rain-season use means a mount with some weather shielding or a phone case that tolerates it; placement matters for glare and for keeping the screen readable at a glance — low, near the cluster, so eyes drop minimally. And the legality/safety line: glancing at navigation is not the same as interacting with it — set the route before moving, and re-route only when stopped; Vietnamese traffic police take a dim view of riding while handling a phone.
