An International Driving Permit (IDP) is the document that makes many foreign licences usable in Vietnam — but only the right kind.

Short answer: international driving permits in Vietnam count when the permit was issued by a 1968 Vienna Convention state and is carried together with the national licence it goes with, and both documents cover the class of bike you ride (Article 57(6)(b) and (8) of the Law). A permit on its own or one issued by Vietnam does not make a rider licensed here, a 1949 Geneva permit is not the permit those clauses name, and a moped needs no licence at all.

## Which IDP counts

Article 57(6)(b) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) recognises IDPs and national licences issued by [countries that are members]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) of the **[1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic]({{ '/articles/1968-vienna-convention-idp-vietnam/' | relative_url }})**, provided the licence is appropriate to the vehicle being ridden. Rules of thumb:

- Carry both your national licence and the IDP — the IDP is a translation, not a standalone licence. Riding on a permit without the matching national licence breaches the carrying duty in Article 57(8)(b); the fine is set in Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended from 15 August 2026, so this page quotes no amount; check the [no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) figures against it
- Your national licence must itself include motorcycles; an IDP cannot add categories you do not hold
- An IDP under the 1949 Geneva Convention is not the basis for legal riding in Vietnam
- Photocopies and photos of documents are not substitutes

### The rule as the Law writes it

Two clauses of Article 57 carry the whole rule. The quotations below are our translation of the Vietnamese text in the consolidated version, 55/VBHN-VPQH.

Clause 6 lists the licences valid for use within Vietnamese territory, and its point (b) names "an international driving licence and a national driving licence, suited to the type of vehicle the holder may drive, issued by a state that is a member of the 1968 United Nations Convention on Road Traffic (the Vienna Convention)".

Clause 8 describes the permit. Under point (a) it is issued "in a uniform form" by the competent state authority of a Vienna Convention member state, lasts "no more than 03 years from the date of issue" and "must be consistent with the remaining validity of the national driving licence". Under point (b), a person holding such a permit "must carry the international driving licence and the national driving licence, issued to suit the class of vehicle being driven and still within their term" when driving on the road in Vietnam, and must comply with Vietnamese road traffic law.

Read together, the clauses give two points; they are our reading, not extra rules. The suitability test appears in both: type of vehicle in 6(b), class of vehicle in 8(b), which makes it a condition of the rule. And 8(b) puts a permit holder under the same traffic law as any other rider; clause 8(c) caps a fixed-period ban on using the licence at the driver's permitted stay in Vietnam. For the three-year limit and the two expiry dates, read [how long an IDP lasts]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Which motorbike class the permit must show

The Law asks that the documents suit the vehicle; it names no word or letter that must appear on a permit. For a motorbike, each document's entry has to cover a motorcycle of your bike's size, and, in our reading, the size limits in Article 57(1) are the yardstick; no text says how an official applies them to a foreign category.

| Bike | Vietnamese class | What to look for on your pair |
|---|---|---|
| Moped as defined in Article 34(1)(g), see below | None | Nothing; no licence is needed (Articles 34(1)(g) and 56(1)) |
| Two-wheeler up to 125 cm3, or electric up to 11 kW | A1 | A motorcycle category that covers this size, on both documents |
| Two-wheeler above 125 cm3, or electric above 11 kW | A | A category that covers larger motorcycles, on both documents |

No text read for this page gives a conversion table for foreign permits; Appendix XII of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA covers only IDPs issued in Vietnam against Vietnamese licences issued before 1 January 2025. Article 28 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA ties the classes on an IDP issued in Vietnam to the classes of the Vietnamese licence, and none of Articles 27 to 30 maps the category letters of a foreign permit to A1 or A. So settle the match before you rent: read the category list on the permit and on the national licence, compare it with the engine size or motor rating of the bike, and ask the issuing authority if your licence is a restricted or power-limited motorcycle class. A permit with no two-wheel category, or a national licence with car classes only, leaves you at the moped line.

## What does not work

### A permit without its national licence

The pair is the licence, so the permit alone is not one. Article 57(8)(b) requires both to be carried, and the national licence sets your categories and your dates: a licence past its term is not valid (Article 57(7)(a)), and the permit has to stay within the national licence's remaining validity (Article 57(8)(a)).

### A 1949 Geneva permit

Clauses 6(b) and 8(a) name the 1968 Vienna Convention, and Article 57 never mentions the 1949 Geneva Convention. A permit of that older kind therefore falls outside the IDP rule, however official it looks; the treaty routes and the moped class below show what remains.

### A permit issued by Vietnam

Article 30(1) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, on the use of IDPs, says an IDP issued by Vietnam under the 1968 Convention on Road Traffic "has no validity for use within the territory of Vietnam". The question comes up because Article 29 lets Vietnam issue permits: to Vietnamese citizens and foreigners who hold a valid Vietnamese licence (29(1)), and to those with a permanent or temporary residence card who hold a valid national licence from a state party to a treaty Vietnam has joined (29(2)); Article 57(8)(d) of the Law is the basis. Such a permit has no force inside Vietnam, so it is no shortcut to riding here.

## Other legal routes: treaties and the moped class

### Treaty routes

Article 57(6) lists two more kinds of licence valid in Vietnam: under point (c), a licence suited to the vehicle from a country or territory with which Vietnam has signed a treaty or agreement on mutual recognition of licences; under point (d), a foreign licence suited to the vehicle from a state recognised under an international treaty to which Vietnam is a party. Article 30(2) of the Circular adds that an IDP recognised under such a treaty is handled according to the treaty itself.

This page names no country under either point. Which treaties exist, and what each one says, is for an official source to confirm, in writing, before you build a trip on it. Without a 1968 pair or a treaty licence, [riders from non-convention countries]({{ '/articles/non-1968-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) have the remaining options.

### The moped class

One class of motorbike needs no licence at all, so it needs no permit either. Article 34(1)(g) describes a moped by three limits: a design speed of 50 km/h at most, and either a combustion engine of 50 cm3 or less or an electric motor of 4 kW or less. Article 56(1) leaves moped riders out of the licence duty, Article 59(1)(a) sets the age at 16, and Article 56(4) asks the rider to know the traffic rules and control the vehicle. The test is the definition, not the label on the bike, so ask for the design speed and the engine or motor figure. [Renting without a licence]({{ '/articles/renting-a-motorbike-hanoi-without-licence/' | relative_url }}) covers the risks above the class.

## Exchanging a foreign licence for a Vietnamese one

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA (in force from 1 July 2026) governs licence testing, issuance and exchange. Under Article 24(1)(a), foreigners residing, working or studying in Vietnam can be considered for exchange of a valid foreign **national** licence into the corresponding Vietnamese class, provided they hold one of the identity documents in Article 24(1)(a), where a visa or temporary residence card must have 90 days or more left. IDPs themselves are not exchangeable — only national licences are. Article 24(1)(c) is the clause that excludes a permit, along with a licence past its term.

For long-term residents, teachers and students, exchange is usually the cleaner option than relying on an IDP indefinitely. The Law sets no day by which a resident must exchange: Article 57(6)(b) names no length of stay, and Article 24(1)(a) speaks of a foreigner who "needs to drive in Vietnam". Article 18(7) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA goes further: where a foreigner holds a national licence and needs to drive in Vietnam, the exchange procedure into the corresponding Vietnamese licence is required, unless a treaty on driving licences to which Vietnam is a party provides otherwise (point b). How that sits beside the 1968 pair on a long stay is for the traffic police to confirm in writing.

Requirements can change; the traffic police have already proposed amendments to Circular 12, so confirm current paperwork before applying. In the event, Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA replaced Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA (Article 41(6)), so any source that still cites Circular 12/2025 describes the previous text (apart from the provisions Article 41(6) keeps in force until the end of 28 February 2027). The [foreign licence exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes you through who qualifies, the dossier and where to file.

## What to carry on the road

Article 56(1) lists what a rider carries: the vehicle registration certificate, the licence that suits the vehicle, an inspection certificate where the law requires one for that vehicle, and the vehicle owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate. For an IDP rider the licence item is the pair under Article 57(8)(b), carried as originals in one place you can reach without unpacking a bag.

Papers that are already linked to an electronic identification account can be shown through that account instead (Article 56(3)); an image in a phone's camera roll is not that, so keep the originals on you. The registration and insurance papers belong to the bike, so ask at handover how they will be shown if you are stopped. [What a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the full list, and [documents needed to rent a motorbike]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) separates what a counter asks for from what the law asks of the rider.

## If you are stopped

Stay calm, hand over the requested documents, and do not hand your passport to anyone who is not a police officer. If your documents are not in order, a fine is the normal outcome; the serious consequences arrive only after an accident. That is a general expectation, not a promise about any one stop. The penalty depends on the offence and the decree in force, and a stop can also end with the bike being held until the papers are produced: [when police can hold a motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the cases, and [stopped by traffic police]({{ '/articles/stopped-by-traffic-police-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the stop itself.

## Insurance: your own policy decides

An IDP is a licence document, not cover. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, and customers arrange their own. The compulsory civil-liability certificate in Article 56(1)(d) is the vehicle owner's paper, a separate matter from cover for you; your travel or medical policy is where the answer sits, and its wording decides.

Read it for any licence condition, any engine-size limit and any exclusion for motorcycling, then ask the insurer in writing whether the licence pair you hold is accepted for the bike you plan to ride in Vietnam, and keep the reply. The [travel insurance fine-print guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-travel-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has more on policy wording.

## Quick answers

### Can I ride a scooter in Vietnam with only an international licence?

Not above the moped class. Article 57(6)(b) names the permit and the national licence together, and Article 57(8)(b) requires you to carry both.

### Is a national licence printed in English enough without an IDP?

Point 6(b) names no exception for a licence that is already in English or another language; it lists the pair. Unless a treaty route under point (c) or (d) applies to your licence, plan on carrying both documents.

### Does an IDP bought online count?

Article 57(8)(a) describes a permit as one issued in the uniform form by the competent state authority of a Vienna Convention member state. A document from a seller with no such authority behind it does not fit that description; your own government's website says which body issues IDPs in your country.

### If a rental business accepts my paperwork, am I covered?

The licence duty in Article 56(1) falls on the person riding, so what a business accepts at the counter does not change what a police officer checks or what an insurer reads. Ask the business what it expects to see, and get your insurer's answer in writing.
