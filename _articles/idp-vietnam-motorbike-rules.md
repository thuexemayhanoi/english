An International Driving Permit (IDP) is the document that makes many foreign licences usable in Vietnam — but only the right kind.

## Which IDP counts

Article 57(8) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) recognises IDPs and national licences issued by countries that are members of the **1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic**, provided the licence is appropriate to the vehicle being ridden. Rules of thumb:

- Carry both your national licence and the IDP — the IDP is a translation, not a standalone licence. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP (Article 18) fines 1968-convention IDP holders who ride without carrying the matching national licence 2–4 million VND
- Your national licence must itself include motorcycles; an IDP cannot add categories you do not hold
- An IDP under the 1949 Geneva Convention is not the basis for legal riding in Vietnam
- Photocopies and photos of documents are not substitutes

## Exchanging a foreign licence for a Vietnamese one

Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA (28 February 2025) governs licence testing, issuance and exchange. Foreigners residing, working or studying in Vietnam can exchange a valid foreign **national** licence for the corresponding Vietnamese licence without a new driving test, provided they hold a diplomatic identity card, service identity card, or a temporary or permanent residence card valid for 3 months or more. IDPs themselves are not exchangeable — only national licences are.

For long-term residents, teachers and students, exchange is usually the cleaner option than relying on an IDP indefinitely. Requirements can change; the traffic police have already proposed amendments to Circular 12, so confirm current paperwork before applying.

## If you are stopped

Stay calm, hand over the requested documents, and do not hand your passport to anyone who is not a police officer. If your documents are not in order, a fine is the normal outcome; the serious consequences arrive only after an accident.
