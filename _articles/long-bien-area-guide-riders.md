Long Bien is the district that Hanoi's postcards miss. East of the Red River, it is where the city's cargo moves — fruit trucks from the orchards of the north, vegetables, flowers — and where some of the most interesting riding in Hanoi hides in plain sight, between the historic bridge, the dyke and the market streets.

## The bridge that defines the district

The Long Bien Bridge, the cantilever structure built in 1902, is the district's spine. It is motorbike, bicycle and pedestrian only — cars use Chuong Duong and the newer bridges — and after a full closure for repairs (28 March to 27 May 2026) it reopened to two-way motorbike traffic. Riding it is a Hanoi essential: the middle track carries the railway, the side lanes carry riders inches apart, and the deck gives the best view of the river islands anywhere in the city. Its own guide covers the crossing in detail.

## The market streets

Long Bien's economy runs at hours that surprise visitors. The fruit wholesale market (Long Bien market, under the bridge approach) peaks through the night and early morning: trucks, trolleys, porters with shoulder poles and scooters stacked with crates share the streets under sodium light. Riding there at 04:00 is a masterclass in defensive riding and one of the most atmospheric experiences in the city; riding there at midday is easy, with wide streets and light flow. The flower market a short ride north runs on a similar nocturnal clock.

## The dyke and the river side

The district's western edge is the Red River dyke, and the roads on and behind it are Long Bien's best riding. The dyke surface is rougher than city asphalt — expect patched concrete, dust in the dry season and slick clay after rain — but the traffic is thin, the river is on one side and the market gardens on the other. The ramp lanes down to the river beaches and the islands' orchards turn this into an easy half-day adventure; the general dyke-riding guide covers the technique.

## Daily riding in the district

Away from the river, Long Bien is a comfortable place to ride. Nguyen Van Cu is the main artery, wide and mostly divided, running from Chuong Duong bridge's approach deep into the district. The side streets are residential and calm by Hanoi standards. Nguyen Tu, the motorbike rental business behind this site, sits on this street at number 112 (Bo De ward), open daily 09:00–21:00 — a practical reference point if you are renting in the east of the city, though availability of any specific model needs to be confirmed directly.

## How Long Bien fits a rider's week

- Early morning: the bridge, the fruit market, sunrise over the river islands.
- Daytime: easy district riding, errands, the wide boulevards.
- Weekend: dyke-road exploring toward the orchards, or a picnic run to the riverbank.

Long Bien is the side of Hanoi that feels like a working city rather than a museum, and for riders who want the river in their daily lives, it is the most practical corner of the capital.
