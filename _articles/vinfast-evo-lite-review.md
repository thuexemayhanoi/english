The Evo Lite is the answer to a legal question: what if the Evo's buyer wants a machine that sits unambiguously under Vietnam's 50 km/h moped line? Figures below are from VinFast's official launch release; fields the release does not publish are left open rather than guessed.

## Official specifications

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Platform | Evo-derived electric scooter (speed-limited version) |
| Top speed | below 50 km/h (deliberately limited) |
| Battery | two removable LFP packs, 1.5 kWh each (inherits the Evo platform) |
| Licence | none — moped class, rider 16+ |
| Charging | platform charging options: battery swap, home charge, public chargers |

What the launch data does not publish for the Lite: motor power, range and pricing specifics for this variant. Those fields stay empty in our database rather than being estimated from the Evo — a speed-limited machine has a different power map, and an Evo range figure would be a guess in the Lite's frame.

## Where the Lite makes sense

Under Law 36/2024/QH15 the sub-4 kW, sub-50 km/h class is the strict licence-free band, and the Lite exists to sit inside it by design rather than by luck. For riders who want to never think about the boundary — or parents buying for a 16-year-old — a machine engineered under the cap is cleaner peace of mind than a faster machine ridden gently. The speed-classes guide explains why the cap exists; the riding-at-16 article covers the young-rider rules.

The trade is honest: the same traffic that a 70 km/h-class scooter escapes with a twist, the Lite navigates at moped pace. On Hanoi's arterial roads that pace is workable; on fast outer highways it is a limitation to accept before buying.

## Who it suits

Riders who prioritise the unambiguous licence-free position, want the swap ecosystem (9,000 VND per battery per swap, rental at 175,000–300,000 VND monthly) and accept the speed ceiling. For everyone else, the comparison against the Amio (the other licence-free formula in the family) and the standard Evo review map the choices.
