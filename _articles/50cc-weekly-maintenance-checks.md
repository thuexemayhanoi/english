Ten minutes a week keeps a 50cc out of ninety percent of trouble. The routine below is ordered so you never need tools you do not have with you, and it works in a parking spot, a kerbside, a courtyard. Intervals beyond this — oil changes, services, part replacement — belong to your specific model's manufacturer manual and your mechanic, never to internet rules of thumb.

## One: engine oil level

Bike upright (or on its stand per the manual), dipstick or sight-glass check — our oil-level article covers the two-minute method. Small engines carry small volumes, so a small leak is a big percentage. Top up with the oil the manual names; if it keeps dropping, find the leak, not a bigger bottle.

## Two: chain (geared underbones)

Press mid-span: a little give, not sag. Look for dryness and rust — the chain article covers lubing and adjustment. A dry chain on a small bike is a wearing chain, and small chains wear fast.

## Three: brakes

Roll the bike at walking pace and brake hard, front then rear, each alone. A lever that nears the bar, a brake that grabs or pulses, or a pull to one side means adjustment or shoes are due — the brake checks article details the diagnosis.

## Four: tyres

- Visually: tread visible across the crown, no cord showing, no sidewall cracks or bulges, no glass or nail heads parked in the rubber
- Pressure by gauge, not by thumb — a station air line with a gauge is usually a minute away. Correct pressures are printed in your manual; never guess them, and never borrow another model's numbers.
- Spokes (if wire wheels): a light squeeze-past with fingers finds loose ones; a ping test with a spanner finds duds by sound.

## Five: lights and horn

Engine on: headlight both beams, taillight, brake light under each lever and pedal, indicators if fitted, horn. Ageing electrics fail here first — the battery article explains what dims lights mean.

## Six: fasteners and cables

Hands on the bars: nothing loose, nothing clicking. Squeeze each cable housing gently for fraying near the levers. Grab the rear rack and body panels and rock them — rattles found at rest are rattles silenced in five minutes with a spanner.

## The notebook habit

Write the date and what you found — ten words is plenty. A year of ten-word entries is the most valuable service history a 50cc can have, worth real money at resale and worth more in fault-finding: "tyre losing pressure, front" repeated three weeks running is a slow puncture announcing itself.

## What this routine is not
