If your motorbike licence was issued before 1 January 2025, it did not become invalid when the new licence law took effect. Article 89 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) keeps pre-2025 licences valid as issued — you keep riding on the old class until a reissue moves you to the new system.

## The old classes and what they still cover

The pre-2025 motorbike classes were broader than today's. Under the transitional mapping recorded against the old regulations:

- Old A1: motorbikes from 50 cc up to but not including 175 cc, and electric machines from 4 kW up to but not including 14 kW
- Old A2: 175 cc and above, or 14 kW and above — the big-bike class
- Old A3: three-wheeled motorbikes

A rider holding an old paper A1 therefore legally covers a 150 cc machine that today's A1 (125 cc ceiling) would not — the transitional rule preserves the wider envelope.

## The new classes they map onto

Today's system has A1 (up to and including 125 cc / 11 kW), A (above that — the single big-bike class) and B1 (three-wheel). On any reissue — lost licence, damaged card, detail change — the new card carries new-class codes. A rider whose old A1 covered up to 175 cc keeps practical coverage of the machines they rode, because the transitional validity continues; the class printed on the new card follows the current Article 57 scheme.

## What changes regardless

Two things did change for everyone on 1 January 2025: the 12-point system applies to old and new licences alike, and the fine schedule of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP applies to every rider regardless of licence vintage. An old-class rider with zero points is in exactly the position of a new-class rider with zero points.

## Why this matters for residents and buyers

Foreigners sometimes buy or inherit a licence-era question: a pre-2025 Vietnamese A1 holder has coverage a fresh A1 applicant does not, and the distinction shows up at checkpoints and in accident liability arguments. The transitional rules are the reason the two systems run in parallel.
