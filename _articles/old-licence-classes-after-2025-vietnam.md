If your motorbike licence was issued before 1 January 2025, it did not become invalid when the new licence law took effect. Article 89 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) keeps pre-2025 licences valid as issued — you keep riding on the old class until a reissue moves you to the new system. Neither Article 89 nor Article 21 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA sets a deadline for that move, and an old A1 comes back as class A with a restriction, not as the new A1.

## The old classes and what they still cover

The pre-2025 motorbike classes were broader than today's. Under the transitional mapping recorded against the old regulations:

- Old A1: motorbikes from 50 cc up to but not including 175 cc, and electric machines from 4 kW up to but not including 14 kW
- Old A2: 175 cc and above, or 14 kW and above — the big-bike class
- Old A3: [three-wheeled motorbikes]({{ '/articles/b1-three-wheel-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }})

A rider holding an old paper A1 therefore legally covers a 150 cc machine that today's A1 (125 cc ceiling) would not — the transitional rule preserves the wider envelope.

That mapping is Article 89(2)(a) to (c) of the Law, in the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH; the quotations on this page are our translation of the Vietnamese. It applies to a licence issued before the Law took effect and not yet exchanged or reissued under it. The list of old classes leaves out two details that sit in the same clause: an old A2 covers the old-A1 range as well as the larger machines (Article 89(2)(b)), and an old A3 covers that range and "similar vehicles" alongside three-wheelers (Article 89(2)(c)).

### How long an old licence stays in use

Article 89(1) keeps a licence issued before the Law took effect in use "for the term written on the licence". New licences follow a different rule, because classes A1, A and B1 have no time limit (Article 57(5)(a)), so read the expiry field on your own card before assuming either. Article 62(3) shows that older licences without a term exist: it names those issued before 1 July 2012 and says only that exchanging them is encouraged.

Article 18(4) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA puts the working rule in one line: a licence holder may ride the classes written on the licence. Neither the Law nor the Circular sets a date by which an old card must be swapped, and [the licence validity guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes terms and revocation apart.

### Where the old scope ends

The old A1 range stops below 175 cc or 14 kW. A machine of 175 cc or more was old-A2 territory (Article 89(2)(b)), and today it is class A territory (Article 57(1)(b)).

A licence issued on or after 1 January 2025 is not an old licence for this purpose, because Article 89 speaks of licences issued before the Law took effect and Article 88(1) fixes that date as 1 January 2025. Such an A1 stops at 125 cc and 11 kW, as [the A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) explains.

## The new classes they map onto

Today's system has A1 (up to and including 125 cc / 11 kW), A (above that — the single big-bike class) and B1 (three-wheel). On any reissue — lost licence, damaged card, detail change — the new card carries new-class codes. A rider whose old A1 covered up to 175 cc keeps practical coverage of the machines they rode, because Article 89(3)(a) gives an old A1 a class A card with a restriction attached: the class printed on the new card is A, not the new A1, and the condition limits it to two-wheeled motorbikes below 175 cc or electric motors below 14 kW.

Article 89(3) is worded for a holder who needs to exchange or reissue, so it describes what the new card says rather than a duty to ask for one. The table sets each old class against both clauses.

| Old class | Covers if never exchanged (89(2)) | Card on exchange or reissue (89(3)) | Condition on the new card |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Two-wheelers from 50 cc to below 175 cc, or electric from 4 kW to below 14 kW | Class A, 89(3)(a) | Yes: only below 175 cc or below 14 kW |
| A2 | 175 cc or 14 kW and above, plus the old-A1 range | Class A, 89(3)(b) | None stated |
| A3 | Three-wheelers, the old-A1 range and similar vehicles | Class B1, 89(3)(c) | None stated |
| A4 | Tractors up to 1,000 kg (89(2)(d)) | Two certificates, 89(3)(d) | Not a motorbike licence |

### Old A1: class A with a condition

Appendix IX of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA (item 4(g)) prints the wording for this card. In translation it reads: issued to a person who may only drive a two-wheeled motorbike with a cylinder capacity up to below 175 cc, or an electric motor power up to below 14 kW. Item 5 of the appendix lists the same limit as code A.03 among the restriction codes of the information system for testing and issuing licences.

When you read such a card, the condition decides what you may ride; the letter A alone suggests more than the card gives. The exchange widens nothing. A machine of 175 cc or more needs a licence that covers it, the old A2 under the old scheme and class A under Article 57(1)(b) now, and the Law does not turn an old A1 into either one by exchange alone. [What the class A licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a-licence-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) covers the higher class.

### Old A2 and old A3

An old A2 becomes plain class A (Article 89(3)(b)), with no condition. Article 57(1)(b) gives class A every machine above 125 cc or 11 kW together with everything A1 covers, so the new card reaches every machine the old A2 did.

An old A3 becomes B1 (Article 89(3)(c)). Article 57(1)(c) gives B1 to three-wheeled motorbikes and the A1 vehicles, which end at 125 cc and 11 kW, while an unexchanged A3 also covers the old-A1 range up to 175 cc. Article 89(3)(c) states no condition for the exchanged card and Appendix IX prints none, so the Law and the Circular leave open whether the 125 cc to 175 cc two-wheeler range carries over. An A3 holder who rides a scooter in that range should ask the Traffic Police Department before exchanging.

### The letter B1 changed meaning

Before 2025 B1 was a car licence, covering cars with up to eight seats (not counting the driver's) and goods vehicles within a weight limit (Article 89(2)(đ) and (e)). Today B1 is the three-wheeled motorbike class (Article 57(1)(c)), and the old A3 is the class that became it. The old car classes convert by their own clauses: an old B1 or B2 becomes B or C1, and an old automatic-only B1 becomes B limited to automatic cars (Article 89(3)(đ) and (e)), so a B1 card issued before 1 January 2025 is not a three-wheel licence.

## Do you have to swap an old licence?

Article 89 leaves the choice to the holder of an old Vietnamese licence. A foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam on a national licence must exchange it for the matching Vietnamese licence, unless a driving-licence treaty that Vietnam has joined says otherwise (Circular Article 18(7)). Clause 3 applies when a person with an old licence needs to exchange or reissue it, and Article 21(1)(b) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA lets a holder of a licence issued by a Vietnamese authority ask for an exchange before it expires, if they wish. Article 62(2) of the Law names the occasions: a lost licence, a damaged one, an exchange before the printed term, and a change in the printed details. The Circular treats a lost licence still in term as a reissue (Article 20(1)) and the rest as exchanges (Article 21(1)).

Two points apply to every swap:

- **The class on the card follows Article 89(3).**
- **No new test is named.** The Article 21(2) dossier lists none; Article 89(4) is a separate rule for a learner trained, or in training, when the Law took effect and not yet tested.

### Keep the old card or swap it

A way to weigh the choice, not a rule:

- **A swap makes sense** when the card is damaged or its details differ from your ID (Article 21(1)(a) and (c)), when you want an electronic or PET licence in place of an old card (Article 18(2)), or when you want a licence without a term and one with a term combined (Articles 18(3) and 21(1)(d)).
- **Keeping the card is also lawful** while it is in term and its form number, issue date and expiry date match the licence information system, or its details are integrated into the electronic identity account or the police applications (Article 18(8) of the Circular). An old A3 holder who rides in the 125 cc to 175 cc range has the clearer scope on the unexchanged card, because Article 89(2)(c) names that range and Article 89(3)(c) does not repeat it.

## IDPs issued against an old licence

Under Article 28 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, an international driving permit (IDP) that Vietnam issues takes its class from the national licence. Appendix XII gives the table for licences issued before 1 January 2025.

| Old Vietnamese licence | Class on a Vietnamese IDP |
|---|---|
| A1 | A1 |
| A2 | A |
| A3 | B1 |

The table matches classes letter for letter and contains no engine or power figures, so it does not say how an old A1 range up to 175 cc reads on a permit. It also concerns only an IDP issued by Vietnam: Article 30(1) says such a permit has no validity inside Vietnam, so it matters for riding abroad. Article 29(1) lets Vietnamese citizens and foreigners with a valid Vietnamese national licence apply, and Article 57(8)(a) of the Law caps a permit at 3 years and makes it fit the remaining validity of the national licence.

Permits from other countries are a different question, and [the IDP rules guide]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) covers them.

## What changes regardless

Two things did change for everyone on 1 January 2025: the 12-point system applies to old and new licences alike, and the fine schedule of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP applies to every rider regardless of licence vintage. An old-class rider with zero points is in exactly the position of a new-class rider with zero points.

Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amended Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026, so the schedule that now applies is the amended one, and this page quotes no amount. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) still carries figures from the 2024 text, so check them against the amended decree.

Article 89 sets old licences apart from the point balance in no way, and Article 58(4) of the Law keeps a licence's points when it is exchanged, reissued or upgraded. [The points system guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-points-system-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains how points are deducted.

## Why this matters for residents and buyers

A pre-2025 Vietnamese A1 holder has coverage a fresh A1 applicant does not, and a resident, a buyer or a renter can each end up reading the two cards against the same scooter. The transitional rules are the reason the two systems run in parallel.

Imagine a resident whose old A1 card cracks and is exchanged. The replacement reads class A with a condition, and the 150 cc scooter they have ridden for years is still inside it, while a friend's A1 issued in 2026 stops at 125 cc.

## Quick answers

### Is an old A1 still valid, and for how long?

Yes, for the term written on the card (Article 89(1)). No deadline for swapping it appears in the Law or in Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA.

### Can an old A1 cover a machine of 175 cc or more?

No. That range belonged to the old A2 and now belongs to class A, and a swap gives the old A1 the same ceiling (Article 89(3)(a)).

### What if I lose an old licence?

A holder whose licence is still in term can ask for a reissue (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 20(1)), and the class on the new card follows Article 89(3). [The lost licence guide]({{ '/articles/lost-motorbike-licence-replacement-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the replacement route.
