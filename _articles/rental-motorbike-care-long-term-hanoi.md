A rental is someone else's bike, but on a long-term rental you are its only rider for weeks. Basic care keeps it reliable, keeps your deposit safe, and keeps your month cheap.

The short answer: wash it gently, watch the chain, use the right fuel, park it dry and report changes early. Most of that needs no tools; anything you would add, adjust or spend money on is a question for the shop first. The riding and parking habits below come from the Honda Vietnam owner's manuals for the Wave Alpha and the Vision, two bikes a renter may be offered; which model and variant you are given is for the shop to confirm, so ask before relying on any figure.

## Washing and cleaning

Hanoi dust and rain leave a film that eats into chains and brake components. A weekly or fortnightly wash — street washes are cheap and everywhere — keeps moving parts clean and makes new leaks or damage visible. Do not pressure-wash electrics hard; a normal hand wash is enough.

Honda's two manuals go further and rule out a high-pressure washer altogether, because it can damage moving and electrical parts and push water into the air cleaner and the carburettor or throttle body. A low-pressure hose and a cloth is the safe reading.

Use the wash as an inspection too. Once the bike is clean and dry, walk round it for scuffs, a loose mirror, a cut in a tyre sidewall and any wet patch where it stood, and photograph anything new with the date before you tell the shop.

## Chain and moving parts

If the bike has a chain, keep it clean and notice when it slackens; chain adjustment is part of the shop's maintenance, so ask for a check rather than adjusting it yourself unless agreed. The published wording is that the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, so confirm what yours covers. [Tyres, Tubes and Consumables on a Long-Term Rental]({{ '/articles/consumables-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains which parts may become your responsibility.

Not every bike has one. The Wave Alpha is a semi-automatic with a drive chain, while the maintenance schedule in the Vision manual lists a drive belt and final-drive oil in its place. Either way, what a renter can offer is a description rather than a repair: when the noise or looseness shows up (a cold start, pulling away, braking).

## Fuel discipline

Use the fuel the bike expects and buy from busy stations. Note the odometer when you fill — a sudden drop in fuel economy is an early warning sign of a problem. Return the bike with the agreed fuel level; [Fuel Policy When Renting a Motorbike in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-fuel-policy-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the usual expectations.

Both Honda manuals want unleaded petrol with no more than 10% ethanol and give a minimum octane that differs by variant, so ask which variant you have, and [the guide to petrol grades on sale]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-fuel-what-petrol/' | relative_url }}) shows what the pumps offer.

## Storage habits

Park under cover where you can, wipe the bike dry after heavy rain, and do not leave the seat open in a downpour. [Keeping a Rental Motorbike Secure Over Months in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers security; this is about protecting the machine itself.

### Where it sleeps: questions for the building and the shop

On a long rental the overnight spot is an important storage decision. Nothing is published about where a rental may be kept, so these are questions, not rules.

- **The building or landlord:** Is there motorbike parking for residents, is it covered, is there a monthly charge, and who is on duty overnight?
- **The shop:** Does it want anything done or avoided at night, and is a cover allowed? Honda's advice for a bike left outdoors is a cover over the whole machine, but whether one is permitted on a rental, and who would supply it, is the shop's call.
- **You:** Which spot gets the afternoon sun on the seat, and where does rain run to?

[Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains how paid lots, building parking and street parking work across the city.

### What Honda's manuals say about parking

Both owner's manuals give nearly the same routine for leaving the bike, with one extra step on the Wave Alpha:

- Choose firm, level ground. If the bike has to stand on a slope, or on ground that is slippery or unstable, park it so that it cannot move or fall.
- Switch off, lower the side stand and lean the bike slowly to the left until its weight is on the stand. Then turn the handlebars fully left, because both manuals say that turning them to the right reduces stability and can topple the bike.
- Lock the steering, take the key out and close the key shutter on a bike that has one. Both manuals also recommend using an anti-theft device.
- On the Wave Alpha, finish by turning the fuel valve to OFF.

### Rain and humidity

Honda's manuals warn that wet brakes work less well and say to apply them while riding at low speed to dry them. [Riding a Rental Motorbike in Hanoi Rain]({{ '/articles/riding-rental-motorbike-hanoi-rain/' | relative_url }}) deals with the road.

Mist inside the headlight is one humidity sign the manuals describe. They say it can appear after a wash or a ride in the rain, does not affect how the light works and clears a few minutes after the engine is started and the headlight is on; a lot of water or ice inside the lens is the case for an authorised Honda shop.

## Riding habits that spare the bike

Honda's manuals are written for owners, but their riding advice applies to anyone in the saddle.

### Run-in, start-up and idling

Both manuals ask for gentle riding over the first 500 km: no full-throttle starts, no rapid acceleration, no harsh braking (the Wave Alpha manual adds quick gear changes) and careful riding generally. A rental may be well past that figure, and the odometer at pickup tells you; if the reading is low, ask whether the bike is still being run in.

The Wave Alpha has a choke lever, and its manual gives a routine for each air temperature. In the normal range of 10 to 35°C the lever goes fully on to start, halfway down as soon as the engine catches and fully off about half a minute later, with the throttle opened and closed lightly in between. At 35°C or above the choke is not used, and at 10°C or below the engine keeps warming after the lever is off until it runs evenly. The Vision manual's instruction is shorter: start and warm the engine, and avoid turning the throttle hard.

Three more habits come from the same pages:

- Do not run the engine on the spot for long. Both manuals warn that this can damage the engine, because cooling and lubrication are not ensured.
- If the Wave Alpha has not started within five seconds, its manual says to switch the ignition off and wait ten seconds before trying again, which lets the battery voltage recover.
- Do not leave the ignition on with the engine stopped, which drains the battery, and never turn the key while riding.

### Loads and passengers

Both manuals allow the rider and one passenger, and both warn that overloading hurts handling, braking and stability. The limits differ by bike: the Wave Alpha manual gives 5 kg as the most for luggage and for its storage box, the Vision manual sets separate limits for the under-seat box, the front hook and the rear grab rail, and each bike's maximum total load differs between variants, so ask which figures apply to yours.

Tie everything down firmly, balanced and close to the centre of the bike, clear of the lights and exhaust, and do not attach a trailer or a sidecar.

## Keys, papers and the seat

The Wave Alpha manual asks the owner to note the key code supplied with the original key and to keep the spare key and the code somewhere safe; if every key and the code are lost, the ignition switch and the key shutter have to be replaced. On a rental, ask how many keys came with the bike and whether a code goes with them, and keep any spare away from the bike. [What Happens If You Lose the Key to a Rental Motorbike]({{ '/articles/lost-key-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers what follows a loss. Check that the key is in your hand before you close the seat, since the Vision manual warns against leaving it in the box under the seat.

Some Vision variants use a smart-key system with a FOB. Its manual says that anyone can unlock the ignition and start the engine while the FOB is in range, even when you are on the other side of a wall or window, and can also open the seat and unlock the steering. So carry the FOB, never put it in a storage box, and switch the smart-key system off if you walk away from the bike with the FOB still in range, for example when you go indoors.

On papers, the Wave Alpha manual describes a document pouch under the seat that can hold the manual, the registration and the motorbike insurance papers. Whether a rental's papers live there, with you or with the shop is not published, so ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop, and who keeps them between rides.

## Accessories, mounts and changes: ask first

The Wave Alpha manual strongly advises against fitting non-Honda accessories or modifying the bike, because either can make it unsafe, and the Vision manual makes the same recommendation about parts and changes that alter the original design. Both say a modification may void the warranty and may be treated as illegal on public roads, tell the owner to be sure a change is safe and legal first, and warn that incorrectly fitted accessories or modifications can lead to a serious accident.

A rental adds a second reason to ask first: the bike belongs to someone else, and the weight of accessories counts towards the maximum load in the specifications. Even a reversible addition such as a phone holder or a seat pad is worth a message before you fit it, and [the guide to rental motorbike modifications]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-modifications/' | relative_url }}) draws the line between a reversible addition and a change to the bike.

## When something feels wrong

A pull to one side, a new rattle, softer brakes — deal with it the day you notice. [Maintenance During a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains what the shop covers, and [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers what to do when something fails outright.

### What to report, and how

A report is useful when it says what changed, since when and at what odometer reading, with a photo. Some cases are worth knowing in advance:

| What you notice | What the manual says | What to send the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Vision: the fuel-injection fault lamp comes on while riding | The system may have a serious fault: slow down and have an authorised Honda shop inspect the bike as soon as possible | A photo of the dashboard with the odometer |
| Vision with smart key: the indicator flashes five times at switch-on, or the range turns unreliable | Replace the FOB battery as soon as possible, at an authorised Honda shop | A photo and a note of when it began |
| A scuff, a bent lever or a fall in the car park | Nothing on cosmetic marks; after a mishap, the Wave Alpha manual says to switch off, check for leaks, bolts, controls, brakes and wheels, and have the bike inspected soon | Photos from two sides, taken before anything is straightened |

Tell the shop about a knock when it happens and ask before paying anyone to repair it. A collision or an injury is a different matter with duties of its own, set out in [the guide to what to do after a motorbike accident in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). A symptom that is still there the next day belongs in a message, and [What to Do If Your Rental Motorbike Does Not Feel Normal]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-feels-wrong/' | relative_url }}) separates the signs that mean stopping now from those that can wait.

The shop's published contact is +84 942 467 674 on phone, WhatsApp and Zalo, and it is open 09:00-21:00 daily. A written message carries a time and a photo, which a phone call does not. In it, ask whether you may keep riding until the shop can see the bike.

## Photos and the condition it goes back in

Photographs taken at each end let you compare the bike you hand back with the bike you were given, so they matter. [The handover checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) sets out the first set and [the return guide]({{ '/articles/returning-a-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the day itself. In between, a monthly set gives dated evidence of what changed and when: one photo of each side, the odometer, the fuel gauge, each tyre's tread and sidewall, and a close-up of any mark.

No hand-back standard is published for a long rental, so settle these questions at the start and keep the replies with your photographs:

- What condition is the bike expected to come back in, and should it be washed first?
- How is normal wear told apart from damage, who decides, and how would a cost be put to you?
- Does every key, and any paper that came with the bike, go back with it?
- How and when is the deposit settled, and what has to happen first?

## The one-line version

Wash it, watch the chain, fuel it properly, store it dry, and report changes early — five habits that make a long rental painless.
