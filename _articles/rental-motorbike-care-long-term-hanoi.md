A rental is someone else's bike, but on a long-term rental you are its only rider for weeks. Basic care keeps it reliable, keeps your deposit safe, and keeps your month cheap.

## Washing and cleaning

Hanoi dust and rain leave a film that eats into chains and brake components. A weekly or fortnightly wash — street washes are cheap and everywhere — keeps moving parts clean and makes new leaks or damage visible. Do not pressure-wash electrics hard; a normal hand wash is enough.

## Chain and moving parts

If the bike has a chain, keep it clean and notice when it slackens; chain adjustment is part of the shop's maintenance, so ask for a check rather than adjusting it yourself unless agreed. Consumables-monthly-rental-hanoi explains which parts may become your responsibility.

## Fuel discipline

Use the fuel the bike expects and buy from busy stations. Note the odometer when you fill — a sudden drop in fuel economy is an early warning sign of a problem. Return the bike with the agreed fuel level; motorbike-rental-fuel-policy-hanoi explains the usual expectations.

## Storage habits

Park under cover where you can, wipe the bike dry after heavy rain, and do not leave the seat open in a downpour. Keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi covers security; this is about protecting the machine itself.

## When something feels wrong

A pull to one side, a new rattle, softer brakes — deal with it the day you notice. Maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi explains what the shop covers, and rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi covers what to do when something fails outright.

## The one-line version

Wash it, watch the chain, fuel it properly, store it dry, and report changes early — five habits that make a long rental painless.
