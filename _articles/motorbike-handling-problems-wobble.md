When a bike pulls, wobbles or steers heavily, it is not being temperamental — it is reporting a mechanical problem. Handling issues deserve fast attention because they strike exactly when you need precision most: mid-corner, or in an emergency stop.

## Pulling to one side

The usual causes, in order of likelihood: uneven tyre pressure (a five-minute check — tyre-care-motorbike-vietnam), a bent or dented rim after a pothole strike (potholes-bad-roads-motorbike-vietnam explains how it happens), unequal tyre wear, a dragging brake on one side, or after a fall, a twisted handlebar or fork alignment. If pressure equalization does not fix the pull, get it inspected before riding far.

## Speed wobble

A shake through the bars at a particular speed range points to: loose steering-head bearings, worn fork bushings, unbalanced or unevenly worn front tyre, loose wheel bearings, or anything that lets the front oscillate — including an overloaded or badly fixed top box. A wobble that appears at the same speed every ride is mechanical, not imagination: ease off, hold the bars firmly but not rigidly, and brake gently to a stop. Do not ride on; find the loose part.

## Heavy steering

Stiff, reluctant steering is almost always dried or overtightened steering-head bearings — the bike wants to hold whatever line it is on, and correcting feels like wrestling. It is a common, cheap fix and one of the most overlooked services on Vietnam's older fleet. Compare against a bike that steers properly and the difference is shocking.

## The final-notice symptoms

Any handling change after a crash, a curb strike or loading the bike heavily should be treated as structural until inspected. And once the handling is fixed, verify the fix in a safe spot with a few gentle stops and figure-eights — the drills in emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam — before trusting the bike back into dense traffic.
