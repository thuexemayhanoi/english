The honest opening for a complete beginner: renting a motorbike in Hanoi without any riding experience is a bad idea on the first day — but with a realistic learning path, it becomes a reasonable idea within weeks. Here is that path. In order: settle which licence you ride on, learn the controls away from traffic, prove the skills against a short checklist, then rent a light automatic for one day.

## Beginner, but legal first

Inexperience and licensing are separate problems. The licensing rule does not bend for beginners: you need a licence that is valid in Vietnam for the class of bike, or a bike in the licence-free moped class, and riding without one carries fines — see [Renting a Motorbike in Hanoi Without a Licence: The Real Risks]({{ '/articles/renting-a-motorbike-hanoi-without-licence/' | relative_url }}) and [The Fine for Riding Without a Licence in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Under-18s are limited to 50cc-class machines ([Minimum Age to Ride a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }})). If you hold no valid licence at all, the licence-free route is the 50cc class — and even then, only after you can actually ride.

### Which licence covers a first ride

Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) lists the licences valid on Vietnamese territory: a Vietnamese licence; an international permit together with a national licence from a state party to the 1968 Vienna Convention; and two routes that rest on treaties, each licence suited to the vehicle. A rental scooter of up to 125 cc falls in class A1, which the same article defines as a two-wheeled motorbike with a cylinder capacity of up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor of up to 11 kW.

For a first-time rider the permit route has a trap. [The IDP rules guide]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) is plain about it: the permit is a translation, not a standalone licence, and your national licence must itself include motorcycles, because an IDP cannot add a category you do not hold. A car licence alone therefore gives a beginner no motorbike entitlement, because under Article 57 a car class and a motorbike class are separate licences.

The table sorts starting points for a rental scooter of up to 125 cc.

| If you hold | Rental scooter, up to 125 cc | Next step for a beginner |
|---|---|---|
| A Vietnamese A1 licence, or a national motorcycle licence with a 1968-convention IDP that has a motorcycle category | Legal, if the licence covers that size of bike | Build the skill first: the licence says you may ride, not that you can |
| A car licence only, or no licence | Not legal | The licence-free moped class, or a licensed training course and the test |
| Any licence, but you are under 18 | Not legal: A1 starts at 18 | The moped class only, from 16 |

### The licence-free moped, stated exactly

Under Article 34(1)(g) of Law 36/2024/QH15 a moped (xe gắn máy) is a motor vehicle on two or three wheels that is designed to go no faster than 50 km/h and has a combustion engine of up to 50 cc or an electric motor of up to 4 kW; Article 56(1) excepts its riders from the licence requirement, Article 56(4) asks instead that they know the traffic rules, can control the vehicle and meet the age and health conditions, and Article 59(1)(a) sets the age at 16 or over.

A licence-free bike is therefore not a safe first lesson in itself: a learner on a 50cc still needs the practice path below. The definition also has several conditions, so ask the shop for the engine size or motor power and the design speed of the exact bike instead of relying on the label "50cc". Helmet and traffic rules apply as on any motorbike: Article 33(2) requires a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly.

No price is published for a 50cc bike, so contact the shop for the rate. Contact us to confirm current availability before you plan around any model.

## Learn off the street, not on it

Hanoi traffic is a poor classroom. Above the moped class, Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 56 provides no learner stage for motorbike riders (its 56(5) covers car learners only) and [the learner-permit guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-learner-permit-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) confirms none exists, so no document lets an unlicensed beginner practise on the street. The moped class needs no licence but still asks for skill (56(4)).

A quiet space is a safety measure, not a legal exemption. Article 56 sets the licence duty for drivers who take part in road traffic, and this guide does not decide whether a particular car park or private yard counts as a road. The safe assumption is that the licence rules above apply to you wherever you ride a rented bike, and the owner of any private space should be asked before you use it.

A good practice space offers:

- room to run on after a mistake, instead of a wall or a row of parked bikes.
- a smooth, clean, dry surface.
- no through traffic and no pedestrians to dodge.

Keep sessions short and frequent, because a tired learner repeats mistakes instead of fixing them. Dress as if a fall will happen: a fastened helmet, closed shoes and long trousers. If you practise on a rented bike, ask the shop the damage question from the list below first.

The workable progression:

1. Practise the machine in a quiet space — an empty parking area early on a weekend morning — until controls are automatic: throttle, both brakes, balance at walking speed
2. Choose an automatic scooter; the difference between beginner-friendly and everything else is in [Easiest Motorbikes for Beginner Riders in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/easiest-motorbikes-for-beginners-hanoi/' | relative_url }})
3. First street rides: Sunday mornings, residential lanes outside the Old Quarter, short loops with a clear exit plan
4. Gradually add difficulty: bigger roads, then rush hour, then carrying anything

## Renting as a beginner

Once you ride competently, the rental process is unchanged: inspect at handover ([Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }})), agree the terms ([Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), deposit typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions. Two beginner-specific additions:

- Tell the shop you are a new rider and ask for a light, low bike; whether one is free on your dates is for the shop to confirm
- Rent for a day first, not a week; discover your real comfort on someone else's schedule

### What to ask when you enquire

Put these to the shop by message or call. [The booking guide]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the channels, the payment options and what to settle in writing, so this list keeps to what a new rider adds:

- Which light automatic is available on my dates?
- What is the deposit for that bike, and what conditions does it depend on?
- What does the agreement say about damage after a tip-over or a fall?

A one-day first rental limits what a change of mind costs. The published day rate is 150,000 VND for a Honda Click or Yamaha Mio and 200,000 VND for a Honda Vision, plus the deposit; contact us to confirm current availability of any of them. Early return carries no automatic refund for unused time, which is the main reason not to start with a week. Do not plan to learn at the counter.

### Papers and insurance

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so you arrange your own. Check your travel or medical policy for any motorbike exclusion and for the licence it expects: a policy can set conditions that a beginner without a valid licence has not met.

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 is a duty to carry papers, so ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop, and keep your own licence on you in the original, with the IDP if you ride on one.

### What a new rider cannot yet judge

An experienced rider notices when a bike pulls to one side, brakes late or idles roughly. A beginner has no baseline, so a fault can pass for how motorbikes feel. Treat anything that feels odd as a question for the shop instead of a quirk to live with, and use the inspection checklist linked above at handover.

## A readiness test before the first street ride

Run this on a bike you may legally ride, in the empty space and in the helmet, shoes and trousers you will wear on the road. A line you cannot do without thinking is a line to practise, not one to skip.

- Start the bike, work the stand and walk the bike backwards, without help.
- Pull away from a stop smoothly, with no lurch when the throttle opens.
- Stop on a chosen mark using both brakes, the front one squeezed progressively rather than grabbed.
- Ride a straight line at walking pace without putting a foot down, then a wide U-turn, then a tighter one.
- Look over your shoulder while holding the line, and signal and cancel the indicator without looking down at it.
- Stop hard from a moderate speed in a straight line and keep the bike upright.

If every line feels dull, the next step is the first street ride in the progression above: a quiet street at a quiet hour. If one line fails, go back a step instead of moving on to see how it goes.

## When not to rent

Say the honest thing to yourself: if you cannot balance and stop confidently in an empty lot, you are not ready for streets; if you are unwilling to wear the helmet, you are not ready at all. Not ready is a result, not a failing, and waiting costs only time.

## Common questions

### Can I learn to ride on a rented scooter in Hanoi?

Above the moped class, only with a licence valid in Vietnam for that scooter's class, since a rental agreement does not make anyone legal to ride. The licence-free moped class in the moped section above is the other route, and practising away from traffic comes before the first street ride in every case.

### Can a friend teach me on their bike?

Above the moped class, not on the street if you hold no licence valid for it: you would be riding unlicensed, and the fine guide linked above notes that the owner who supplies the machine faces a penalty of their own. A friend can still watch you work through the readiness test above.

### Is a 50cc a better first bike than a 110cc automatic?

Not by itself: the licence decides before the machine does, and the moped and licence sections above set out what each class needs. If you hold a licence valid for the larger bike, the progression above recommends an automatic scooter.

## The one-line version

Licence valid, skills real before streets, automatic scooter, day rentals first. Hanoi rewards patient beginners and punishes impatient ones.
