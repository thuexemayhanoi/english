Weight is the scooter spec riders feel most but check least. A 10 kg difference changes parking, paddling through jams, catching a tip-over and loading onto a ferry. This table uses only weights published by Honda Vietnam and Yamaha Vietnam on official product pages (retrieved 23 September 2026) - wet weight where Yamaha publishes it, kerb weight for Honda.

## The verified weight table

| Scooter | Weight (manufacturer figure) |
|---|---|
| Honda Vision 110 | 97 kg |
| Yamaha Latte 125 | 100 kg (wet) |
| Yamaha Grande | 100 kg (wet) |
| Honda Air Blade 125 | 110 kg |
| Honda Vario 125 | 111 kg |
| Honda Air Blade 160 | 113 kg |
| Honda Lead 125 | 114 kg |
| Honda Sh mode 125 | 116 kg |
| Honda Vario 160 | 118 kg |
| Yamaha NVX 155 | 125 kg (wet) |
| Honda SH160i | 134 kg |

## How to read it

The spread is nearly 40 kg across the class. The lightest three (Vision, Latte, Grande) are all small-wheel city machines - light weight is their whole character, as our lightest motorbikes guide explains. The middle band (110-118 kg) is the modern 125-160 cc mainstream, where Honda's eSAF-framed newcomers sit. The two heaviest - NVX and especially the SH160i - buy their stability with mass: long wheelbases, big tyres, big tanks.

## Weight versus stability

Heavier is not worse; it depends on your roads. Heavy and long-wheelbase machines feel planted on fast, broken arterials; light and short-wheelbase machines win where you push, park and U-turn constantly - see our wheelbase explainer for the geometry side.

## Who should care most

Newer riders and smaller riders should stay near the light end of the table - catching a 134 kg scooter at a red-light tip-over is a different event from catching a 97 kg one.
