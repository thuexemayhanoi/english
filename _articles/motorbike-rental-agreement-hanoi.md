A Hanoi motorbike rental can be concluded in a few minutes of conversation, and a smaller shop may run on a handshake plus a deposit. But the terms still exist even when the paper does not, and agreeing them out loud before money changes hands is what protects both sides.

## The terms that matter

- Period and rate: the daily, weekly, or monthly price, and exactly when the rental day starts and ends
- Deposit: the amount, what it covers, and when it comes back
- Late return: hourly charge and when a full day kicks in
- Damage and consumables: which repairs are the shop's responsibility and which are yours
- Insurance: whether any is included — confirm rather than assume
- Fuel: the level the bike should come back with
- Permitted use: who may ride it, and whether trips outside Hanoi are acceptable
- Contact: the shop's phone number and what to do in a breakdown

For Nguyen Tu's published position on each of these — deposit typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND, late return 20,000 VND per hour with a full day possibly charged after six hours, no motorbike insurance provided, cash/bank/QR payment — see [Motorbike Rental Deposits in Hanoi: Typical Amounts and Return]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), [What Happens If You Return a Rental Motorbike Late in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/late-return-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), and [Is Insurance Included with Motorbike Rentals in Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/is-insurance-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). (More than 6 hours late, a full additional rental day may be charged.) Confirm current terms directly with the shop before renting; terms for your specific rental are what you agree at handover.

## What the Civil Code says about a rental

Article 472 of the Civil Code (Law 91/2015/QH13) defines a lease of property as an agreement under which the lessor hands property to the lessee to use for a period and the lessee pays rent. A motorbike rental matches that definition, so the lease articles are a useful frame for reading any rental paper. This is a general guide to what those articles say, not legal advice, and the official text is in Vietnamese.

### Price, period and handover

- **Rent and period (Articles 473 and 474).** The parties agree the rent (473(1)) and the period (474(1)); with no agreed period it follows the purpose of the hire. If the rent was not agreed, or was agreed unclearly, Article 473(2) sets it at the market price at the place and time the contract was made.

- **Handing over (Article 476).** The lessor must hand the property over in the type, quality, condition, time and place agreed. If it is not of the agreed quality, the lessee may ask for a repair or a lower rent, or cancel and claim damages (Article 476(2)).

- **Keeping it fit (Article 477).** The lessor must keep the property in the agreed condition and fit for its purpose for the whole period, and repair damage and defects, except minor damage that custom leaves to the lessee. Because these articles do not define "minor" (477(1) refers to custom, 479(1) puts maintenance and minor repairs on the lessee), a line saying who handles tyres, tubes and chain adjustment closes a gap. A renter who thinks of repairing a fault should message the shop first, and [Can You Use Outside Repair Shops on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) looks at that question.

### The renter's side

- **Care and use (Articles 479 and 480).** The lessee must look after the property, carry out maintenance and minor repairs, and must compensate if they lose or damage it, but is not responsible for natural wear from use (Article 479(1)). The property is to be used for its proper function and the agreed purpose, and use outside that lets the lessor end the contract and claim damages (Article 480).

- **Paying and returning (Articles 481 and 482).** Rent is paid in full at the agreed time; failing that, at the time local custom sets; failing that, on return (Article 481(1)). The bike goes back in the condition received, natural wear excepted, or as agreed (Article 482(1)). After a late return the lessor may demand the bike back, rent for the delay and damages, with a penalty only if one was agreed, and the lessee bears the risk meanwhile (Article 482(4) and (5)).

### Deposit, penalty and damages

- **Deposit (Article 328).** A deposit is money or another valuable handed over for a period to secure making or performing a contract. It is returned or set against the sum owed once the contract is concluded or performed; if the depositor refuses to make or perform it, the receiver keeps it; if the receiver refuses, it returns the deposit plus an equal sum, unless the parties agree otherwise. Whether a shop's deposit is meant to work this way is for the paper to say.

- **Penalty clause (Article 418).** A penalty is a sum the party in breach pays under an agreement, in an amount the parties set, unless a related law provides otherwise (Article 418(2)). If one was agreed and the paper does not say that damages are payable as well, only the penalty is owed (Article 418(3)). So it matters whether a late-return charge is written as rent for the extra time or as a penalty.

- **Damages (Articles 360 and 419).** A party whose breach causes damage must compensate all of it, unless the parties agree otherwise or the law provides otherwise (Articles 360 and 13). Article 419(2) lets the injured party claim the benefit the contract would have brought and the costs caused by non-performance, without counting anything twice. These articles set no ceiling on their own: a limit such as "liability stops at the deposit" has to come from the agreement or from another law.

## A clause-by-clause checklist

Find each clause below in the paper, or in the booking messages if there is no paper, and ask the question where it is missing or unclear. The last column says where it comes from: the Civil Code articles above, which are general rules, or the shop's own terms. The checklist suits a rental of days; renewal and exit over several months belong to [Long-Term Motorbike Rental Agreements in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-long-term-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

### Money clauses

| Clause | What to look for | Question to ask | Comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent and period | Start and end dates, the time a rental day ends, the total, how and when each sum is paid | "What time does my rental day end?" | Articles 473-474 and 481(1); the figures and the payment method are the shop's terms |
| Deposit | The amount, what it is held against, how and when it is settled | "What is it held against?" | Article 328 defines a deposit; the amount and conditions are the shop's terms |
| Late return | The hourly charge, the point where a full extra day applies, any further claim | "Is the hourly charge the whole consequence of being late?" | Articles 482(4) and 418; the figures are the shop's terms |
| Early return | What happens to unused days | "Is any unused time refunded or credited?" | The shop's terms |

### Condition and care clauses

| Clause | What to look for | Question to ask | Comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handover condition | The model, the plate, existing marks, the fuel level, the keys | "Can we note the marks together?" | Article 476(1) for the agreed condition; the record is yours and the shop's |
| Damage at return | How natural wear is told from damage, and how a repair figure is set | "How will a repair price be worked out and shown to me?" | Articles 479(1) and 482(1) exclude natural wear; the pricing is the shop's terms |
| Maintenance, repairs and tyres | Which upkeep the shop does, and who pays for a repair, a puncture or a worn tyre | "Who pays if something breaks, and who do I call?" | Articles 477 and 479 in general terms; who carries out the upkeep and who pays are the shop's terms |

[What Is Included in a Hanoi Motorbike Rental?]({{ '/articles/what-is-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the maintenance and support lines Nguyen Tu publishes, so read those against the paper. Nguyen Tu's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, and consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.

### Use, people and papers

| Clause | What to look for | Question to ask | Comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permitted use | Who may ride, for what purpose and where | "May a friend ride? May I ride out of Hanoi?" | Article 480 (function and agreed purpose); who may ride is the shop's term; support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, and [riding outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) has its own guide |
| Insurance and papers | Who arranges cover; how the papers will be shown | "How will each paper be shown at a stop?" | Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 56; the Documentation section below has the detail |
| Theft, loss and keys | Whether liability stops at a stated sum, and what report is needed | "Is my liability capped, and at what sum?" | Article 479(1) (the lessee pays if they lose or damage the property), Article 482(5) (risk during a late return) and Article 360; how theft is treated and any cap are the shop's terms |
| Contact | The phone number, opening hours, what to do in a breakdown | "Who do I call, and until when?" | The shop's terms; Nguyen Tu is open 09:00-21:00 daily and does not promise 24/7 rescue |

## What to check before agreeing

Test the questions on the shop, not just the bike: "What happens if the bike is stolen?", "What if I get a puncture outside the city?", "Can my friend ride it?", "What happens if I come back two days early?" Early return, for example, carries no automatic refund entitlement — the answer you receive at handover is the one that counts, and it is easier to negotiate before paying than after.

## Reading the paper before you sign

### Ask for it before you pay

Ask to see the written terms before any money moves, and read them in your own time. If there is no paper, the messages are the record, and [how to book by WhatsApp, Zalo or walk-in]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) shows how to turn a booking thread into a confirmation.

### Four checks on the text itself

1. A blank is a risk. Any date, figure or plate number left empty should be filled in before you sign.

2. The total adds up. The sum written should be the quoted rate for your days, with the deposit on its own line.

3. Open-ended wording gets a limit. A charge with no figure, liability with no ceiling or a right to change the price later is worth asking to be made specific.

4. Identity documents stay separate from the deposit. A paper that asks you to leave a passport is a different arrangement from a cash deposit, and [Do Hanoi Motorbike Rentals Keep Your Passport? Deposit Practices]({{ '/articles/do-rentals-keep-your-passport-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out what to check before handing over any document.

### If the paper is not in English

Ask for an English version, or at least for the lines that move money (rent, deposit, late return, damage and loss) to be put into English. Do not sign text you cannot read: have the shop go through those lines with you, then send them back in a message so the English wording is on both phones.

### Signing and keeping a copy

Sign after the walk-round, not before, so that the condition on the paper is the condition you saw. Leave with a copy, on paper or as a photograph of every signed page, and keep it with the handover photographs until the rental is closed and the deposit question is settled.

## Documentation

Ask for a receipt for the deposit, or at minimum photograph the bike from all four sides plus the odometer at handover — timestamped photos settle damage disputes faster than memory. Note existing scratches in front of the shop. The full handover routine is in [Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}).

### Papers beyond the photos

Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15 puts the carrying duty on the person riding. Among the papers it lists are the vehicle registration certificate and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate (Article 56(1)(a) and (d)). Papers integrated into an electronic identification account can be shown through it (Article 56(3)). Nguyen Tu's published terms say nothing about how a rental's papers reach the rider, so ask at handover how those two certificates would be produced if you were stopped. [What Documents Must You Carry While Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the full list.

## If the agreement is broken

Disagreements are easier to settle when the terms were spoken aloud at the start. When they are not, the deposit return is the pinch point: [How Deposit Returns Work at Hanoi Motorbike Rentals]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) describes what a fair return process looks like, and theft or loss situations follow the responsibility rules in [Theft and Loss Responsibility on Rental Motorbikes in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/theft-loss-responsibility-rental-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

One habit worth keeping even with the friendliest shop: put the agreed key terms in a message to the shop's phone at handover. A one-line written record protects both of you.

## Questions before signing

### Does a Hanoi motorbike rental need a written contract?

The lease articles quoted above define a lease as an agreement between the parties and do not, in those articles, say that a short rental must be on paper. The general rules on the form of a contract sit outside those articles, so this is not a legal conclusion. A written record is still the practical protection: the signed paper, or the booking messages and handover photographs where there is none.

### What if a charge appears at the counter that I did not expect?

Under Article 473(1) the rent is set by agreement, so ask where the new figure comes from and compare it with your messages and the paper.

### Can a friend ride the bike once I have signed?

Article 480 requires use for the property's proper function and the agreed purpose. It does not say who may ride, so that is a term to ask about and keep in writing. The legal side of letting someone without a licence ride is in [Lending Your Motorbike to an Unlicensed Rider in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Can I hand the bike back somewhere other than the shop?

For a movable such as a motorbike, Article 482(2) makes the lessor's residence or seat the place of return unless the parties agree otherwise. Delivery and pickup in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement, so agree the place and the time of any other return before you sign.
