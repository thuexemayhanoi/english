On a silent bike, visibility is your horn. These are the layers that keep a quiet machine seen in Hanoi traffic.

## Lights

Modern electrics ship with strong lighting — VinFast's Viper uses LED projector headlights — so use them as safety equipment, not decoration:

- **Run lights in daytime drizzle, dusk and dawn** — the low-sun hours when drivers' pupils are worst.
- **Aim the headlight properly** so it marks you without blinding; a misaimed beam gets you flashed at and overlooked in equal measure.
- **Keep the lens clean.** City film cuts light output surprisingly fast; wipe it weekly.

## Gear

- **A light-coloured or reflective helmet** does more than any jacket — it sits at the height drivers scan.
- **Reflective strips on bags and jacket backs** catch headlights from behind, where the quiet bike is most at risk.
- **A bright rear box or pannier** turns a commuter setup into a rolling signal board.

## Positioning

The most powerful visibility tool costs nothing: never sit in a blind spot, ride where the driver ahead can see you in their mirror, and at junctions take the position that says "I am here" before it says "I am fast". When passing anything that could move — parked cars, market stalls, bus stops — assume it will move, and set your line accordingly. Our silent-riding safety guide covers the full technique.

## The honest summary

You cannot make an electric loud, so make it bright and put it where eyes already point. Lights on, gear reflective, position deliberate — the silent bike becomes a seen bike.
