The front basket is the most Vietnamese accessory on this list — the workhorse of market runs, deliveries and school pickups. It is also mounted precisely where it most affects steering, so it deserves more respect than it usually gets.

## Types

Metal wire baskets bolt to the leg shield or front frame; woven and plastic baskets hook or clamp on and lift out to carry into the shop. Aftermarket kits range from flimsy clamp-on pieces to proper steel baskets with braced mounts. Whatever you buy, the mount is the product: a basket is only as good as its attachment to the frame, not the plastic leg shield.

## The steering physics

A loaded basket moves weight onto the steering assembly — you feel it in the bars at walking pace and in crosswinds at speed. Keep loads light and low: heavy items belong in the under-seat bay (scooter-under-seat-storage-guide) or on the rear (top-boxes-racks-motorbike-vietnam), not up front. A basket loaded like a market trolley makes slow-speed control harder — the skill side of which is in slow-speed-control-motorbike-vietnam — and sharpens wobble in wind. Load rules from carrying-luggage-motorbike-safely-vietnam apply with a multiplier: forward weight is steering weight.

## What belongs in it (and never does)

Good: light groceries, a handbag, a helmet, small bags you want in reach. Bad: anything heavy, loose items that can bounce out over potholes, hot food balanced unsecured, and absolutely never a child or pet. Cover or net loose contents; items that eject from a basket at speed become hazards for the rider behind you.

## Theft and weather

Lift-out baskets get lifted out by people other than you — take the contents or the whole basket in questionable areas. Cheap mounts corrode fast in humid air; check the bolts periodically, because a rattling basket mount that finally lets go mid-corner is a remembered experience.
