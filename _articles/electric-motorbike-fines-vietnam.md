The traffic-fine schedule does not have a discount for electric drive. What an electric rider pays depends on which of the legal classes the machine sits in — and the difference between those classes is worth millions of dong.

## The key rule: class, not fuel

Under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, electric mopeds — the sub-4 kW, 50 km/h class — are fined at the same levels as motorbikes under Article 7. Only pedal-type electric bicycles, legally non-motorised vehicles, fall under the lower non-motorised fine schedule of Article 9. The machine's classification under Law 36/2024/QH15, not its battery, sets the price of mistakes.

## What the common mistakes cost (motorbike/moped levels)

| Violation | Fine (VND) |
|---|---|
| No helmet or unfastened strap | 400,000 – 600,000 |
| Handheld phone while riding | 800,000 – 1,000,000 |
| Red light | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Wrong way down a one-way street | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Sidewalk riding (non-exempt) | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Riding a machine above your licence class | 2,000,000 – 4,000,000 (up to 125 cc / 11 kW class) |
| No valid compulsory insurance certificate | 200,000 – 300,000 |
| No plates | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |

The same provisions carry licence-point deductions and, at the top of the alcohol scale, suspension — and riders under 16 are warned rather than fined for the ordinary violations, though the machine's owner may still face consequences.

## The electric-specific trap

The expensive mistake is class mismatch: a rider with no licence on a Dat Bike Weaver 200 — a 6,000 W, A1-class machine — is unlicensed in law, whatever the bike looks like. Our power-and-licence guide maps which verified Vietnamese machines need which licence. Under-16 riders have their own rules explained in the moped-at-16 article.

## The cheap defences

Helmet strapped, phone pocketed, one-way streets respected, insurance certificate and registration copy carried. None of these is difficult, and together they remove most of the schedule.
