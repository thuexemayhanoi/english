Defensive riding means accepting a simple truth: in Vietnamese traffic, you will be the one who pays for everyone else's mistakes, so ride accordingly. It is a mindset, not a technique — and it is the single biggest factor separating riders who commute for years without incidents from those who do not.

## Space is your only protection

A motorbike has no crumple zone. Keep distance from the vehicle in front — at least a two-second gap at speed, more in the wet. Never sit in a bus or truck's blind zone; either pass it decisively or drop back. The blind-zone map is in riding-motorbike-blind-spots-vietnam.

## Read behaviour, not signals

Vietnamese traffic communicates through movement: a car drifting left is going to turn left regardless of indicators; a motorbike pausing at a curb will U-turn across you without looking. Watch wheels and the shoulders of riders ahead, not brake lights alone.

## Assume you are invisible

Ride as though drivers have not seen you, because often they have not. This is not timidity — it is why you slow before parked buses, cover brakes near junctions, and avoid the door zone of parked cars. Keep your own visibility high too: lights on, bright gear, predictable lines — visibility-riding-motorbike-vietnam covers that side.

## Have an exit always

At any moment you should know where you would go if the vehicle ahead stopped dead — the gap to your left, the curb space, the escape lane. When there is no exit, create one by dropping speed. Emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam covers what happens when the exit still closes.

## When it still goes wrong

Even defensive riders fall. What to do at a scene — helping, first aid, legal duties — is covered in motorbike-accident-first-aid-vietnam and motorbike-accident-duties-vietnam. Defensive riding reduces the odds; it never removes them.

## The one-line version

Keep space, watch wheels, assume nobody saw you, always know your exit — ride like the only adult on the road.
