The Old Quarter is the hardest motorbike terrain in Hanoi: alleys that barely fit a bike, tourists stepping off kerbs without looking, food stalls claiming the roadway, and parked bikes lining every wall. The bike matters more here than anywhere else in the city.

## What the streets demand

- Width above all: the alleys (hẻm) and the walking-street fringes reward the slimmest machine. Handlebar-to-handlebar passing is constant.
- Weight: you will paddle the bike at walking speed, sometimes for a full block on weekends.
- Low speed control: the traffic never exceeds a crawl; power is irrelevant, throttle control is everything.
- Turning circle: three-point turns inside an alley are normal.

These demands come from the district itself. The Old Quarter's 36 guild streets were named for the trades that settled north of the lake, and the Vietnam Tourism website calls the area today a "hectic place" of "shops pouring into sidewalks, motorcycles navigating narrow streets". Only a few streets still sell what they were named for, so the old names tell you little about what is in the road now.

### What a bike choice can and cannot fix

A lighter, easier-to-balance bike helps with the things you control: how hard it is to paddle through a crowd, how smoothly you hold a crawl, and how small a space you need when you park. It does nothing for the things you do not control, such as one-way routing, pedestrians treating the carriageway as shared space, or weekend closures. Routing is covered in [the Old Quarter one-way streets guide]({{ '/articles/old-quarter-one-way-streets-riding/' | relative_url }}) and riding technique in [the narrow streets and alleys guide]({{ '/articles/riding-hanoi-old-quarter-narrow-streets/' | relative_url }}). This page stays on one question: which bike to rent.

## The classes, ranked

1. Semi-automatic underbones - the Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius class. Among the narrowest common rentals, the lightest-feeling steering, and a machine many local residents ride for exactly these reasons. Day rates start at 150,000 VND.
2. Small automatic scooters - the Click and Mio class (150,000 VND/day) and the Vision (200,000 VND/day). Nearly as nimble, easier for new riders, with under-seat storage for a shopping run. The fairing edges are the only thing to mind in the tightest alleys.
3. Licence-free options - a 50 cc bike or e-bike is the legal answer for riders without a motorcycle licence, and the low speeds suit the district's pace perfectly.

Anything large or wide is simply the wrong tool here - and there is nothing a bigger engine can do at 15 km/h.

The Wave Alpha 110 is the Wave on record, and the Wave you are handed may be a different variant, so ask which one it is. The manufacturers do not necessarily state weight on the same basis, so treat a few kilograms as a tie.

Three readings follow from the table:

- Width separates these bikes less than the ranking above implies. The spread from 665 to 689 mm is 24 mm, so width alone is a weak tie-breaker. The real gap is between this group and larger machines, which this guide does not recommend.
- Weight is where the Air Blade 125 stands apart: at 110 kg it is 13 to 14 kg heavier than the Vision and Wave Alpha. That matters every time you paddle the bike backwards out of a parking row.
- Honda lists 23.2 L of under-seat storage, enough for two half-helmets, on the Air Blade 125. If you carry shopping or a laptop it is a real advantage. If you carry nothing, you are paying for weight you do not use.

## Riding notes that are not bike choice

The weekend walking streets close to traffic; the alleys flood in heavy summer rain; and the sidewalk is not a shortcut - riding on the sidewalk is a fine. Keep the speed down to the crowd's pace, cover the brakes continuously, and expect pedestrians to treat the roadway as shared space. [The lane-rules article]({{ '/articles/motorbike-lane-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers where bikes legally sit on the bigger roads around the district.

The law on the sidewalk is set out in [the sidewalk riding guide]({{ '/articles/riding-on-sidewalk-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), which places it in the same fine band as running a red light. In practical terms, if the street is blocked, dismounting and walking the bike a few metres costs less than the fine.

## Weekend evenings: a bike cannot get you in

The Vietnam Tourism website notes that Hoan Kiem Lake is closed to vehicles at weekends. The detail comes from a VietnamPlus report of 1 July 2026 on the first phase of a low-emission zone pilot, which runs from 1 July to 31 December 2026.

- Zone 1 covers the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the weekend night market. Motorcycles, motorbikes and all cars, except those operated by the Ministries of National Defence and Public Security, are prohibited from entering between 19:00 and 24:00 every Friday, Saturday and Sunday.
- The report does not separate bikes by class or engine type, so no rental bike choice changes that rule.
- Zone 2, the wider Old Quarter and lake area inside Ring Road 1, has peak-hour restrictions from 06:00 to 09:00 and 16:00 to 19:30. In the report they apply to fossil-fuel passenger vehicles with more than 16 seats, not to motorbikes.

Because this was a first-phase pilot with a stated end date, confirm the current notice before a weekend plan depends on it. The practical response is to ride to the edge, park once and walk. [The Hoan Kiem walking street guide]({{ '/articles/hoan-kiem-walking-street-motorbike-visitors/' | relative_url }}) covers approach routes and parking on those nights. On those evenings, the best bike is the one that is easiest to slot into a crowded lot.

## How each class copes with kerbs, alleys and parking rows

This section is practical guidance rather than measured data, apart from the tyre and wheel sizes quoted from Honda's pages.

### Wheels, step-through and the crawl

Honda lists a 70/90-17 front tyre for the Wave Alpha 110, a 17-inch rim, and the Sirius RC 110 shares those tyre sizes. Honda lists the Vision's front wheel as 16 inch. As a general rule, a larger wheel rolls over a raised kerb edge, a drain cover or a pothole edge more easily than a smaller one,.

The automatic scooter has its own advantage in a crowd. With no gear to select, you hold a steady crawl on the throttle and brake alone, and the step-through frame lets you put a foot down or step off without swinging a leg over a seat. A foot-shifted bike asks you to stay in first gear and pull away smoothly without a hand clutch. Neither is hard, but a rider who has never shifted with a foot will find the scooter kinder in a stop-start crowd.

### Bodywork, mirrors and parking rows

Scooters carry more plastic bodywork, so a drop usually costs more to put right than on an underbone. Mirrors are the part most often caught in tight rows on either, and folding them in when you park is a cheap habit. A narrower bike also needs less of a gap when attendants stack bikes in a row.

## Parking in the Old Quarter

Parking is the real constraint: street space is tight and attended lots (gửi xe) are the norm around the walking streets. A slim bike fits into lot rows that a big scooter cannot, and a lighter bike is easier to walk into an alley-side spot.

Use an attended lot where you can, keep the ticket, take your helmet and leave nothing under the seat. [The parking guide]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers fees, no-parking zones and etiquette. If you are staying in the district, ask your hotel where renters usually park before you collect the bike. Delivery to a hotel may be available depending on location and arrangement, within opening hours of 09:00 to 21:00; [the Old Quarter delivery guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-delivery-old-quarter/' | relative_url }}) explains how that works, and [the Old Quarter rental guide]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-old-quarter/' | relative_url }}) covers the rest of the rental logistics.

## Checks at handover that matter here

The full list is in [the rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}). These are the extra checks that matter for Old Quarter use:

1. Sit on the bike and put both feet down. You will stop and start constantly, so you need to reach the ground without stretching.
2. Test the rear brake for lever travel and bite. You will cover it for most of the ride.
3. Check that both mirrors adjust and hold their position, and that the horn works. A light tap tells people facing away that you are there.
4. Look at the tyres. The Sirius RC 110 runs tubed tyres according to Yamaha, and kerbs and potholes are what damage tubes and tyres. Consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so ask who covers a puncture before you leave.
5. Check that the steering lock and key work smoothly. You will lock the bike many times a day.
6. Photograph existing scratches. Parked bikes touching each other is normal here.

## Mistakes that make the wrong bike feel right

- Choosing for engine size. At the district's crawl, extra power is unused and extra weight is not. The Air Blade 125 is the heaviest of the four published bikes above.
- Renting a foot-shifted bike without having tried the shift. Try it at the shop before you ride into the district, not after.
- Planning a Friday, Saturday or Sunday evening ride into Zone 1. Per the 1 July 2026 report, the bike is not allowed in during those hours.
- Treating the pavement as an overflow lane. It is a fined violation, and the guide above explains where the line is.
- Renting the biggest bike "just in case". If your trips go beyond the district, a larger scooter may suit those days, but the Old Quarter itself still favours the small bike.

## Common questions

### Is a 50cc bike or e-bike enough for the Old Quarter?

On pace, yes: traffic in the heart of the district moves at a crawl. A 50cc petrol bike has no fixed published price, so contact us, and the E-Bike day rate is 200,000 VND. The licence rules for each class are in the 50cc and e-bike guides linked above.

### Wave or scooter for the narrowest alleys?

Both work. The Wave Alpha 110 and the Vision differ by only 3 mm in published width, so choose by transmission: a foot-shifted Wave if you already ride one, an automatic if you do not.

### What do the small bikes cost per day?

The approved day rate is 150,000 VND for the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius, Honda Click and Yamaha Mio, and 200,000 VND for the Honda Vision and Honda Air Blade. Contact us to confirm current availability.

### Can I ride into the Old Quarter on Saturday night?

Not into Zone 1. According to the 1 July 2026 VietnamPlus report on the pilot, motorcycles are prohibited there from 19:00 to 24:00 on Friday, Saturday and Sunday until the first phase ends on 31 December 2026. Park at the edge and walk in.

### Do I need a licence to ride these bikes?

The rules depend on the bike class and your documents, and they are set out in [the rental requirements guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). A 50cc bike or pedal e-bike sits in a different legal box from the 110 cc to 125 cc models above.
