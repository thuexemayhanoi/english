The Honda Vision and the Honda Lead 125 are two of Honda's city scooters, and they split the choice cleanly: the Vision is the light default commuter, the Lead is the cargo specialist. Both comparisons below use only Honda Vietnam's published figures (retrieved 23 September 2026), apart from one labelled Yamaha storage figure, and the Honda pages were read again on 27 October 2026.

## Verdict in brief

Choose the Vision for the lighter scooter: 97 kg, a 16-inch front wheel, 175 mm of ground clearance and the lower claimed fuel use. Choose the Lead 125 when underseat space comes first: Honda says it takes two full-face helmets, and it adds a liquid-cooled 125 cc engine, a 760 mm seat and a 6.0-litre tank. The single-model write-ups are the [Honda Vision 110 review]({{ '/articles/honda-vision-110-review/' | relative_url }}) and the [Honda Lead 125 review]({{ '/articles/honda-lead-125-review/' | relative_url }}).

## Engine

Vision: 109.5 cc air-cooled eSP engine, 6.59 kW at 7,500 rpm, 9.29 Nm at 6,000 rpm. Lead: 124.8 cc liquid-cooled engine, 8.22 kW at 8,500 rpm, 11.7 Nm at 5,250 rpm. The Lead is stronger on both published peak figures - the practical difference shows up two-up or on bridges; alone in city traffic both should be adequate. [Our 110 vs 125 explainer]({{ '/articles/110cc-vs-125cc-scooters/' | relative_url }}) covers the licence and insurance angles of the class step.

The peak-power gap is 1.63 kW and the torque gap is 2.41 Nm. The Lead also reaches its torque peak at a lower engine speed (5,250 rpm against 6,000 rpm) and its power peak at a higher one (8,500 rpm against 7,500 rpm). If kW and Nm are unfamiliar, our [power and torque explainer]({{ '/articles/scooter-power-torque-explained/' | relative_url }}) explains what each one measures.

Cooling is the other difference: the Vision is air-cooled and the Lead liquid-cooled, so the Lead has a coolant system for a mechanic to check and the Vision does not. Honda's pages do not say which copes better with a Hanoi summer. This is a maintenance point, not a performance one.

## Weight and size

Vision: 97 kg, seat 785 mm, 16-inch front wheel. Lead: 114 kg, seat 760 mm, 12/10-inch wheels. The Vision is easier to paddle through jams and park on kerbs; the Lead's lower seat helps shorter riders despite the extra 17 kg - our seat-height table has the numbers.

The Lead is the shorter scooter (1,844 mm against 1,925 mm) but the wider one (714 mm against 686 mm). Height differs by 6 mm and wheelbase by 4 mm, which is not a reason to choose either, and neither page gives a turning-circle figure.

Two gaps are big enough to feel. One is the 17 kg when you push a scooter out of a parking row. The other is ground clearance, 175 mm against 140 mm, which matters over raised kerbs, ramps and patched road edges. Seat width and shape are not in Honda's table, so sitting on both tells you more than the 25 mm height gap.

## Fuel economy

Vision: 1.82 L/100 km published from a 4.8-litre tank. Lead: 2.1 L/100 km from a 6.0-litre tank. The Vision is the cheaper horse to feed; the Lead carries more fuel, so tank-to-tank range is closer than the economy gap suggests. Honda's pages read do not state the test conditions behind the consumption figures.

Doing the sum goes one step further. A 4.8 L tank at 1.82 L/100 km is about 264 km, and a 6.0 L tank at 2.1 L/100 km is about 286 km. These are our arithmetic from Honda's two figures, not a range Honda claims, so on paper the Lead covers about 22 km more per tank while using 0.28 L more per 100 km.

Honda's Vision page also lists an idling-stop system that switches the engine off after more than 3 seconds stopped and restarts it when you twist the throttle; Honda says it reduces noise and saves fuel. The LEAD ABS page text read does not mention one. Try it before you decide, since how much it helps depends on how often your route stops. The [scooter fuel economy comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-fuel-economy-comparison-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets both against more models.

## Storage - the Lead's kingdom

Vision: an underseat bay Honda calls large but gives no litre figure for; the [storage comparison]({{ '/articles/scooter-underseat-storage-comparison/' | relative_url }}) lists other scooters' bays in litres. Lead: more than 37 litres (Honda's wording) - two full-face helmets, level with the Yamaha Latte 125 for the biggest bay in our verified data. If your scooter doubles as a shopping trolley or delivery platform, the Lead is the clearer choice on Honda's published storage figures; Honda also offers an ABS version of the Lead.

Yamaha Vietnam lists the Latte 125 at 37 litres underseat as well (read 23 September 2026); see our [Yamaha Latte 125 review]({{ '/articles/yamaha-latte-125-review/' | relative_url }}), so on published figures the Lead and the Latte are comparable. Honda's Vision page adds only that its bay takes a helmet and personal items, so the helmet counts, not litres, point to the Lead as the bigger bay.

For the Lead, Honda describes a divider panel, an interior light and room for two large full-face helmets. The Lead page also lists a front compartment with a USB-C port (marked with an asterisk), so confirm it on the version you choose. Bring your own helmet to try in the bay.

## Brakes and tyres

Honda's LEAD ABS page ties front-wheel ABS to the special version and says it limits wheel slip under hard braking, including on wet, slippery roads. The Vision page text read lists no ABS and gives no brake type, so this page makes no brake claim for the Vision. Honda's price list shows three Lead versions, so confirm that the one you are looking at has ABS.

The Vision rolls on 80/90-16 at the front and 90/90-14 at the rear; the Lead on 90/90-12 and 100/90-10. As a rule of thumb, bigger wheels take the sting out of bumps and smaller wheels are easier to flick around at low speed; the [16-inch wheel guide]({{ '/articles/16-inch-wheel-scooters-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the trade-off. Honda describes the Vision's 16-inch front wheel as giving a high, upright riding position and a wide view. The pages read do not say whether the tyres are tubeless.

## What the differences mean on Hanoi streets

### Narrow lanes and parking

Riding in Hanoi mixes stop-start main roads with lanes where you sometimes have to turn on the spot. The Vision's lower weight and the Lead's shorter body pull in different directions, so the table cannot say which turns tighter. Both have Smart Key, which on Honda's pages locates the scooter and unlocks it remotely, a help in a crowded parking row. The Lead adds an alarm on the special and premium versions.

### Wet roads

The Lead's front ABS, which Honda's page ties to the special version, is the one wet-road feature Honda describes for either scooter. Neither page gives wet-grip or wading-depth data, so the clearance gap and the tyre sizes cannot be ranked. The [rainy-season scooter guide]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-rainy-season-commuting-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what to look for.

### Carrying a passenger

The Lead's 8.22 kW and 11.7 Nm give it more in reserve with two people aboard, although it is also the heavier scooter before anyone sits on it. Neither page text read gives a maximum load, so check the owner's manual that each Honda page links to.

## Common questions

### Is the Honda Lead 125 faster than the Honda Vision?

Honda publishes no top-speed figure on the pages read. On the peak figures it does publish the Lead is stronger, at 8.22 kW and 11.7 Nm against 6.59 kW and 9.29 Nm, while its extra weight (114 kg against 97 kg) works the other way.

### Which one uses less petrol?

The Vision, on Honda's claimed figures: 1.82 L/100 km against 2.1 L/100 km. The Lead's larger tank gives it a longer arithmetic range per tank, and real use depends on speed, load and traffic.

### Do the Vision and the Lead need different licences?

Not on engine size. The [A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) on this site covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc under Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15, and the Vision is 109.5 cc while the Lead is 124.8 cc. Foreign visitors should read that guide for how a foreign licence and an International Driving Permit are treated.
