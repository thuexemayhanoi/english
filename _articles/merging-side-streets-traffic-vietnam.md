Coming out of an alley, a shopfront or a parking lot into a stream of moving motorbikes is one of the most unnerving moves in Vietnamese traffic. The good news: the stream is usually slower and more forgiving than it looks. The bad news: nobody will stop to let you in, so you have to build your own gap.

## Position, then commit

Roll to the edge of the side street and stop with your front wheel close to the flow. Look left, then right, then left again — Vietnamese traffic runs both ways and larger vehicles sometimes use the "wrong" side of a narrow lane. When you see a gap of a few bike-lengths, move out decisively at the speed the stream is moving. Hesitant half-merges cause more trouble than confident ones.

## Match speed, do not force priority

Right of way at an unmarked merge is theoretical. Practical rule: the traffic already on the road keeps going, and you join without making anyone brake or swerve. Accelerate in a straight line first, settle into the stream, then change position gradually. Never cross three lanes in one motion.

## The horn is information

A short beep as you nose out tells the flow you exist. That is normal communication in Vietnam, not aggression — read more in defensive-riding-motorbike-vietnam.

## Watch the blind-spot mirrors of bigger vehicles

A bus or truck heading toward your merge point may not have seen you at all; assume you are invisible until proven otherwise. riding-motorbike-blind-spots-vietnam covers riding around large vehicles in detail.

## Practise the move off-peak

Do your first merges on a quiet Sunday morning, then graduate to rush hour once the timing makes sense. For the general skill base, start with slow-speed-control-motorbike-vietnam, and for when traffic thickens, traffic-at-rush-hour-riding-hanoi.
