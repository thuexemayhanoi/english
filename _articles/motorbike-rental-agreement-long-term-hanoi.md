Every rental has an agreement; a long-term rental has one that deserves to be taken seriously, because every term in it operates for weeks or months instead of days. The general agreement basics are in [Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) — this is the long-term layer.

Several terms change when a rental runs for months: how it is renewed or ended, when each payment falls due, who looks after the bike between visits, what the deposit is held against, and how the agreement stays accurate as plans and papers change. Nguyen Tu publishes a position on some of these and none on others, and the table further down keeps the two apart. Longer-term rental is negotiable, so the figures and dates for your term are for you to discuss with the shop.

## The terms that only matter at month scale

- Maintenance schedule: the shop's maintenance (oil, chain, tyre pressure, inspection) may be part of a long-term deal; agree how and when visits happen — see [Maintenance During a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})
- Consumables: tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation — a long-term ambiguity to close early, so get it in writing ([Tyres, Tubes and Consumables on a Long-Term Rental]({{ '/articles/consumables-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}))
- Usage scope: commute-only or trips outside Hanoi — agree it, per [Can You Take a Rental Motorbike Outside Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }})
- Riders: who may ride the bike; only the licensed rider agreed with the shop ([Lending Your Motorbike to an Unlicensed Rider in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the legal stakes)
- Deposit over the term: the band (typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND) and the return standard — [Deposits for Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-deposit-monthly-hanoi/' | relative_url }})
- Renewal and exit: notice expectations, renewal rate basis ([Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), and the no-automatic-refund reality of early ending ([Shortening a Long Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/shortening-long-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}), [Ending a Monthly Motorbike Rental Early]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}))

## Structure: fixed-term or rolling

Two shapes work: a fixed multi-month term agreed once (a rate to discuss with the shop, less flexibility — negotiate the early-exit clause explicitly), or month-to-month renewal (flexible, renewed by message each month). Match the structure to how certain your stay is.

Nguyen Tu's published rental periods are daily, weekly and monthly, with longer terms negotiable: contact the shop to discuss longer-term rental for your dates. Nothing is published about how either shape is arranged, so what follows is a set of questions, and the answers belong in the thread with the shop. [The three-month rental guide]({{ '/articles/three-month-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lays a quarter out month by month under both shapes; this page stays with the terms themselves.

### What each shape leaves open

A fixed term leaves open what an early exit would cost and whether an extension is possible at all. A rolling arrangement leaves open how much notice the shop wants before each month ends, whether the rate and your bike's availability are confirmed again each time, and what happens if a month is not renewed in time.

Nothing published makes either shape the cheaper one. If both are on offer, set them side by side line by line: deposit, maintenance, early exit and extension.

## What the Civil Code adds over several months

The short-term agreement guide linked at the top of this page walks through the Civil Code (Law 91/2015/QH13) lease articles on rent, handover, care and return. This section adds the articles that matter more when a rental lasts months. It is a general guide to what those articles say, not legal advice, and the official text is in Vietnamese.

### Term, notice and renewal

Article 474(2) deals with a lease that has no agreed term and no term that can be worked out from the purpose of the hire: either party may end the contract at any time, but must give the other reasonable notice beforehand. The article does not say how long reasonable notice is, so a notice period written into the thread is worth more than the fallback. None of Articles 472 to 482 sets out how a lease is renewed, which leaves a renewal to whatever the paper and the messages say.

### Rent paid by period

Article 481(2) applies where the parties agree that rent is paid by periods, such as month by month. The lessor may then end the contract if the lessee fails to pay for three consecutive periods, unless the parties agree otherwise or the law provides otherwise. Because the rule gives way to an agreement, ask what the paper says about a late or missed payment; the published facts say nothing on it.

### Faults over a long hire

Over months a bike has more time to develop a fault, which is why Article 477 matters here. If the bike loses usable value through no fault of the renter, Article 477(2) lets the renter ask the lessor for one or more of a repair, a lower rent or, where the bike has a defect the renter did not know about or cannot be repaired so that the purpose of the hire is not met, a replacement or an end to the contract with damages.

Under Article 477(3), if the lessor has been told of the fault and does not repair it, or repairs it late, the renter may repair it at reasonable cost, must tell the lessor, and may ask the lessor to pay that cost. Because that route starts from the lessor having been told, report a fault in the thread as soon as it appears, with a photograph and the date. Ask who pays before any repair is made; the published wording already leaves tubes and tyres open as the renter's possible cost.

Article 477(1) leaves minor damage that custom puts on the renter out of the lessor's duty to repair, so ask where the line falls.

### Ending the agreement

The lease articles give a renter several specific exits, among them Article 477(2) in the cases described under faults above, and Article 478(2) where a dispute over who owns the bike stops the renter from using it steadily. The general rule in Article 428(1) lets a party end a contract without owing damages where the other has seriously breached it, where the parties have agreed it or where the law provides it.

In the articles read for this guide, a change in the renter's own plans is not one of those grounds, apart from the no-fixed-term case in Article 474(2) above, unless the parties have agreed it, which is why the early-exit clause is worth negotiating.

Article 428(5) adds that a party who ends a contract without one of those grounds counts as the party in breach and takes civil liability for it. That sits beside the published position that early return carries no automatic refund entitlement, and it makes an early exit a conversation to hold before the date, not a notice delivered at the door.

Where a ground does exist, Article 428(2) requires the party ending the contract to tell the other straight away, and Article 428(3) ends the contract from the moment the other party receives the notice. The date and hour of a notice therefore matter, and a message in the thread with the shop carries both.

## Recording it

The Hanoi-practical standard: the spoken agreement at handover, then a one-message written summary to the shop — dates, rate, deposit, the long-term points above, and the return arrangement. Photos of the bike close the record. This is one short message plus the photographs, and it gives both sides one record to check against if a disagreement arises.

### At the start

One message at handover can carry every row of the table above that applies, plus who may ride, by name. Ask the shop to reply with a yes or a correction to each line, and keep the reply with the handover photographs.

### Each month

On a fixed day each month, add to the same thread the odometer reading, any new mark with a photograph, any fault reported and the answer it got, and each payment with its proof. If rent is paid period by period, those proofs show which periods are settled. The same entry is a natural moment to ask whether a check is due, since the shop owner's 1,500 km guidance is counted in distance, not months.

### At the end

Well before the end date, say in the thread whether you will renew or return. Settle a renewal before the date rather than after it: the published late-return charge applies to a bike that comes back late, and whether time agreed afterwards is charged that way is a question to settle in advance. Then ask how and when the deposit will be returned and whether and how the bike will be checked at return, and keep the answers with the first message so that the start and the end of the record read together.

## When terms need updating

An agreement made in month one can drift from reality by month three: a new address ([Moving House During a Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/moving-address-during-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), a different bike ([Switching Bikes Mid-Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/switching-bikes-mid-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), a licence about to expire ([When Your Licence or Visa Expires Mid-Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/licence-expiry-during-long-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})). Update the record by message when facts change — an agreement is a living document, not a one-time event.

### Licence and visa dates across the term

If your licence, any international driving permit or your visa expires inside the term, tell the shop in the thread and plan the renewal or the return around it. [The long-term rider's licence checklist]({{ '/articles/long-term-rider-licence-checklist-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the documents to hold and a monthly check to put in your phone.

### Swapping the bike mid-term

A swap changes the agreement, not only the bike. Nothing published promises a replacement or says how the deposit and the days carry across, so ask first and record the answers: the plate and odometer reading of the bike you hand back, the condition of the new one, the rent for the rest of the term and whether the deposit stays as it is.

### Passing the bike on to someone else

Article 475 of the Civil Code lets a lessee sublease the property only if the lessor agrees. If you are leaving Hanoi early and thought of renting the bike on to a friend for the months still to run, ask the shop before anything changes hands. Who may ride the bike is a separate term from subletting it, and belongs with the riders line in the list at the top. Whatever changes, put the new end date, the amount for the new period and any altered term in the thread, so that the first message and the update read as one record.
