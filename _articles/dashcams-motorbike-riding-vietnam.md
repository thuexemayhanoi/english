In any dispute after an accident, the question is always "what actually happened?" A dashcam answers it. Vietnam's traffic generates constant close calls, and riders increasingly mount small cameras for exactly the reason drivers do: an objective record.

## What a rider's cam actually buys you

Evidence after an incident is the main value: footage settles fault disputes far better than two versions of events. Many riders find a second, quieter benefit — reviewing your own clips is the fastest way to see your riding mistakes. What a cam does not do is prevent anything; never let a camera tempt you into riding to get footage.

## Mounts, vibration and helmets

Handlebar mounts are simple but vibrate; chest mounts give stable footage of the road and your hands; helmet-mounted action cameras record what you actually see. Whatever you pick, check the mount tightens with tools — clamp-style mounts survive Vietnamese roads, stick-on ones peel in the heat. Vibration ruins footage more than resolution does.

## Power and heat

Small action cameras last one to two hours per charge: enough for a commute, not for a Ha Giang loop. Carry a power bank, or hard-wire a dedicated cam. Midday heat cooks batteries and SD cards — park in shade where you can.

## Theft is the real risk

A visible camera on an unattended bike is an invitation, especially in tourist areas. Take it with you or hide it in the under-seat storage — the same habits as keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi. On a rental bike, use mounts that leave no marks and confirm what the owner is comfortable with.
