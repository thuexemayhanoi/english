Across the Red River and into Bac Ninh province lies one of the most pleasant short loops from Hanoi: But Thap, northern Vietnam’s most completely preserved ancient pagoda complex, and Phu Lang, the less-touristy pottery village that locals rate above Bat Trang for atmosphere. Both sit in the Thuan Thanh craft-and-heritage belt roughly 30–45 kilometres from the city.

## The loop out

Cross to the east bank (Chuong Duong or the newer cable-stayed bridges) and
 follow the Duong River corridor northeast. Two riding styles work: the fast line straight out the Bac Ninh highway, or the slower, better dyke-top road that shadows the Duong toward Thuan Thanh — flat, green and lightly trafficked. Count 45 minutes to an hour to the pagoda area.

## But Thap Pagoda

But Thap (Ninh Phuc Tu) is the high point of the trip: a 17th-century complex of courtyards, towers and a celebrated thousand-eyed, thousand-armed Guanyin statue, all set beside the Duong dyke. It is calm, shaded and architectural in a way few Vietnamese pagodas are — arrive early and you may have the courtyards to yourself. Modest dress applies; a small entry fee is charged.

## Phu Lang pottery village

From But Thap, loop south to Phu Lang on the east bank of the Cau River. Phu Lang is the ceramic village with the terracotta aesthetic — rustic, wood-fired glazes rather than Bat Trang’s celadons — and it receives a fraction of the visitors. Watch kilns and wheels in the village lanes, buy directly from family workshops, and wrap purchases well for the ride home (the carrying-luggage guide is worth reading before you shop). Lunch of Bac Ninh-style spring rolls in the village rounds out the day.

## Practical notes

- The loop rides as a half day at a brisk pace, a relaxed full day with lunch and shopping.
- Dyke-road surfaces vary after rain — the standard caution applies.
- The return crossing into Hanoi is busiest from late afternoon; earlier is calmer.
