Electric two-wheelers have quietly become day-trip machines — Dat Bike itself markets the Weaver 200 with Hanoi–Ha Long as an example trip. The skill that makes it work is honest range math.

## Step 1: Know your real number

Take the claimed range and cut it for your actual pace. The Weaver 200's own official figures give the method: 200 km at 35 km/h, 130 km at 70 km/h. Trip riding sits between the two, loaded and into wind: plan on roughly 60-70 percent of the low-speed claim for a mixed-speed day.

## Step 2: Route out the charge stops

- **Swap-model riders (VinFast Evo/Feliz II/Viper):** 9,000 VND per battery per swap, cabinets across 34 provinces and cities — swap at the outward coffee stop and the range question disappears.
- **Dat Bike riders:** Dat Charge stations give the Weaver++ 100 km in 20 minutes; plan lunch within reach of one. Home-charged Weaver 20
0s can also charge over a long stop (1 hour buys 100 km).
- **Moped-class bikes:** their 60-165 km claims suit short loops (Bat Trang, Dong Mo, Chuong My) rather than coast-bound routes.

## Step 3: The margin rule

Plan to arrive home with 20 percent or more in reserve. Headwinds, hills, a passenger and detours all bill the same battery. If the plan only works at 0 percent, it is not a plan.

## Route families that fit today's electrics

- **Inside 60 km round trip:** any electric, including pedal e-bikes.
- **60-120 km:** sub-4 kW scooters with care; A1 machines comfortably.
- **120-250 km:** Weaver-class machines with one planned charge or swap stop — Ha Long territory, as the brand itself suggests.

Always confirm station and cabinet locations before departure, not after the gauge drops.
