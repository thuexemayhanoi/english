Mai Chau is the classic first overnight motorbike trip from Hanoi: a green valley of Thai stilt-house villages about 140 kilometres southwest of the city, reachable in roughly four to five hours of riding on National Highway 6 (QL6). You leave in the morning, climb over Thung Khe Pass in the afternoon, sleep in a homestay, and ride home the next day. For riders who have only done day trips so far, it is the gentlest possible introduction to spending a night in the mountains with a motorbike.

## Day 1: Hanoi to the valley

The standard line out of the city runs via Thang Long Avenue to the Hoa Lac area and Xuan Mai, after which the road settles into countryside and the traffic thins out. From there you stay on QL6 through Luong Son, past Hoa Binh city, and along the Hoa Binh lake area to Cao Phong, Tan Lac and finally Thung Khe Pass, which drops you into the Mai Chau basin. Published routings put the whole distance at about 139-140 kilometres.

Treat the first 30 or so kilometres out of Hanoi as the least pleasant part — busy, dusty and urban — and plan your first real stop around Hoa Binh city, where the lake and dam make a natural tea break. After Hoa Binh the road becomes genuinely scenic, following the Black River reservoir with limestone walls on one side. Fuel is easy to find along QL6; top up in Hoa Binh anyway so you never think about it again.

Thung Khe Pass is the technical highlight: a winding climb with a famous white-rock section and a viewpoint near the top. It is a real mountain pass, so apply everything in our guide [to mountain pass riding]({{ '/articles/mountain-pass-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) — slow in, steady throttle, no overtaking on blind bends. On clear days the view over the valley is one of the best in the region; in fog it becomes a careful, low-speed descent, so check our low-visibility riding advice before you set off.

## Sleeping in Mai Chau

Most riders stay in or around Lac village and the neighbouring Mai Chau villages, where Thai family homestays serve dinner and breakfast on raised stilt houses among rice paddies. Our overnight-stays guide covers what to check before booking: secure night parking for the bike, how homestay pricing usually works, and why you should confirm parking when you book rather than on arrival. Book ahead on weekends and in the rice season, when the valley fills up.

## Day 2 options

Two common plans for the ride home: the direct return back over QL6 (about four hours to Hanoi), or the loop south and east through Pu Luong toward Thanh Hoa, then back via the Ho Chi Minh-road area toward Ninh Binh and home. The direct return is the right choice for a first overnight trip — you will already have five or six hours of riding in your legs, and our guide to how far to ride in a day explains why finishing before dark matters more than adding kilometres.

## Practical notes

- Best seasons are autumn (September-November) and spring; mid-summer is hot on QL6 and the pass can cloud over quickly. Our North Vietnam trip-seasons guide has the full calendar.
- Run through the trip-prep checklist the evening before: chain, brakes, tyre pressures, lights, documents.
- A 110-125cc scooter or underbone manages this route comfortably solo; a pillion plus luggage makes Thung Khe Pass noticeably harder work.
- Motorbikes are not permitted on the expressways; QL6 is the legal, correct road for bikes, and it is the scenic one anyway.
