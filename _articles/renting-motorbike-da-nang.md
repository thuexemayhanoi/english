Da Nang is the most rider-friendly big city in Vietnam: broad boulevards, a spectacular coastline, and — uniquely — an official tourism portal that openly lists motorbike rental services. Rental here is cheap, easy and genuinely useful, because the city is the natural base for two of the country's classic rides: the Son Tra peninsula loop and the Hai Van Pass.

## Prices and where to rent

Published guides put semi-automatic underbones at roughly 80,000-120,000 VND per day and automatic scooters at 120,000-160,000 VND, with a wider 80,000-180,000 VND band across the city's shops depending on model and season. Monthly rates run about 1,500,000-2,500,000 VND for the scooter class — useful for the city's long-stay expat and digital-nomad crowd, one of the largest in Vietnam.

Rental counters cluster around My Khe beach, the An Thuong backpacker streets, and the city centre; most hotels arrange bikes as well. As everywhere, a cash deposit beats surrendering your passport, and the helmet should come with the rental by law.

## The law

The same national rules apply: over 50cc requires a Vietnamese licence or a 1968-convention IDP endorsed for motorbikes. Police enforcement in tourist areas of Da Nang is more active than in many cities, precisely because so many visitors ride without valid papers. Our tourist-riding overview explains what you can legally ride.

## The three classic rides from Da Nang

**Son Tra peninsula (Monkey Mountain).** A half-day loop on a sealed, winding forest road from the city beach to the Linh Ung pagoda and viewpoints over the coast. The steepest sections are short but constant; an automatic 110-125cc scooter manages it easily. Watch for monkeys on the road surface, and go early before the tour jeeps.

**Hoi An.** Flat, fast and scenic along the coast road — 30-40 minutes each way, ideal for an evening in the old town with the bike parked in a guarded lot (see our nationwide [parking guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-parking-vietnamese-cities/' | relative_url }})). Alternatively the quiet route past rice paddies via Cam Thanh.

**The Hai Van Pass.** The reason many travellers rent in Da Nang at all: the full pass ride from the city to Hue (or there and back in a long day). Our dedicated Hai Van Pass guide covers the route, the tunnel rules for motorbikes and what the pass demands from a rider. A scooter handles the pass; respect the downhill and the fog that gives the pass its name ("Sea of Clouds").

## Practical notes

- The monsoon hits central Vietnam hard in September-November: check our nationwide seasons guide before planning a coastal riding week.
- Sand on beach-road corners after wind is the most common local hazard; our after-rain surface guide applies the same caution.
- Motorbikes are banned from expressways nationwide, including the Da Nang tunnels and bridges marked CT — the coast roads are the correct route anyway.
