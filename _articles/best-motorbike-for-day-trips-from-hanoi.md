A day trip changes the job description: higher sustained speeds, highway sections, unfamiliar roads and a bike that must not become the story of the day. The answer is a 110-125 cc automatic scooter or a semi-automatic underbone, and the trip itself is worth raising with the shop first, because support from Nguyen Tu is primarily for Hanoi and local use.

## Raise the trip with the shop before you book

Nguyen Tu's support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, and every recommendation below assumes that limit. A day out of the city takes the bike away from the place where that support is based, so say where you plan to ride when you ask about a bike, and ask the shop about the trip before you book.

What is not offered matters as much. No roadside rescue, recovery or route support is published, and nothing published covers delivery to, or collection from, a place outside Hanoi. Delivery and pickup in Hanoi may be available depending on location and arrangement. Plan the day so that it does not depend on outside help, and do not assume that a call to the shop will end with a recovery truck or a replacement bike.

Questions to settle before you pay:

- Is the destination acceptable, and is there a distance the shop wants you to stay within?
- Who do you call if the bike stops, and when will the call be answered? The shop's published hours are 09:00-21:00 daily, on +84 942 467 674 (phone, WhatsApp or Zalo).
- What return time applies, and what happens if you are late? Late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and more than 6 hours late a full additional rental day may be charged (typically 150,000-200,000 VND depending on motorcycle).
- If a tyre is punctured on the way, who pays? Inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation.
- Which of the bikes does the shop think suits your route?

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance; customers arrange their own cover before they ride. The guide to [taking a rental motorbike outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) goes through the rental-terms side in more detail.

## What a day trip demands

- Sustained 50-60 km/h ability: the common 110-125 cc rental class holds these speeds comfortably all day. Anything smaller works but works hard on the open stretches.
- Comfort for 2-4 hours of riding: seat padding and posture matter more than they do inside the city.
- Reliability: a day-trip breakdown is a day lost. Start from a shop that maintains its fleet - oil, chain, tyres, inspection - and do the checklist at handover.
- Storage: under-seat bay or a rack bag for water, layers and a lock.

The list leaves out raw power on purpose. The route guides for [Bat Trang]({{ '/articles/bat-trang-pottery-village-motorbike-ride/' | relative_url }}), [Thay and Tay Phuong]({{ '/articles/thay-pagoda-tay-phuong-pagoda-motorbike-route/' | relative_url }}), the [Perfume Pagoda]({{ '/articles/perfume-pagoda-motorbike-trip/' | relative_url }}) and [Tam Dao]({{ '/articles/tam-dao-day-trip-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) each cover one destination; this guide covers the bike that carries you there.

## The classes that suit

Automatic scooters - the Honda Vision, Honda Air Blade, Click, Yamaha Mio class - are the default day-trip tool: comfortable, quick enough, storage, and the Air Blade's size suits the faster sections best of the common rentals. Semi-automatic underbones (Wave, Sirius) take day trips too - that is what they are built for across rural Vietnam - with the foot-shift as the trade-off. Rates: 150,000-200,000 VND per day.

### Automatic or semi-automatic

On a day out the difference is what your left foot and your luggage have to do. A scooter asks nothing of your feet and has a bay under the seat. An underbone asks you to change gear, which is a nuisance at village crossings and a help when you want to hold a lower gear on a descent.

## How far a tank goes

Range is arithmetic on two published numbers: the tank, and the fuel consumption the manufacturer claims. Honda and Yamaha publish both for the two semi-automatic models. The [long-distance guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-long-distance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works the range sum: about 215 km for the Wave Alpha 110 (3.7 L, 1.72 L/100 km) and about 202 km for the Sirius RC 110 (4.2 L, 2.08 L/100 km). This guide quotes no tank or consumption figure for the scooters, so ask the shop for the tank size and claimed consumption of the bike you are given and work the same sum (tank in litres divided by litres per 100 km, times 100).

### A planning figure, not a promise

A calculated range is the distance to an empty tank under the manufacturer's own test method, not a day plan. Real-world consumption changes with speed, load, temperature and terrain: a passenger, luggage, a headwind or a climb all push it up.

A working rule that follows from the half-tank margin in the roads section below: plan to refill when the gauge reaches half. Half of the two calculated ranges is about 108 km and about 101 km, so roughly 100 km between fills is a sensible planning figure for these two bikes. That is a planning choice for this guide, not a manufacturer figure, so note the odometer at each fill as a second check on the gauge.

### Against the day-trip distances

The route guides, citing travel-site figures, give one-way distances of about 30 km from central Hanoi to the Thay and Tay Phuong pagoda area, about 55 to 60 km to the Perfume Pagoda and about 85 km to Tam Dao. Doubling them gives round trips of about 60 km, 110 to 120 km and 170 km, before any detour, wrong turn or loop between sites. Bat Trang's guide gives a time, not a distance: 20 to 30 minutes from the centre. For how long a loaded ride really takes, the guide to [how far you can ride in a day from Hanoi]({{ '/articles/how-far-motorbike-day-trip-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers average speeds and how far is too far for a return trip.

Set against roughly 100 km between fills, the Thay and Tay Phuong round trip fits within one half-tank margin if you start full. The Perfume Pagoda round trip is a little over it, so plan one refill. Tam Dao needs at least one refill, and its final climb is the part of the ride where you will want margin in the tank. Check the figures against your own route before you leave.

## Comfort, luggage and a passenger

### Comfort over a morning in the saddle

Sit on the bike at handover with your helmet on and the bag you will carry. The seat should feel supportive, your hands and feet should sit naturally, and the mirrors should show the road behind you rather than your elbows. Tall riders should test leg room in particular on a small bike. Plan a break at the far end and another on the way home; a stop is also the moment to look at the tyres and the chain.

### Luggage

Under-seat space varies by bike. Honda quotes 23.2 L for the Air Blade 125, and this guide quotes no bay figure for the Vision, Click, Mio, Wave or Sirius, so ask what carrying space the bike has before you commit. Water, a rain layer, a lock and a power bank are small enough for most arrangements. Keep heavy bags off the handlebars. The guide to [what to carry while riding]({{ '/articles/what-to-carry-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) separates what stays on you from what goes in the bike.

### A passenger

The default under Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 is the rider plus one passenger, with narrow exceptions for a second, and everyone on the bike must wear a helmet; the [passenger rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-passenger-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the detail. Ask whether a helmet comes with the bike and whether a second one is available. Two people also use more fuel and need longer to stop, so ask which bike suits two adults and refill earlier than the single-rider plan.

## The roads you will actually ride

Day-trip routes out of Hanoi mix national highways (fast, busy, trucks) with provincial and commune roads (potholes, dogs, unmarked corners). The highway sections are where day-trip riders collect speeding fines - [the speed-limit article]({{ '/articles/motorbike-speed-limits-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) has the numbers. Take the main roads out of the city before you relax, and fill the tank at the city edge: fuel exists out there, but the first half-tank of margin is cheap insurance.

Two rules shape the route. First, the default limits for motorbikes outside built-up areas are 70 km/h on dual carriageways and one-way roads with two or more lanes and 60 km/h elsewhere, and inside them 60 km/h on those roads and 50 km/h on narrower ones; a posted sign overrides the default. Second, [expressways are closed to motorbikes]({{ '/articles/motorbikes-on-expressway-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) of every class, so a route planned for a car can lead to a ramp your bike may not enter. Set your navigation app to motorbike mode if it has one, and look at the whole route before you set off.

## Checks before you leave the city

The full handover walk-round is in the [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}). For a day out, make these the last things you do before you leave the street:

1. Fuel: note the level, ask which petrol the bike takes, and fill at the city edge.
2. Tyres, brakes, chain, lights, mirrors: run the handover walk-round linked above; test brakes at walking pace before a main road.
3. Papers: ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop, and carry your licence.
4. Phone: shop number saved, battery full, offline maps downloaded, route and expected return time shared with someone in the city.
5. Weather: read the forecast for the destination, not for Hanoi, and let rain change the plan.

## The licence reminder

Day trips cross the speed ranges where licence checks happen. The A1 class covers the common rental sizes; a home licence plus 1968-convention IDP covers it for most visitors. A day trip on the wrong paperwork is a long way from home for a fine.

Under Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 the A1 class covers petrol two-wheelers up to and including 125 cc, which takes in every bike in the table with a published engine size. A visitor on a foreign licence relies on the home licence plus an International Driving Permit that includes a motorcycle category, or on another route in Article 57; the [A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) sets out the rule.

## Common questions

### Can I take a rental bike out of Hanoi for the day?

No rule is published either way, which is the reason to ask: name the destination and raise it with the shop before you book. The guide to [rental terms outside Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists what to ask.

### What if the bike stops far from the shop?

Move it off the road, then call the shop and describe the symptom plainly. No roadside rescue or response time is published, so ask what the shop can do from where you are, and ask the shop before another workshop repairs it. The guide to a [rental breakdown in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the first ten minutes.

### Is a 110 cc bike enough for a day out of the city?

The Thay and Tay Phuong, Bat Trang and Tam Dao guides each name a small scooter or underbone as adequate for their ride, and the default speed limits above do not ask for more. What changes with distance is range and comfort, which is why the tank sum and the seat check matter more than engine size.

### Do I need a bike bigger than 125 cc?

The bikes in the table with a published engine size are 125 cc or under, and A1 covers petrol bikes up to and including 125 cc; a larger bike needs a larger licence class. A bigger engine matters more across several days than on a day trip, and the [long-distance riding guide]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-long-distance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers that case.

### Can two of us ride one bike for the day?

Yes, as rider plus one passenger, both helmeted, as the passenger section above explains. Tell the shop there will be two of you when you ask about a bike, and plan fuel stops earlier than for a single rider.
