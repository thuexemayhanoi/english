Semi-automatic underbones carry the country: two adults on a Wave-class bike is how much of Vietnam actually moves. But a passenger changes the machine's behaviour, especially in the first seconds of a launch, and knowing what changes keeps those first seconds smooth.

## What a passenger changes on a xe so

A centrifugal clutch engages by engine speed. With a passenger's weight aboard, the same revs engage the clutch later and softer — the bike feels gutless off the line if you use your solo habits. The fix is more throttle, fed gradually, and patience: the clutch bites at a higher rpm, the bike rolls, then builds speed. Launching with a wrist-snap instead makes the bike lurch and the passenger's helmet meet yours.

## Shifting two-up

Shift up slightly earlier than solo — the engine is working harder, and holding lower gears at high revs with a passenger is noisy, slow and hard on the centrifugal clutch. When you shift, do it with the throttle momentarily closed, then re-open gently. Downshift one gear earlier than solo before junctions and hills so the engine is never caught labouring.

## Braking

Two people can nearly double a small underbone's moving mass, and stopping distances grow accordingly. Brake earlier, brake longer, finish slower. The rear brake does more of the work on a two-up underbone than riders expect — use both brakes and keep weight transfer gentle. On descents, downshift early (first on the approach, second on mild slopes) and let the engine help the brakes rather than riding them all the way down.

## Balance and posture

Give the passenger the same briefing you would on any bike: pegs, knees in, lean with the bike, grab rail or waist, no weight shifts at low speed, mount and dismount only when you say. On an underbone the pillion seat is high relative to yours, so a passenger who sits upright and still effectively raises the bike's centre of gravity — a calm, centred passenger is worth more than any technique on your side.

## Slow-speed honesty

Long creeping sections two-up are where semi-autos feel least stable: the engine's flywheel effect is small and the passenger's mass exaggerates wobble. Prefer steady small throttle openings at a brisk walking pace over an anxious crawl, and when a queue truly stops, both feet down and walk the bike.
