A push start (bump start) spins the engine by wheel power instead of the starter motor. It works for a flat battery or a weak starter, on any bike with a manual clutch or a semi-automatic gearbox — but not on an automatic scooter, which has no starting gear connection to the rear wheel.

## Before you push

Check the obvious first: kill switch, fuel tap, side stand. A bike that will not start for a fuel or ignition fault will not push-start either. If the starter spins the engine but it will not fire, a push start rarely helps. If the battery is simply flat, it usually does.

## The technique

1. Turn the key on and make sure the kill switch is in the run position.
2. Point the bike down a gentle slope if you have one, or find a flat stretch with room to run.
3. Manual bike: shift into second or third gear, hold the clutch in.
4. Push the bike to a brisk jog — faster than walking pace, two or three solid strides.
5. Hop on the seat (or swing a leg over as you release) and let the clutch out sharply. The wheel drives the engine.
6. The engine fires: pull the clutch back in immediately, blip the throttle to keep it alive, and brake gently to a controlled speed.

Semi-automatics: put it in second or third and simply push and hop on — the centrifugal clutch engages as the engine spins, no lever needed.

## Safety limits

Never push-start in traffic, on a downhill toward a junction, or with helpers you cannot coordinate. Two people minimum for a bigger bike — one steering, one pushing. If two or three attempts fail, stop: the fault is not the battery.
