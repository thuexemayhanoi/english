An International Driving Permit is a temporary solution. Foreigners living in Vietnam for months or years are expected to go further and exchange their national driving licence for a Vietnamese one. The exchange is a paperwork exercise for most residents, and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA of the Ministry of Public Security sets the current rules.

Article 24 of that circular, signed on 29 June 2026, is the exchange procedure, with record keeping in Article 26. The circular took effect on 1 July 2026 and ended Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA from that date, apart from a short list of provisions that Article 41(6) keeps until 28 February 2027. This guide follows Article 24 in order: who may ask, what to file, where to file and how fast, and how long the new licence lasts.

In short: a foreigner who resides, works or studies in Vietnam, holds a national licence still in term and has a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more left (or another listed paper) files one dossier (certified translation, health certificate unless the visa-or-card exception applies, copy of the paper) with the Traffic Police Department, a commune-level police reception point or the National Public Service Portal, pays the exchange fee, and receives an electronic licence or, on request, a printed one.

## Who can exchange

The circular ties eligibility to residence. An applicant needs one of the residence papers the circular lists — for a foreigner on a visa or temporary residence card, 90 days or more of validity remaining — together with a national driving licence that is still valid in the country that issued it.

### The test in Article 24(1)(a)

The test has three parts, and all three must be met:

1. The person is a foreigner who resides, works or studies in Vietnam.
2. The person holds a national driving licence that is still within its term.
3. The person holds one of four papers: a diplomatic identity card, an official identity card, a visa or temporary residence card with 90 days or more of validity remaining ("còn thời hạn từ 90 ngày trở lên"), or a permanent residence card that is still valid.

A person who meets all three and "needs to drive in Vietnam" ("nếu có nhu cầu lái xe ở Việt Nam") can be "considered for exchange" ("được xét đổi") into the corresponding Vietnamese class, in line with the term of the licence, visa or card.

Three points follow from that wording. A visa with plenty of days left is a paper, not a status: the text asks for the status and the paper. "Considered" is the circular's own word, and if the office does not exchange the licence, Article 24(3)(c) obliges it to answer in writing and state the reason. And the text does not say on which day the 90 days are counted, so ask the reception point which date it uses and file with a margin.

The three-month period in Article 24(1)(b) is a different rule. It concerns Vietnamese citizens who were issued a foreign licence during three or more months of continuous residence, study or work abroad. The foreigners' test is the 90-day paper above.

### Licences that cannot be exchanged

Two things do not count as a basis for exchange. First, an [International Driving Permit]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}): IDPs are recognised for riding as permits, but they cannot be swapped for a Vietnamese licence. Second, an expired national licence: if the home licence has lapsed, the applicant may need to qualify through the Vietnamese testing route instead of the simple exchange.

Article 24(1)(c) is longer than those two. It also excludes a temporary foreign licence, one erased or torn so far that it no longer carries enough information, one showing "a difference in identification" ("sự khác biệt về nhận diện"), and a licence whose issuer was not a competent authority. The text does not say what counts as a difference in identification; if the name or birth date on your licence does not match your passport, ask the reception point before you pay for a translation.

Nothing in Article 24 mentions the 1968 Convention: the tests are the licence's term, its condition and its issuer. A rider from outside that convention can read about the riding side in [the guide to non-1968 licences]({{ '/articles/non-1968-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and a licence that cannot be exchanged leaves the test route, covered in [the guide to the Vietnamese licence test]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}).

## The dossier

The exchange dossier is short but must be exact: Article 24(2) lists three items, and Article 24(3)(a) asks for one set of them. [The guide to exchange documents]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through each item; the headline list is this:

1. A Vietnamese translation of the foreign licence, with the translation quality certified by a notary office in Vietnam, or a translation by a Vietnamese representative office abroad where the translator works, stamped across the join with a copy of the licence ("đóng dấu giáp lai").
2. A valid health certificate. Article 24(2)(b) says the electronic version is used, or, where a technical fault prevents that, the paper version from a medical facility that meets the standards of the law on medical examination and treatment. A foreigner who exchanges for the term shown on the entry visa or temporary residence card is excepted from that certificate.
3. A copy or electronic copy of one of the qualifying papers listed above.

The health-certificate exception names the entry visa and the temporary residence card only. Paragraph (b) names no exception for a permanent residence card or a diplomatic or official identity card, and it says only that the certificate must be in force ("còn hiệu lực"), without a number of months.

A copy without certification is checked against the original at the counter, while a copy that already has the force of an original under the law on certification needs no original (Article 24(2)(c)). A visa sits in a passport, so bring the passport as the original, although Article 24(2) does not list it as a separate item. It lists no application form either: online you complete the portal's declaration, and at the counter the office enters your details electronically (Article 24(3)(a) and (c)).

## Where to file and how long it takes

Applications go to the Traffic Police Department (Phòng Cảnh sát giao thông) or a commune-level police point that accepts them. Article 24(3)(a) words the second option as a commune-level police office "where a reception point is arranged", and it adds an online route: the National Public Service Portal.

### Filing in person

You hand in one set of the dossier. For the photo the circular allows three routes: have it taken at the counter, use the photo held in identity data, or hand in a white-background portrait of 3 cm by 4 cm. You show the originals for comparison, apart from any already sent, and you complete the fee obligation. The circular states no fee amount, so ask the office what applies on the day.

### Filing online

On the National Public Service Portal the photo can come from identity data or be updated by you. You fill in the declaration as instructed, you are responsible for its accuracy and legality, and you pay the exchange fee through the portal's payment function. Any other service fee is paid only if you choose that service.

### After you file

If the dossier is valid, the office enters the information and prints an appointment slip for collecting the result (Article 24(3)(c)). The text describes the slip only that way and does not make it a licence to ride. Where there is doubt about entry or residence, or about the training, testing and issue of the licence abroad, the exchange authority can ask the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the immigration authorities to verify (Article 24(3)(b)); the text sets no time for that check.

Article 24(3)(d) gives 1 day from a complete dossier for the Department to pass the data to the Traffic Police Bureau for central management and issue of the licence, electronic version included; a printed licence on request takes up to 2.5 working days. The printed licence is handed back at the receiving office or sent by post at your request (Article 24(3)(đ)).

If you are renting a bike in Hanoi while this is under way, [the guide to documents for renting a motorbike]({{ '/articles/documents-needed-rent-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists what a counter asks for. [The guide for foreigners renting in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the rental side.

## What you get and how long it lasts

After processing, the applicant receives a Vietnamese licence matching the vehicle categories of the original. A licence covering only cars converts to Vietnamese car categories; motorbike categories convert only if the home licence already included them. Article 24(5)(b) puts it in general terms: a national licence that covers several classes is exchanged for the corresponding Vietnamese classes.

### The term printed on the new licence

Article 24(5)(a) gives the term in three parts. A Vietnamese licence issued to a foreigner has to run in line with the term on one of the Article 24(1)(a) papers and in line with the term of the foreign licence. It may not run longer than the term set for the Vietnamese licence. Article 57(5) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets those Vietnamese terms: A1, A and B1 have no time limit, B and C1 run ten years from issue, and the other classes five years.

Read together, a motorbike class has no ceiling of its own, so the date on an exchanged licence is set by the papers behind it. The clause gives no formula, so rely on the date printed on the issued licence rather than a calculation of your own. Article 57(7)(a) says a licence that has passed its term is not valid, so that printed date is the day the Vietnamese licence stops counting.

Article 24 describes no extension. Article 21(1)(b) of the same circular covers a foreigner exchanging, before it expires, a licence issued by a competent Vietnamese authority; ask the office whether it applies to an exchanged licence whose date follows your visa. A second question for the office is what happens to the licence date when you renew a visa or card early. [The expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) weighs the exchange against the test route for a long stay.

### What stays with you, and what the office keeps

Article 24(4) says the original dossier stays with the driver for safekeeping: the documents in Article 24(2) and the foreign licence. The exception is the papers in Article 26(3), the certified translation and the copy of the qualifying visa or card. The receiving office stores those for two years and is responsible for storing them (Article 26(4) and (5)). The foreign licence is therefore not handed over for good, so keep it with the health certificate and the originals.

## Why the exchange matters

A rider with no licence that Vietnam recognises faces the penalties of Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Fines are set by the road-traffic fines decree as amended in 2026; [the no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the bands, and any figure there should be checked against the decree text in force. A long-term resident is open to those penalties whenever police check documents, and an IDP counts only together with a national licence that is still within its term (Article 57(8)).

## Exchange or IDP — which one applies

[Tourists on short stays]({{ '/articles/can-tourists-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) ride on a [1968 Vienna Convention IDP]({{ '/articles/1968-vienna-convention-idp-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) carried with the national licence. Foreigners with residence measured in months should start the exchange instead. The two systems are complements, not competitors, and the dividing line is residence.

Two texts have to be read together here. Article 18(7)(a) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA says a foreigner who needs to drive in Vietnam and holds a national licence is to go through the exchange procedure for the corresponding Vietnamese licence; point (b) applies a driving-licence treaty to which Vietnam is a party where that treaty provides otherwise. Article 57(6)(b) of the Law recognises an in-term IDP together with a national licence issued by a state party to the 1968 Vienna Convention, without naming a length of stay. Neither text sets a date by which a resident must switch, so ask the traffic police to confirm in writing how the two apply to your stay.

| | IDP with national licence | Exchanged Vietnamese licence |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | Article 57(6)(b) and 57(8) | Article 57(6)(a) and Circular Article 24 |
| What you need | An IDP and a national licence issued by a state party to the 1968 Vienna Convention, both in term and fitting the bike | A national licence in term, resident, worker or student status and a qualifying paper |
| Time limit | The IDP lasts three years from issue at most and within the national licence's term (Article 57(8)(a)) | The term follows the papers and the foreign licence (Article 24(5)(a)) |
| What you do | Carry both documents (Article 57(8)(b)) | Translation, health certificate unless the exception applies, filing, fee |

Two further routes in Article 57(6)(c) and (d) cover a licence from a country with a mutual-recognition agreement and a licence recognised under a treaty that Vietnam has joined. Test either route against an official source before planning a long stay around it. [Which countries' IDPs count]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [how long an IDP lasts]({{ '/articles/idp-validity-period-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cover the permit side.

Article 30(1) of the circular adds a boundary from the other direction: an IDP issued by Vietnam under the 1968 Convention has no validity in its territory, so a Vietnamese-issued IDP cannot stand in for a licence inside Vietnam.
