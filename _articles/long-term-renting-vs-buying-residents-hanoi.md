Every long-term resident in Hanoi eventually asks whether to keep renting or finally buy. The honest answer is that both work, and the deciding factors are financial risk, paperwork appetite, and exit plans — not riding.

Residents with multi-year stays and no fixed exit date may do better buying; most other residents lean towards renting by the month, and the table below sorts the cases by length of stay.

This page is for someone who will be here six months or more and is weighing a long-term rental against a bike registered in their own name. It concentrates on what each path asks of you in papers, responsibilities and exit, and ends with steps you can follow with your own dates.

## The rental side of the ledger

A monthly rental on a published-rate model (see [Motorbike Rental Monthly Prices in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-monthly-price-hanoi/' | relative_url }})) runs 1,000,000–2,000,000 VND/month depending on the bike. For that, the shop's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection, and there is no resale problem when you leave. Consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, and the deposit is a separate figure, typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on the vehicle and conditions, so ask for the deposit for your model in writing. The six-month rental pattern is described in [Renting a Motorbike for 6 Months or Longer in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-six-months-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and the year-scale arrangement in [Renting a Motorbike for a Year in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-year-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

What a rental does not give you is a fixed long-term price. Longer-term rental is negotiable, so contact us to discuss your dates and ask for the figure in writing instead of multiplying a monthly rate; [the long-term rental agreement guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-long-term-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the terms worth settling. Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use, so ask what applies before you rely on the shop for trips out of the city. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance: [the long-term renter insurance guide]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the questions to put to an insurer, because cover for you as a rider is yours to arrange on either path.

## The buying side

A used underbone or scooter can cost less than a year of rental. Against that purchase price, a buyer takes on: repairs and parts as they fail, tyres and battery, paperwork in Vietnamese, the risk of buying a problem (odometer tampering and hidden engine wear are real in any used market), and the resale scramble when leaving Vietnam — often at a loss, often in a hurry. There is no warranty culture to fall back on.

That describes the used market; a new bike from an official dealer is a different purchase, and any manufacturer warranty comes with terms to read before you pay. The owner's legal duties are not in the list above, and the sections on papers, insurance and inspection below take them one by one.

## The crossover, honestly

On pure cash over a long enough stay, buying can win. But the crossover is further away than people assume once real repair costs, tyre replacements and resale loss are counted, and it only holds if the purchase was a good one — which you cannot know in advance. Renting pays an amount you can fix in writing before you start; buying trades a smaller known amount for an unknown risk. The general short-term comparison is in [Renting vs Buying a Motorbike in Hanoi: Cost and Convenience]({{ '/articles/renting-vs-buying-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}); this article is the resident-scale version of the same decision.

## A decision framework by length of stay

Cash is the input you know least about, because repairs and the resale price are unknown at the start. The length of stay you can name, and how firmly, is a more reliable place to begin.

| Your stay | Leans towards | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Six to twelve months, end fixed by a contract, course or lease | Renting | The exit is a return on a date, and a longer term can be negotiated with the shop |
| Six to twelve months, end date uncertain | Renting, with renewal points | Ask whether the shop would agree renewals, so that each one is a moment to decide |
| A year or more with a settled base | Cost both paths on written figures | A longer-term quote and the full papers and price of one specific bike make the comparison honest |
| Several years, no fixed exit | Buying deserves a proper hearing | The owner's duties are familiar by then and the day you must sell is far away |
| Frequent riding beyond Hanoi | Ask the shop before you rely on it | Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use |

Two dates can overrule a row: your licence dates (for an exchanged licence, the visa or residence card behind it) and the end of your lease or work contract. Plan around the earliest.

## The non-financial factors

- Paperwork: a buyer registers and insures in their own name; a renter's paperwork stays with the shop
- Exit: renters return a bike; sellers must sell one, sometimes against a departure date
- Breakdowns: renters call the shop (see [Breakdowns During a Long-Term Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/breakdowns-during-long-term-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})); owners negotiate with mechanics
- Variety: renters can change models as life changes — see [Switching Bikes Mid-Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/switching-bikes-mid-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})

The next sections cover the legal side of owning in turn: papers, insurance and inspection, the exit and the licence.

## What owning asks of a resident: papers in your own name

A motor vehicle may take part in road traffic only with a registration certificate and a plate (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 35(1)(a)). A first registration needs proof of the vehicle's origin, proof of lawful ownership and proof that the financial obligations on it are complete, and a change of owner also needs the certificate that the previous registration and plate were revoked (Article 39(1) and (2)). A second-hand bike comes with a chain of papers to be shown, not assumed.

The identity papers the office asks of an owner depend on who the owner is. The consolidated text of Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA (11 September 2025) asked a foreign owner who lives and works in Vietnam, other than members of diplomatic, consular and international-organisation missions, for a permanent residence card or a temporary residence card with six months or more of residence remaining (Article 10(2)(b)). Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA is still in force but was amended in 2026, including by Circular 62/2026/TT-BCA, so confirm the current steps with the registration office before you plan around a date, and ask the same question if your status is different.

Article 36(2)(h) of the Law provides plates with the symbol NN for vehicles of foreign organisations, representative offices and foreign individuals, apart from the staff covered by point (g), so a foreign owner should ask which plate series the office would issue.

[The guide to unregistered vehicle fines]({{ '/articles/unregistered-motorbike-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what follows when a bike cannot show valid registration, and [the used motorbike inspection guide]({{ '/articles/used-motorbike-inspection-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the machine itself. Under Article 43(2)(d), a person with an unsettled traffic violation cannot have the registration or inspection of that vehicle processed until it is dealt with, so ask how a buyer can confirm a bike has no open violation before paying.

## Insurance, upkeep and the emissions test

A rider carries, among other papers, the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 56(1)(d)); the Government's decree on that insurance is Decree 67/2023/NĐ-CP, as amended by Decree 220/2026/NĐ-CP. As an owner you hold the certificate and keep its end date in a diary. [The compulsory motorbike insurance guide]({{ '/articles/compulsory-motorbike-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) describes it as third-party cover, so it does not stand in for cover for you as a rider.

Motorbikes are inspected differently from cars. Article 42(2) limits the inspection of motorbikes and mopeds to an emissions test, done under environmental-protection law at testing stations that meet the national technical standard, and Article 42(5) leaves its procedure to the Minister of Construction. Which bikes are tested, how often and from what date are set outside the Law, so ask a testing station or the registration office what applies before you count the test as a cost or a deadline.

## Who carries what, side by side

| Responsibility | If you rent | If you own |
|---|---|---|
| Registration certificate | Ask at handover how the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate will be shown at a stop | Yours, issued in your name under the office's current rules |
| Compulsory insurance certificate | Ask how it is shown, as in the row above | Yours to hold and carry (Article 56(1)(d)) |
| Routine maintenance | See the rental section above | Yours, through a mechanic you find; Article 43(2)(b) puts the duty on the owner and the rider |
| Tyres and tubes | See the rental section above | Yours |
| Emissions test | Ask whether the bike needs one and who arranges it | Yours, at a testing station (Article 42(2)) |
| Trouble outside Hanoi | Ask the shop what applies outside the city, and agree it before you go | Yours to solve wherever you are |
| Leaving | Return the bike; late return is charged 20,000 VND per hour | Transfer the bike and revoke the registration |

## The exit: returning a bike or transferring one

A rental ends with a return and ownership ends with a transfer, and the transfer is the part to plan early. When a bike changes hands, the old registration must be revoked and a new certificate issued to the buyer (Law, Article 39(2) and (6)(a)). Until that is done, the person named on the certificate keeps an owner's responsibility (Article 43(2)(c)), a reason not to sell on a handshake.

The 2025 consolidated registration circular added detail: the seller keeps the certificate and plate rather than handing them to the buyer, and submits them at the revocation. The seller must start the revocation within 30 days of the transfer paperwork, after which an administrative penalty procedure comes first (Article 6(5), Articles 15-17). Because that circular was amended in 2026, confirm the current steps with the registration office well before your last week. [The guide to selling a motorbike before you leave Vietnam]({{ '/articles/selling-motorbike-before-leaving-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers where to sell and how to time it.

The rental exit has its own rules to ask about: late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, early return carries no automatic refund for unused time, and the deposit is a question for the shop, so ask how it is dealt with at the end of your term. Put all three in the written summary of your term.

## Your licence is the same question on either path

Neither option changes what you need in order to ride. Article 24 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, which replaced Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA on 1 July 2026, sets the exchange rules for foreign nationals living, working or studying in Vietnam. The route starts from a national licence that is still valid plus one of the residence papers that article names, such as a visa or temporary residence card valid for 90 days or more or a permanent residence card. A holder who meets both may be considered for an exchange into the matching Vietnamese class, valid for the term on those papers and on the foreign licence. An International Driving Permit cannot be exchanged (Article 24(1)(a), (1)(c) and (5)(a)).

[The foreign licence exchange guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [the long-term expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) compare the routes. Either way, a bike you own can outlast the licence you may ride it on.

## Who should buy anyway

Residents with multi-year stays, mechanical interest or willingness to learn, Vietnamese-language support, and no fixed exit date. Everyone else — including most one-year expats — is usually better served by monthly rental, negotiated for the term (contact us to discuss longer-term rental).

## A decision path for a resident

1. Write down four dates: the end of your lease, of your contract or course, of your visa or residence card, and of the licence you ride on. The earliest is your planning horizon.
2. Ask the shop for a longer-term rental figure in writing, with the term, the deposit for your model and what applies on early return. No longer-term price is published, so the answer is the figure you compare.
3. Take one specific bike you could buy and list what falls on an owner and not on a renter: registration in your name, the compulsory insurance certificate, the emissions test where it applies, parts and tyres, and the discount a hurried sale forces. The break-even formula in the short-term comparison linked above turns the list into a number.
4. Check the paper trail before the price: the seller's registration, any open violation, and the identity papers the registration office will accept from you.
5. Choose a review point halfway through and ask the same questions again with real figures.

## Questions residents ask

### Can a foreigner register a motorbike in their own name?

The Law provides for it: Article 36(2)(h) has a plate series for vehicles of foreign individuals. The papers the office asks for depend on status and on the circular in force, so confirm the current list before you pay a seller.

### Is a longer rental cheaper per month?

No longer-term price is published, and longer-term rental is negotiable: contact us to discuss your dates. Do not assume a longer commitment lowers the monthly figure; ask for the number in writing.

### Do I need my own insurance if I rent for months?

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so any cover you want is yours to arrange, and the [insurance questions for long-term renters]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) are the place to start.

## The one-line version

Renting is a cost you can agree in writing before you start, with no resale to manage; buying is a lower known cost with unknown risk — and your exit plan decides which risk you can afford.
