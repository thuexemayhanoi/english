Tet, the lunar new year, changes Hanoi around the holiday: public offices close, people who work far from home travel, shops may close for a time, and streets can swing from empty to festival-crowded around the Old Quarter. That is a general picture, not a measurement. A monthly rental spanning Tet needs a little planning. If your monthly motorbike rental in Hanoi runs through Tet, settle three things with Nguyen Tu before the break: the shop's open days, a renewal or end date that does not land inside it, and where the bike sits while you are away.

The part that can be pinned down is the calendar. The Government portal reports that civil servants rest from Thursday 4 February to Wednesday 10 February 2027, and, counting from those dates, the new lunar year begins on Saturday 6 February. What a rental shop does on those days is the shop's own decision, so this page is about lining up your renewal date and your plans with the holiday. If you are renting a bike for Tet rather than keeping one through it, [Renting a Motorbike in Hanoi During Tet and Holidays]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-hanoi-tet-holidays/' | relative_url }}) is the guide for that.

## Settle the dates with the shop early

Nguyen Tu operates 09:00–21:00 daily, but around Tet individual days can differ — confirm opening and any holiday arrangements directly with the shop before the holiday, especially if your rental period ends or renews during Tet. If you want to extend across the holiday, [Extending or Renewing a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/extending-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains the renewal process — do it before the holiday rush, not on the last working day.

### What the official calendar says

As the Government portal's report describes it, the break is seven days in a row, from the 28th of the last lunar month to the fifth of the first. Working back from the fifth day puts the start of the new lunar year on Saturday 6 February. The Tet rental guide linked at the top sets out the notice in full.

### Where your term can land

Find the day your term ends or renews in your agreement or in your last message with the shop, then read across. The dates below are calendar arithmetic from the figures above, not information about the shop.

| Your term ends or renews | How it sits against the break | What to settle |
|---|---|---|
| Up to Wednesday 3 February | Before the civil-service rest begins | Whether you can renew or return in person first, and how payment will be made |
| Thursday 4 to Wednesday 10 February | Inside the rest; 6 February is the first day of the new lunar year | A new end date, an early renewal or an early return, agreed before the break |
| Thursday 11 to Tuesday 16 February | After the rest, but the final week of the term overlaps it | Send the renewal message before 4 February |
| From Wednesday 17 February | The break does not touch the date | Fuel, servicing and where the bike will sit |

The third row is the easy one to miss. The renewal guide linked above calls a message in the final week of the term the clean pattern, and for a term ending from 11 to 16 February that week reaches into the break, so the message needs to go earlier.

### Choosing an arrangement

Nothing in the next table is a rule of the shop. Each row is a conversation to have, and the answers come from Nguyen Tu by phone, WhatsApp or Zalo on +84 942 467 674.

| Your plan over Tet | Arrangement to ask about | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| Stay in Hanoi and keep riding | Renew ahead of the break if the term ends in or near it | The rate for the new term, and whether your own bike is free for it: contact us to confirm current availability |
| Go away without the bike | Return it before the break and start a new term afterwards, or keep the term running and park the bike | Whether the days you do not ride count towards the term, what happens to the deposit, and the fact that early return carries no automatic refund entitlement |
| Ride out of the city | Agree the trip with the shop before you book it | The route, the days away and who to call |

Do not assume a holiday rate in either direction. Ask what the rate for the new term is and keep the answer where you can find it. [Ending a Monthly Motorbike Rental Early]({{ '/articles/early-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the early-return position, which is no automatic refund entitlement for unused time, so returning before the break and starting again is a choice to discuss with the shop first.

### One message, written once

One message can carry most of these questions, and a reply in the same chat is easy to find again. Adapt it to your own term:

- My term ends or renews on [date]. I would like to renew, change the end date or return the bike before the break.
- Which days will the shop be open between 4 and 10 February, and at what hours?
- What is the rate for the new term, and will my bike be free for it?
- How and when do I pay: cash, bank transfer or QR payment?
- If my term ends while the shop is closed, where does the bike go, and does the late-return charge keep running?
- Who can I contact if the bike fails during the break?

## If the term ends while the shop is closed

Late return is charged at 20,000 VND per hour, and a return more than six hours late may be charged as a full additional rental day, typically 150,000–200,000 VND depending on the motorcycle. The published late-return figures say nothing about a shop that is shut on the day a term ends. Settle first the case of a term ending from 4 to 10 February (second row of the first table): does the charge run while the shop is closed, where does the bike go and who receives it, and how is the deposit settled afterwards?

[Returning a Long-Term Rental Motorbike Late in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/late-return-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out the rule itself; the questions are for the shop to answer.

Payment is a separate question. Nguyen Tu accepts cash, bank transfer and QR payment, so ask whether a renewal can be settled by transfer or QR before the break and what confirmation you will receive. [Paying for a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/paying-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) describes the payment routes and the records worth keeping. An arrangement that differs from your written agreement is worth confirming by message as well.

## If you stay in Hanoi

The city may be quieter, but some services may be thinner: some repair shops may be shut, some stations may keep different hours and roadside help may be harder to reach. These are possibilities to plan for, not findings; the fuel notes below give one official report on 2026. Ride with a fuller tank than usual and sort any bike issues before the holiday. Parking around Old Quarter festivities gets busy; [Daily Parking as a Monthly Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/parking-monthly-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the normal daily system. Treat that as a planning assumption, not a count.

### Fuel

A Ministry of Industry and Trade report of 10 February 2026 describes the Acting Minister's inspection of fuel supply in Hanoi for Tet 2026. In it, Petrolimex's Director-General said the group had a plan to keep its distribution system supplied before, during and after the holiday and that its retail stores keep selling during their registered hours, and the Acting Minister asked the group to direct its stores and agents to operate normally within their set hours.

That shows what one company's network was asked to do in a past year. It does not show what every station did, and it says nothing about next February or about stations outside that network. So the fuller tank is a habit rather than a response to a known closure: top up before the break starts and before any longer ride, and check the station you plan to use. [Using Petrol Stations in Hanoi: A Rider's Practical Guide]({{ '/articles/hanoi-petrol-stations-guide-riders/' | relative_url }}) explains how stations work for motorbikes.

### Repairs and servicing

Nguyen Tu's maintenance may include an oil change, chain adjustment and tension, tyre-pressure checks and normal inspection. If the bike is due for any of that, ask whether it can be done before the break, since a fault found a week earlier is easier to plan around than one found during it. Consumables such as inner tubes and tyres may be the renter's responsibility depending on the situation, so ask who pays for a puncture repair before you need one.

Support is primarily for Hanoi and local use and there is no 24/7 rescue service, so decide in advance what you would do if the bike stopped on a day when the shop is not reachable. [Breakdowns During a Long-Term Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/breakdowns-during-long-term-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) describes the first call to make, and [Maintenance During a Monthly Motorbike Rental in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/maintenance-during-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists the questions worth asking about service visits.

## If you travel for Tet

Long-distance riding during Tet is a serious undertaking — heavy traffic before the holiday and packed roads after it, both planning assumptions rather than measurements. If you plan to take the rental bike out of Hanoi, agree it with the shop in advance, confirm the support situation (support is primarily for Hanoi and local use), and read up on the long-distance considerations in [Best Motorbike for Long-Distance Riding in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-long-distance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) before committing.

If you fly home instead, arrange guarded parking: [Keeping a Rental Motorbike Secure Over Months in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) explains why a bike left on the street night after night is the risk to avoid, though that guide speaks about nights over months and says nothing specific about Tet.

### If you leave the bike behind

Whether the bike stays under your term or goes back first, the practical question is where it sits for the break. A locked or guarded space is the arrangement the security guide above prefers for overnight storage, so use these questions to test the space you have in mind:

- Is the space locked, gated or watched, and by whom?
- Will anyone see the bike during the break, or will it sit unseen?
- How are fees charged for the days you are away, and when are they paid?
- Where will the key be while you travel: with you or with someone you trust, not left in the bike?

Take dated photographs of the bike and the parking space as you leave, so that you have something to compare with when you return. No space removes the risk, but a locked or watched one answers the questions above better than a kerb does.

### If you ride out of the city

A ride to a home province is a question to put to the shop before you book it, and [Can You Take a Rental Motorbike Outside Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-outside-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out what to agree. Agree the route, the days and the person to call, and expect the answer to depend on how far you plan to go.

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance, so cover for a holiday journey is the renter's own arrangement, and [Arranging Your Own Insurance as a Long-Term Renter in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/insurance-for-long-term-renter-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) is the place to start.

For the ride itself, [Tet by Motorbike: Riding Vietnam During Lunar New Year]({{ '/articles/tet-holiday-motorbike-travel-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) describes the weeks either side of the holiday and the days in between.

## Questions about a monthly term over Tet

### Does a monthly term pause over Tet?

Nothing published about Nguyen Tu's rentals says that a term pauses for a holiday, so do not assume one. Ask whether the days you will not ride count towards the term, and keep the answer in the chat.

### When should I ask the shop about its Tet days?

As soon as you know your term meets the break, and by early January at the latest. The report says employers outside the public sector must announce their pattern at least 30 days ahead, which for a rest starting between 3 and 5 February points to the first week of January. That is a reason to ask early, not a promise about when this shop decides.

### Can I ride the rental to my home province for Tet?

Ask the shop before you book it. The section above on riding out of the city sets out what to agree and where the insurance question sits.

### Is it safe to leave the bike parked while I am away?

A locked or guarded space is better than the street. The questions in the section above on leaving the bike behind help you test one.

## The one-line version

Confirm shop days, renew before the rush, fix the bike beforehand, and never leave it parked on the street if you travel — Tet rewards renters who plan two weeks ahead. Treat two weeks as the minimum: the break itself runs seven days in 2027, and the dates above point to settling the renewal by early January.
