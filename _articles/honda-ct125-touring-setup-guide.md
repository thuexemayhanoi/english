The Honda CT125 is Vietnam's most touring-capable underbone straight from the factory: a 123.9 cc air-cooled single with an adventure posture, a big tank and the highest ground clearance in Honda's range. Honda Vietnam's page (retrieved 2026-09-23, recorded in our model database) lists 6.76 kW at 6,250 rpm, a very tractable 10.9 Nm at 4,750 rpm, a 5.4-litre tank, 1.61 L/100 km claimed economy, an 800 mm seat, 117 kg kerb weight, 90/80-17 front and 80/90-17 rear tyres, and 165 mm ground clearance.

## Why it tours well

- **Range**: the 5.4-litre tank with the claimed economy gives a theoretical range around 300-plus km, with real-world range varying with load, speed and terrain. For northern Vietnam, where fuel stations thin out on the mountain roads, that tank is the CT125's core touring feature.
- **Torque where it matters**: peak torque arrives at low revs, so mountain climbs and rough tracks are taken at walking-pace revs without distressing the engine. It is not fast — accept 40 to 55 km/h cruising as its natural rhythm.
- **Clearance and wheels**: 165 mm of clearance takes rutted village tracks and construction detours that stop a Wave-class bike. The 17-inch wheels roll over broken surfaces better than small scooter wheels.
- **Posture**: the 800 mm seat and upright bars give a commanding, comfortable position for hours at a time — taller riders in particular find it the first underbone that fits.

## Setup for a trip

- **Luggage**: Honda styles the CT125 with racks front and rear; the practical touring kit is a roll bag strapped to the rear rack under a cargo net, valuables on your body, and a small dry bag for electronics. Keep the load low and moderate — the luggage guide covers tie-down patterns.
- **Tyres**: the stock dual-purpose tyres are credible on gravel; check pressures before a long day, not just at the rental counter.
- **Pre-ride checks**: chain, oil level, brake feel, lights. The high-mileage inspection checklist applies before any long route.
- **Weather kit**: the tall stance offers no wind protection; a proper visor and layers matter in the northern winter and on mountain passes.

## Honest limits

It remains a 12-horsepower underbone: sustained highway pace is not its habitat, and its four-speed rotary gearbox with centrifugal clutch means downhill sections still need early gear choice and disciplined brakes.
