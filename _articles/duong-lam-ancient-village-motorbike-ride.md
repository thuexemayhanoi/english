Duong Lam, in Son Tay on the western edge of Hanoi, is the village that preservation-minded travellers cite as the antidote to the city: a commune of laterite-block houses, some centuries old, threaded by narrow lanes under banyan trees. It is roughly 45–50 kilometres from central Hanoi — an easy half-day or relaxed full-day ride.

## The route

The classic line follows Route 32 west toward Son Tay and then picks up Road 21 to the village, passing through market-garden country and small towns the whole way. It is flat, mostly well-surfaced riding with a steady stream of local traffic through the towns — the sort of ride where the pleasure is in the ordinary Red River delta scenery rather than any single viewpoint. Riders who want a quieter return can drop onto the dyke-top roads that run parallel to the main corridor and cruise back into Hanoi along the river.

## In the village

Duong Lam's lanes are far too narrow to ride — park at the village gate lots and walk. The houses of dark honey-coloured laterite are the draw, along with the village wells, the communal houses and the family courtyards where owners will often show visitors around. Two temples to early Vietnamese kings sit at the village edges, and the whole place has the unhurried feel of a working village rather than a museum piece.

Food is part of the trip: Duong Lam is known for its caramel-coloured "che" congee made with local soybeans, and for rice products cooked the village way. Small restaurants and homestays serve lunch on the main lane.

## Planning the day

- Combine the ride with a loop through Son Tay citadel or the nearby Ao Vua/Khoang Xanh valleys if you want a longer day in the hills to the north.
- Mornings are cooler for the westbound ride; leave the city by 8 a.m. to enjoy the village before the tour groups.
- Rain affects the village lanes more than the highway — laterite gets slick, and you will walk more than you ride inside the commune.
