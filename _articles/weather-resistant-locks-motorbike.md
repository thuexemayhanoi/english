A lock that no longer opens is not security — it is a delay, and in the wrong moment it is a bike you cannot move. Vietnam’s humidity, rain and road salt quietly destroy locks, and the failures happen exactly when you need them.

## What fails

Locks fail in three ways: the barrel seizes from corrosion and grit, the mechanism gunk ups and stops turning, and cheap shackles rust into frozen lumps. Budget padlock-style devices with no weather sealing fail in a single rainy season. The problem mirrors the general corrosion war on bikes — motorcycle-rust-prevention-vietnam — applied to a device with moving parts.

## What to look for buying

Sealed or shrouded keyways keep water and dust out of the barrel. Stainless or hardened boron shackles resist both bolt cutters and rust better than chromed soft steel. Simple beats clever: a solid disc lock with a straightforward mechanism outlasts a feature-rich one with tiny springs. Keys matter — cheap copies wear barrels; use the originals. ## Maintenance that actually works

A lock-safe lubricant a few times a season — never thick oil that gums — worked in by cycling the key a dozen times. Rinse mud off the mechanism, dry it, lube it. Store the lock under the seat, not dangling in the rain if you can help it; a wet lock in a sealed under-seat bay still corrodes, so dry it before stowing.

## The habit

Test every lock when you park, and again when you leave: a lock that needs two hands and a wiggle is telling you something. Replace locks when they start sticking persistently rather than when they fail completely — because the failure always comes at the worst possible time.
