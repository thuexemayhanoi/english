Three transmissions, one city. The choice is easier than it looks because each transmission answers a different kind of rider.

## Automatic scooters (Honda Vision, Click, Yamaha Mio)

Twist and go. No gears, no clutch, no left hand doing anything but the brake. Learning time: minutes. In stop-start Hanoi traffic the automatic is a genuine safety margin - all your attention goes to the road, none to a gearbox. Under-seat storage for helmets and shopping. The trade-offs: slightly higher rental rate on some models, less ground clearance on rough roads, and a drivetrain that is less self-serviceable in the provinces.

## Semi-automatic underbones (Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius)

The Vietnamese default. Foot-shifted four-speed gearbox, but no hand clutch - the gearbox does it. Narrower than a scooter, lighter, cheap to run, simple enough that every roadside shop in the country knows them. The trade-off: a small learning curve for the foot-shift, and storage means a bag on the rack.

## Manual (clutch) motorcycles

Left-hand clutch, left-foot shift, full control. For riders who already ride manuals, this is home, and a manual is the right tool for mountain trips where engine braking matters. For everyone else it is the hardest possible way to learn Vietnamese traffic - the clutch turns every junction into a workload. Rent a manual only if you ride one at home.

## The decision

- Never ridden, or riding in Vietnam for the first time: automatic. Full stop.
- Ridden before, want the cheapest and simplest: semi-automatic.
- Confident manual rider with proper licence coverage: manual, and only if your routes leave the city.

One legal note: in Vietnam, licence class, not transmission, decides what you may ride - the A1 class covers the common rental sizes; bigger manuals need the A class.
