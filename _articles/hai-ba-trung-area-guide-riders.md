Hai Ba Trung — named after the two sisters who led a rebellion against Chinese rule — is the long central-south strip that connects the edge of the Old Quarter to the Red River. Its wards were reshaped by the July 2025 restructuring, but riders still use the name for the whole corridor between Hoan Kiem and Vinh Tuy Bridge, and it remains one of the most-ridden pieces of Hanoi.

## What the area is

This is workhorse central Hanoi: hospitals, wholesale streets, older apartment blocks and some of the city's best-known local food, stretched along wide boulevards. Landmarks anchor each end — the Hai Ba Trung Temple on Le Dai Hanh, dedicated to the two sisters, sits in the middle of the strip, while the giant Bach Mai hospital zone defines its centre, and the modern Times City complex with its aquarium and park rises near the southern edge. At the far east, Vinh Tuy Bridge carries riders across the Red River toward Long Bien and Gia Lam.

## How it rides

The boulevards here are wide by Hanoi standards, which means speed goes up and margins go down. Buses and trucks use the same corridors, lane discipline is loose, and the long straight sections encourage drivers to overtake on whichever side has space. Ride at the flow of traffic, check mirrors before every lateral move, and expect ride-hail motorbikes to appear from behind at speed.

The two main corridors are Le Dai Hanh–Minh Khai flowing south, and Bach Mai–Nguyen Khoai flowing toward the river. Both are rush-hour rivers: between roughly 07:00–08:30 and 17:00–19:00 they move slowly but safely; the midday window is when the wide lanes feel almost relaxed.

## Parking and stopping

Around Bach Mai hospital, parking is organized but crowded — attendants run lots along the side streets, and they fill early. At Times City, the complex has dedicated motorbike parking. Around the temple and the older residential streets, kerbside attendant parking is the norm. The hospital zone deserves a note: never park where ambulances or clinic entrances need clearance, and expect the streets around visiting hours to be congested in every direction.

## Why riders end up here

Two reasons dominate. First, Vinh Tuy Bridge is a main motorbike crossing to the east bank — lighter than Long Bien and open to all engine sizes, it is the rider's practical alternative for reaching Gia Lam. Second, the strip is a food-and-errands territory: the markets and food streets here are where Hanoi eats, with far less tourist pricing than the Old Quarter.
