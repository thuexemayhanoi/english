Electric motorbikes carry their energy as weight — batteries are heavy — and weight is what brakes fight. That makes brake maintenance more, not less, important than on a comparable petrol scooter. The good news is that electric drive helps the brakes last longer, if you let it.

## How regen changes the job

On machines with throttle-controlled regenerative braking (verified on the Dat Bike Weaver 200), easing off the throttle slows the machine through the motor before the friction brakes ever engage. In stop-and-go city riding, regen does a share of the work pads used to do, so pads run cooler and last longer. The discipline is to ride so regen comes first: look ahead, ease early, and save the levers for the final stop and emergencies. The regen guide covers the technique.

## The friction side still matters

The Weaver 200 carries hydraulic disc brakes front and rear, and battery weight means the friction system still does real work — especially loaded, two-up, or downhill where sustained regen is not enough. The monthly checks are the familiar ones, weighted for weight:

- **Pad thickness** — check visually; replace at the wear limit per the pads article.
- **Lever feel** — spongy means air or moisture in the line; hydraulic systems on heavier machines should not be ridden on a soft lever.
- **Disc condition** — scoring, rust rings and lip at the edge; see the disc inspection guide.
- **Brake light** — critical on a silent machine: following drivers get no engine cue, so the brake light is your voice. Test it monthly.

## Weight and wet grip

A heavy machine on wet Hanoi streets stops on its tyres as much as its brakes — the tyre-checks article covers pressures and wear for electric weights. And after riding through flooded streets, dry the brake discs with a few gentle early stops: a wet disc's first grab is unpredictable.

## Service intervals

No home-guessing of intervals: follow the manufacturer's schedule for the model, and treat any change in braking feel, noise or stopping distance as a shop visit, not an adjustment. Brakes are the one system where the electric rider has no motor to save them — everything else on this site can wait a week; this cannot.
