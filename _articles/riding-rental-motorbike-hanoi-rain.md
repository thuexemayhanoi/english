Hanoi's rain arrives fast, mainly between May and October, and it changes everything about a ride: slow down early, switch the headlight on, brake earlier than usual with both brakes, stay out of water you cannot see through, and stop under cover when you can no longer read the road surface. The traffic does not stop, so the difference is in how you adapt.

## The first ten minutes are the worst

Fresh rain lifts oil and dust off the asphalt, making the first minutes of a shower the most slippery of the whole ride. Ease off before the rain even starts falling hard — brake earlier than usual, and treat every painted line, manhole cover, and metal drain as ice. Standing water hides potholes; Hanoi has them, and a pothole at speed bends wheels and forks.

Honda Vietnam's owner's manual for the Vision points the same way in its wet-weather advice: road surfaces become very slippery when wet, so take particular care when braking. Expect the riders and drivers around you to adjust late. Some will slow or pull over without warning to put on rain gear, so leave more room than your dry-road habit suggests.

### Surfaces that turn slick first

Not every wet surface is equally slippery. These catch riders out most easily, and each has a simple rule.

| Surface | Why it catches riders out | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Painted lines, arrows and crossings | Wet paint gives less grip than the asphalt around it | Cross upright and off the brakes; do not brake or lean on it |
| Manhole covers and metal plates | Smooth metal gives little grip | Steer around it if safe, otherwise cross straight and steady |
| Smooth or patched asphalt | A polished or patched surface can stay slick while the road around it grips | Treat shine as a warning and leave extra space |
| Mud, sand and grit | Washed onto corners and low points, often hidden under water | Slow before the corner; stay off brakes and throttle in it |
| Leaves and litter | They hide what is underneath and slide when wet | Treat a leaf-covered corner like an unpaved one |

### Smooth inputs

Wet-road riding is mostly smoothness. Open the throttle gradually, keep any lean gentle, and slow down in a straight line before a bend instead of braking inside it. The Vision manual gives matching braking advice: hard braking can reduce the stability of the bike, and if possible you should reduce speed before turning, otherwise the bike may skid.

## Ride the crown of the road where water is shallow

Flooded sections concentrate at curbs and dips. Choose the highest visible line, keep a steady throttle, and do not swerve hard in deep water — if an oncoming bus pushes a bow wave at you, slow right down rather than fighting for your line. If water reaches above the axle, find another way around; water in the airbox or exhaust of a rental bike becomes a breakdown conversation (see [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }})).

### Reading standing water

Judge water before you enter it, not while you are in it. Brown water hides potholes, open drains and debris, and a puddle that looks flat can be deeper than the road around it. If you cannot see the bottom, treat the depth as unknown, watch how other vehicles cross, and let a bus or car go through first instead of following it closely. [Riding Through Flooded Streets on a Motorbike]({{ '/articles/flooded-streets-riding-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through depth judgement and when water means stopping altogether.

### If the engine stalls

Do not keep cranking an engine that has stalled in water: water drawn into an engine can damage it, so push the bike to higher ground and have a mechanic look before it is run again. Tell the shop what happened before arranging a repair elsewhere. After any crossing, ride slowly for a short distance and test the brakes before you need them.

## Lights and visibility

Rain without lights makes you invisible. Turn your headlight on: Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 20(1), requires the front light to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility. [Night Riding: Motorbike Light Rules in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/motorbike-night-lights-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets the rule out in full. In rain, run lights at any hour, whatever the clock says.

Article 20(2) of the same law asks for dipped beam, not high beam, in several situations, including when you meet an oncoming vehicle (a median strip that blocks the glare is the exception). In rain, high beam mostly lights up the drops in front of you and dazzles other riders. The driver behind you sees a rider through spray and a wet windscreen, so a working tail light and brake light matter as much as the headlight, and a bright or reflective outer layer helps.

Helmets with clear visors beat sunglasses in a downpour; wipe the visor when you stop. A visor that mists up is a hazard of its own: slow down until you can see clearly again instead of riding on with a smeared view. After dark, wet asphalt turns streetlights and oncoming headlights into glare and hides lane markings, so ride slower than you would on the same road in daylight.

## Braking and distance

Honda's manual for the Vision says that damp brakes do not work effectively and, in its braking section, that tyres slip easily on low-friction surfaces and the braking distance becomes longer. Whatever type of brake the bike has, assume it is weaker than usual until it has dried.

Brake early, use both brakes, and double your following distance. Honda's manual gives the same instruction for the best braking: use the front and rear brakes together. Keep out of the middle of the lane where oil collects. [Emergency Braking on a Motorbike in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/emergency-braking-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers hard stops, which a wet road makes less forgiving.

### Slides, damage and the deposit

Avoid lockup: a sliding rental bike turns into a conversation about damage and the deposit, so ask the shop before you ride how damage from a slide or fall is handled — see [How Deposit Returns Work at Hanoi Motorbike Rentals]({{ '/articles/how-deposit-returns-work-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). Nguyen Tu's deposit is typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND depending on vehicle and conditions; what happens to it if the bike is damaged is a question to settle in advance, and [Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) lists what to ask.

Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance and renters arrange their own, so a slide on a wet road is a good reason to sort out cover before the rental starts (see [Is Insurance Included with Motorbike Rentals in Hanoi?]({{ '/articles/is-insurance-included-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})).

## Gear and timing

A rain suit is easier to ride in than soaked clothes, and a two-piece set stays put at speed where a loose poncho can flap. Cold, wet hands brake badly, so gloves matter, and closed shoes with a grippy sole beat sandals on wet footboards. [Rain Gear for Motorbike Riders in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/rain-gear-motorbike-riders-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) compares the options, and whether a rental comes with any rain gear, or anywhere to keep it, is worth asking about rather than assuming.

Beyond that, the honest answer about Hanoi rain is that waiting can be the sensible choice: showers differ in how long and how hard they last, and the safest decision is often a coffee under an awning. Riding all day through the rainy season is covered from the rental-planning side in [Renting a Motorbike in Hanoi in the Rainy Season]({{ '/articles/renting-motorbike-hanoi-rainy-season/' | relative_url }}).

### When to stop and wait

Pull over when any of these is true:

- You cannot see the road surface or the lane markings clearly.
- Water covers the road from side to side and you cannot tell how deep it is.
- Lightning or thunder is close.
- Gusts, or spray from large vehicles, are pushing the bike around.
- Your hands are cold or shaking and you are braking worse than when you set out.

Wait somewhere solid, such as a shopfront awning or a cafe, away from lone trees, and park where the bike does not block a doorway or the pavement. [Storms and Sudden Weather: When to Stop Riding]({{ '/articles/storms-when-to-stop-riding-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) goes further on reading a storm before it arrives.

### How often to expect rain

The WMO's World Weather Information Service publishes the Viet Nam Meteorological and Hydrological Administration's figures for Hanoi. For 1991-2020 it lists average monthly rainfall above 100 mm in each month from May to October (126 mm in October, 335 mm in August) and below 100 mm in the other six. Its table also counts 16 to 18 rain days a month from May to August, so rain is recorded on more than half the days then.

Averages say nothing about how long one shower lasts or how hard it falls, so plan to ride in rain with the habits above instead of planning around a time it will stop.

## After the rain

Wipe the seat, check that the chain is not visibly slack (a wet chain on a rental may need attention — see [What to Do If the Chain Feels Loose on a Rental Bike]({{ '/articles/loose-chain-on-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }})), and let the brakes dry with a few gentle applications before you need them hard. Honda's manual for the Vision gives the same instruction for damp brakes: apply them while riding at low speed to dry them.

The road recovers more slowly than the sky. Dust and oil lifted by the rain, mud and leaves, and wet paint and metal can stay slick after the asphalt looks dry, which is the ground [After the Rain: Slippery Roads, Mud and Leaves]({{ '/articles/after-rain-slippery-roads-motorbike/' | relative_url }}) covers.

If the bike ran through deep water or took heavy rain for a long stretch, look for water where it should not be: lights or indicators behaving oddly, a starter that clicks without turning the engine, or brakes that feel weaker than before. Report anything that feels wrong instead of riding on. [What to Check After Your Motorbike Gets Soaked]({{ '/articles/check-motorbike-after-heavy-rain/' | relative_url }}) lists the checks in order, and a bike that has slid or fallen needs the longer routine in [What to Check Mechanically After a Fall]({{ '/articles/checking-motorbike-after-fall-accident/' | relative_url }}).

## Common questions

### Does the rental agreement stop me riding in the rain?

That depends on the terms of your own rental, and only the shop can tell you what they say about weather, flooded streets and water damage. Ask before you pay, and use the questions in [this rental agreement guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) as a checklist of what to raise.

### Do I need the headlight on in light rain?

The text of Article 20(1) turns on whether rain limits visibility, not on how hard it is falling. In practice, switch the headlight on whenever you ride in rain: it takes one switch and makes you easier to see through spray.

### What should I do if the bike slides or falls?

Stop somewhere safe, check yourself before the bike, and get medical help if you are hurt. Then look the bike over, and tell the shop what happened before any repair or any talk about cost.

### Does a passenger change how I ride in the rain?

A passenger adds weight behind you and lengthens your stopping distance, so brake earlier and more gently than when riding alone and leave more room. Ask your passenger to sit still and lean with the bike rather than against it.
