Every bought-bike trip in Vietnam ends the same way: an airport departure date and a motorbike that needs to become money before then. Selling well is mostly timing and paperwork, and the difference between a good exit and a bad one is often several million dong.

## Set the right expectation first

Selling guides are blunt: plan to recover a fair share of what you paid on a healthy bike, and expect the classic backpacker Win to resell for whatever the market feels that week. The real goal of a good sale is not profit — it is exiting cleanly, with papers transferred, before your flight. Riders who start a month ahead routinely do better than riders with three days left; the consensus from sellers' reports is that at least a week is needed for a fair price, and more is better.

## Where to sell

- **Facebook groups and expat/backpacker marketplaces.** The main channel for foreigner-to-foreigner sales in Hanoi, Da Nang and Saigon. Photos, honest description, price slightly above your floor.
- **Buyback from a touring company.** If you bought through a buyback scheme, the terms apply: pre-agreed percentage back at their shop. Simple, fast, priced-in.
- **Consignment through a shop.** The shop sells for you and takes a cut — guides suggest treating it as the fallback option, since you leave without the final price settled.
- **Direct to a Vietnamese dealer or mechanic.** Fastest, lowest price. The classic endgame for a bike sold the day before the flight.
- **To the next traveller.** The dream sale: full asking price, no middleman — but the next traveller's arrival date and yours rarely align.

## The paperwork

Selling legally means transferring ownership: the bluecard, a signed sales contract between you and the buyer, and both parties' ID. Buyers who know Vietnam will walk away from a bike with no contract or mismatched papers — see our bluecard [guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-bluecard-ownership-papers-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) for why the document is half the bike's value. Keep your own copy of everything, and do not hand over originals before payment clears.

## Timing and price strategy

- Start advertising well before your final week; drop the price in steps as departure nears.
- A washed, oiled, tyre-pumped bike with fresh photos sells visibly faster at the same price.
- Keep the last 48 hours for a dealer fallback rather than discovering it.
- Selling in Saigon at the end of a north-to-south ride works (demand is steady), but the classic buy-in-Hanoi/sell-in-Saigon arithmetic only pays if the bike was sound to begin with.

## The alternative calculation

Our renting-vs-buying guide runs the numbers honestly: for most trips shorter than a couple of months, renting beats the buy-sell cycle once mechanical risk, paperwork hours and selling stress are priced in. If that conclusion lands after you already own a bike — sell early, sell honestly, and budget the loss into the trip you will remember anyway.
