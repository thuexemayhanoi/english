Every Vietnamese driving licence now runs on points. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) gives each licence 12 points, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP deducts them violation by violation — on top of the [money fines]({{ '/articles/motorbike-fines-vietnam-overview/' | relative_url }}), not instead of them. For motorbike riders, the system quietly became the second half of every penalty.

The rules for the ledger itself sit in Article 58 of the Law. It came into force with the rest of the Law on 1 January 2025 (Article 88(1)) and reads the same in the 2024 text and in the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes the amendments of Law 118/2025/QH15. This page follows that article clause by clause and quotes no deduction per offence, because those numbers sit in the decree, which Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP changed from 15 August 2026.

## How deduction works

Points are deducted when a penalty decision takes legal effect, not at the roadside. Article 58(1) words it as a record: the data on the deduction goes into the road traffic order and safety database right after the penalty decision takes effect, and the person deducted is to be told. [The guide to being stopped by traffic police]({{ '/articles/stopped-by-traffic-police-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what happens at the roadside itself.

The same clause carries two more points. The ledger belongs to the licence: 12 points, kept on that database to manage how the driver complies with road traffic law. And the number deducted for each violation depends on the nature and degree of the violation, so the Law fixes no table of its own: the deduction wording of the whole Law sits in this one article.

Clause 5 gives the power to deduct points to the person who has the power to sanction administrative violations. Article 58 does not name the officials concerned; clause 6, covered below, leaves that to the Government.

### Where the numbers come from

Clause 6 hands the detail to two rule-makers. The Government sets out the detail of clause 1, the procedure and authority for deducting and restoring points, and a roadmap for putting the article into practice. The Minister of Public Security sets out the detail of clause 3, the zero-points rule. Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP is the Government's text on violations and deductions, and Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP, issued on 26 June 2026, amended it from 15 August 2026.

Where an older guide on this site quotes a deduction or a fine for one offence, it follows the decree as issued in 2024. Check the current amount in the decree as amended before you rely on it; the sources list carries the Official Gazette record of Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP.

## When points come back without a test

The twelve points are not a one-way count. Article 58(2) restores a licence to the full 12 points when two conditions hold together: the licence has not been fully deducted, and it has had no deduction for 12 months counted from the date of the most recent one.

Read literally, the clock is tied to the latest deduction. Imagine a licence at 8 points after one deduction. If no further deduction follows for 12 months from that date, clause 2 restores it to 12. If a second deduction arrives in month nine, the most recent date is now the second one, so the 12 months start again from there. The balances here are illustrations of how the words fit together, and the clause says nothing of a partial return.

So points do reset with time alone, but only for a licence that still has points left, and only after a clean 12 months. A licence already at zero is outside clause 2, which speaks only of a licence not yet fully deducted; clause 3 governs it.

## What zero points means

A licence with [all 12 points deducted]({{ '/articles/lose-all-licence-points-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is no longer usable. Article 58(3) is exact about it: the holder may not drive on that licence, and the same clause sets the way back. After at least six months from the day the points ran out, the holder may sit a test of knowledge of road traffic law run by the traffic police, and passing restores the full 12 points. Six months is a floor, not a schedule. Clause 6 leaves the detail of this clause to the Minister of Public Security, and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA has no article on licence points, so ask the traffic police for the current arrangements before you plan around a date. [The zero-points guide]({{ '/articles/lose-all-licence-points-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the zero-points consequences.

## A new card does not reset the ledger

Clause 4 says a licence issued after an exchange, a reissue or an upgrade keeps the points of the licence it replaces. Replacing a lost card, moving up a class or swapping a pre-2025 licence for a current one changes the document, not the balance.

A pending case can hold the new card up as well. Article 62(4) of the Law says no licence is issued, exchanged or reissued for a person who has violated road traffic law while that person has not yet done what the competent state authority requires to settle the administrative case. Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA repeats the rule for reissue (Article 20(2)) and for exchanging a Vietnamese licence (Article 21(1)(đ)), and Article 21(3)(b) has the office that takes an exchange dossier look up the licence in the licence information system and look up the driver's violations.

The route for a lost or damaged card is in [the guide to replacing a lost licence]({{ '/articles/lost-motorbike-licence-replacement-vietnam/' | relative_url }}), and the swap from the pre-2025 classes is in [the old licence classes guide]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}).

### Holding a licence with a term and one without

Article 18(2) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA gives each person one licence number, shared by licences with a term and licences without one, and Article 18(3) lets a holder integrate the two through an exchange under Articles 21 to 24. Article 58 speaks of the points of the licence and does not mention a person who holds two. The texts read for this page do not say how a balance is counted while a person still holds the two as separate cards. An A1 licence (no term, Article 57(5)(a)) beside a class B car licence (10 years) is one example, and the holder should put that question to the traffic police rather than assume the balances are pooled or kept apart.

## Points, validity and effect are different things

A licence can fail a rider on separate grounds, and Article 58 covers only one of them.

| Question | Where | What the text says |
|---|---|---|
| Is there an end date? | Article 57(5) | None for A1, A and B1; 10 years from issue for B and C1; 5 years for the other classes |
| Is the licence in effect? | Article 57(7) | Not once its term has run out or it has been revoked under Article 62(5) |
| Are there points left? | Articles 56(1) and 58(3) | A driver needs a licence with points left, in effect and suited to the vehicle; at zero, the holder may not drive on it |
| Who is outside the licence rule? | Articles 56(1), 56(4) and 59(1)(a) | The moped rider: no licence, from age 16, but knowledge of the traffic rules and skill to control the vehicle |

A fully deducted licence is not among the two cases in which Article 57(7) says a licence has no effect; Article 58(3) deals with it on its own terms. A licence can therefore be within its term, never revoked, and still not be one the holder may drive on. [The licence validity and renewal guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-licence-validity-renewal-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers terms and revocation, and [what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) covers the exception.

## How the points interact with fines

The same act can cost money, points and a suspension at once. Drink-driving in the mid band, for example, combines the 6–8 million VND fine with point deduction and a licence suspension measured in months. That band is the earlier figure on this page and has not been checked against the current decree: Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP changed Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026, so look up the current amount before relying on it.

Article 58 speaks only of points. It sets no fine and no suspension period, so those have to be read in the decree. Two guides on this site carry bands from the 2024 text, [the drink-driving limits guide]({{ '/articles/drink-driving-limits-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [the guide to the no-licence fine]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}); treat their amounts and deductions as 2024 figures and check them against the amended decree.

Because the person deducted is to be told, the balance is not meant to arrive as a surprise. The Law does not say how the notice is delivered, since clause 6 leaves the procedure to the Government, so keep the penalty decision and any notice you receive, and ask the traffic police for the date of the latest deduction, because clause 2 counts its 12 months from that date.

## Foreign riders

Points apply to Vietnamese licences, including licences issued to foreign residents by exchange. Article 58 does not divide licences by who holds them: it speaks of the licence, and clause 4 keeps the points through an exchange. [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the route, and [the expat licence guide]({{ '/articles/expat-motorbike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) compares it with riding on an IDP.

An IDP is a different document, and Article 58 does not mention it. The text read for this page does not say how a deduction is recorded against an IDP holder. What the Law does say is in Article 57(8). A person riding on an IDP issued by a member of the 1968 Vienna Convention must carry the permit and the national licence, both in term and suited to the class of vehicle, and must follow Vietnamese road traffic law (point b). If such a person is banned for a fixed period from using the licence, the ban may not run longer than the stay the person is allowed in Vietnam (point c). [The IDP rules for motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) set out the pair of documents.

## If you rent a motorbike in Hanoi

Article 56(1) puts the duty on the person driving: it is the driver who must hold a licence with points left, in effect and suited to the vehicle, unless the rider is on a moped. Article 9(7) separately lists handing a motor vehicle to a person who does not meet the legal conditions to drive it as a prohibited act. If you hold a Vietnamese licence, check that your balance is above zero before you book.

## Questions for the traffic police

Where the text leaves a point open, put the question in writing and keep the answer:

- Which text of the decree applies to the violation I was penalised for, and what does it deduct?
- What is my current balance, and from which date does the 12-month count run?
- If I ride on an IDP, how is a deduction recorded against me?
- If I hold a licence with a term and one without, how is my balance counted?
