Every Vietnamese driving licence now runs on points. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) gives each licence 12 points, and Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP deducts them violation by violation — on top of the money fines, not instead of them. For motorbike riders, the system quietly became the second half of every penalty.

## How deduction works

Points are deducted when a penalty decision takes legal effect, not at the roadside. The decree lists which violations cost points and how many: smaller offences such as wrong-lane riding cost 2 points; mid-band offences such as red-light running and carrying too many passengers cost 4; the heavy categories — drink-driving bands, violations causing accidents — run to 10 and, in the accident-causing group, the full slate. Deductions stack across the year; the account does not reset merely because time passes.

## What zero points means

A licence with all 12 points deducted is no longer usable. Riding on a fully deducted licence is punished as riding without a valid licence — the multi-million-dong bands — and the only route back is the formal restoration process: after at least six months, a knowledge test on road law run by the traffic police, and a restored 12 points on passing.

## How the points interact with fines

The same act can cost money, points and a suspension at once. Drink-driving in the mid band, for example, combines the 6–8 million VND fine with point deduction and a licence suspension measured in months. The points ledger is the part riders forget, because it has no receipt — the first reminder is often the checkpoint that reads the licence and finds the balance at zero.

## Foreign riders

Points apply to Vietnamese licences, including licences issued to foreign residents by exchange. An IDP rider has no Vietnamese points account — but also no points buffer: their exposure is the fine and suspension rules directly, and repeat violations surface at the licence-exchange stage rather than in a points ledger.
