Short answer: bikes of 50cc or less do not require a driving licence in Vietnam, but there are conditions, and "50cc means no rules" is wrong.

The first condition is that the bike really is a moped in law. That depends on its design speed and its engine capacity or motor power, not on a "50cc" label on a listing or a fairing. A machine that passes needs no licence from a rider aged 16 or over; one that fails is a motorcycle, and a motorcycle licence (starting with A1) and the minimum age of 18 come into play.

## The current position

Article 34(1)(g) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15) defines "xe gắn máy" (mopeds) as two- or three-wheel engine-driven vehicles designed for no more than 50 km/h, with engines of 50cc or less, or electric motors of 4 kW or less. The requirements are:

- No driving licence needed
- The rider must be at least 16 years old (Article 59(1)(a))
- Article 56(4) requires moped riders to understand traffic rules, have the skill to control the vehicle, and meet the age and health conditions (per Article 59(1)(a) and Article 59(2))

One point that is often misread online: there is no rule that every 16-to-18-year-old must hold a training certificate before riding. Decree 151/2024/NĐ-CP organises road-safety knowledge and safe-moped-riding skills training for high-school and vocational students, and traffic police have publicly clarified that 16-to-18-year-olds may ride mopeds (including electric mopeds up to 4 kW) without a licence or certificate. The training requirement operates through schools, not as a universal riding permit.

Article 6(2) of the Law points the same way: the traffic police take the lead, with the bodies that manage upper-secondary schools and vocational institutions, in organising guidance on safe moped-riding skills for students at those institutions. It is guidance given where students study, and Article 56(4) does not make it a condition of riding. [The guide to school training for moped riders]({{ '/articles/50cc-training-high-school-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) describes the programme.

Mopeds still follow all other traffic rules: helmets, lights, lane discipline, sober riding. Under Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, mopeds and motorbikes are fined at the same levels for the same violations (Article 7 covers both). Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amends Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026, so this page quotes no fine amounts; check any figure in the current text, starting with [the guide to fines for 50cc moped riders]({{ '/articles/50cc-fines-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP also has an age offence for a 16- or 17-year-old who rides a motorbike instead of a moped. [Five myths about 50cc bike laws]({{ '/articles/50cc-motorbike-myths-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) takes "50cc means no rules" and four other misreadings in turn.

## What the law still asks of a moped rider

The exemption from a driving licence is narrow, and what replaces the licence is written into Article 56(4). The Vietnamese text reads:

> Người lái xe gắn máy tham gia giao thông đường bộ phải hiểu biết quy tắc giao thông đường bộ, có kỹ năng điều khiển phương tiện; đủ điều kiện về độ tuổi, sức khỏe theo quy định tại điểm a khoản 1 và khoản 2 Điều 59 của Luật này.

A plain English rendering, not an official translation:

> A person who drives a moped on the road must understand the road traffic rules and have the skill to control the vehicle, and must meet the age and health conditions of point (a) of clause 1 and of clause 2 of Article 59 of this Law.

### What the rider must have

The clause says what a rider must know and be able to do. It names no test, course, certificate or card that proves it. Article 59(1)(a) sets the age at 16 or over, and Article 59(2) asks for health that suits the vehicle, leaving the standards and the medical check to the Minister of Health.

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, in force from 1 July 2026, is the Ministry of Public Security's text on licence tests, the issue, exchange and use of driving licences, and international driving permits (Article 1). The word for a moped (xe gắn máy) does not appear anywhere in it, so it adds no test, course or certificate for moped riders. The law's silence on a test is not a reason to skip the skill: a rider who has never handled a bike is better off practising on a quiet street than learning in dense traffic.

### What the rider must carry

A licence is only one of the papers named in Article 56(1). The clause lists four for a driver: the registration certificate; a licence that fits the vehicle; the technical-safety and environmental-protection inspection certificate for motor vehicles, as the law prescribes; and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate. The moped exception sits in the licence wording of the clause, and the list of papers does not say the others drop away for a moped rider. Under Article 56(3), papers integrated into an electronic identification account can be shown through that account.

For a rental, the registration and insurance certificates are the practical questions: ask how each would be shown if you were stopped. A renter arranges their own motorbike insurance, because Nguyen Tu does not provide it. [The guide to the papers a rider carries]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through the whole set.

## When a "50cc" is not a moped

The word "50cc" describes an engine, and the law sorts a bike by more than its engine: Article 34(1)(g) adds a design speed of no more than 50 km/h to the limit of 50 cm3 for a combustion engine or 4 kW for an electric motor. [The guide to what counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) gives the definition word for word, with the class table. This section is about how a bike misses it.

### Capacity above 50 cm3

A combustion engine above 50 cm3 puts the bike in the motorcycle class (Article 34(1)(e)), whatever the bike is called. Honda Vietnam's model pages list the Vision at 110 cc and the Wave Alpha 110 at 109.2 cc, so a scooter or underbone of that kind is a motorcycle. A two-wheeled motorcycle up to 125 cm3 falls under class A1 (Article 57(1)(a)), and the minimum age for an A1 licence is 18 (Article 59(1)(b)). [The A1 licence guide]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) lists what the class covers.

### What changes for the rider

Once a bike is a motorcycle, the moped answers stop applying. The rider needs a licence valid for the vehicle (Article 56(1)) and, for an A1 licence, must be 18 or over. A 16- or 17-year-old therefore cannot ride it, as [the guide to riding a 50cc at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-50cc-at-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out, and a visitor with no licence that is valid in Vietnam for motorcycles cannot treat a 110 cc scooter as a substitute for a moped.

## Do not confuse 50cc bikes with electric bicycles

A pedal-type electric bicycle ("xe đạp điện") is defined separately in the law as a bicycle with motor assistance that cuts out when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h — it is a non-motorised vehicle. A 50cc petrol moped or a sub-4 kW scooter-style electric that is also designed for no more than 50 km/h is a moped, not a bicycle. The distinction matters for licence rules and for where you may ride (Article 31(4) sends non-motorised vehicles ("xe thô sơ") to the part of the road reserved for them, where there is one), but not for helmets, which Article 31(3) also requires of the rider and any passenger on a motorised pedal bicycle.

## Electric scooters: the 4 kW line and the 50 km/h line

An electric machine has to clear both lines of Article 34(1)(g) to be a moped. If it misses either, it is a motorcycle under Article 34(1)(e): class A1 for a two-wheeler with a motor of up to 11 kW and class A above that (Article 57(1)(a) and (b)), both from age 18.

Published top speeds are the figures a renter can check, while the law's own term is design speed (vận tốc thiết kế). ## If you are visiting Vietnam

The moped rule is written without reference to nationality: Article 56(4) speaks of whoever drives a moped, and Article 59(1)(a) of a person aged 16 or over. A visitor who meets those conditions, on a bike that is really a moped, has no licence question to answer, and so no International Driving Permit question either. The helmet, the rules of the road, the papers and the insurance question all remain. ## Questions people ask

**Do you need a licence for a 50cc motorbike in Vietnam?** Not for a moped: Article 56(1) excludes the moped driver from the licence requirement, and Article 56(4) asks instead for knowledge of the traffic rules, the skill to control the vehicle, and the age and health conditions. A bike that misses the Article 34(1)(g) limits is a motorcycle and needs a licence.

**How old do you have to be?** Sixteen for a moped (Article 59(1)(a)). An A1 licence needs 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), so a 16-year-old cannot ride a 110 cc scooter.

**Is there a test, course or certificate for moped riders?** Article 56(4) names none, and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA does not mention mopeds. School guidance on safe moped riding runs through high schools and vocational institutions.

**Is an electric scooter licence-free?** Only if its motor is 4 kW or less and its design speed is 50 km/h or less. Otherwise it is a motorcycle: class A1 up to 11 kW, from age 18.
