Two of the most atmospheric pagodas near Hanoi sit in the hills southwest of the city: Thay Pagoda, the 11th-century home of Vietnamese water puppetry, and Tay Phuong Pagoda, famous for its lacquered arhat statues. The pair is about 30 kilometres out — a half-day trip on the bike, and one of the shortest rides on this site that still feels like leaving the city entirely.

## The ride

From central Hanoi, head southwest past the built-up districts and out toward Quoc Oai on the main corridor; total distance to the pagoda area is around 30 kilometres, or roughly 45 minutes of riding in light traffic. The two pagodas are a short ride apart, so the sensible plan is Thay first, then loop to Tay Phuong, then roll home the same corridor. The roads are plain, surfaced and flat — no technical challenge, only the usual attention needed at junctions and market-town crossings.

## Thay Pagoda

Thay (Thien Phuc Tu) sits at the foot of a limestone outcrop beside a small lake, with a water-puppet stage on the water — the art form is said to have originated at this village. Behind the pagoda, rough steps and a path climb the rock to small shrines and hermitages with views over the delta. The climb is short but worn underfoot; ride-appropriate shoes double as temple-appropriate shoes here. Modest dress applies — cover shoulders and knees.

## Tay Phuong Pagoda

Tay Phuong is the classic red-lacquered timber pagoda, reached by a long flight of steps. Inside, its 18th-century arhat statues are considered among the finest pieces of traditional sculpture in northern Vietnam — each figure a distinct, worldly character. The setting is a quiet hilltop with a compound of old trees; it is the slower, more contemplative stop of the two.

## Timing and practical notes

- A comfortable plan is a late morning at Thay, lunch nearby, and Tay Phuong early afternoon, home by mid-afternoon.
- Festival seasons (Thay's is in early spring) bring crowds; weekday visits are quiet.
- Steps and temple grounds mean you park in village lots and walk — bring a lock.
