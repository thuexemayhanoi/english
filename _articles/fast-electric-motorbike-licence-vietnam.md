Speed decides whether an electric two-wheeler [needs a licence]({{ '/articles/electric-bike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) in Vietnam. Under the vehicle definitions of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), the licence-free category stops at 50 km/h design speed and 4 kW motor output. Everything faster or stronger is legally a motorbike, and motorbikes need motorbike licences. Power then sorts the motorbikes: class A1 runs up to 11 kW and class A starts above it (Article 57(1)).

## The dividing line

The law defines the moped category (xe gắn máy) as up to 50 cc or an electric motor up to 4 kW, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h — no licence required from age 16. Article 34(1)(g) sets those limits, Article 56(1) and (4) take the moped rider out of the licence duty, and Article 59(1)(a) sets the age of 16. [What counts as a moped under Vietnamese law]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) gives the definition in full.

An electric machine whose design speed exceeds 50 km/h, or whose motor exceeds 4 kW, does not fit that definition; it falls into the motorbike classes and needs A1 (up to 11 kW) or class A (above 11 kW) from age 18. The Law defines the motorbike by exclusion, as a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle that is not a moped (Article 34(1)(e)), so missing either moped limit is enough to leave the moped class. Classes A1 and A are written for two-wheeled motorbikes and the age of 18 comes from Article 59(1)(b); a three-wheeled motorbike takes class B1 instead (Article 57(1)(c)).

### From the spec sheet to the class

Take the motor power and the stated top speed from the maker's page for the exact variant, treat the top speed as a working figure for the design speed, and read them against the rows.

| The machine's figures | Legal type | Licence class |
|---|---|---|
| Motor of 4 kW or less, design speed of 50 km/h or less | Moped (Article 34(1)(g)) | None; rider aged 16 or more |
| Motor of 4 kW or less, designed for more than 50 km/h | Motorbike (Article 34(1)(e)) | A1 (Article 57(1)(a)) |
| Motor above 4 kW, no more than 11 kW | Motorbike | A1 |
| Motor above 11 kW | Motorbike | A (Article 57(1)(b)) |

## The market reality

Vietnam's electric two-wheeler market spans both sides of the line. The manufacturer figures checked for this guide show how wide the spread is. The checked rows list the Honda ICON e: as an electric moped with a maximum of 1.81 kW and a top speed of 48 km/h, which is inside the moped limits of Article 34(1)(g) if the stated top speed is taken as the design speed. The Dat Bike Weaver 200 lists 6 kW and 90 km/h and the Honda UC3 about 6.0 kW and 82 km/h, so both are motorbikes on power, and on speed too if the stated top speed is taken as the design speed.

Speed alone is enough to leave the moped class. Taking the stated top speed as the design speed, the VinFast Evo, Feliz II and Viper are motorbikes although their motors are only 2.45 kW and 3 kW, because each is listed at up to 70 km/h and Article 34(1)(g) tests design speed and motor power together. [The kW ladder of checked models]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-power-kw-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets the rows out in order.

None of the electric models with a checked power figure is above 11 kW, so none of them is a class A machine on those figures, and the ones outside the moped class are A1 machines. That says nothing about a model that has not been checked: class A is a line to read off the spec sheet, never to assume.

### Speed-limited versions

Some makers sell a speed-limited version of a faster model beside the standard one, and the licence question then turns on the version. The moped limit of Article 34(1)(g) is a design speed, and the Law does not mention speed limiters. Article 2(7) defines modification as a change to a registered vehicle that alters its vehicle type as the authority prescribes, and the texts read for this page do not say whether lifting a limiter is one. Ask the traffic police before changing a machine that was sold as licence-free.

## Which licence covers which power band

The class has to match the machine's power, and the licence in your hand decides which bands it reaches. The table sets four cards against three bands of motor power for a two-wheeler.

| Licence in hand | 4 kW to 11 kW | Above 11 kW, below 14 kW | 14 kW or more |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class A1 issued from 1 January 2025 | Covered (57(1)(a)) | Not covered | Not covered |
| Old A1, not yet exchanged | Covered (89(2)(a)) | Covered (89(2)(a)) | Not covered |
| Class A from exchanging an old A1 | Covered (89(3)(a)) | Covered (89(3)(a)) | Not covered |
| Class A with no restriction | Covered (57(1)(b)) | Covered (57(1)(b)) | Covered (57(1)(b)) |

An old A1 is one issued before the Law took effect on 1 January 2025 (Articles 88(1) and 89(2)). [The electric bike licence guide]({{ '/articles/electric-bike-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works through the old A1 and the restriction on its exchanged card, and [old licence classes after 2025]({{ '/articles/old-licence-classes-after-2025-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the other old classes. An old A2 not yet exchanged covers 14 kW and above together with the old A1 band (Article 89(2)(b)), and it becomes plain class A on exchange (Article 89(3)(b)).

For a rider starting without a Vietnamese licence, [the step-by-step route to an A1 licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the training and the test, and [what the class A licence lets you ride]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a-licence-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) covers the higher class.

### Fast machines under 4 kW

A machine under 4 kW that is designed for more than 50 km/h is a motorbike (Article 34(1)(e) and (g)), and a new A1 covers it because Article 57(1)(a) sets no lower power bound. Article 89(2)(a) is worded differently: it describes the electric band of an old A1 as 4 kW to below 14 kW, so it does not name a machine under 4 kW, and the texts read for this page do not say how an old card that has not been exchanged applies to one. The exchanged card has no lower bound, since Article 89(3)(a) says up to below 14 kW. For a fast machine under 4 kW, the cautious course on an old A1 is a written answer from the traffic police.

### Other cards

A car class does not reach a two-wheeler, because the car classes are separate branches of Article 57(1); the guide on [a car licence and electric motorbikes]({{ '/articles/can-you-ride-electric-motorbike-with-car-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) works through the point. A foreign licence or an IDP is read by the type of vehicle it permits (Article 57(6)(b) to (d)), and no text read for this page turns a foreign category into the 11 kW line. Under Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 18(7), the holder of a foreign national licence who wants to drive in Vietnam must exchange it for the matching Vietnamese class, unless a treaty Vietnam has joined provides otherwise. [Exchanging a foreign licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and [the IDP rules for motorbikes]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) cover the two routes.

## Near the 11 kW line

A machine at exactly 11 kW stays within A1, because Article 57(1)(a) runs up to 11 kW and Article 57(1)(b) starts above it. Both clauses speak of the power of the electric motor, and neither says whether rated or maximum output is meant. The texts read for this page do not say which figure counts.

Makers can publish both figures for one motor. The checked rows for the Honda CUV e: give a rated 4.2 kW and about 6.0 kW at maximum, and both sit on the same side of the 4 kW line, so the class does not depend on the choice. A pair that straddled a line would leave the class open.

Imagine a machine with a rated 10 kW and a maximum of 13 kW. A rider with a new A1 is inside the licence on the first figure and outside it on the second, while a rider with an old A1 that has not been exchanged is inside on both (Article 89(2)(a)). The example is illustrative: no model checked for this guide has such figures. Until a written answer exists, the cautious course is to treat the higher figure as the one that counts and to ask the traffic police for that answer.

## Reading the card before you ride

Article 56(1) requires a licence with points left, in force and matched to the machine being ridden, and lists the licence among the papers a rider carries (Article 56(1)(b)). Article 18(4) of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA ties the holder to the classes recorded on the licence, so the printed class is the test for the machine.

Appendix IX of the Circular sets out the card. The front carries the class (point 2(đ)), and the back lists the road motor vehicles the holder may drive, in Vietnamese and English, beside a QR code (points 2(c), 2(e) and 3). Two restriction lines can follow. For an old A1 exchanged to class A, point 4(g) prints a wording that limits the holder to two-wheeled motorbikes below 175 cm3 or an electric motor below 14 kW, and point 5 gives its system code, A.03. For class A, point 4(h) prints a wording that limits the holder to two-wheeled motorbikes of 125 cm3 or more, or an electric motor of 14 kW or more, that use a continuously variable transmission, and point 5 gives its code, A.02.

The Law words class A without any transmission condition (Article 57(1)(b)), and the articles of the Circular read for this page do not say who is given the second line. A holder whose card carries either line should ask the traffic police office that issued it what the line means for the machine in question before riding.

## Helmet and equipment rules follow the classification

A fast electric is a motorbike for every rule, not just licensing: helmet requirements, lane rules, speed limits and the fine schedule of Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP apply in the motorbike versions. Article 33 words the helmet, passenger and riding rules for the two-wheeled motorbike and the moped together, so the helmet duty reads the same for both (Article 33(2)), and the classes part company on the licence and the minimum age (Article 56(1) and Article 59(1)), among other points.

A fast electric is also a motor vehicle, so Article 35(1)(a) asks for a registration certificate and a plate, and Article 56(1)(a) and (d) put the registration certificate and the owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate among the papers to carry.

## If the card does not cover the machine

A card that does not reach the machine does not meet the condition of Article 56(1), and Article 9(1) names driving a motor vehicle without a licence as the law requires among the strictly prohibited acts. Another prohibition is aimed at the person who hands the machine over: Article 9(7) bars giving a motor vehicle to someone who does not meet the legal conditions to drive it. [The lending guide]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the owner's side.

Fines and points for each offence come from Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, and Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP changed that decree from 15 August 2026, so this page quotes no amount. The [electric motorbike fines guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-fines-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and the guide to the [fine for riding without a licence]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) give figures that follow the 2024 text, so check any amount there against the amended decree. In the Law, a licence carries 12 points (Article 58(1)), and once all of them are deducted the holder may not ride on that licence (Article 58(3)).

## Questions riders ask

### Do I need class A for a fast electric motorbike?

Only above 11 kW. From above 4 kW up to 11 kW the machine is an A1 machine whatever its top speed, and a machine of 4 kW or less whose design speed is above 50 km/h is an A1 machine too (Article 57(1)(a) and Article 34(1)(e) and (g)).

### Is a motor of exactly 4 kW a moped?

It can be. Article 34(1)(g) sets the electric limit at no more than 4 kW, so 4 kW is inside it, but the design speed must also be no more than 50 km/h. A 4 kW machine designed for a higher speed is a motorbike and needs class A1.

### Do the plate and the registration certificate show which class I need?

Not by themselves. A moped is a motor vehicle too, and Article 35(1)(a) asks every motor vehicle for a registration certificate and a plate, so having both does not separate a moped from a motorbike. The class follows the design speed and the motor power under Article 34(1).

### Can a 17-year-old ride a fast electric motorbike?

No licence class is issued before 18 (Article 59(1)(b)), and a machine above the moped limits is outside the moped exemption of Article 56(1) and (4). [What a rider of 16 can ride]({{ '/articles/riding-electric-moped-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out what a younger rider can do.
