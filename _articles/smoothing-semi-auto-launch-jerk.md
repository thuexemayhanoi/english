A semi-automatic underbone should glide away from a stop like a small automatic. When it instead lurches, surges or shudders through the first few metres, either the rider or the bike is fighting the centrifugal clutch — and usually both can be fixed cheaply.

## The rider-side causes (most common)

- **Too few revs.** The clutch engages progressively with engine speed. Launching at idle guarantees a shudder — the clutch grabs, releases, grabs again. Use a deliberate, steady quarter-to-half throttle and hold it constant until rolling.
- **Snapping the throttle.** A wrist-spike spins the clutch suddenly into engagement — that is the neck-snapping lurch. Openings should be smooth in the first three seconds of every launch.
- **Launching in the wrong gear.** First gear on a rotary four-speed is the launch gear; second-gear launches on an incline labour and shudder.
- **Feathering backwards and forwards.** Nervous riders roll on-off-on-off through the engagement range, which is exactly the recipe for jerking. Set the revs, then commit.

Drill it: on a quiet street, launch ten times with one steady throttle opening and no correction. Smoothness is a habit your right wrist learns in one session.

## The bike-side causes

If smooth throttle still produces grabbing, shuddering or slipping, look at the machine:

- **Worn centrifugal clutch shoes** grab and shudder instead of engaging progressively — a common high-mileage issue, and the clutch-care guide covers the symptoms.
- **Contaminated or old gearbox oil** makes engagement harsh; a gearbox oil change is cheap and often transformative on neglected bikes.
- **Sticking linkage or worn clutch components** can hold the clutch partially engaged at idle (creeping at stoplights is the giveaway) or fully release late.
- **Engine running rough** — a badly tuned carburettor or fouled plug surges at exactly the revs the clutch engages, felt as jerkiness that is really misfire.

One warning sign separates the cases: a jerk that only happens during the first metres of engagement points to the clutch; surging at all speeds points to fuelling.

## Two-up and loaded

Every symptom above gets more obvious with a passenger or cargo: the clutch works harder, engagement takes longer, and bad habits get punished. Feed more revs and more patience, not more wrist.
