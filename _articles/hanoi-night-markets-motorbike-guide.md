Hanoi changes shifts after dark. The day markets close, and a second city of commerce opens under the streetlights: the weekend walking-street night market around the Old Quarter, the flower markets that peak before dawn, the fruit trucks at Long Bien, and the food streets that earn their fame between nine at night and two in the morning. All of it runs on motorbikes — the vendors', and yours.

## The weekend Old Quarter night market

On Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings, the streets north of Hoan Kiem Lake (around Hang Ngang, Hang Dao, Hang Ma) close to vehicles and fill with market stalls, part of the wider walking-street zone that runs from Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight. The rider's job is to arrive at the edge, not the middle: park in the perimeter lots near the closure line — they fill fast after 19:30 — and walk the market on foot. Trying to ride into the market streets during closure hours is both prohibited and pointless: the space is a crowd.

## The nocturnal trade markets

For a stranger kind of night out, ride to the markets that run on the city's clock rather than the tourists':

- **Long Bien fruit market**: peaks from around 22:00 through 04:00. Riding there at midnight is a genuine Hanoi experience — trucks, headlamps, porters and pyramids of fruit under the bridge approach lights. Go slowly, park where an attendant points, and drink the ca phe that appears from nowhere.
- **The flower markets** (the big ones sit in the west of the city toward Me Linh, with smaller traders at Quang Ba at the north end of West Lake): the trading hours run overnight into early morning. Quang Ba is the easy one — a lakeside stop at 05:00 pairs perfectly with the dawn lake loop.
- **Street-food night stretches**: the famous grilled-meat and noodle streets open properly after 18:00; parking is the sidewalk-edge convention with attendants, and the rule is simple — park where the bikes park, eat where the bikes aren't.

## Riding home late

Night riding in Hanoi deserves its own respect: the traffic thins, the speeds rise, and the collision geometry changes — fewer, faster cars, dim side streets, buses off duty and food trolleys without lights. The night-riding safety guide covers the detail; the market-specific additions:

- **Load discipline**: after a market trip you are carrying bags. Cargo belongs behind you, netted or strapped, never in your hands or on the handlebars.
- **Helmet on, every time**: night is when the police checks and the serious accidents both cluster.
- **The one-drink rule**: market food comes with beer culture; riders who drink hand the keys to someone who has not, or ride home on a ride-hailing service and collect the bike in the morning. The fines and consequences of drink-driving in Vietnam are severe and enforced.

## A rider's perfect market night

Start at the Old Quarter night market edge at 19:00 while parking is still easy, walk the stalls for an hour, ride the empty Sunday-night boulevards to Long Bien for the fruit market at 22:30, then finish with pho at a late-night shop and the bridge view — the city electric on one side, the river black on the other. It is the most Hanoi evening there is, and it only works on two wheels.
