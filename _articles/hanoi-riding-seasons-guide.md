Hanoi has real seasons — a claim few tropical cities can make — and for a rider they are four different cities. The bike you rent and the clothes you ride in should follow the calendar; here is how it reads.

## Spring (roughly February–April)

The transition months are the connoisseur's pick: the late-February-to-early-March window is regularly named among the most favourable for riding, with mild temperatures, blossom season in the peach villages, and the Tet crowds gone home. Humidity stays tolerable and the light is soft. This is the season for the lake and dyke loops and for the first craft-village rides. The one caveat: light drizzle returns as spring ages, and the "unexpected shower" habit — checking the forecast every morning — starts here.

## Summer (roughly May–August)

Hot, humid, and storm-prone. Midday riding becomes a heat-management exercise: the hot-weather and hydration guides stop being optional reading. The workable summer pattern is to ride early (before 09:00), hide from 11:00–16:00, and come out in the evening — Hanoi's summer evenings are glorious riding hours, with the food streets and lake loops in full life. Afternoon thunderstorms arrive fast and hard; the heavy-rain guide covers the stop-and-wait logic, and carrying rain gear is a summer rule, not a winter one.

## Autumn (roughly September–November)

The famous season: drier air, cool mornings, and the clearest light of the year. The late-October-to-early-November window is the other frequently recommended riding period. If you are photographing the city, this is when; if you are planning the long day rides, this is when. Expect the year's best combination of visibility and comfort, and book your rental ahead — everyone else has the same idea.

## Winter (roughly December–January)

Hanoi's cold is a surprise to visitors: mornings can drop to around 10°C and below, damp and penetrating, with the coldest stretch in January — which is also, notably, one of the drier months. Riding in it means real gloves, layers and a closed helmet; the winter riding guide covers the technique. The compensation is the atmosphere: mist on the lakes, hot food streets, and the city at its most atmospheric. Electric riders should also note that cold shortens range — the winter-range guide covers that.

## The rider's short version

Best overall windows: late February–early March and late October–early November. Best spectacle: the Tet fortnight (crowded, book ahead).
