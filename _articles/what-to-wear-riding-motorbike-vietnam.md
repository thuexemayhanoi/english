Vietnam's heat pushes everyone toward shorts and sandals; its roads argue for coverage. The working compromise is light coverage — because at road speed, the sun is less of a problem than bare skin is in a slide, and light layers breathe better than most visitors expect.

## The daily city kit

Long trousers, closed shoes, a light jacket or long sleeves. Gloves sound excessive until the first tropical downpour or the first slip on a wet manhole cover: ventilated gloves protect grip as much as skin. Sneakers beat sandals absolutely; the exposed foot is the most commonly injured body part in low-speed falls. Helmet-fit-guide-vietnam covers the top piece of the kit.

## Dress for the season

Hot season: loose, light-coloured long sleeves and ventilated gloves — hot-weather-riding-motorbike-vietnam covers hydration, the other half of summer comfort. Winter: a windproof layer underneath, waterproof gloves, a warm layer that blocks the wind chill of damp 13°C mornings — winter-riding-motorbike-hanoi covers the season. Rainy season: a proper rain jacket lives under the seat from June onwards.

## Night and long trips

At night, one bright or reflective layer answers the visibility problem cheaply — night-riding-motorbike-safety-vietnam covers the wider problem. For long trips, add real coverage: a mesh riding jacket and proper shoes pay for themselves the first time gravel tests them. For daily errands, the practical floor is closed shoes, long trousers, helmet and gloves — everything beyond that is margin, but that floor is not optional.

## What not to bother with

Flip-flops on a moving bike — a foreign rider in Hanoi loses that argument eventually. A rain poncho that balloons at speed: get a fitted rain jacket instead.

## The one-line version

Light coverage year-round, closed shoes always, gloves once you believe in them, rain jacket in season — dress for the fall you are planning not to have.
