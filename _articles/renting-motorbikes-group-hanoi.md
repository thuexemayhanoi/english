Riding out with friends is the best version of motorbike Hanoi — but a group multiplies everything: booking complexity, breakdown odds, and the cost of one bad decision. Here is how group rental works.

## How many bikes

The general rule: pairs who ride together daily can share; everyone else gets their own. Nothing slows a group like a nervous pillion on an overloaded bike. Group size also determines how much advance notice the shop needs — for several bikes on the same dates, book ahead rather than walking in (how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi), and confirm current availability of the models you want in advance. Nguyen Tu rents by arrangement — contact us to confirm current availability for group requests.

## Matching machines and riders

A group does not need identical bikes; it needs compatible ones. Put the least experienced riders on light automatics, and the confident ones on whatever the route needs. The spread of options among common rental categories is in best-scooters-to-rent-hanoi and easiest-motorbikes-for-beginners-hanoi. One thing worth insisting on: everyone rides a transmission they already know. A group trip is the wrong place to learn clutch control.

## Every rider needs a licence

Group riding multiplies the unlicensed-rider risk: one rider without a valid licence stopped by police can consume the group's whole afternoon. Check everyone's paperwork against the rules in motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi before booking.

## Deposits and agreements per bike

Each bike carries its own deposit — typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND each depending on the bike — and its own agreement. Decide as a group who is financially responsible for what, and read motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi for the terms that matter. Inspect each bike separately at handover (rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist): in a group handover it is easy to assume someone else checked your bike. Nobody did.

## Group riding technique

- Ride in staggered file, never side by side; Hanoi lanes are narrow
- Put the most experienced rider last (sweep) and a confident navigator first
- Agree hand signals and a regroup point before setting out; intersections eat groups
- Slow the group to the pace of the least confident rider — always
- On day trips, refuel when the first bike hits half a tank; see best-motorbike-for-day-trips-from-hanoi

## Parking and stops

Groups overwhelm small kerbside spots; plan stops around attended lots — the parking logic is in where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi.

## Breakdowns in a group

One dead bike does not end a group trip: the sweep stays with it, the rest ride on to the destination. Agree this pattern in advance. On a rental, the shop's number is the first call — the decision tree is in rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi and outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike.

## The short version

Book ahead, licence-check everyone, inspect every bike, stagger the file, sweep the rear, regroup by agreement. Groups that follow those six lines come back with stories instead of problems.
