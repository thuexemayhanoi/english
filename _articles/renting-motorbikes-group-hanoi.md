Riding out with friends is the best version of motorbike Hanoi — but a group multiplies everything: booking complexity, breakdown odds, and the cost of one bad decision. Here is how group rental works.

## How many bikes

The general rule: pairs who ride together daily can share; everyone else gets their own. Nothing slows a group like a nervous pillion on an overloaded bike. Group size also determines how much advance notice the shop needs — for several bikes on the same dates, book ahead rather than walking in ([How to Book a Motorbike Rental in Hanoi (WhatsApp, Zalo, Walk-In)]({{ '/articles/how-to-book-a-motorbike-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }})), and confirm current availability of the models you want in advance. Nguyen Tu rents by arrangement — contact us to confirm current availability for group requests.

## Matching machines and riders

A group does not need identical bikes; it needs compatible ones. Put the least experienced riders on light automatics, and the confident ones on whatever the route needs. The spread of options among common rental categories is in [Best Scooters to Rent in Hanoi for Foreigners]({{ '/articles/best-scooters-to-rent-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [Easiest Motorbikes for Beginner Riders in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/easiest-motorbikes-for-beginners-hanoi/' | relative_url }}). One thing worth insisting on: everyone rides a transmission they already know. A group trip is the wrong place to learn clutch control.

## Every rider needs a licence

Group riding multiplies the unlicensed-rider risk: one rider without a valid licence stopped by police can consume the group's whole afternoon. Check everyone's paperwork against the rules in [Motorbike Rental Requirements in Hanoi: Documents, Licence, Age]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) before booking.

## Deposits and agreements per bike

Each bike carries its own deposit — typically 2,000,000–5,000,000 VND each depending on the bike — and its own agreement. Decide as a group who is financially responsible for what, and read [Understanding a Motorbike Rental Agreement in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-agreement-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) for the terms that matter. Inspect each bike separately at handover ([Rental Motorbike Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before You Ride]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }})): in a group handover it is easy to assume someone else checked your bike. Nobody did.

## Group riding technique

- Ride in staggered file, never side by side; Hanoi lanes are narrow
- Put the most experienced rider last (sweep) and a confident navigator first
- Agree hand signals and a regroup point before setting out; intersections eat groups
- Slow the group to the pace of the least confident rider — always
- On day trips, refuel when the first bike hits half a tank; see [Best Motorbike for Day Trips from Hanoi]({{ '/articles/best-motorbike-for-day-trips-from-hanoi/' | relative_url }})

## Parking and stops

Groups overwhelm small kerbside spots; plan stops around attended lots — the parking logic is in [Where to Park a Motorbike in Hanoi: Rules and Etiquette]({{ '/articles/where-to-park-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}).

## Breakdowns in a group

One dead bike does not end a group trip: the sweep stays with it, the rest ride on to the destination. Agree this pattern in advance. On a rental, the shop's number is the first call — the decision tree is in [What Happens If a Rental Motorbike Breaks Down in Hanoi]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-breakdown-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) and [Can You Use Outside Repair Shops on a Rental Motorbike?]({{ '/articles/outside-repair-shops-rental-motorbike/' | relative_url }}).

## The short version

Book ahead, licence-check everyone, inspect every bike, stagger the file, sweep the rear, regroup by agreement. Groups that follow those six lines come back with stories instead of problems.
