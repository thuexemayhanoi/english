In Vietnam the choice between 50cc and 110cc is really a choice between two legal worlds and two amounts of engine — and the price difference is the smallest part of the decision.

## Licence and age

A 50cc-or-under moped needs no driving licence, but the rider must be at least 16. A 110cc bike is a full motorbike (xe máy): it requires an A1-class licence — which you can hold from 18 — and riders aged 16 to under 18 may not ride one at all. That single rule decides the question for most teenagers, and it matters for foreign visitors too: an IDP that covers motorcycles opens the 110cc class; without one, only the moped class is legally open to you if you are 16 or older. Our licence guide covers the details.

## Power you can feel

The jump from roughly 50cc to 110cc is not marginal. A 110cc underbone such as a Honda Wave Alpha or Yamaha Sirius — both long-standing fixtures of Vietnamese streets — has roughly double the power, and it shows exactly where a 50cc hurts:

- pulling away from lights with a passenger
- long bridge approaches and flyover ramps
- riding into a headwind on open road
- keeping pace with 50–60 km/h arterial traffic

If your route crosses Hanoi's bridges daily, the 110cc class is not a luxury; it is a safety margin. Our bridges riding article explains why.

## What stays the same

Both classes are air-cooled carburetted-or-injected singles, both are cheap to run by world standards, and both use the same underbone architecture with big wheels. Maintenance habits transfer one-to-one: chain, oil, brakes, tyres. Nothing about a 110cc is intimidating after a 50cc — the controls are identical.

## Costs

New, a 110cc underbone from an official manufacturer carries a published list price; 50cc bikes, being mostly grey imports or older stock, have no official VN price list at all. Second-hand, both trade in the same informal market where condition matters more than class. Fuel use on a 110cc is slightly higher but small in absolute terms. Insurance, registration and parking costs follow the vehicle, not the engine size.

## Recommendation

- Under 18, or no licence-valid motorcycle entitlement: 50cc, legally the only option.
- Licensed adult riding inside one district: 50cc is workable but the 110cc class is more comfortable.
- Licensed adult commuting across the city, riding two-up or touring: 110cc, clearly.
