Battery fires are rare — and almost always preventable. E-bike charging safety in Vietnam comes down to a handful of habits.

## The rules that matter

1. **Use the manufacturer's charger, only.** Packs, chargers and battery-management systems are engineered together; a substituted charger is the single most common cause of charging incidents. Replace a lost charger with the same part number.
2. **Charge in ventilated spaces** — a balcony, garage or open corridor corner, not a sealed bedroom or the space under the stairs that is your only fire exit. Charging packs want airflow.
3. **Hard surfaces, clear surroundings.** Charge on tile or concrete with nothing flammable stacked around the pack. Never on a bed, sofa or cardboard.
4. **Never charge a wet bike or wet connectors.** Dry the charging port and contacts first — water bridging terminals is a genuine hazard, which is why flooded bikes need inspection before their next charge.
5. **Do not run cheap extension reels or multi-socket stacks** for a hours-long charge. Plug into a wall socket in good condition; if the plug or socket warms, stop and have it looked at.
6. **Unplug when full** rather than leaving packs on charge for days.

## Chemistry context

VinFast chose LFP chemistry for its swappable packs specifically for safety and durability — a real engineering margin, not just marketing. Whatever your bike runs, the habits above remain the actual protection: chargers paired, spaces ventilated, connections dry.

## Special cases

- **Overnight charging is normal practice** — but do it in the ventilated, clear-of-clutter space, not beside your sleeping head.
- **A pack that smells, swells or gets hot off the charger goes outside, away from the building, and to the dealer.** Do not keep charging it to confirm.
- **Long storage:** park the pack at partial charge, indoors, cool — our storage guide covers it.
