Downshifting is where new clutch riders feel clumsy: the bike jerks, the lever feels rushed, the front dips. Smooth downshifting is just a matter of sequence and patience.

## The basic downshift

1. Roll the throttle closed.
2. Squeeze the clutch in.
3. Click down one gear.
4. Release the clutch smoothly while the revs rise naturally to match the road speed.

One gear at a time. Skipping from fourth to second works on some bikes but punishes beginners with a lurch and a chance of the rear wheel chattering.

## Matching the revs

When you release the clutch in a lower gear, the engine must spin faster for the same road speed. If the revs are too low, the bike snatches forward. A small throttle blip while the clutch is in — enough to raise the revs toward where the engine will want to be — makes the engagement almost invisible. This is rev matching, and it is worth practising at low speeds until it is instinct.

## Braking while you shift

In everyday traffic, brake first, downshift as you slow, and release the clutch only when your speed suits the chosen gear. The gear you want at the end of braking is usually first or second for junctions, second for slow rolling.

## Downhill

Downshift before the slope, use the engine to hold your speed, and add the brakes in the corners. Our engine-braking guide covers descending properly — never coast a descent with the clutch pulled in, because you give up exactly the control you need most.

When downshifts become impossible or noisy even with good technique — jumping out of gear, grinding, a clutch that bites late — that is a mechanical symptom, not a technique problem.
