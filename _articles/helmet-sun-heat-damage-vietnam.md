A helmet spends its life in the sun. Parked on the mirror, hung on the handlebar, clipped to the seat — in Vietnamese traffic that is hours of direct UV a day. Most riders inspect for scratches after a drop, but the slower damage happens without any impact at all.

## What actually degrades

Three parts age. The outer shell loses UV inhibitors and gradually chalks; the finish dulls and the colour fades, which is cosmetic, but the same radiation works slowly into the underlying resin. The EPS foam liner — the crushable layer that absorbs impact — is more stable against UV but hates heat cycling: parked in direct sun, the inside of a black helmet can reach extreme temperatures, and years of expansion and contraction slowly embrittles the foam. The comfort padding and straps suffer most visibly: sweat plus heat hardens foam cheek pads and rots stitching.

## Where helmets get damaged fastest

The classic Vietnamese habits each have a cost. Hanging the helmet on the mirror or handlebar puts the strap under permanent tension at the stitching. Leaving it resting on the seat in the sun bakes the EPS and degrades the retention buckle. Parking the bike under direct sun with the helmet in the underseat box is hotter still — trapped heat with no airflow. A breathable helmet bag or shaded storage beats all three, and a parked bike in the shade is also better for paint and plastics generally: motorbike-covers-parking-outdoors-vietnam covers parking protection.

## Warning signs

Faded, powdery or chalky shell; a strap gone stiff or furry at the edges; padding that has compressed so the helmet now rocks when strapped; a buckle that has gone brittle or discoloured. A helmet that suddenly feels loose after a year of commuting has not stretched — its padding has compressed, and the fit corrections in helmet-fit-guide-vietnam no longer apply. That helmet is due for replacement on the schedule in helmet-care-replacing-guide-vietnam, and a heat-damaged shell would fail the same certification checks explained in helmet-safety-standards-explained-vietnam.

## Practical habits

Park in shade when you can; carry the helmet with you or store it shaded rather than baking it on the bike; let a hot helmet air out rather than sealing it in a bag damp with sweat; and replace rather than attempt to restore padding once fit has gone. None of this conflicts with hot-weather comfort — ventilation matters for riding, helmet-ventilation-hot-climate — but comfort features never offset a shell or liner that the sun has already retired.
