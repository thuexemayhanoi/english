Vietnam's administrative fine system for traffic offences was substantially raised by Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, effective 1 January 2025. If you last read about Vietnamese fines before that date, the numbers you remember are too low.

## How the system works

Traffic offences are administrative violations handled with monetary fines, processed either on the spot at a checkpoint or at the local traffic police office. For motorbikes, fines are set per violation type in a range, with the exact amount at the officer's discretion within that range. Serious cases (significant injury crashes, repeated offences) escalate beyond administrative fines into criminal liability.

## Common motorbike violations and rough ranges

Fines change with each decree revision; the figures below reflect Decree 168/2024 as commonly reported, and should be verified before being quoted:

| Violation | Approximate fine |
|---|---|
| No helmet / unfastened strap | 400,000–600,000 VND |
| Riding without a valid licence | from 1,000,000 VND range |
| Running a red light | from 1,000,000 VND range |
| Riding the wrong way / wrong lane | from 600,000 VND range |
| Using a phone while riding | from 600,000 VND range |
| Drink-driving (any detectable alcohol) | from 2,000,000–3,000,000 VND at low BAC, rising steeply |

See our helmet law and drink-driving guides for the detail behind those rows.

## How payment works

Fines can be paid at the police office, by bank transfer to the state treasury, or through designated channels. As a foreigner you will be asked for identification, and for rental bikes the rental agreement may also be examined. Keep receipts.

## Points and repeat offences

Decree 168 also introduced a licence-point deduction system for some violations, though helmet offences are point-free. Points matter mostly to licence holders — for foreign riders, the fine and the insurance consequences are the practical exposure.

## The part that matters more than fines

Every one of these violations also weakens your position after an accident. Insurance claims routinely fail on "rider was unlicensed / unhelmeted / over the alcohol limit." The fine is the cheap outcome.

*Information, not legal advice; fine amounts are revised by decree — always verify the current figures.*
