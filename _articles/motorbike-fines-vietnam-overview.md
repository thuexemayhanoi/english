Vietnam's motorbike fines were raised sharply by Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. This overview summarises the commonly enforced violations. The Ministry of Public Security publishes a summary of Article 7 fine levels, which is the source used here.

## Verified fine bands (Article 7)

| Violation | Fine (VND) |
|---|---|
| Running a red light | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Riding the wrong way down a one-way street | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Riding on the sidewalk (non-exempt cases) | 4,000,000 – 6,000,000 |
| Behaviours causing traffic accidents (failing to observe, wrong manoeuvre causing a crash) | 10,000,000 – 14,000,000 |
| Riding without a helmet or unfastened strap | 400,000 – 600,000 |
| Riding with alcohol below 50 mg/100 ml blood (0.25 mg/L breath) | 2,000,000 – 3,000,000 |

Higher alcohol bands carry substantially larger fines plus licence points. Several other violations — riding without a licence, phone use while riding, lane violations that do not cause an accident — are pending verification against the current decree text and are intentionally not quoted here yet.

## Points to understand

- Fines are per offence and can compound after an accident
- Foreign riders are subject to the same fines
- An invalid licence or alcohol involvement can also complicate insurance claims and liability — the paperwork after a crash usually costs more than the fine
- Fine levels have changed repeatedly in recent years; always check the current decree

## Practical takeaway

The most commonly fined tourist mistakes — wrong way down a one-way street, sidewalk riding to skip traffic, red lights "when it looks clear" — now each cost more than a month of scooter rental. Ride like the fines are real, because they are.
