The document list for renting a motorbike in Hanoi is short. Two items matter, and one of them is commonly misunderstood. Those two are what you bring yourself; the bike's own papers are a third group, and the law expects them on the road with you. This guide separates the documents needed to rent a motorbike in Hanoi, shown at the counter, from the papers you carry to ride.

## Show to rent, carry to ride

A rental raises two questions at two moments. At the counter it is whether the shop will hand over the keys; on the road it is whether you may ride at all.

| Moment | What is involved | Who sets the rule |
|---|---|---|
| At the counter | An identity document for the contract, a deposit, payment | The shop's own terms |
| On the road | Your licence, the bike's registration certificate, the owner's compulsory insurance certificate | Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 56 |
| Before you book | The licence class for the bike, and every expiry date | You, checked against the law |

Passport plus valid licence: that is the whole list of documents you bring yourself, once the shop has confirmed which identity document it accepts. Everything else a shop asks for is commercial, not documentary: the deposit, the payment method and the contract terms. The bike's own papers are different, because the law and not the shop expects them on the road. A shop accepting your paperwork is therefore not a ruling on whether you may ride, and the [rental requirements guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-rental-requirements-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) places these documents next to age, helmets and the deposit.

## Document 1: your passport

The shop needs an identity document to write the rental contract. Your passport is that document. It is normal for the shop to note the passport number on the agreement; it is not necessary for the shop to physically hold your passport for the whole rental - leave a cash deposit instead, which is standard practice.

Treat that as the usual case, not a rule: which identity document a shop accepts is set by its own contract terms, so confirm it when you book rather than at the counter. ### Questions to put to any shop

Four questions settle the counter side before you travel:

- Which identity document does the contract need, and is the passport enough by itself?
- What is recorded from it (the number, a photo of the page or a copy), and what happens to that record after the rental?
- Will the passport stay in your hands throughout the rental?
- Which deposit applies to this bike, and how is it paid? The typical deposit runs from 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 VND, depending on the vehicle and conditions; Nguyen Tu accepts cash, bank transfer and QR payment.

## Document 2: a licence valid for the bike

No document makes you legal except a licence Vietnam recognises. The three working combinations:

- A Vietnamese licence - under the 2025 law the [A1 class covers bikes up to 125 cc]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}), which includes most rental scooters (A1 licences issued before 2025 stay valid up to 175 cc).
- Your home-country motorcycle licence plus an IDP issued under the 1968 Vienna convention. Permits issued under other conventions, or a permit alone without the national licence, do not count.
- A national licence formally exchanged for a Vietnamese one, available to longer-term residents.

For bikes of 50 cc or less, no driving licence is required - the rider must be at least 16.

The combinations rest on Article 57(6) of Law 36/2024/QH15, cited here in the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH (the law as amended by Law 118/2025/QH15). The law took effect on 1 January 2025 (Article 88(1)), and Article 89(2)(a) lets an A1 licence issued before that date, if not yet exchanged, keep covering two-wheeled motorbikes from 50 cc up to under 175 cc.

### The moped class: no licence, but not no rules

The last line above holds for the moped class only, and the test is the legal definition, not the label on the bike. Article 34(1)(g) defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeled vehicle designed to go no faster than 50 km/h, with either a combustion engine of no more than 50 cc or an electric motor of no more than 4 kW; a bike over any one of those limits belongs to a licence class.

Article 59(1)(a) and (b) set the minimum age at 16 for a moped and 18 for classes A1, A and B1, and Article 56(4) adds that a moped rider must know the traffic rules, control the vehicle and meet the age and health conditions. The [50 cc licence guide]({{ '/articles/50cc-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) goes through the detail, and the [minimum-age guide]({{ '/articles/minimum-age-ride-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers age. Ask for the engine size or motor rating of the exact bike, because the model name does not settle the class. Historically the shop has offered 50 cc and electric bikes; ask us to confirm current availability before you plan around one.

### Which licence for which rider

The table sorts renters by the document they hold. Articles are those of Law 36/2024/QH15 unless the row names the Circular.

| You hold | You show and carry | What it covers | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Vietnamese licence | The licence | Class A1: two-wheeled bikes of 125 cc or less, or electric up to 11 kW; class A above that | Art 57(1), 57(6)(a) |
| A foreign licence (1968 Convention state) plus an IDP | Both, each fitting the bike | The motorcycle category shown on both | Art 57(6)(b), 57(8) |
| A foreign licence, no IDP, no treaty route | Not enough for a motorcycle | Nothing: a moped needs no licence, from age 16 | Art 34(1)(g), 59(1)(a) |
| A Vietnamese licence by exchange (long stay) | That licence | The matching class, within the term of the visa or card and foreign licence | Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Art 24(1)(a), (5)(a) |
| An IDP issued by Vietnam | Nothing for riding here | Not valid inside Vietnam | Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Art 30(1) |

Three points sit behind the rows.

**The pair must fit the bike.** Article 57(6)(b) recognises an IDP and a national licence issued by a country that is party to the 1968 Vienna Convention, each appropriate to the vehicle, and Article 57(8)(b) requires the holder to carry both. The permit adds no category that the national licence lacks. [Which countries' IDPs count in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/which-countries-idp-valid-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the states, and the [IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) explain the pairing.

**A treaty route is the exception.** Article 57(6)(c) and (d) add licences covered by a recognition treaty or agreement between Vietnam and the issuing country or territory, or by a treaty Vietnam has joined. Check any such route with an official source before relying on it; the [guide for foreign renters]({{ '/articles/can-foreigners-rent-a-motorbike-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) works through the cases.

**An exchanged licence is tied to dates.** It runs only for the term of the visa or residence document used and of the foreign licence (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a)); the [exchange dossier guide]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-documents-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists what an applicant files.

## What you do not need

- An IDP on its own. The permit only translates the licence; without the national licence behind it, riding on it counts as riding unlicensed. Read the line as "an IDP alone is not enough", not as "skip the IDP": with a foreign licence it is the second half of the pair.

- Insurance in your own name as a legal paper. Compulsory third-party cover attaches to the vehicle, and the owner normally holds it. What shops do not provide is insurance for you - arrange travel or medical cover yourself. Nguyen Tu does not provide motorbike insurance; check your own policy for motorcycle exclusions before riding. The owner's compulsory certificate is still a paper the law expects on the road, as the bike's papers below show.

- A residence card, work permit or student visa. Renting is not an immigration matter. Article 56 lists no residence document among the papers a rider carries, and a shop's own requests are contract terms, not legal ones, so ask what it wants when you book. The exception is the exchange route in the table above, which depends on a visa or residence card.

## Expiry dates to check before you book

A document that is valid on the day you book can lapse before the day you return the bike, and under Article 57(7)(a) a licence past its term is not valid, whatever the rental contract says. Four things set how long each document lasts:

- **Your national licence:** the expiry date printed on it.
- **Your IDP:** no more than three years from its issue date, and no later than the national licence behind it (Article 57(8)(a)).
- **An exchanged Vietnamese licence:** the term follows the visa or residence document used for the exchange and the foreign licence (Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(5)(a)).
- **A Vietnamese A1, A or B1 licence:** no time limit (Article 57(5)(a)).

The earliest date that applies to you ends your legal riding, so measure it against the day you return the bike, not the day you collect it. Imagine a renter whose IDP reaches its three-year limit a week into a month-long rental: the contract runs on for the rest of the month, but the permit stops counting after the first week. The [guide to a licence or visa that expires mid-rental]({{ '/articles/licence-expiry-during-long-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) covers the long-term side.

## Carrying documents while riding

When you ride, carry the licence and be able to show it at a stop. If the bike's registration papers are not with the bike, traffic police can hold the vehicle until papers are produced - one reason to rent from a shop that hands you the registration certificate with the keys. How a given shop handles that certificate is a question for handover and not an assumption, and [when police can hold a motorbike]({{ '/articles/police-impound-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) lists the cases.

Fine amounts sit in Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, as changed by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026; this page quotes none, so check any figure against the current decree. The [guide to the documents a rider must carry]({{ '/articles/documents-to-carry-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers what follows when a paper is missing.

### Papers that travel with the bike

Article 56(1) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out the papers a driver must have on the road, and for a rented bike they fall into two groups.

- **Yours:** the licence that fits the vehicle (Article 56(1)(b)) and, with a foreign licence, both the IDP and the national licence (Article 57(8)(b)).
- **The bike's:** the registration certificate (Article 56(1)(a)) and the vehicle owner's compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate (Article 56(1)(d)), which pays third parties and is not cover for you, as the [compulsory insurance guide]({{ '/articles/compulsory-motorbike-insurance-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains. Article 56(1)(c) adds an inspection certificate if the law requires one for the vehicle; Article 42(2) limits motorbike inspection to exhaust emissions, so ask whether one applies to your bike.

A certified copy of the registration certificate counts only for a vehicle mortgaged to a credit institution or foreign bank branch, and it travels with the original of that institution's confirmation still in force (Article 56(1)(a)); the article describes no other kind of copy. Article 56(3) lets a paper linked to the electronic identification account be produced through that account instead, but you cannot judge from the counter how the bike's papers were linked, so ask.

### What to ask at handover

1. Ask to see the registration certificate and compare the plate number on it with the plate on the bike.
2. Ask to see the compulsory insurance certificate and check that the period printed on it includes the days you will ride.
3. Ask how both papers would be produced if you were stopped, and who carries them while you ride.

If the bike is delivered instead of collected, the same checks apply at the door; delivery in Hanoi may be available, depending on the location and the arrangement made.

## What to photograph and keep

Before you leave the counter, record on your phone:

- The plate on the bike, with the registration and insurance certificates if you are shown them.
- Every page of the signed contract, including the deposit figure.
- Proof of payment: the bank-transfer confirmation or QR receipt, or, for cash, a line in the contract stating the amount.
- The bike's existing scratches and dents, following the [rental inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}).

Keep the originals of your licence and IDP on you while you ride, not in the bike's storage compartment. Store photos of your passport page, licence and IDP away from the phone you carry, for example in cloud storage: they help if something goes missing and do not replace the original at a stop.

## Quick answers

### Can I rent a motorbike in Hanoi with only my passport?

A passport covers the identity side of the contract, if the shop accepts it, and nothing more: it is not a licence.

### Can I show a photo of my licence on my phone?

Article 56(1)(b) lists the licence, and the only copy Article 56(1)(a) accepts is the certified registration copy for a mortgaged vehicle. The electronic route in Article 56(3) is for papers that have been linked to an electronic identification account, which a photo in your camera roll is not. Carry the originals.
