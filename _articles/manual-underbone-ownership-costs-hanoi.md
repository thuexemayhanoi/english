Owning a small underbone in Hanoi is one of the cheapest forms of motorised transport on earth — but "cheap" has a structure, and knowing where the money actually goes helps you budget honestly. This guide is about the cost structure; specific prices change over time, so where we quote figures they are manufacturer list prices from our verified model database, and everything else is framed by category.

## Purchase

New mainstream semi-autos list from roughly 18.8 million VND (Wave Alpha) through the low twenties (Blade, Sirius, Wave RSX) to around 30.6 million (Future 125 FI). Used examples cost a fraction of that, with the usual inspection discipline. The dealer-buying guide covers the new-purchase process.

## The running costs, ranked

1. **Fuel — the big one.** On verified manufacturer claims of 1.47 to 2.08 L/100 km across the mainstream class, even heavy daily riding consumes small amounts of petrol. Your riding habits move this more than your model choice: the fuel-habits guide explains how much.
2. **Oil and consumables.** Small engines take under a litre of oil per change; doing it by the schedule is the cheapest thing you will ever do for a bike. Chains and sprockets are wear items — cleaning and lubrication stretch their life dramatically.
3. **Tyres.** Underbone tyres are inexpensive but wear faster under Hanoi's daily grind and winter-rain conditions. Budget for a set every couple of years of heavy use.
4. **Compulsory insurance.** Mandatory for every registered motorbike, inexpensive for small engine classes — the insurance guide explains what it does and does not cover.
5. **Repairs.** The hidden benefit of owning the most popular machines on earth: street-mechanic labour rates are low and parts are on every corner. This category stays small if items 2 to 4 are not skipped.

## The hidden costs people forget

- **Parking**: paid lots in central districts are a small but constant drip; home parking needs to be secure.
- **Paperwork**: registration and inspection obligations come with any registered vehicle.
- **Theft exposure**: an underbone is the most-stolen vehicle class in the country. A disc lock is cheap; complacency is not.
- **Weather damage**: a covered parking spot or a bike cover extends the life of everything rubber and electrical.

## Renting versus owning

Renting makes sense for stays under a few months — monthly rates, no paperwork, no theft exposure, maintenance handled. Owning wins on longer horizons and full-time residence, once the purchase price has amortised. Our monthly-rental guide covers the renting side; if you are on the renting side of the line, contact us to confirm current availability.

Whichever side you choose, the economics of this class are unbeatable — which is exactly why half the country rides them.
