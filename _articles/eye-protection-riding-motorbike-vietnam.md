At 40 km/h, a fly hits your eye with real force, and Hanoi's dry-season dust gets under eyelids within minutes. A rider blinking through dust or rain is a rider riding blind in half-second instalments. Eye protection is not optional equipment here — it is basic vision maintenance.

## The options

A helmet visor is the best all-round solution: sealed against dust, clear against rain, and removable. Open-face helmets take visors too; without one, closed sunglasses or clear safety glasses are the minimum. Ordinary prescription glasses are far better than nothing but leak dust from the sides. Contact lens wearers should note that dust under a lens ends the ride — glasses or a visor over lenses are the safer combination.

## Clear versus tinted

Tinted lenses are for daylight only: at night they turn every oncoming light into a smear and every unlit bike into a ghost. Carry both, or use a visor with an internal drop-down sun shield. Night-riding-motorbike-safety-vietnam covers the wider dark-visibility problem.

## Keeping them usable

Dust film on a visor builds a starburst around every light at night; wipe the visor clean before evening rides. In rain, an anti-fog treatment keeps a closed visor from fogging from inside — riding-motorbike-heavy-rain-vietnam covers the wet-weather picture. A scratched visor, like a scratched windscreen, should be replaced: the optical distortion is a real hazard at speed.

## The low-tech rule

If you would not walk through a sandstorm with your eyes open, do not ride through one. Helmet-fit-guide-vietnam covers choosing the helmet the visor attaches to; what-to-wear-riding-motorbike-vietnam covers the rest of the kit.

## The one-line version

A visor or sealed glasses always, tinted only in daylight, wiped before night rides — your eyes are the only instrument you steer with.
