No. There is no learner permit for motorbike classes in Vietnam — no provisional licence, no L-plate, no supervised-on-road stage. The system runs on a different idea: you train off-road, you test, and only then do you ride on the road at all. The Law and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA both leave out a learner category for riders, and above the moped class the licence is needed from the first ride on a public road.

## What the texts say about a motorbike learner permit

Three places in the Law and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA come close to the idea of a learner permit. None of them is a licence for a motorbike learner.

| Provision | What it says | Why it is not a motorbike learner permit |
|---|---|---|
| Law, Article 56(5) | A person learning to drive a car, or sitting a car test, practises on a training or test vehicle and route, with an instructor or examiner backing up the steering | It names cars; no matching clause exists for motorbikes |
| Law, Article 60(7) | The Government sets how a "training vehicle permit" (giấy phép xe tập lái) is issued, reissued and withdrawn | The permit belongs to a vehicle, not a person, and the clause names no vehicle class |
| Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 24(1)(c) | A foreign temporary licence is not exchanged for a Vietnamese one | It governs foreign papers and creates no Vietnamese learner stage |

What the Law does say about riding is brief. Article 56(1) asks a driver on public roads for a licence that is valid, still has points and suits the vehicle, with an exception for moped riders (Article 56(4)). Article 57(1)(a) puts two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cm³ or 11 kW in class A1, and Article 59(1)(b) fixes the age at 18. Article 9 lists riding without the required licence (clause 1) and handing a motor vehicle to a person who falls short of the legal conditions for riding it (clause 7) among the prohibited acts. [The guide to lending a bike to an unlicensed rider]({{ '/articles/lending-motorbike-unlicensed-rider-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the owner's side.

## How learning actually works

The law (Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15) conditions motorbike riding on age, health and an appropriate licence — there is no intermediate permission. The one carve-out is the moped class, covered further down. What stands in for a learner stage is the training course. Since 1 September 2025, Circular 14/2025/TT-BXD of the Ministry of Construction fixes minimum training hours and skills before a candidate can even be registered for the test: class A now runs 32 hours including 20 theory and 12 practical (table below); A1 follows a shorter programme of the same structure. An earlier press report on Circular 14/2025/TT-BXD as first issued gave different practical hours; the figures here follow Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD. Completing the course — attendance verified — is the precondition for the exam.

The Law sets the same order from the test side. Article 61(1) lets a person register for a test only after completing the training programme and meeting the age and health conditions, and Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA turns that into paperwork: a training centre's confirmation of completion (or a primary certificate, or a confirmation of completing the programme) goes into the test dossier (Articles 15(2) and 16(2)(a)).

### The hours, as amended from 1 July 2026

Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD amends Circular 14/2025/TT-BXD and replaces the hours table in its Article 5(1) from 1 July 2026 (Article 16). For the motorbike classes the minimum is now:

| Class | Theory | Practical | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 9 hours (7 on road traffic law, 2 on driving technique) | 3 hours | 12 hours |
| A | 20 hours (16 on road traffic law, 4 on driving technique) | 12 hours | 32 hours |

The table counts each learner's own riding time and sets no riding distance for A1 or A. A completion confirmation issued before 1 July 2026 stays usable (Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD, Article 15(2)).

### Who teaches, and the "other forms" in the Law

Article 60(2) says a learner is trained at a driver-training facility or in "other forms of driver training" (a translation), and Article 60(7) leaves the Government to specify those other forms. The texts read here do not describe them, so this page cannot say whether a self-study route exists for a motorbike licence; ask a training centre or the traffic police department before relying on any shortcut.

## What this means practically

You cannot legally "learn on the road" on a motorbike in Vietnam. That is the position above the moped class; the moped class stands differently, as set out further down. A person with no licence riding around a quiet street to practise on a bike above the moped class is, legally, an unlicensed rider before the first wobble ends. [The no-licence fine guide]({{ '/articles/no-licence-fine-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the penalty side; its figures follow the 2024 decree text, not the Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP amendment. This page quotes no amounts, because the penalty decree, Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP, was changed by Decree 238/2026/NĐ-CP from 15 August 2026.

The practice happens inside the training centre's yard, on the same figure circuits the test uses. The Law's own vocabulary fits: it lets motorbike tests be held at a sân tập lái, a training yard, that meets the facility standards (Article 61(3)). Article 5 of Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA lists what such a yard must provide, including the test figures and automatic-scoring equipment.

### Where the texts are silent

For car learners the Law says how to practise on public roads (Article 56(5)). For motorbike learners the Law and the circulars read here say nothing of the kind: they do not say whether a private car park, an empty lot or a closed stretch of road is somewhere a person without a licence may ride, and this page cannot supply a rule they do not contain. [The monthly-rental learning guide]({{ '/articles/learning-to-ride-monthly-rental-hanoi/' | relative_url }}) sets out a graduated practice plan for riders who hold a licence valid for their bike or who ride in the moped class.

## The contrast with car licences

The car system historically had explicit learning documentation, and the training structure is similar. The Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD tables for cars also build a road stage into the course: class B and C1 learners have 24 to 48 hours of driving on public roads, depending on the class and the gearbox.

But the absence of any motorbike learner permit is the point visitors should register: in Vietnam there is no document that lets an unlicensed friend teach you on their scooter. The only legal route to a first ride is course, [test]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}), licence — or an [exchanged foreign licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}). Two things sit outside that line: the moped class, where no licence is needed from age 16, and a visitor whose own licence counts as valid in Vietnam through one of the routes in Article 57(6), who has no first-ride problem to solve.

## The route to a licence, step by step

For a rider with no licence, the texts put the steps in this order.

| Step | What the text says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Age and health | 18 or over for A1 and A, counted to the test date; health suited to the vehicle, shown by a health certificate | Law 59(1)(b) and 59(2); Circular 108, Articles 15(1) and 16(2)(b) |
| 2. Training | Complete the programme for the class at a training facility or in another form the Government regulates | Law 60(1)-(2); Circular 17/2026/TT-BXD, Article 1 |
| 3. Dossier | The training centre files the request and candidate list; each candidate adds the completion paper and a health certificate, and a foreigner also adds a copy of a qualifying residence paper | Circular 108, Article 16(1)-(2) |
| 4. Test | A theory paper on a computer, then riding tests in the yard; failing one part bars the next | Law 61; Circular 108, Articles 4 and 12(1) |
| 5. Licence | An electronic licence for those who pass, and a PET card on request | Law 62(1); Circular 108, Articles 18(2) and 19(1) |

[How to get an A1 licence]({{ '/articles/how-to-get-a1-licence-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) walks through the same route in plain language, and [what an A1 covers]({{ '/articles/a1-licence-vietnam-what-you-can-ride/' | relative_url }}) says which bikes the card allows.

### What the test looks like

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA, Article 4, gives the A1 and A test its shape: a computer theory paper, then riding tests in the yard, in the order theory, test 1, test 2. There is no road test for A1 or A, because Article 4(3) puts the on-road test on the car, truck and bus classes only, and a candidate who holds a valid car licence is exempt from the theory part (Article 4(1)(c)). Failing the theory paper bars test 1, and failing test 1 bars test 2; a theory pass is kept for one year (Article 12(1)).

The content rules do not all apply yet. Article 41(2) applies the test content set by Article 4(4) (for A1 and A, Appendix I) from 1 March 2027, except the content of practical test No. 2 from 1 January 2028, while Article 41(6) keeps the provisions of Circular 12/2025/TT-BCA on the form and content of tests in force to the end of 28 February 2027. So a test sat before 1 March 2027 follows the earlier circular's content rules.

Appendix I describes a theory paper of 40 questions in 27 minutes with a pass mark of 36 (from 1 March 2027), and a second yard test of 11 situations over two laps, scored out of 100 with a pass mark of 80 (from 1 January 2028). [The A1 test guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-a1-licence-test/' | relative_url }}) covers the test as candidates sit it today, so ask the centre which version applies on your test date.

## The moped class: the legal way to ride before you hold an A1

The moped class is the one lawful way to ride on a public road without a licence, and it starts at 16 (Article 59(1)(a)). Article 56(1) exempts moped riders from the licence requirement, and Article 56(4) puts a duty on the rider instead: knowledge of the road traffic rules, skill in handling the vehicle, and the age and health that Article 59 requires. No test or certificate is named. The Law asks for skill here without saying how the skill is gained or checked.

Whether a machine counts as a moped turns on its design speed and its engine or motor, not its name. [What counts as a moped]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-moped-vietnam-law/' | relative_url }}) sets out the definition, and [riding a moped at 16]({{ '/articles/riding-50cc-at-16-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers the age rule.

## A note for foreign residents

Foreigners sometimes ask whether a home-country learner permit or provisional licence carries over. It does not — a learner document is not a licence, and the exchange procedure requires a full national licence. Tourists are in the same position: a learner permit is not an IDP.

Circular 108/2026/TT-BCA points the same way. Article 24(1)(c) lists a foreign temporary licence (giấy phép lái xe tạm thời), an IDP and an expired foreign licence among the papers that are not exchanged; it does not name learner permits. Under Article 18(7), a foreigner with a national licence who needs to drive in Vietnam is to exchange it for the matching Vietnamese class, unless a treaty on driving licences that binds Vietnam says something different. [How foreigners exchange a licence]({{ '/articles/foreign-licence-exchange-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) sets out the dossier, while [the IDP rules]({{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}) cover visitors who rely on the routes in Article 57(6).

A foreigner with no licence at all who wants the Vietnamese test files the same dossier, with a copy of a residence paper added: a visa or temporary residence card that still has at least 90 days to run, a permanent residence card still in force, or a diplomatic or official identity card (Article 16(2)(c)). [The test-language guide]({{ '/articles/vietnam-licence-test-in-english/' | relative_url }}) covers the language question.

## Questions people ask

### Can a friend with a licence supervise me on a public road?

Nothing in the texts creates a supervised stage for motorbikes, so a learner above the moped class is still riding without a licence. Article 9(7) separately prohibits handing a motor vehicle to a person who falls short of the conditions for riding it.

### Do I have to take a course before the A1 test?

Article 61(1) lets only a person who has completed the training programme register for the test, and the dossier carries a completion paper. The Law also mentions "other forms" of training that this page cannot describe. The A1 minimum is 12 hours (9 theory, 3 practical) in the table in force from 1 July 2026.

### Does a motorbike learner permit from my home country work in Vietnam?

No. A foreign temporary licence is not exchanged (Article 24(1)(c)), and a learner document is not an IDP.
