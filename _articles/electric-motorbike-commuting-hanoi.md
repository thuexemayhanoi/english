The daily commute is where electric two-wheelers make the most sense in Hanoi: fixed distances, predictable hours, and a place to park at both ends. Here is how to make the routine work.

## Budget the range, not the claim

Work out your real weekly distance. A 10 km each-way commute is about 100 km per working week — comfortably inside the real-world range of most modern electrics, even after the haircut from speed and load. The Dat Bike Weaver 200's claimed 200 km at 35 km/h (130 km at 70 km/h) means a week of commuting on one overnight charge; smaller moped-class packs with 60-100 km claims need charging every night or two. Use our real-world [range guide]({{ '/articles/electric-motorbike-range-real-world-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) to convert your bike's claim into your commuting number.

## Fix the charging rhythm

- **Home socket at either end:** charge overnight, arrive full. This is the whole trick.
- **No socket:** battery swapping (9,000 VND per battery per swap at V-Green cabinets) turns fuelling into a one-minute stop.
- **Top-ups:** a lunchtime hour on the charger buys the evening's riding on fast-charging models (the Weaver 200 claims 100 km per hour of charge).

## Choose routes for the quiet advantage

Electric excels where stop-start punishes petrol: district lanes, school streets, short hops. On bridge and arterial routes, match the flow rather than the maximum — riding flat-out drains the pack and the advantage over a petrol scooter disappears.

## Keep the routine honest
