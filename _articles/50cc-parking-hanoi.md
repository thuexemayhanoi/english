Parking is the quiet half of 50cc ownership in Hanoi. Small, light bikes are the easiest machines in the city to move when parked badly — which cuts both ways: easy for you, and easy for someone who should not be moving it.

## The parking landscape

- **Official lots** — supermarkets, malls, hospitals, larger buildings — charge a small posted fee, issue tickets, and stack bikes in rows. Small bikes usually pay the standard two-wheeler rate.
- **Street-side informal attendants** — marked by a person and a cardboard sign — are everywhere in the districts. They watch, they arrange, and a helmet sometimes comes back with the bike, sometimes not.
- **School and market parking** — the daily reality for teenage riders: crowded, fast-moving, scratch-prone. The helmet goes with you, always.
- **Overnight** — under cover, off the street, ideally behind something. Our home-parking article covers the overnight standard.

## Small-bike theft reality

Light machines get lifted, not hotwired. The practical defences, in order of value:

1. **A disc lock or chain, every single stop** — the lock that lives under the seat is the lock that gets used. Our locks article covers the types.
2. **Park where attendants or foot traffic exist**, nose-in among other bikes rather than on the end of a row.
3. **Take the helmet with you or lock it** — helmet theft is the petty tax of street parking; our helmet-locks article covers the options.
4. **At night, the bike sleeps inside if the household can manage it.**

## The scratch economy

Hanoi parking is contact parking: rows shuffle, handlebars trade paint. Fold mirrors in, keep your bag off neighbouring bikes, and accept that a working bike with parking scratches is the local baseline.

## What leaves with you

Registration and insurance copies ride with you, not with the bike — our documents article covers the standard set. And the two-minute machine check at the lot gate (tyres visibly up, brakes grab, lights work) is covered in the pre-ride checklist.

## Etiquette that keeps you welcome

Do not block shopfronts, doorways, or the already-mythical "someone's spot" in front of a house — Hanoi's street is lived on, not just driven on. Ask where there is no sign. The fee is small; the goodwill is the actual product.
