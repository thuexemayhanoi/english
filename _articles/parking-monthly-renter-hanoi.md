Parking is a daily event for a monthly renter, and Hanoi handles it differently from most cities. Learn the system once and the rest of the term is easy.

## The paid lots

Most destinations in central Hanoi have organised motorbike parking: lots near markets, shopping centres, apartment basements and guarded street-side rows. Fees are typically a few thousand dong for a short stay and more for a full day. Someone watches the bikes, which is exactly what a renter wants — see keeping-rental-motorbike-secure-hanoi for why guarded parking matters more than locks alone.

## At home

If you are renting long-term, your building likely has a basement or a courtyard arrangement, sometimes with a monthly parking rate that is cheaper than daily payments. If your accommodation has nothing, look for a nearby garage that sells monthly parking. Sorting this out in week one saves a month of nightly worry.

## Street parking

Free street parking exists but comes with caveats: sidewalks where bikes are allowed, spots where they are not, and occasional towing from badly chosen spots. The local rules and common pitfalls are covered in renting-motorbike-old-quarter, and they apply city-wide in spirit even outside the Old Quarter.

## During Tet

If your rental runs through the lunar new year, parking patterns change — streets empty, many lots close, and residential areas get crowded with returning families. Monthly-rental-through-tet-hanoi covers that period separately.

## The one-line version

Organised lots by day, a guarded home spot by night, and caution with free street parking — build the habit in week one and the month runs itself.
