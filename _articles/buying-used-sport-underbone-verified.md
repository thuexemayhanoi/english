Sport underbones are the most ridden-hard, most modified, and most crash-prone bikes in the young Vietnamese market. A used Winner R or Exciter 155 can be a bargain — or someone else's discarded problem. The difference is a disciplined inspection.

## Why these bikes need extra scrutiny

Both are liquid-cooled, rev-happy six-speed manuals that their owners ride the way the styling suggests. That means the used population skews towards high-rev hours, amateur tuning and slid-down-the-road history more than any commuter underbone. The general underbone buying checklist applies; below are the sport-specific additions.

## Engine and cooling

- **Radiator**: straight fins, no bent-from-crash damage, no white residue at hoses, coolant at level and clean. Ask when it was last changed.
- **Oil condition**: check the oil level and colour. Honda publishes the Winner's oil capacity as 1.1 L at drain; unusually dark, thin or over-filled oil on a "fully serviced" bike tells you about the seller.
- **Cold start**: insist on starting it cold. A bike started ten minutes before you arrived hides cold-start rattles and smoke. Listen for big-end knock at idle (a knock that rises with revs, not just valve tick).
- **Smoke**: blip the throttle at the back of the shop — blue smoke means engine work is coming.

## Clutch and gearbox

Cycle all six gears stationary, then on a test ride feel for a grabbing, slipping or juddering clutch, and notchy or false-neutral shifts. Sport bikes get launched; clutches are consumables on them. Budget for a clutch replacement if the bite point is vague or the lever pulls oddly light.

## The crash history list

- Fairing panels: colour-match between panels, fresh paint over old scuffs, cracked mount tabs.
- Front fork seals and straightness (walk the bike, turn bar to lock, look for fork-leg alignment).
- Rear swingarm side-to-side play; wheel alignment by eye down the chain line.
- Frame and engine cases: road rash on the cases or bent lever-mounts tell the story the odometer does not.
- Aftermarket exhaust and air filters: ask what else was "tuned", and price the return to stock.

## Papers and provenance

Registration papers matching the frame number, the owner's ID matching the papers, and a service history if the owner has any. Odometer clocking is common on these bikes — a spotless 9,000 km example with worn grips, footpegs and chain sprockets is not a 9,000 km bike.
