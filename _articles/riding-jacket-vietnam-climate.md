The jacket nobody wears protects nobody. In Vietnam's heat, the real riding-jacket question is not "which armour rating" but "which jacket will I actually put on in April at 35°C" — and the answer shapes everything.

## Mesh first, armour inside

For the hot months, mesh jackets — open woven shells with impact armour at shoulders, elbows and spine — are the standard answer in tropical-motorcycling countries: air flows through, armour stays in place. The mesh protects against abrasion less than full textile, but it beats a cotton shirt by a wide margin, and it is wearable in heat, which is the whole game. Textile (Cordura-style) jackets suit the cooler north-winter months and rain protection; a wind-and-water layer over mesh is the modular local approach. The general dressing-for-Vietnam overview is in what-to-wear-riding-motorbike-vietnam.

## What actually matters in the fit

Armour that does not sit on the joint it protects is decoration. When trying on: shoulders covered while leaning forward, elbow cups centred on elbows with arms bent, cuffs long enough to meet gloves, back coverage while seated in a riding lean rather than standing straight. Sleeves that ride up in the tuck position are the most common failure of cheap jackets. Pairing with proper gloves completes the coverage picture — riding-gloves-vietnam-climate covers the hands.

## Rain and the two-season reality

Hanoi's summer downpours punish everything. A jacket's rain layer matters from May to October, and mesh-plus-rain-shell handles both seasons. Riding in the heat has its own physiological rules — hydration and pacing — covered in hot-weather-riding-motorbike-vietnam; a jacket you can tolerate is part of that discipline.

## The armour question

For riders who want more protection without a full jacket, separate impact armour worn under normal clothes is a real alternative for the style-conscious — that option is laid out in body-armour-motorbike-riders. Whatever route: certified gear from a proper shop, not marketplace replicas, because counterfeit armour is foam with confidence.

The best jacket for Vietnam is the one whose armour is on your body at the moment of the fall — which means the one you tolerate in heat.
