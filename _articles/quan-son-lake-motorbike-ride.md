Quan Son is the quiet alternative to the famous destinations in the same southwestern corner of Hanoi: an 860-hectare lake among limestone outcrops in My Duc district, roughly 40–60 kilometres from the centre depending on the approach. Where the Perfume Pagoda valley next door is polished and busy, Quan Son is still a local weekend spot — boats, lotus and karst ridges without the crowds.

## The ride

Head southwest out of the city toward My Duc on the same corridor used for the Perfume Pagoda; Quan Son lies further along the district roads. The last stretch runs through farm country on narrow but sealed roads. Count roughly 1.5 hours each way. If you start early you can pair it with the Perfume Pagoda valley for a full day, though most riders prefer Quan Son as the whole point — take the slow way around the lake roads.

## At the lake

The ecotourism area rents small boats for tours among the islands and coves — the limestone rising out of still water is the reason people compare it to a miniature Ha Long. On the lake’s edge, stilt restaurants serve fish and country food, and families picnic on the banks. In lotus season, from mid-May to mid-June, the shallow arms of the lake bloom thickly enough to ride a boat through — the single best time to visit.

## Practical notes

- Bring cash: the area is rural, with few card facilities.
- Midsummer afternoons bring thunderstorms — do the boat trip before lunch.
- Roads around the lake flood in prolonged rain; check conditions in the wet season.
