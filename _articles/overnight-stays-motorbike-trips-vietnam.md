Every overnight motorbike trip in the north has the same moment: you arrive in a town at dusk and ask — before you ask about the room — "where does the bike sleep?" Get that right and the trip is easy. Here is how experienced riders choose.

## The parking question

Ask these, in order, when booking:

- **Is there on-site parking?** City hotels have garages or contracts with nearby lots; homestays usually have a courtyard. "Park on the street" is the answer to avoid.
-
 **Is it covered?** Rain is a fact of northern nights — covered parking saves a wet saddle and a drowned electrics morning.
- **Is it watched overnight?** Attended lots and locked courtyards are the norm in tourist towns; confirm someone is around after the gates close.
- **Is it included?** Some hotels charge a small nightly fee for garage parking; homestays rarely do.

For what can go wrong and who bears it, the rental-security guide applies on the road as much as in Hanoi: use your steering lock, disc lock if you carry one, and take the helmet inside — helmets walk.

## Homestay versus hotel

The north’s best overnight areas — Hoa Binh lake villages, the Mai Chau valley, Thai Nguyen tea country, the Ninh Binh karst villages — run on homestays: stilt houses, family dinners, and courtyards where the bike sleeps ten metres from your door. Hotels win in the towns (Thai Nguyen, Viet Tri, Hoa Binh city) for comfort and price consistency. The lake regions blend the two: boat transfer to a village homestay is a standard trip structure at Thung Nai and around Hoa Binh lake.

## Booking pattern for riders

- Book the first night before you leave Hanoi in peak season (September–October and spring); wing it off-season.
- Tell the host you are on a motorbike — hosts then route you to the rider-friendly entrance and warn you about ferry/road closures they know about.
- Ask for a ground-floor or early-morning checkout if you want to ride at dawn; mountain mornings are the best riding of the day.
