## The legal position

Helmets are compulsory for every rider and every passenger on every motorbike, everywhere in Vietnam. Riding without one is a fine for the rider. The helmet must be a proper motorcycle helmet, not a construction hard-hat or a bicycle token - and it must be fastened. The rule sits in Article 33(2) of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15), and the text sets no age threshold: it applies to the person riding and to the person carried, a child included.

### What Article 33(2) says

The wording is from the consolidated text 55/VBHN-VPQH, which includes the amendments of Law 118/2025/QH15:

> Người lái xe, người được chở trên xe mô tô hai bánh, xe mô tô ba bánh, xe gắn máy phải đội mũ bảo hiểm theo đúng quy chuẩn kỹ thuật quốc gia và cài quai đúng quy cách.

In plain English, the rider and the person carried on a two-wheel motorbike, a three-wheel motorbike or a moped must wear a helmet that conforms to the national technical regulation and must fasten the strap correctly. Three things follow from the sentence itself:

- Who: the person riding and the person carried. The sentence names no minimum age and no minimum trip length, and it lists no exception: whether the fines decree excuses any case is answered in the helmet law guide.
- What: a helmet that conforms to the national technical regulation, which the next section describes.
- How: with the strap fastened correctly. A helmet resting on the head with the strap hanging free is not worn as the sentence describes.

A moped (xe gắn máy) is named in the same sentence, and Article 31(3) places the same duty on the rider and passenger of a motorised pedal bicycle (xe đạp máy). The penalty is a separate matter that sits in the fines decree, not in Article 33(2); the [helmet law guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) is the place to check it, so this page quotes no amount.

## The helmet standard behind the rule

Article 33(2) asks for conformity with the national technical regulation without naming it. For helmets worn on motorbikes and mopeds that regulation is QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, issued by the Ministry of Science and Technology through Circular 04/2021/TT-BKHCN. The points below come from a summary published by QUATEST 3, a testing centre that the Ministry designated to test and certify these helmets:

- Scope: helmets for riders of motorbikes, mopeds, motorised pedal bicycles, electric mopeds, electric bicycles and similar vehicles.

- Start date: from 1 January 2024, helmets made in Vietnam or imported must meet it before they go on sale; helmets certified or declared under the earlier QCVN 2:2008/BKHCN and already in circulation before that date could stay on sale until the end of 31 December 2024 (the Circular's Article 4 transition). The summary does not say how this treats a helmet already in use.

- Sizes: nine, by head circumference, from 460 mm to 620 mm, which the summary describes as running from child to adult.

- Visor: besides clear visors, the version covers light-tinted ones, with a light transmission of at least 50 per cent and a note on the visor that it is for daylight use only.

- Conformity mark: imported helmets must pass a state quality inspection and carry the CR mark before they are sold, and helmets made in Vietnam need a declaration of conformity.

Ask the seller or the shop to point to the label and the mark. A mark describes a model when it was made; it cannot show whether this particular shell has been dropped, baked in the sun or worn at the strap, which is why the handover checks below still matter. [Helmet safety standards in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/helmet-safety-standards-explained-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) explains the foreign marks, ECE and DOT, that appear on imported helmets.

There is one gap. The summary describes helmets placed on the Vietnamese market and says nothing about a helmet that a visitor brings from abroad for personal use. This page therefore cannot say whether a helmet certified only to a foreign standard satisfies Article 33(2) at a police stop. A helmet certified to Vietnam's national standard is the reading that matches the wording of the rule.

## What rental helmets are like

If a shop does supply helmets, the type and the range of sizes are the shop's to confirm, so ask for both before collection. Quality varies: a good shop keeps clean, undamaged, correctly-sized helmets; a corner shop may hand you a sun-bleached shell with a stretched strap. At handover, check three things: the shell has no cracks, the strap works, and it fits your head snugly without pressing. Ask for another if any check fails, and do not ride on a helmet you do not trust.

### The helmet check in six steps

The [rental motorbike inspection checklist]({{ '/articles/rental-motorbike-inspection-checklist/' | relative_url }}) walks round the bike; this is the helmet's share of the same walk-round. Do it in daylight, with the person who hands the helmet over still there, and do it for the passenger's helmet too.

1. **Label and mark.** Look for the label inside and for a conformity mark such as the CR mark (the regulation requires it on imported helmets). If the label has worn away or there is no mark, say so and ask for a different helmet.

2. **Shell.** Turn it in the light and look for hairline cracks, deep scratches, dents and a chalky or faded finish. A shell can look fine while the foam beneath is compressed, so also look at the liner for dents or crumbling.

3. **Fit.** Put it on. It should feel snug all round, with the front edge just above the eyebrows. Shake your head and push the helmet forwards and back: it should move your scalp, not slide over your forehead.

4. **Strap and buckle.** Fasten it, pull the strap firmly and release it. The buckle should click positively, and the strap should have no frayed stitching and should not be stiff or stretched. The [helmet fasteners guide]({{ '/articles/helmet-fasteners-retention-systems/' | relative_url }}) compares double-D rings and quick-release buckles.

5. **Visor and peak.** A visor should be clear enough to see through, free of deep scratches and held firmly at both pivots. A lightly tinted one should carry the daylight-only note, which means it is not meant for riding after dark.

6. **Pads and hygiene.** Smell it and look at the pads: stained, flattened or damp pads are a reason to ask for another helmet. Ask whether the pads can be washed or replaced.

## When to bring your own

- Long rentals: a month on a loaner helmet is a month of questionable fit and other people's history.
- Riders who care about standards: if you would not rent a car seat, do not rent a helmet for a long trip - bring a certified full-face or open-face helmet from home. Whether it fits in your hand luggage depends on the airline's size rules, so check before you fly.
- Passengers: ask whether the shop can provide two helmets; confirm when booking rather than discovering one at the kerb.

If you would rather buy than carry one from home, a helmet whose seller can show the label and the CR mark is the reading that matches the rule, though a mark says nothing about this particular helmet's condition. [Buying a second helmet for passengers]({{ '/articles/second-helmet-passengers-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers buying for the person on the back.

## Passengers and children

Every person carried needs a helmet of their own. Article 33(2) names the person carried alongside the rider, so one helmet for two people is not an option. Ask for the passenger's helmet in the same message as your own.

Article 33(1) separately limits who may be carried: a two-wheel motorbike or moped may carry only one person besides the rider, with up to two people carried in the listed cases, among them a child under 12 (Article 33(1)(c)). The [passenger rules guide]({{ '/articles/motorbike-passenger-rules-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) and the guide to [child passengers on motorbikes in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) cover those cases. The point for helmets is that the duty in Article 33(2) applies to everyone carried, whichever case they fall under.

Size is the practical difficulty. Nine head sizes exist, as the standard section above lists, so a child-size helmet is a regulated product and not a toy. Ask whether the shop has one for the child. If it does not, buy one before the first ride rather than loosening an adult helmet. [Choosing helmets for child passengers]({{ '/articles/kids-helmets-choosing-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers sizing and weight.

## Fit matters more than price

A helmet that is too large shifts on your head at speed and does nothing in a fall. Too small, and you will not wear it. Adjust the strap so two fingers fit under it snugly. This is the one piece of safety equipment in the whole rental that is genuinely yours to get right.

Hair, a cap, the arms of sunglasses and sweat on a hot afternoon all change how a helmet sits, so try it the way you will ride, with your head moving. [How to choose and fit a helmet in Vietnam]({{ '/articles/helmet-fit-guide-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) gives the full method. Test the loaner at handover, and bring your own if the rental is long or the fit is wrong.

## Cleaning and storage over a long rental

A helmet used for weeks needs a routine, whether it is borrowed or your own.

- After a wet or sweaty ride, unfasten it and stand it open, in shade and in moving air, so the pads can dry.
- Do not leave it on the mirror or the seat in the sun, where heat ages the shell and the straps and where it can be taken. [Helmet care, cleaning and replacing]({{ '/articles/helmet-care-replacing-guide-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers storage, and [helmet theft, locks and carrying]({{ '/articles/helmet-locks-theft-vietnam/' | relative_url }}) covers leaving one with a parked bike.
- Clean the shell and the visor with mild soap, water and a soft cloth rather than solvents or petrol.
- Repeat the six handover steps from time to time on a long rental, and again after any drop.

If the helmet belongs to the shop, cleaning is a shared question: ask whether you may wash the pads, and whether it would swap the helmet if it becomes unpleasant to wear. Photograph it with the bike at handover and again at return, so that both sides see the same helmet in the same condition.

### Do motorbike rentals include helmets in Hanoi?

Not as a confirmed item: nothing published by Hanoi Motorbike Rental Nguyen Tu says a helmet is supplied, so confirm before collection. The law requires a fastened helmet for each person.

### Can I use a helmet from home?

A helmet certified to Vietnam's national standard, QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, matches the wording of the rule. In either case the strap must be fastened.

Ask for another size or another helmet. If none is available, buy or bring one. Do not ride on a helmet that moves on your head, shows cracks or has a strap that will not hold.
