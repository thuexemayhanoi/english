Almost every 50cc bike in Vietnam fits one of two shapes, and choosing between them is the first real decision for a buyer — or the first thing to understand about the bike you already have.

## The step-through underbone

This is the shape Vietnamese streets are built around: an open frame with a flat footboard, a small fuel tank under or beside the seat, and a rotary-shift gearbox operated by your left foot. No hand clutch — you back off the throttle, click the gear lever, and open the throttle again. Riders who learned on a wave-style bike feel at home immediately.

Strengths: big wheels (usually 17 inch) that cope better with potholes, kerbs and unpatched alleys; simple mechanicals; easy to repair at any neighbourhood shop; light and narrow for filtering.

Trade-offs: you must learn the foot-shift rhythm, and the flat frame offers no enclosed storage.

## The scooter-style moped

The second shape looks like a small automatic scooter: bodywork enclosing the storage area under the seat, a twist-and-go throttle with a centrifugal transmission, smaller wheels, and often a front apron leg shield.

Strengths: nothing to shift — the easiest possible introduction to two wheels, which is why many parents prefer it for a first bike; under-seat storage for a helmet or a school bag; better weather protection for the legs.

Trade-offs: smaller wheels are more sensitive to potholes and wet paint; the enclosed CVT transmission needs its own care; body panels cost more to replace after a drop.

## Which suits which rider

- A 16-year-old who will ride mixed city roads with broken patches will usually be safer on big wheels — the underbone.
- A short rider, or someone doing short market-and-school runs, often prefers the low seat and simplicity of the scooter-style moped.
- Anyone planning to learn bigger bikes later benefits from the underbone's gearbox habits, which transfer directly to a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius.

## The skills either way

Whichever shape you choose, the first rides should be somewhere quiet, with the basic controls practised before joining Hanoi traffic.
