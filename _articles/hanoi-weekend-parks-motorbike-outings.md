Hanoi is dense, but it is not parkless — and almost every good green space in the city is best reached on two wheels. Weekend park-hopping by motorbike is a genuine local institution: load the family on the bike, ride twenty minutes, and spend the afternoon under the trees. Here is the rider's map of the city's green weekends.

## The central classics

- **Thu Le (Dong Da/Ba Dinh edge)**: the lake-and-zoo park, the classic family destination. Attended parking at the main gates; weekends are busy by 09:00, so arrive early or come after lunch.
- **Bach Thao botanical garden (west of the Old Quarter)**: the city's oldest green space — big trees, greenhouses and the Thuy Ta lakeside cafe culture. Small parking at the gates; go early, it is the calmest large park in the centre.
- **Hang Dau water tower garden and the Hoan Kiem waterfront**: pocket-sized parks, useful as breathing stops on a city loop rather than destinations.
- **Thong Nhat (Reunification) park corridor**: the long lake-and-tree strip through Dong Da, built for strolling and popular with runners; easy parking on the surrounding streets.

## The bigger escapes

- **Yen So park (southwest)**: the largest planted park in the city, built around water-treatment lakes — wide, modern, and often nearly empty on weekday mornings; on weekends the picnic mats appear in force. The ride out through the southern corridors is easy and fast.
- **The West Lake shoreline**: not a park exactly, but the loop functions as one — green, watery and rideable. Combine it with a stop at the lakeside park strips at the northeast corner.
- **Ecological areas further out**: for a full weekend, the parks merge into the day-trip territory toward Dong Mo and Ba Vi; those belong to the trips-from-Hanoi cluster, but they are the natural upgrade once the city parks feel familiar.

## The rider's park weekend

A satisfying pattern: morning ride to Yen So while it is cool, midday food stop back in the city, afternoon at Thu Le or Bach Thao, evening shoreline loop of West Lake. Total riding is under two hours across the whole day; the bike is the connector, not the event.

## Practicalities

- **Parking**: every major park has an attended lot; weekend mid-mornings are the crunch, holidays worst.
- **Heat**: from May to September, parks are morning-and-evening places; the midday sun makes both the riding and the sitting unwise.
- **Kids**: Hanoi's park weekends run on children on motorbikes — legally, a child passenger needs a proper helmet and the driver a licence; the child-passenger rules guide covers the specifics.
- **Rain**: park visits flip easily; half the lakeside parks have cafe cover within a two-minute ride.

The green Hanoi is a real city, not a rumor — and it has better parking than the museum district. Spend a weekend proving it to yourself.
