The charger is the least glamorous component on an electric motorbike and one of the most expensive to get wrong. Vietnamese humidity and heat attack chargers faster than batteries, and a failing charger quietly damages the pack it feeds. Care is unglamorous too: keep it dry, cool, matched and cabled properly.

## One pack, one charger

Use the charger the machine came with. Packs, battery-management systems and chargers are designed as a matched set — voltage curves, cut-off points, connectors — and a borrowed or generic substitute is how packs get overcharged or chronically undercharged. If a charger dies, replace it with the manufacturer's part for that model, not the market's cheapest similar wattage. For VinFast-class swap riders this matters less day to day (the cabinet does the charging), but the home charger still deserves the care.

## Humidity and heat

Hanoi's damp season kills electronics by corrosion. Keep the charger off floors, away from windows and never in a locked metal box in the sun. After charging, unplug and store it indoors in a dry spot — a charger that lives permanently on a humid balcony socket is a charger on a countdown.

## Cables and connectors

- Coil the cable loosely, never tight or knotted — kinked cables fail at the kink.
- Route so nothing pinches the cable in a door or under the bike.
- Check both connector faces monthly for green corrosion or blackened pins; both are early signs of resistance heating.
- Replace damaged cables with manufacturer parts — tape is not a charging repair.

## Charging habits that protect both boxes

Charge where you can see it, on a hard surface, away from bedding and sun. Do not charge a heat-soaked pack (let it cool, especially in summer). And never charge a machine that has just been soaked — the washing and flood articles explain the drying routine.

## Storage between rides

For bikes that sit — a long holiday, a winter break — the advice in the long-storage guide applies to the charger too: store it dry and indoors, and run a maintenance charge on the pack as that article describes. When you return, the machine that starts is the one whose charger was treated like part of the bike, not an accessory to lose.
