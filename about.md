<h2>How legal content is researched</h2>
<p>Vietnam rewrote its core road traffic law with effect from 1 January 2025 (the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, No. 36/2024/QH15), and restructured its administrative fines with Decree 168/2024/NĐ-CP. Licence classes, fine amounts and rules for foreign riders all changed. Guides written before those changes are wrong in important ways, which is why the legal articles on this site were checked against the actual legal texts.</p>
<p>The research priority for legal content is:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Vietnamese official legal texts — the full published text of the law, decree or circular itself</li>
  <li>Vietnamese government and ministry sources — official portals and police publications</li>
  <li>Official international treaty sources, where a topic involves a convention such as the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic</li>
  <li>Secondary sources — used only for discovery and context, never as the evidence for a published legal claim</li>
</ol>
<p>The current legal guides were reviewed against primary sources, and each records when it was last reviewed. That matters for topics like the <a href="{{ '/articles/vietnam-motorbike-licence-classes-explained/' | relative_url }}">licence classes</a>, the <a href="{{ '/articles/idp-vietnam-motorbike-rules/' | relative_url }}">International Driving Permit rules for foreigners</a>, the <a href="{{ '/articles/helmet-law-vietnam/' | relative_url }}">helmet law</a> and the <a href="{{ '/articles/motorbike-fines-vietnam-overview/' | relative_url }}">fine levels</a>, where getting a number wrong could genuinely cost a reader money or their licence.</p>
<p>Two honest limits: the people who write and check these guides are not lawyers, and the content is informational, not legal advice. Laws change, decrees are amended, and the authorities' implementation of edge cases (like licence exchange for foreign residents) evolves. Where a rule is uncertain or in flux, the guides say so rather than presenting a guess as settled law. For decisions that matter, verify with official sources or the authorities.</p>

<h2>Technical and motorbike content principles</h2>
<p>Maintenance and specification content follows similar rules. Manufacturer documentation is preferred for model specifications; where a figure is not available from a reliable source, the article says so rather than inventing one. Concretely:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Torque values, oil capacities, electrical values and service intervals are never guessed. If a procedure needs a model-specific number, the guide points to the manufacturer's documentation or a qualified mechanic rather than publishing an unverified figure.</li>
  <li>General maintenance guidance (chain care, tyre pressure habits, pre-ride checks) is clearly presented as general guidance, not as a model-specific service procedure.</li>
  <li>Practical owner experience is labelled as practical guidance, not as manufacturer specification. For example, on long rentals the owner historically recommends periodic checks around every 1,500 km or more of use — that is useful practical advice, and it is presented as exactly that, not as a factory service interval.</li>
</ul>
<p>Riding in Hanoi daily is hard on bikes — dust, heat, humidity, constant stop-start traffic — so maintenance matters more here than in gentler conditions. The <a href="{{ '/topics/maintenance/' | relative_url }}">maintenance guides</a> are written with that reality in mind.</p>

<h2>Why this site exists</h2>
<p>Most English-language information about riding in Vietnam is fragmented. Rental shop pages rarely explain licence law; legal summaries rarely explain what a semi-automatic bike is; travel blogs rarely connect any of it to maintenance or safety. A rider arriving in Hanoi has to assemble a picture from a dozen sources of uneven quality, several of which still describe the pre-2025 licence classes.</p>
<p>This guide exists to put that picture in one place, in English, from a business that rents bikes to foreign riders every day and sees the practical consequences of bad information — people riding without the right licence, without understanding the helmet rules, or on a bike that does not suit their experience. The goal is that after twenty minutes on this site, a first-time visitor knows what they can legally ride, what it costs roughly, what to check on the bike, and how to get help. The <a href="{{ '/faq/' | relative_url }}">FAQ</a> condenses that into quick answers, and the <a href="{{ '/articles/' | relative_url }}">guides</a> go deeper where depth is needed.</p>
